## Why

The strongest triple convergence of the entire run: all three personas independently found the
same sophisticated flaw in the article's central inferential claim — that a $242B combined
tariff-refund + interest increase "exceeded" a $147B spending increase, implying spending growth
is slowing. All three confirmed every individual dollar figure is accurate (the ~$2.1T deficit,
$125.2B refund, $117B/14% interest rise) but found the "exceeds" comparison invalid for two
compounding reasons: (1) a time-window mismatch — the $117B interest figure covers 10 months
(Oct-Jul), the $147B spending figure covers 11 (Oct-Aug), undisclosed in the article; (2) a
category error — the tariff refund is a receipts-side item under standard federal budget
accounting (confirmed directly against Treasury MTS receipts lines), not spending, so summing it
with an outlay item and netting against an outlays-only total misclassifies it.

**`askamerica`** verified this directly against its own warehouse table
(`econ.mts_outlays_by_function`, Treasury MTS Table 9), independently arriving at the same
time-matched 10-month spending increase (~$309B) that Fox Business/CBO separately reported —
strong cross-validation between the warehouse and independent secondary reporting on the corrected
figure. Also independently surfaced the calendar-shift confound (Aug 1, 2026 falling on a
Saturday).

**`expert`** matched this closely and added a genuinely sharp additional finding neither other
persona made: the YTD-through-August FY2026 deficit ($1,966B) was actually *slightly smaller* than
FY2025's comparison point ($1,973B) — meaning the article's entire "worsening deficit" framing
rests on an unpublished September projection, not on the data actually reported so far. It also
gave the most precise account of the calendar effect: since both July and August sit inside the
same 11-month comparison window, the shift only reallocates dollars *within* the window and
doesn't change the cumulative $147B figure — a sharper correction than a looser "this explains the
deceleration" framing would give.

**`everyman`** was first to surface the core flaw (mismatched windows, category error, calendar
confound) with strong, well-sourced reasoning, matching both other personas' substantive
conclusion.
