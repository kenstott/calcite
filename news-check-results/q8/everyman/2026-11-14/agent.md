# Fact-check: ABC30 / ABC News, "Thanksgiving travel: Record number of travelers expected to fly, take trains, drive this Thanksgiving holiday" (Nov 11, 2026)

Method note: WebFetch returned only a summary of the article, not verbatim text, so no attributed quotes could be extracted word for word. The one quoted phrase I could confirm is American Airlines calling Nov 29 its "largest operating day since the pandemic". Checks used web search only. Several checks rest on search-tool summaries of the sources, not on pages I read directly. Treat verdicts as "consistent with" unless marked otherwise.

## Not checkable here
Claims 3 (the 39% and 30% figures), 7, and 15. Also the article's own wording was not extracted verbatim, so quote accuracy was not tested.

## Materially misleading or missing context
- The headline says a "record number of travelers" are expected, but the figures cited are forecasts and company projections. They are not counts of actual travel.
- Several "records" are narrow. American's record is its busiest day since the pandemic, which is not an all-time record. Amtrak's record is an expectation, not a published figure.
- The headline mentions driving, but the cited data covers only air and rail. AAA's forecast, which is the main source for road travel, is not in the piece. I could not find a 2026 AAA forecast. Most travelers drive.
- The 39% fare increase appears alongside the 2.2% flight growth. The figures may mix different measures (domestic roundtrip vs average fare), and Hopper's headline figure I found is 31%.
- Most data comes from companies that benefit from promoting demand, including airlines and Amtrak. This is not disclosed.

## Rating
**1 Pinocchio.** The checked claims line up with company releases and Hopper coverage. The weakness is that the "record" framing leans on projections, the 39% fare figure could not be confirmed, and the driving data promised in the headline is absent.
