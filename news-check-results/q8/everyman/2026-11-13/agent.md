## Possible misleading framing

- The headline's "eases" rests on a quarter-over-quarter move (about 5.6% to 5.2%). Unemployment is still well above the 3.6% of March 2019 and above the roughly 4% overall rate, so "easing" is modest; the piece does include the 2019 comparison.
- Quarterly figures are noisy; one quarter is not a trend.
- Underemployment rising while unemployment falls means some of the drop may reflect grads taking non-degree jobs. The article notes this tradeoff.
- Rating is tentative because the full article text and any omitted paragraphs were not seen verbatim.
