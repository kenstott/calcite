# Fact-check: WJLA/National News Desk, "Among the latest cyber vulnerabilities: Attacks on water systems" (Kristine Frazao, Nov 23, 2026)

Method: WebFetch of the article (syndicated copies) plus WebSearch. CSIS and The Hill returned 403, so those were checked only through search snippets. I did not read the full FBI PSA myself, only search summaries of it.

## Claims and verdicts
1. FBI and EPA warned in July that malicious actors are targeting the water and wastewater sector via internet-facing PLCs. SUPPORTED. FBI alert (fbi.gov, 2026) is dated about July 30. It covers Rockwell/Allen-Bradley MicroLogix 1100/1400 controllers. Utilities in at least 7 states reported incidents since July 27.
2. Attackers changed IP addresses and passwords, causing loss of monitoring and control. CONSISTENT with the advisory summary, but I did not see the primary text. Partially verified.
3. Recommended defenses (remove PLCs from the internet, gateways and firewalls, strong passwords, ACLs). Standard advisory content. Plausible and consistent. Not read in the primary source.
4. "Of the 12 states said to be attacked, nine have publicly confirmed" (MI, MN, GA, NJ, SD, UT, OR, AR, AL). PARTLY CHECKED. Search results repeat the same figure from syndicated copies of this same article. The FBI's own count is "at least seven states". The 12-state figure has no primary source. Treat it as an unverified press tally.
5. An IRGC-linked actor, CyberAv3ngers, claimed responsibility ("warn America to back down"). The article says CSIS attributes this. Press summaries say the attribution comes from CSIS and press, not from the FBI/EPA advisory, and the claim is not an official attribution. The article words it correctly as a claim. The CSIS page was inaccessible (403).
6. Taylar Rajic (CSIS Strategic Technologies Program) called water a high-value target with far-reaching effects. Attributed opinion. Not independently confirmed because the CSIS page was blocked.
7. Rep. James Walkinshaw (D-Va.) warned of cyber threats to critical infrastructure. Attributed statement. Not verified in a primary source.
8. DNI Jay Clayton was asked whether Americans should stock up on water. CLAYTON'S ROLE VERIFIED: ODNI says he was sworn in as the ninth DNI on Aug 3, 2026, after Gabbard. The specific interview exchange is NOT CHECKABLE HERE. I found no transcript. A fetched copy of the article names the host as "CBB host Dana Bash", which looks like a typo for CNN or CBS.
9. VP Vance: "We do not see some crisis coming down, but you have to be prepared for everything." QUOTE MATCHES reporting (Fox News/Ingraham, Nov 16, per search summary; The Hill headline "We don't see a crisis coming down"). The primary video was not viewed.
10. Aging infrastructure makes more disruptions likely. General expert opinion. Not a checkable fact.

## Rating: 1 Pinocchio (mostly accurate, minor gaps)
Core facts hold: the advisory, its date, the attack vector, and the officials' titles and quotes. Not primary-verified: the 12 and 9 state counts, and the Clayton exchange.

## Misleading or context concerns
- The "12 states / 9 confirmed" tally exceeds the FBI's "at least 7". The article does not say who counted or reconcile the two.
- Pairing the Vance and DNI "stock up" remarks with the water attacks implies a link to a looming crisis. Reporting I found says the remarks were about general tech outages and doomsday social-media predictions. Authorities have not announced evidence of drinking-water contamination.
- The attribution to Iran rests on a group's own claim and on think-tank analysis, not on an official government attribution.
- Minnesota alone reported more than 30 systems targeted. This scale is absent from the piece.

## Not checkable here
- The Clayton and Bash interview exchange.
- The 12-state figure.
- The Walkinshaw and Rajic quotes, which I did not verify in primary text.
