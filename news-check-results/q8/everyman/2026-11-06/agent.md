# Fact-check: NPR, "A new study gives us a clearer picture on how many people have been deported" (Oct 23, 2026)

Method note: npr.org fetch timed out. I read NPR member-station syndications (KAXE) only as a tool-generated summary, not verbatim text, and cross-checked against reporting on the underlying Migration Policy Institute (MPI) study (Reuters, AP, Newsweek, Maryland Matters, MPI press-release summaries via search). MPI's own pages returned 403 and its PDF was unreadable, so I verified through secondary coverage of the study. NPR's exact wording and any direct quotes were not available, so quote-level checks are not done.

## Materially misleading or missing context

- "Deported" mixes formal removals with border turnbacks and voluntary departures after arrest. The 980,000 is not directly comparable with the 1.86M for 2000, or with the administration's figures, which count removals differently.
- The "20% have criminal convictions" figure is easy to read as favorable or unfavorable. The more informative split is that among long-term residents, 71% had no conviction. The summary I saw gave only the 20%.
- The self-departure range is wide and uncertain, as MPI itself states. It is a modeled estimate, not a count.
- The piece sets the 3 million government claim beside the MPI estimate without noting that the two are not measured the same way.

## Overall rating: 1 Pinocchio on the 0-4 scale (essentially accurate, one point for loose framing)

I found no factual errors in the claims I could check. The main weakness is the lack of definitional care around "deported". This rating is provisional because I could not read NPR's full text or any direct quotes.
