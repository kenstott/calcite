# Fact-check: Virginia Tech News, Thanksgiving meal and farmer share (Nov 2026)

Method note: WebFetch returned only a summary of the article (it refused verbatim text), so claims below are as summarized, and exact wording of the one quote could not be verified. No Python or charts were used.

## Claims and verdicts

1. Typical 2026 Thanksgiving dinner costs about $80 ($8 per person). PARTLY CHECKABLE. The Farm Bureau (AFBF) 2026 survey is not published until Nov 18, 2026. The 2025 AFBF expanded meal was $77.09 ($7.71/person), so $80 is plausible but cannot be confirmed against the 2026 source. Not checkable here (2026 figure).
2. Farmers get about 19% of the retail dollar (2024 data). SUPPORTED. USDA ERS Food Dollar: food-at-home farm share 18.5 cents in 2024 (rounds to 19%).
3. About $15 to farmers of the $80, $65 to marketing. ARITHMETIC OK (19% x 80 = 15.2).
4. Take-out: farmers get about 7%, roughly $53 of $750 for 10 people. SUPPORTED. ERS food-away-from-home farm share was 7.1 cents in 2024; 7% x 750 = 52.5. Caveat: 7.1% is the average for all food away from home, not take-out specifically.
5. Cracker Barrel meal $180, farmers get about $13. ARITHMETIC OK (7% x 180 = 12.6). The $180 price and the meal's contents are not independently verifiable (restaurant pricing, no primary source).
6. Households spend about 56% of food dollars dining out. ROUGHLY SUPPORTED. ERS: food away from home was 58.9% of food spending in 2024 and 56.3% in 2025 (per a later ERS page), so 56% matches the latest year; "households" vs total-economy spending basis is a minor mismatch.
7. Davis quote: "we are mainly buying convenience or marketing services, not food." Attribution to George Davis, VT agricultural economist with joint appointment (AAEC and HNFE), is consistent with the page. Quote wording not verified verbatim. Opinion/interpretation, not a factual claim.

## Overall rating: 1 Pinocchio (minor issues; essentially accurate)
Numbers trace to USDA ERS data and the arithmetic is right.

## Potentially misleading context
- ERS reports two farm-share figures: 5.8 cents per dollar of all food spending, and 11.8 cents for domestically produced food (2024). The 19% and 7% figures are the at-home and away-from-home components; a reader seeing "19%" may not know the all-food average is far lower. ERS also revised methodology in 2026, so figures are not comparable with older releases.
- Applying the average food-at-home farm share to a turkey-dinner basket is an approximation; turkey, produce and processed items have very different farm shares.
- Same-year mismatch: 2024 farm-share data paired with a 2026 meal cost.
- "Mainly buying convenience, not food" is framing: restaurant costs also cover labor, rent and service, which are real costs.

Sources: ERS Food Dollar summary findings (ers.usda.gov/data-products/food-dollar/summary-findings); ERS food prices and spending; AFBF Thanksgiving survey (fb.org/2026-thanksgiving-dinner-cost-survey).
