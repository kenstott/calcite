# Fact-check: News4Jax/AP, "Trump plans a 20% tariff on Chinese goods and new import taxes for the EU and a dozen other major trading partners" (2026-11-25)

Caveat: WebFetch returned only a summary of the article (it refused verbatim text), so quotes were not extracted word for word. The claims below come from that summary. I checked them against CNBC, Boston Report and a web-search roundup, which relay USTR/Reuters. I could not open the USTR primary documents.

## Claims and verdicts
1. USTR (Greer) proposed Section 301 tariffs on 14 economies over "market distorting practices"/overcapacity. SUPPORTED. The 14 are China, the EU, Japan, South Korea, Switzerland, Taiwan, Vietnam, India, Singapore, Bangladesh, Cambodia, Indonesia, Malaysia and Thailand. That is China, the EU and 12 others, which matches the headline.
2. Rates run 15% to 35%. SUPPORTED. Vietnam is up to 35%, India up to 25%, Singapore up to 20%, and the five Southeast/South Asian economies up to 18%. The EU, Japan, South Korea, Switzerland and Taiwan are up to 15%. The rates are "up to" caps. Lower rates apply if a deal is reached, for example Vietnam 20% and India 17%.
3. China gets 20%, stacking to about 45% on some goods. SUPPORTED. The 20% sits on top of about 25% of existing tech-transfer tariffs. Some goods get up to 10% instead. Another source says it is "up to 20%", which differs slightly from the article's flat "20%".
4. About $30 billion of Chinese goods exempt. SUPPORTED, via Reuters as relayed by Boston Report.
5. US-China truce expires Jan. 10, 2027. SUPPORTED.
6. Mexico and Norway are under investigation with no findings yet. SUPPORTED.
7. Hearing Jan. 25 (2027). Effective date likely February or March. SUPPORTED. Greer said "a couple months" at the earliest. Written comments are due Jan. 5.
8. Brazil gets a 10% tariff on aircraft (Embraer) and higher sugar duties. SUPPORTED as a separate same-day action. Brazil is not among the 14 economies. A reader could take it as part of the same package.
9. Supreme Court ruled in February that Trump could not use the economic emergency for across-the-board tariffs. PARTLY CHECKED. A CNBC link is dated Feb. 20, 2026, consistent with that.
10. Duties are meant to replace lost revenue. PARTLY SUPPORTED. The Section 122 10% tariff expired after 150 days, and forced-labor 301 tariffs of 10% to 12.5% replaced it. I did not confirm that "revenue replacement" was the stated purpose.
11. Taiwan and South Korea semiconductors and high-tech goods exempt. NOT CHECKABLE HERE. Boston Report says some products are exempt but does not list them.
12. Duties could cover about three-fifths of US imports. NOT CHECKABLE HERE. No source I read gives a share. Two sources note that the forced-labor tariffs cover 60 economies and 99.4% of imports, so the figures are easy to confuse.
13. Goldman Sachs: tariff rate rises from 7.8% to 9.2%. NOT CHECKABLE HERE.
14. Sen. Wyden criticism, We Pay the Tariffs opposition, Brazil retaliation threat, China foreign ministry opposition. NOT CHECKABLE HERE. These are plausible reactions, but I did not find the statements.

## Rating
1 Pinocchio (mostly accurate). Every checkable core fact matches. The issues are framing: the rates are caps and proposals, not enacted, and Brazil is a separate action.

## Possibly misleading
- These are proposals with comment and hearing periods, not imposed tariffs.
- A flat "20%" overstates certainty, because some goods get a lower rate and the rates are "up to".
- Whether the 20% stacks on existing duties is ambiguous across sources. Greer called most rates "not really additive" but said China is the exception.
