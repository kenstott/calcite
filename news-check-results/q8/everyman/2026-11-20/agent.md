# Fact-check: Fortune, "The seeds of the next recession are being sown in today's booming economy" (Nov 18, 2026, Eleanor Pringle)

Method: WebFetch returned only a summary of the article (not verbatim), so claims are as summarized. Verification used web search; I could not open primary pages (BEA, ADP, NY Fed, FRED, BLS) directly. Most checks rest on search-result summaries of those sources.

## Claims

| # | Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Q3 2026 GDP grew 3.4% | Supported. BEA advance estimate (Oct 29): 3.4% annual rate. Second estimate is due Nov 25. |
| 2 | GDP above 2% every quarter in 2026 | Partly checked. Q3 3.4% and Q2 2.2% confirmed. Q1 not checked. |
| 3 | Unemployment steady near 4% | Roughly supported. Reports put September at 4.2%, up from 4.1%. "Steady" is a little generous. October figure not found. |
| 4 | Sahm Rule at 0.00% for September and October | September 0.00 supported (FRED, via search). October not found. |
| 5 | S&P 500 up 7% year to date | Not checked. |
| 6 | Goldman: about $500B AI-related debt issued in 2026 | Supported. Goldman Research estimate is near $500B. |
| 7 | Goldman: $420B more expected in 2027 | Partly supported. $420B appears in Reuters, citing Goldman data, as hyperscaler borrowing in 2027. Other coverage says $440B, and broader AI-linked debt is put near $1.2T. The article seems to apply a narrower figure to the $500B broader total, so the comparison is apples to oranges. |
| 8 | ADP: small businesses added 28,000 jobs in October vs 24,000 for large firms | Supported. ADP shows small firms +28k, medium +33k, large +24k, total +86k. |
| 9 | NY Fed puts recession probability before October 2027 at 12.4% | Plausible but unconfirmed. The figure appears only in a secondary blog post. It comes from the NY Fed's yield-spread model, from an October spread of about 0.98 points. The NY Fed page was not reached. |
| 10 | Goldman forecasts 2.7% GDP growth in 2027 vs 2.2% consensus | Not checkable here. No primary source found. |
| 11 | Mericle quote ("Growth should follow the same pattern in 2027 as in 2026") | Not checkable here. |
| 12 | Pearce / Oxford Economics note "Icarus is flying towards the sun", with the quotes "deeper and more painful" and "barely begun" | Not checkable here. Oxford Economics notes are paywalled. Attributions are the article's own. |
| 13 | Oxford data show small firms' share of gross job gains falling through 2026 | Not checkable here. It is in tension with ADP's October small-business gain. The article itself notes this. |
| 14 | Dot-com comparison: job losses lasted well after GDP recovered | Historical claim, broadly consistent with the 2001-03 "jobless recovery". Not checked in detail. |

## Rating: 1 Pinocchio (minor issues)

The numbers that could be checked mostly hold. Problems and caveats:
- The $500B and $420B figures likely measure different things, so reading them together overstates or misframes the 2027 comparison.
- "Steady near 4%" glosses a rise to 4.2%.
- The small-business hiring worry is partly undercut by ADP data that the piece itself cites. It frames a mixed picture as a vulnerability.
- The recession argument is forecasting and opinion. A "late-cycle" warning cannot be fact-checked.
- The 12.4% figure is shown without noting that the yield-spread model has produced false alarms.

Not checkable here: claims 10 to 13, plus claims 2 (Q1 only), 4 (October only) and 5.
