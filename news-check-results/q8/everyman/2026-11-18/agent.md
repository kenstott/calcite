# Fact-check: Grist/Yahoo "Nitrates in drinking water are a 'public health crisis,' environmentalists warn" (Nov 17, 2026)

Method: WebFetch of the Yahoo page (returned only a summary) and the Grist original, plus WebSearch of coverage (Food & Water Watch press release, EWG, Iowa Public Radio, WBEZ, New Lede, KCRG). I did not open the petition PDF, the Environment Research paper or EPA's Federal Register notice directly, so several items rest on secondary reports.

## Claims
| # | Claim | Verdict | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Coalition (NRDC, UCS, others) petitioned EPA on Monday to cut nitrate limit 10 to 1 mg/L | Accurate | Petition dated 2026-11-16 (Monday); multiple outlets and FWW press release |
| 2 | 10 mg/L dates to 1975, formally adopted 1991, mainly to prevent blue baby syndrome | Accurate (simplified) | Consistent across coverage; 1975 was the interim standard, 1991 the final rule. Primary Federal Register text not opened |
| 3 | Petition says nitrates "causing a public health crisis" | Accurate as a quote of the petitioners | Attributed correctly; it is advocacy language |
| 4 | Studies link nitrate to colorectal cancer, thyroid disease, fetal abnormalities | Accurate but unqualified | Association evidence; the article does not say evidence at 1-10 mg/L is mixed and causation is not settled |
| 5 | 2019 study: up to 12,594 cancer cases/yr attributable | Accurate number, misleading framing | Temkin et al., Environmental Research 2019, by EWG (a petitioner). It is a modeled upper bound, not observed cases, and the article does not name its author or say EWG is a petitioner |
| 6 | One in three Americans (about 120M) served water above 1 mg/L | Petitioners' estimate, reported as fact | Matches the petition/press release; not independently verified. Note: 120M is about 36% of the US population |
| 7 | Cost $94.5B over 50 years, benefits $364B | Accurate as reported, source is partisan | One Water Econ report commissioned by petitioners. Benefits are modeled and the report says they are not complete |
| 8 | EPA completed review in July 2024, found nitrate rule "not a candidate for revision" | Accurate | EPA statement quoted in several outlets. Federal Register notice not opened |
| 9 | Obama began reassessment 2016, Trump paused 2019, Biden restarted 2023, Trump II cut short by eliminating office | Plausible, not verified here | Rests on reporting and advocates' characterization; not checked against EPA IRIS records |
| 10 | Petition asks for interim benchmark and enforcement of "existing protections such as the Clean Water Act" | Petition request reported; legal framing loose | Drinking water standards fall under the Safe Drinking Water Act, not the Clean Water Act (which covers source-water discharges) |
| 11 | Lobdell (Food & Water Watch): "People are paying with their health right now" | Quote consistent with the Grist text | Not checked against audio |
| 12 | Pollution largely from fertilizer and manure runoff; disproportionately affects rural, low-income, Latino and farmworker communities | Broadly supported | Standard in the literature; the petition's demographic claim not checked directly |

Not checkable here: the exact petition text and the One Water Econ report body (not opened); the Obama/Trump/Biden reassessment timeline against primary EPA records; the verbatim Lobdell quote.

## Overall rating: 1 Pinocchio (minor issues)
The facts are mostly accurate and correctly attributed. The weakness is framing, not fabrication.

## Materially misleading or missing context
- Most numbers (120M people, $94.5B/$364B, 12,594 cases) come from the petitioners or their allies, and EWG authored the cancer study. Headline numbers are presented without that caveat.
- 12,594 is a modeled maximum, not a count.
- A 10x tightening to 1 mg/L is far below the level tied to the acute blue baby risk; the article does not give the scientific disagreement over chronic effects at low doses, or any response from water utilities or agriculture groups. Only EPA's brief statement appears.
- "Clean Water Act" for drinking water protection is imprecise.
- The headline puts "public health crisis" in the voice of "environmentalists", which is accurate attribution.
