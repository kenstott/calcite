# Fact-check: Motley Fool, "Social Security's Trump Bump-Led 2027 COLA Is a Double-Edged Sword" (Sean Williams, 2026-11-07)

Method: WebFetch of the article returned only a summary (not verbatim text), so there are no direct quotes to check and claims are as paraphrased by the fetch. WebFetch of ssa.gov returned HTTP 403, so SSA figures were confirmed via search-result excerpts of ssa.gov pages and secondary reports. This is a limit on my verification, not a finding against the article.

## Claims and verdicts

1. SSA announced a 3.6% 2027 COLA on Oct. 14. TRUE. SSA release 2026-10-14 (via search excerpts) and Fox Business, Newsweek, The Hill, GMA all report 3.6%. Prior year was 2.8%. About 72 million beneficiaries, average +$75/month.
2. COLA "ties for the fifth-largest in 35 years." DEPENDS ON THE WINDOW. Recalled SSA COLA history (not re-fetched; search could not confirm): larger than 3.6% are 2023 (8.7), 2022 (5.9), 2009 (5.8), 2006 (4.1), and 1992 (3.7). 2012 also 3.6. That makes it a tie for sixth if 1992 is counted, fifth if the window starts in 1993. Minor, borderline. Not confirmed from a primary source in this session.
3. Sixth straight year of at least 2.5%. TRUE on recalled history (5.9, 8.7, 3.2, 2.5, 2.8, 3.6). Not confirmed by search; the 2.5% for 2025 is the tight one.
4. Higher COLA reflects tariffs/trade policy and the Iran conflict/Strait of Hormuz fuel shock. PARTLY SUPPORTED, CAUSAL ATTRIBUTION NOT CHECKABLE AS STATED. BLS: September 2026 gasoline +34.2% y/y, energy +20.4% y/y, CPI-W +3.9% y/y, CPI-U +3.7%. So an energy shock is well supported. Searches found only a generic "Middle East conflict" link, nothing on Hormuz specifically or on tariffs as the driver; core CPI rose only 0.3% in September. Apportioning the COLA to tariffs ("Trump bump") is the author's interpretation, not a verified fact.
5. 2026 Medicare Trustees Report projects Part B premium up 3.25% to $209.50 in 2027. TRUE as a projection. 2026 premium $202.90, +$6.60 = 3.25%; MOAA, AARP, Kiplinger (Kiplinger's 3.5% is internally inconsistent). Official 2027 premium is set by CMS later in November and may differ.
6. About 29 million traditional Medicare enrollees could keep more of their raise. NOT CHECKABLE HERE. I found no primary source for the 29 million count. Logic is sound: a $209.50 premium (+3.25%) is below the 3.6% COLA, but the premium hold-harmless rule and the average $75 raise mean the benefit is small, and the premium is only the projection.
7. OASI trust fund reserves depleted in Q4 2032. TRUE. 2026 Trustees Report moved it from Q1 2033; consistent across AAF, PGPF, Fox Business, CRS.
8. Unfunded 75-year obligation of $29.3 trillion. TRUE but needs a label. This is the OASDI (combined) open-group figure, present value, 1.5% of GDP, up from $25.1T. The article ties it to OASI context; it is not OASI-only.
9. Projected cut about 22% when reserves run out. TRUE. Income covers about 78% of scheduled OASI benefits. Combined OASDI funds (2034) face about 17%; the article uses the OASI figure, which is consistent with its OASI framing.
10. A larger COLA drains reserves faster; the cut could arrive sooner than six years and be bigger than 22%. OPINION/SPECULATION. Directionally plausible (higher COLA raises outlays), but the Trustees already assume COLAs on projected inflation; the effect of one year's surprise is small, and the Trustees' own date was set in a report that predates the COLA. Not a fact claim that can be verified; presented with more confidence than evidence supports.

## Overall rating: 1 Pinocchio (on the 0-4 scale)

The numeric claims are accurate. The weak points are the unverified ranking window, the confident causal story ("Trump bump", tariffs, Hormuz) that the data support only for energy, and the speculative "sooner and deeper than 22%" warning.

## Materially misleading even though facts check out

- "Trump bump" framing credits or blames tariffs for the COLA while the BLS data show an energy price surge (gasoline +34% y/y) with muted core inflation. Attribution to tariffs is asserted, not shown.
- The $29.3T figure is OASDI combined and the 22% cut is OASI-only; mixing them without labeling can overstate or confuse the scale.
- "Double-edged sword" implies the bigger COLA materially hastens insolvency; the effect is small relative to the demographic and fertility assumptions the Trustees say drive the deficit (the report attributes the increase in the deficit mainly to a lower fertility assumption).
- The Medicare Part B offset rests on a Trustees projection, not the final premium.
