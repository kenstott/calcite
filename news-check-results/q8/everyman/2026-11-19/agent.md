# Fact-check: IPWatchdog, "GAO Report: USPTO's Backlog Reduction Goals Unsustainable if Workforce Strain, Examiner Attrition Continue" (Nov 5, 2026)

Primary source: GAO-27-108491, "Patent Backlog Reduction Efforts Pose Risks to Examiner Retention and Examination Quality" (https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-27-108491/index.html).
Method caveat: WebFetch returns a model-written summary, not verbatim text, so the article's claims below are as paraphrased by the tool. Wording-level findings (items marked *) should be confirmed against the raw article.

| Claim | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Backlog peaked ~838,000 in Jan 2025 | True | GAO highlights |
| Backlog ~756,000 in Sept 2026, reported by Director Squires* | Partly wrong | GAO: 756,000 as of **August 2026**, not attributed to Squires. Squires' March 2026 testimony targeted ~688,000 by end FY2026 |
| USPTO goal under 599,000 | True | Announced Sept 2025, no time frame |

Not checkable here: the exact article wording of each claim (only a summary was retrievable), and the headline's "unsustainable" framing, which is the outlet's paraphrase. GAO's actual conclusion is the backlog goal is unlikely before 2031 without lower attrition.

## Rating: 1 Pinocchio (minor slips)
Substance is accurate and tracks the GAO report closely. Slips: August vs September date and attribution of 756,000, "junior" vs "new" examiners, eligibility-training wording, and the statute citation (if the article indeed says 117).

## Context that could mislead
- Headline "unsustainable" is stronger than GAO's "unlikely before 2031 absent lower attrition".
- Backlog fell from 838k to 756k despite high attrition; the GAO concern is sustainability and quality, not failure to date.
- GAO notes the training length was later adjusted to 10 weeks.
- The audit was requested by minority-party members, and USPTO declined to participate or comment, so the agency's side is absent.
