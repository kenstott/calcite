# Fact-check: News4Jax/AP "Trump plans a 20% tariff on Chinese goods and new import taxes for the EU and a dozen other major trading partners" (2026-11-25)

## Summary: mostly accurate, rated 1 Pinocchio
The core facts match the USTR notice of 2026-11-25: Section 301 excess-capacity determinations for 14 economies (China, EU, Taiwan and 11 others), proposed rates of 15% to 35%, comments due 2027-01-05, hearing 2027-01-25, Mexico and Norway not decided. Weaknesses: headline "plans a 20% tariff" is a proposal, "up to 20%" (10% for Annex A goods), net of other 301 duties. The "45% total" and "no stacking" claims are the paper's reading of a senior official and Greer, and USTR's own text says "net of". Several claims could not be verified.

## Verified (primary or strong secondary)
- 14 economies, 15%-35% range, Vietnam up to 35%, EU/Japan/Korea/Switzerland/Taiwan 15% net of MFN: USTR press release.
- Hearing Jan 25, 2027; Mexico/Norway no determination: USTR.
- Supreme Court February ruling against IEEPA tariffs (6-3, 2026-02-20): Cornell LII, law-firm memos.
- Forced-labor Section 301 duties 10%/12.5% on 60 economies, effective 2026-07-24: USTR fact sheet, Thomson Reuters.
- Greer CNBC quotes ("baked in"; no effect for "a couple months"): CNBC.
- China total about 45% (20% on top of ~25%): senior official, CNBC. Truce expiry Jan 10, 2027: reported. Trump-Xi Shenzhen Nov 19 and G20 Doral/Miami in December: MFA China, SCMP.
- Democrats won House and Senate: NPR/AP race calls. Brazil sugar and 10% aircraft: ABC News (hearing Jan 14).

## Partly / misleading
- Headline "plans a 20% tariff": proposal; USTR says "up to 20%", net of forced-labor 301 rate; Annex A up to 10%; Annex B "30-for-30" exemptions.
- "Three-fifths of imports": not in USTR; CNBC's 99.4% figure belongs to the forced-labor tariffs. Unverified.
- "Won't stack on forced-labor levies": USTR says net of existing 301 duties; one law-firm source says stacking is possible. Greer says "not really additive". China is the exception.
- Taiwan/Korea tech exemptions: USTR says "certain goods", unnamed.

## Not checkable here
Goldman 7.8% to 9.2%; Mark Williams/Capital Economics quote; AP VoteCast two-thirds figure; Scissors, Wyden, Anthony, Majerus quotes (not located in sources searched; Wyden gist confirmed by ABC); 456-page China report; $30 billion exemption (not in USTR page; Greer attribution unverified).

## Confidence
Medium-high on structure and rates (primary USTR page), low on the unverified items above. Limits: WebFetch returns model summaries, article text not read verbatim.
