Camden poverty story: 2025 figures unverified, and they sit far from every earlier vintage I could check

Findings: provisional 2 Pinocchios (unverified, with a large unexplained discrepancy)

I could not confirm the headline 24.9% city poverty rate for 2025. The Census API required a key and redirected to a missing-key page, and Census Reporter has no 2025 ACS release. QuickFacts returned HTTP 403. Anything about 2025 therefore rests on the Inquirer alone.

  • Poverty rate: ACS 2024 1-year gave Camden city 23,632 of 69,729 in poverty (33.9%). The 2024 5-year gave 30.2%. Data USA's 2022 1-year gave 31.6%. A fall to 24.9% in 2025 is about 9 points in one year. That is possible but extraordinary, and the article does not say how large the margin of error is.
  • County rate: the article's 9.9% compares with 11.6% (2024 1-yr) and 12.4% (2024 5-yr). Direction is plausible.
  • Median income: $45,900 against $38,735 (2024 1-yr, B19013), about +18% in a year. The article says income peaked at $47,200 in 2021 (inflation-adjusted) and fell after, but Data USA lists 2021 nominal at $30,247. These cannot be reconciled without the 2025 tables. Falling poverty beside income still below its peak also deserves explanation.
  • Poverty line: $32,150 for four people in 2025 is correct (HHS/ASPE, 90 FR 5917).
  • Not checkable here: "lowest since at least 2010" (no 2010 to 2023 city series retrieved), 76% of households above the line but struggling (United Way report), 10th lowest among cities of 65,000+, 8% vs 14% real income growth since 2010, Cathedral Kitchen's 6,000 people a month, all quotes (Golderer, Flewelling, Noonan), the stipend program, and the mayor's office not responding.

What would change this: the actual 2025 ACS 1-year row for Camden city (S1701, B19013) with margins of error. If it reads 24.9% and $45,900 the numbers are accurate and the grade drops to 1 or less. The article's framing that people are still struggling is supported by the 2024 data (a third in poverty).
