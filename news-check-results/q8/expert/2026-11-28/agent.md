# Fortune 2026-11-27 "They'll all go to Florida" fact-check

Rating: 1 Pinocchio (mostly accurate; minor framing issues).

Every checkable number matched sources. Gaps: raw IRS SOI tables not fetched directly (relied on Pioneer Institute/Boston.com reproduction); Peter Barnes and Chester Spatt quotes not independently verified; Maine/Rhode Island surcharge details not confirmed.

## Claims
- MA net loss 31,384 individuals 2023-24: confirmed (Pioneer). Confidence: medium-high (secondary of IRS).
- AGI loss $3.64B vs $4.18B prior: confirmed. 
- NH +7,818, FL +6,032 = 44% of net loss: arithmetic 44.1% OK.
- FL $1.29B, NH $869M = 59% of AGI loss: 2.159/3.64=59.3% OK.
- Ages 26-34 = 43% of net filer loss; $200k+ = $2.32B (64%): confirmed in Pioneer; 2.32/3.64=63.7%.
- FY26 surtax $3.38B preliminary vs $2.4B budgeted: confirmed (MA DOR, 2026-07-20). "Certified" is preliminary; $2.4B is a budget benchmark, not certified.
- California Prop 40 one-time 5% billionaire wealth tax rejected Nov 3: confirmed (about 53-47, AP/CalMatters).
- Washington 9.9% tax over $1M kept by voters (I-645 repeal rejected): confirmed (Bloomberg Tax, Ballotpedia). Tax starts 2028.
- MassBudget: affordability not taxes drives outmigration: confirmed as MassBudget's stated position (April 2026).
## Misleading context
Net AGI flows are not millionaire flows: 64% of lost income is from $200k+ filers, not surtax payers (> about $1.1M). Net-migration data cannot show causation; the surtax-era loss fell 13% in 2024. The headline quote frames the debate; IRS data are filer-based residence changes, not tax motivation. Pioneer is an advocacy think tank, MassBudget advocates the other side.
