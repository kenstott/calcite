Fact-check: nitrates in drinking water (Grist via Yahoo, Nov 17 2026)

Mostly accurate, one framing problem: 1 Pinocchio

The report's numbers match the petition filed Nov 16, 2026 and EPA's record. The weakness is framing: nearly every statistic comes from the petitioners and their commissioned analysis, and "dangerous nitrate levels" overstates what the 120 million figure measures.

  • 119.7 million people (article: "nearly 120 million", one in three) is the petition's count of people served by 13,205 community water systems that recorded at least one nitrate sample at or above 1 mg/L in 2021-2023. It is not a count of people drinking water averaging that level, and 1 mg/L is one tenth of the legal limit (10 mg/L as nitrogen). Misleading by omission.
  • $94.5B cost and $364.1B benefit over 50 years match the One Water Econ analysis (Sept 19, 2026), commissioned by petitioners; the article says so. Benefit-cost ratio 3.9 is theirs. Not independently reviewed.
  • 12,594 cancers per year is the upper bound of Temkin et al. 2019 (range 2,300-12,594), an EWG study. The petition says "between 2,300 and 12,594"; "up to" is accurate, but the lower bound matters.
  • EPA statement: the July 23, 2024 Six-Year Review 4 did not select nitrate for revision (89 FR 59623); confirmed.
  • Regulatory history: the petition dates the nitrate reassessment (IRIS) to September 2017, suspended 2019 under the first Trump administration, restarted 2023, with the Office of Research and Development eliminated under the second. The report's "started under Obama in 2016" and its description as the EPA "periodic review" do not match the petition; I could not resolve the 2016 start date. Likely conflation of the IRIS assessment with the Six-Year Review.
  • Not checkable here: the 62% share for West/Midwest states, Iowa 42% (EWG's release says 46%, a source conflict I could not resolve), Illinois over 2 million, and the top states (CA, TX, AZ). They rest on Exhibit 1 appendices I could not parse.

What would change the rating

If the 62%/Iowa/Illinois figures are wrong or the Obama-era date is inaccurate, move toward 2 Pinocchios. Confounds: exposure is measured by a single sample at or above 1 mg/L, not by population-weighted averages; these are state-level aggregates, so ecological inference applies.
