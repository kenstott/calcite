# Fact-check: Grist, "As black lung disease surges, the Trump administration proposes deregulatory protections for miners" (Katie Myers, Nov 19, 2026)

Caveat: WebFetch returned a summary of the article, not verbatim text, so claims were checked as summarized.

## Verdict: 1 Pinocchio (mostly accurate; one figure overstated, one framing point)
- Verified in the Federal Register proposed rule (docket MSHA-2026-0200, RIN 1219-AC22): PEL stays 50 ug/m3; 21% error factor; citations only on MSHA's own samples; periodic evaluation and post-evaluation sampling removed; quarterly sampling replaced by "as frequently as necessary"; new MNM miner baseline exams voluntary; total savings $28.7M/yr (2024$, 7%); coal $2.43M (8%), MNM $26.3M (92%); "expected to be a deregulatory action under E.O. 14192"; virtual hearing Dec 15, 2026; comments due 60 days after publication (Nov 12) = Jan 11, 2027. The 2024 rule was stayed by the 8th Circuit (April 2025); the article says "last year" which fits the 2025 stay.
- Overstated: "more than 1 in 3 Central Appalachian miners" vs NIOSH 32.5% (91/280), which is below one in three and covers working underground veterans with 25+ years, not all miners. The "50-year high" is "nearly 50 years" in NIOSH coverage (2018 rate 20.6%).
- Misleading by emphasis: $28.7M savings is 92% metal/nonmetal; coal savings are $2.43M. Black lung is a coal story, yet the voluntary baseline exam applies to MNM miners, and the coal medical program is separate (30 CFR 72.100). Muckian-Bates' "effectively nullify" is an advocate opinion, not checkable as fact. The 21% factor is an existing MSHA practice for its own samples (FR text), now extended to operator samples.
- Not checked: Robinson's quotes and diagnosis at 47 (personal testimony); exact Muckian-Bates quote (article not fetched verbatim); "less frequent sampling in underground mines" specifics only partly confirmed.
