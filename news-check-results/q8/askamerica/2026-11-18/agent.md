The Grist/Yahoo article's claims about the nitrate petition, EPA review history and cost-benefit figures are mostly accurate and cut against its own case.

**Rating: 1 Pinocchio.** Article: https://yahoo.com/news/us/articles/nitrates-drinking-water-public-health-091500537.html (Grist original, Nov 17, 2026; both loaded and match).

Report: http://127.0.0.1:57796/a/ec1189fc4e785d8ad2ee274c727c8d37.html (report_id 20261118-081041-the-grist-yahoo-article-s-claims-about-t)

## Method
Warehouse first: the catalog has no system-level nitrate concentration or population-served table, so the 120M figure could not be recomputed. Context query on environment.drinking_water_violations (nitrate code 1040, MCL violations): 861/890/932/897/731 violations at 398/389/405/408/369 systems in 2021 to 2025. The figures were graded against the coalition's 107-page EPA petition, searched in full. The ScienceDirect and EWG pages returned HTTP 403 and were not read.

## Claims
- "120 million people, dangerous levels" (Lobdell, central): partially false. 119.7M matches the petition, but that counts systems above 1 mg/L, a tenth of the legal limit. The petition itself says only 2.9M people (2.4%) are served by systems with a sample at or above 10 mg/L.
- $94.5B cost and $364B benefit: true; it is the petition's central least-cost case. Full treatment is $130.4B and the benefit-cost ratio drops to 2.8 at a 7% discount rate.
- Obama began the EPA reassessment in 2016: mostly true. The petition says IRIS began in September 2017; 2019 pause and 2023 restart match.
- Trump eliminated the office responsible: true (ORD eliminated 2025; no nitrate action since 2023).
- Iowa 42%: mostly true. Petition Table 3 says 45.5%.
- Top states "cluster in the West and Midwest" with 62%: partially false. The 62% is ten states and includes PA, NJ, MD and OH.
- 12,594 cancer cases a year: mostly true. The range is 2,300 to 12,594.
- 10 mg/L set 1975, formalized 1991: true.
- EPA completed its review in July 2024: true.
- Des Moines restrictions a second year: true (central Iowa lawn-watering ban from June 8, 2026, after 2025).
- Studies link nitrate to colorectal cancer, thyroid disease and birth defects: true.
- Illinois: more than 2 million served above 1 mg/L (Hirschfeld): unsupported. No Illinois figure was found in the petition or elsewhere.

## Materially misleading framing
- The article never says that over 97% of the 120M are served water within the current legal limit. The one-in-three headline rests on the advocates' 1 mg/L benchmark.
- The cancer figure is the top of a range, and the cost-benefit numbers are the petition's best case, from an analysis the advocates commissioned. The article does say the groups commissioned it. Only one brief EPA statement offsets them.
