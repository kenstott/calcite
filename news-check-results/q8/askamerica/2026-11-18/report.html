Summary

Verdict: 1 Pinocchio (minor inaccuracies, with a material framing caveat). Nearly every number matches the advocacy petition the piece reports on: 120 million people (119.7M), $94.5B cost, $364B benefit, 10 mg/L to 1 mg/L, 62% in the top states. The problems are framing and a few mismatches. It repeats the coalition's 'dangerous levels' language for any water above 1 mg/L, but the petition's own table shows only 2.9 million people (2.4% of the 'affected' population) are served by systems with any sample at or above the legal 10 mg/L limit. It gives Iowa as 42% where the petition table says 45.5%, dates the EPA reassessment to the Obama era (2016) where the petition says September 2017, and says the top states 'cluster in the West and Midwest' when the petition's list includes PA, NJ, MD and OH. One claim (Illinois, more than 2 million) could not be verified.

The warehouse has no table of population-weighted nitrate concentrations by system, so the 120M figure was checked against the petition text, not recomputed. Materially misleading framing

  • 'Dangerous levels' for water above 1 mg/L: 597 systems serving 2.9M people (2.4%) had a sample at or above 10 mg/L. The article never says over 97% of the 120M are served water within the current legal limit.
  • $364B vs $94.5B is the petition's central least-cost partial-treatment case; full treatment is $130.4B cost, and the benefit-cost ratio falls to 2.8 at a 7% discount rate. The 12,594 cancer figure is the top of a 2,300 to 12,594 range.
  • Only one brief EPA statement offsets the advocates' numbers.

Limits

ScienceDirect and EWG pages returned HTTP 403 and were not read; the 2019 study range was taken from the petition's description. CalMatters loaded and supports only background statements. The Grist article matches the Yahoo copy.
