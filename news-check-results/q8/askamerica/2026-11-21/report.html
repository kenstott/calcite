Summary

Rating: 1 Pinocchio. Grist's article is mostly accurate: the 50 ug/m3 limit is retained, a uniform 21 percent error factor is proposed, new metal/nonmetal exams become voluntary, estimated savings are $28.7M/yr ($2.43M coal), the proposal was published Nov 12 with comments due Jan 11, 2027 and a Dec 15 hearing, and the court stay dates from April 2025. Weak spots: the NIOSH prevalence line (NPR reports 32.5 percent over five years among miners with 25+ years underground, versus 'more than 1 in 3 ... past three years' for Central Appalachian miners) and the claim that the 2024 rule required sampling every six months (it required six-month periodic evaluations and exposure-tiered sampling). Omitted context: the proposal also adds mandatory exams for overexposed sampled miners, and the 21 percent factor codifies existing enforcement while replacing method-specific factors; the National Mining Association's rebuttal on prevalence is absent.

AskAmerica's warehouse has no black lung, silica sampling or rulemaking tables (search_catalog returned only energy.eia_coal_mines, MSHA coal production), so all claims were graded from primary documents. The AJRCCM journal page and OSHA PDF returned HTTP 403 and the public-inspection index returned 403; the Federal Register mirror and the public-inspection PDFs were read instead. The legacy ~100 ug/m3 figure and the 'age groups' finding were not verified.
