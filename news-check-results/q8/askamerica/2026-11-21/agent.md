The Grist article's claims about the MSHA silica proposal, black lung prevalence and the 2024 rule are mostly accurate.

**Rating: 1 Pinocchio.**

Source: Grist, Katie Myers, Nov 19, 2026. The connector fetched the article directly.

**Warehouse check.** search_catalog found only energy.eia_coal_mines (MSHA coal production by mine). That table does not bear on any claim here. There are no black lung, silica sampling or rulemaking tables. All claims were graded from primary documents.

**Verified true**
- The proposal retains the 50 ug/m3 limit (proposed rule, p.6).
- A uniform 21 percent error factor is proposed (p.38). The article's "about 10 ug/m3 more" follows arithmetically: 50 x 1.21 = 60.5.
- Medical exams for new metal/nonmetal miners change from mandatory to voluntary (p.48).
- Savings estimate: $28.7M per year, of which $2.43M is coal (p.97). It is in 2024 dollars at a 7 percent discount rate and is an agency estimate.
- The proposed rule was published Nov 12, 2026 (91 FR 73062). Comments are due Jan 11, 2027. A virtual hearing is set for Dec 15.
- The Eighth Circuit stayed the 2024 rule on April 11, 2025 (FR 2026-06584).
- The "deregulatory action" quote appears on p.147, citing E.O. 14192.
- The 50 ug/m3 limit in the 2024 rule is confirmed by the DOL release.

**Problems**
1. **Prevalence figure (mostly false as worded).** The article says "more than 1 in 3 miners in Central Appalachia" tested positive "in the past three years." NPR's coverage of the NIOSH letter in AJRCCM reports 32.5 percent (91 of 280). That is over five years, among miners with 25 or more years underground, and slightly under 1 in 3. The journal page returned HTTP 403, so the "all age groups" finding was not verified.
2. **2024 sampling schedule (partially false).** The article says the 2024 rule required sampling every six months. The 2024 rule required periodic evaluations at least every 6 months. Sampling frequency was tied to exposure level, quarterly at or above the action level (proposal pp.30 and 113).
3. **1960s-1970s claim (mostly true).** NIH says one-third of long-tenured miners had black lung in the early 1970s. The article widens that to the 1960s and 1970s.

**Misleading by omission**
- The proposal also adds mandatory exams for sampled miners above the PEL, and respirators at the action level. The article does not mention either.
- The 21 percent factor is framed as a new loophole. MSHA says it matches current enforcement, and the 2024 rule already used method-specific error factors (MSHA P-2 and P-7). The proposal unifies these into one factor.
- The National Mining Association's rebuttal is absent. The NMA argues that current disease reflects past exposure, not current conditions.
- The article does include a dissenting expert, Emily Sarver, who prefers advancing the proposal.

**Not verified or not checkable**
- The legacy standard of roughly 100 ug/m3 was not verified against a primary text.
- The coal compliance date of April 2025 was not pinned to a day.
- The Muckian-Bates and Robinson quotes are advocate opinion and personal testimony, so they are not checkable.
- The OSHA PDF and the public-inspection index returned HTTP 403. The Federal Register mirror (thefederalregister.org) and the public-inspection PDFs were read instead.
