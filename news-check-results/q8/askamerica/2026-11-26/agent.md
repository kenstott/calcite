## Materially misleading or unverified
- **China 20% and "45% total" (partially false).** USTR proposes "up to 20 percent, net of the rate of duty applied under the forced-labor Section 301 investigation" (up to 10% on Annex A goods). It reflects the October 2025 Kuala Lumpur arrangement, with an alternative of up to 40% if engagement fails. The new tariff is therefore not a plain 20-point add-on, and the 45% total appears nowhere in the notice. The earlier AP version's "additional 20%" is looser wording than the notice. Scissors' own quote that the proposal "barely raises existing tariffs" fits the net-of reading.
- **"Announced" tariffs (mostly true).** These are proposals for public comment, and none is in effect. The article says so later.
- **Brazil (mostly true).** Brazil is not among the 14 economies, despite the headline. Argus reports USTR "separately" proposed sugar and aircraft-parts tariffs. The 10% aircraft rate and the Brazil notice text were not read.
- **Unsupported:** the Goldman 7.8% to 9.2% estimate (no source found); semiconductor exemptions for Taiwan and Korea (annexes not read); and "last year's tariffs contributed to higher inflation" (not tested, and the linked AP article returned 403).
- **Opinion quotes, not checkable:** Williams (Capital Economics), Wyden and Scissors.
