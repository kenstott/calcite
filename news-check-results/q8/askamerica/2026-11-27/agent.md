The WJAC article's claims about Pennsylvania's Dec. 1 electric price-to-compare changes, Penelec, West Penn and PJM are accurate.

**Rating: 0 Pinocchios.**

**Sources read in full:**
- PUC release of 2026-11-23: https://www.puc.pa.gov/press-release/2026/puc-encourages-consumers-to-prepare-for-rising-winter-electric-costs-as-december-1-brings-price-changes-11132026
- PUC release of 2025-11-24: https://www.puc.pa.gov/press-release/2025/puc-alerts-consumers-of-december-1-electric-price-changes-11242025

**Warehouse check:** search_catalog found no table with utility-level price-to-compare. The closest is energy.eia_electricity_prices_monthly, which gives the PA residential average price. It was 22.20 cents/kWh in Sept 2026 against 20.43 in Sept 2025, up 8.7%. That fits the article's direction but is not the same measure. The utility figures were therefore graded from the PUC primary releases.

**Claims, all verified**
- 10 of 11 utilities raise their price to compare: true. The PUC table shows 10 increases and UGI Electric at -1.72%.
- Penelec 13.142 to 13.564 cents, +3.2%: true. It recomputes to 3.21%.
- Penelec last December 11.004 to 11.747, +6.75%: true (2025 release).
- The new Penelec rate is about 15% above the post-December-2025 rate: true. 13.564 / 11.747 is +15.5%.
- West Penn 12.075 to 12.386: true (+2.6%).
- West Penn last December 10.318 to 10.947: true (+6.1%).
- The new West Penn rate is about 13% above the post-December-2025 rate, with a smaller increase this year: true. 12.386 / 10.947 is +13.1%, and 2.6% is less than 6.1%.
- Nine of 11 raised rates last December, 10 this year: true. In the 2025 release, 9 rose (Duquesne, Pike, Met-Ed, Penelec, Penn Power, West Penn, PECO, PPL, Citizens'), and Wellsboro and UGI fell.
- Only default-service customers are affected, and colder weather and heavier use raise total bills: true.
- The PUC urges customers to contact their utility early about budget billing, payment arrangements and assistance programs: true.
- Shapiro criticized PJM and says he will keep working with other governors on reform: true. The Governor's office has a 2026 statement criticizing PJM's governance vote and has the PJM Governors Collaborative.
- Facebook commenters' quotes (Alicia Sherwood, Missy Ann): not checkable here. They are personal anecdotes and opinion, and no dataset can test them.

**Minor issue:** the article says the PUC tied the changes to wholesale costs "which can include PJM capacity costs". The PUC release says only that prices are based on "wholesale market rates" and does not mention PJM capacity. The PJM link is the reporter's context. It is plausible but not the PUC's words. This is the only slip, and it is small.

**Context and framing:**
- Penelec's "current" 13.142 is already well above the 11.747 set last December. The gap mostly came from a mid-year reset, not from December changes.
- The piece does not name UGI as the one decrease.
- It omits the larger increases elsewhere: Citizens' +11.9%, Wellsboro +10.4%, PECO and PPL +10.3%.
- None of this is materially misleading, because the article reports local-utility figures accurately and its "smaller than last year" framing is correct.
