The Fortune article's claims about the $8 trillion global defense spending figure, the Pentagon budget and European rearmament are partly false.

**Rating: 2 Pinocchios.**

Article: Fortune, Nov 15 2026, "Pete Hegseth's $1.5 trillion Pentagon budget request is DOA after Democrats swept midterms, but global defense spending could still hit $8 trillion" (Sasha Rogelberg). Fortune's own URL returned HTTP 308 to the engine. I read the Yahoo Finance syndicated copy instead, which says it was "originally featured on Fortune.com".

**Central claim: mostly false (misleading framing).** The headline and lede say "global defense spending is expected to top $8 trillion." The body attributes the figure to Carlyle and Stavridis, and it applies only to the G7. Reuters columnist Mike Dolan's coverage of the Carlyle note, as summarized in search results, says the roughly $8T is additional G7 commitments over the next decade, under a 3.8%-of-GDP scenario. It is not annual global spending. SIPRI puts actual 2025 world military spending at $2,887B, which is 2.5% of global GDP. A reader of the headline would take away a figure nearly three times the real annual global total. I could not open the Carlyle note itself (HTTP 403), so I graded it on secondary reports.

**Other claims**
- True: G7 at 2.0% of GDP, "highest in more than three decades", with 3.8% as a projection. This matches secondary reports of Carlyle.
- True: The US is about one-third of world spending (SIPRI: $954B in 2025).
- Mostly true, with a caveat: US spending "surging past the $1 trillion mark in fiscal 2026." OMB National Defense outlays for FY2026 are $966B in the warehouse (econ.federal_outlays_by_function, function 050). The $1T-plus figure holds only for budget authority (CRFB). SIPRI shows US spending fell 7.5% in 2025, so "surging" is loose.
- True: The $1.5T FY2027 request is a more than 40% increase (CRFB: 42%).
- True: EU defense spending of €418B in 2025 (2.2% of GDP) and €454B projected for 2026 (2.4%). I confirmed this through the European Defence Agency release and search results, because the Council page returned 403.
- True: EU and member states have provided $82.9B in military aid to Ukraine (EEAS, fetched).
- True: Democrats won both chambers on Nov 3 (multiple outlets).
- True: Adam Smith's Nov 4 letter says a defense budget above $1T is not needed and calls for oversight. This is confirmed through search summaries and a Crowell analysis, not the letter text. A "probe of contractors" is likely a paraphrase of that oversight language.
- True: Global military spending has grown for 11 straight years (SIPRI).
- Not checkable: the prediction that the midterms "may have put the kibosh" on the budget increase. It is a hedged forecast.
- Not independently verified: the Hegseth Shangri-La quote, and the Bilmes, Patterson, van Steenis and Stavridis quotes. I did not fetch the primary pages. Also not verified: the Pentagon's July withdrawal from the NATO-based Ukraine support group, and the French supermarket freezer outage.

**Why 2 Pinocchios:** Nearly every individual figure checks out. The headline and lede, though, turn a G7-only, decade-long, scenario-based commitment into "global defense spending," which misleads the reader on the piece's central number. Minor issues are that the FY2026 "$1 trillion" figure is budget authority rather than outlays, and that "Secretary of Defense" is used where the department now styles him Secretary of War.
