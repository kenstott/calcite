**Claims**
- Vance, $6B stopped in suspected fraud: **unsupported as independent fact.** The post says this exactly, but the dollar figure's only source is the administration (Vance, Rollins, the White House Fraud Ledger, USDA). The catalog has no fraud-stopped measure. The Epoch Times passage is Rollins relaying the same figure, so it is not independent.
- Vance, 6,400 retailers and 4,600 devices, posted Thursday (Nov 19): **true.**
- USDA's earlier $5.8B, nearly 6,000 retailers and 2,000 devices: **true** against the Sept 18 release. The Epoch Times reported 5,335 retailers and about 1,840 devices on Sept 16.
- About $100B a year: **true.** The warehouse (fiscal.snap_benefits_by_geography) shows $93.5B in FY2024 and $95.8B in FY2025, using monthly rows only. Adding the annual Total rows double counts to $187B and $191.5B.
- 170 New York City retailers: **true.**
- DOJ surge (40+ defendants, nearly $42M) and the $29.7M, $4.4M and $1.09M cases: **true** as allegations. I checked these against Breitbart's own Oct 21 report quoting DOJ, not the DOJ page.
- Task force created in March with Vance as chair: **true.**
- Kansas City grand jury indictment of an Illinois store owner: **unsupported.** The DOJ page was empty and search found no matching release. This does not mean it is false.

**Misleading framing**
- The White House Fraud Ledger defines "fraud stopped" as dollars saved annually through administrative actions. The $6B is therefore an estimate with no published method, not fraud proven or recovered. The headline credits a "Task Force", while Vance's post credits USDA.
- The article omits ProPublica's finding that confirmed recipient fraud is about 0.08% of benefits. ProPublica also reports about $470M stolen electronically from recipients in 2025 and cuts to USDA fraud staff.
- The DOJ figures are mostly alleged or intended losses.
