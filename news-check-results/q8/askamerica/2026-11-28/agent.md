The Fortune article's claims about Massachusetts' millionaires tax, IRS migration data and wealth-tax debate are accurate.

**Rating: 0 Pinocchios.**

Fortune (Emma Witman, 2026-11-27) loaded directly, with no 403. It is largely a relay of Boston.com, Mass. DOR and CommonWealth Beacon.

**Verified**
- IRS 2023-24 net loss of 31,384 residents and $3.64B AGI, down from $4.18B the year before. These match Boston.com's report of the IRS release. I did not open the IRS file itself, and the warehouse migration table (fiscal.county_migration_flows) ends at 2022. Its 2022 file shows a net AGI loss of about $4.0B, the same order of magnitude.
- New Hampshire (+7,818) and Florida (+6,032) are 44.1% of the net loss. Their AGI of $2.159B is 59.3% of $3.64B. I recomputed both and they match.
- Inflows from New York (13,394), Florida (11,728) and California (8,728), and net gains from New Jersey (855), Louisiana (130) and Indiana (121), match Boston.com. So does the claim that all five New England neighbors gained from Massachusetts.
- Surtax revenue: DOR's release of 2026-08-13 says $3.380B certified preliminary for FY2026, against a $2.4B budget benchmark. FY2025 was $2.987B (CommonWealth Beacon), so FY2026 is a record, up 13%. The $1.8B reported in May 2024 against $900M expected also checks out, per Fortune's 2024 article. That figure came with three months of the fiscal year still left.
- Washington's I-645 repeal failed (about 58% no). Maine (LD 2212) and Rhode Island (FY2027 budget) enacted millionaire surcharges in 2026. California's Prop 40 (one-time 5% on billionaires' net worth) failed.
- MassBudget's argument is accurately described. It is based on 2022-23 filings.
- Barnes's France point: the 2018 law replaced the ISF with a real-estate-only IFI. The "flight" causation is a contested view, not a measured fact.
- Spatt's quote is an opinion and not checkable. It appears in Fortune's 11/23 piece.

**Minor imprecision**
- Prop 40 was called Nov. 9 by AP, not Nov. 3.
- "Nearly four years" since the surtax took effect on Jan. 1, 2023 is about 3 years 11 months, while Boston.com says three.

**Not independently verified**
- The Pioneer Institute splits (ages 26-34 at 43% of the net loss, filers earning $200,000 or more at $2.32B or 64% of the AGI loss) are verified only as Boston.com relays. I could not locate Pioneer's own 2024 report. The arithmetic ($2.32B of $3.64B is 63.7%) is consistent.

**Framing caveats (not factual errors)**
- AGI flows cover all movers, not surtax payers, who earn above about $1.1M. The piece does note that the figures "capture moves, not their motives."
- The Pioneer high-earner share is given without MassBudget's counterpoint that about 87-90% of leaving households earn under $200,000.
- That counterpoint does appear in MassBudget's release, which Fortune cites.
