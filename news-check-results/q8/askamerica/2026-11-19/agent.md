**Method.** The warehouse has no table for examiner attrition, backlog, or PPAC meetings (the patents schema holds grants and applications only; "examiner" matches nothing in the catalog). So claims were graded against the primary source, GAO-27-108491. gao.gov returned 403 to the engine fetch, so I read the GAO report HTML at files.gao.gov and searched it for each figure. I also read the linked IPWatchdog Sept 29 hearing article and the Jan 2025 Stewart article. Cornell's 31 U.S.C. 717 page failed on a certificate error, so I read it with the client WebFetch.

**Verified true**
- Central claim: attrition rose from 2.9% (FY2020) to 7.6% (FY2025); the 2020-2025 average was 4.8%; the 12-month rate was 7.6% as of August 2026; GAO says attrition must fall below 4.8% to reach under 599,000 by 2031.
- Backlog of about 838,000 in January 2025 (hearing article: 837,928), 756,000 now, and last under 599,000 in November 2020. USPTO announced the 599,000 goal in September 2025 with no timeframe.
- About 18 months in queue before examiner assignment; first-year junior attrition about 40% in FY2021-2023.
- Training cut from 16 to 8 weeks (a June 2025 webinar), reduced remote work, AI tools not yet effective, quality surveys unpublished since 2024, external survey paused, quality reviews not used for training, and the recommendation that USPTO assess risks.
- USPTO provided no comments on the report; the General Counsel's December 2025 non-participation letter and the August 2026 statement of facts are accurate.
- PPAC "only two meetings since January 2025": GAO says it met 26 times in 2023-2024 and twice since January 20, 2025.

**Errors**
1. The article calls Raskin and Johnson Ranking Members of the "Senate Judiciary Committee and the Senate IP Subcommittee". The GAO letter addresses them as Ranking Members of the House Judiciary Committee and its Courts, IP, AI and the Internet Subcommittee. The Senate IP Subcommittee ranking member is Schiff. This is partially false.
2. The article cites "31 U.S.C. § 117". GAO cites § 717(b)(1); the article's own link points to 717.
3. The article says training hours were eliminated "for subject matter eligibility". GAO says the 25-hour annual subject-matter (technical) training bank was eliminated; eligibility is a different legal concept. Also, a March 2026 USPTO webinar said initial training would be 10 weeks, which the article omits.
4. "All-time high" for the 838,000 backlog is not stated in GAO's text, and the "82,000 over 19 months" is attributed to Squires but is only arithmetic (837,928 to 756,110 = 81,818, about 20 months).

**Materially misleading by omission (minor).** GAO's headline is that the backlog fell to 756,000 while applicant wait times for a final decision rose 11% since January 2025 and total pendency rose from 26.2 to 29.0 months. The article gives the backlog gain without that offsetting finding, and so leaves a rosier impression than GAO's. It does report GAO's central warning accurately.
