# Fact-check: "$1 billion in dark money" ahead of 2026 midterms

## Bottom line
The claim is accurate. The $1 billion topline, the Wisconsin figure, and the Delaware figure all check out against independent sourcing. The "$400M vs $294M" MAGA Inc figures are not actually in tension — they're from different points in time, and the framing in the question (implying $294M is the "more recent" number) has it backwards. $294M is the *older* figure; $400M+ is the *more recent* one.

## 1. The $1 billion topline claim — CONFIRMED
The New York Times (published August 31, 2026) reported that "about $1 billion in anonymous money is sloshing through the political landscape in 2026," based on a review of advertising data, campaign finance filings, and tax records, and characterized this as "almost certainly a low-end estimate" given how hard dark money is to trace. This was independently corroborated by secondary coverage from Political Wire (politicalwire.com, 8/31/26) and the Brennan Center for Justice, both summarizing the same NYT figure. The Brennan Center's own reporting adds detail consistent with the topline: on the right, Koch-network-linked spending accounts for $50M+ from undisclosed sources; on the left, a cluster of three environmental nonprofits have put in nearly $50M of dark money. Multiple independent outlets converge on the same $1B figure and the same "low-end estimate" characterization, so this claim is well-supported.

## 2. Wisconsin: Virginia-based group, "nearly $13 million" — CONFIRMED
A Virginia-registered express-advocacy LLC ("First Amendment LLC," reportedly tied to a consulting firm called Make Liberty Win) reported spending nearly $13 million in Wisconsin ahead of the fall elections — $9.3 million of it targeting Milwaukee County Executive David Crowley, the Democratic candidate for governor, plus six-figure buys across roughly 15 state legislative races. As an express-advocacy (not PAC) entity, it is not required to disclose its donors. Confirmed by WisPolitics, Urban Milwaukee, Wisconsin Public Radio, and Hoodline reporting from September 2026 — all reporting the same ~$13M total and $9.3M Crowley-specific figure. Accurate.

## 3. Delaware: Working Families Party National PAC, "nearly half a million" — CONFIRMED
The WFP National PAC spent $455,000 supporting six Delaware legislative candidates (five of them primary challengers to incumbent Democrats) without disclosing its funding source in its state filing. Delaware election officials (spokeswoman Cathleen Hartsky-Carter) disputed the PAC's claim that non-disclosure was permitted, calling it a "misunderstanding," while the PAC says it was told separate FEC filing sufficed. Confirmed by Spotlight Delaware (8/31/26) and corroborating outlets. "Nearly half a million dollars" ($455K) is an accurate characterization.

## 4. MAGA Inc: $400M vs $294M — RESOLVED, not a real contradiction
Both figures are real, but they come from different filing periods, and the more recent one is the larger one:
- **$294 million**: MAGA Inc.'s cash on hand as reported in an FEC disclosure filed January 2, 2026, covering activity through the end of 2025 (the PAC raised $102M in H2 2025). This is the OLDER figure.
- **$400M+ (specifically ~$403.5M–$416M range)**: MAGA Inc. held more than $400 million as of July 31, 2026, per a Detroit News report and other outlets from July 2026. A subsequent FEC filing showed the PAC started August 2026 with $403.5 million and closed the month at $415.8 million, after continued large-donor fundraising through the year (Trump has since said the PAC will spend up to $500M on the midterms).

**What explains the difference**: This is not a discrepancy between two conflicting reports of the same moment — it's normal PAC growth over roughly seven months (January to July/August 2026), during which MAGA Inc raised tens of millions more from major donors while spending little on candidates. The claim in the prompt that the "more recent" disclosure shows $294M is backwards: $294M is the earlier (January) number, and $400M+ is the later (July/August) number. If a source describes $294M as "more recent" than $400M, that source itself has an error or is referencing an even earlier/different filing — the plain chronology of FEC filings shows cash-on-hand rising from ~$294M to $400M+ over 2026, not falling.
