# Fact-check: WNEM, "Michigan violent crime hits seven-year low, governor's office says" (Oct. 26, 2026)

**Rating: 2 Pinocchios (half true / needs context).** Every headline number is the governor's office's own, and I found no independent statewide confirmation. The piece does attribute them correctly. One figure is internally inconsistent across the office's own materials, and a 40% murder drop is presented without the base-year or crime-type context.

## Method
- The WNEM page was fetched only as a summary. It is a News 10/WILX story by Brayden Willis, and I could not get the verbatim text.
- Michigan.gov's press release returned HTTP 403.
- I used the WXYZ rewrite of the release, the governor's Aug. 10, 2026 release (via govdelivery), and searches for independent data.
- Independent statewide MSP or FBI figures were not retrievable here.

## Claims
| # | Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Michigan's violent crime rate is the lowest in seven years | **Unverified.** It is the governor's office's claim, and no dataset or agency is named. I could not reach MSP or FBI data to confirm it. The Aug. 10 release said only that violent crime was down 11% "over the last 5 years", with no baseline year or source. A seven-year low and an 11% five-year decline are compatible, but they are a far smaller change than the 40% murder headline. |
| 2 | Murders down 40% since 2021 | **Partly corroborated, with context.** WXYZ reports the office's wording as murders down "more than 40%" since 2021, and the 2021 figure was a pandemic-era peak. Detroit homicides fell from 308 in 2021 to 165 in 2025, about 46%, which supports the direction. I could not find a statewide MSP murder count. The sources I found disagree on whether the 40% refers to murder or all violent crime, so the wording matters. |
| 3 | Robberies down 50% since Whitmer took office | **Unverified.** WXYZ describes it as "cut in half", with no start year or numbers. The only local data I found was Flint, where 2025 robberies rose 9% over 2024. Flint is one city and not a statewide measure. |
| 4 | More than 1,000 illegal guns taken off the streets | **Supported as an official claim.** It comes from the Operation Safe Neighborhoods results in the Aug. 10 release. It has no start date and is not independently audited. |
| 5 | Nearly $2 billion invested in public safety since 2018 | **Unclear.** The Aug. 10 release says "nearly $2 billion since taking office". WXYZ reports the office's own itemized list at roughly $128 million (a $100M revenue-sharing grant, $25.2M for violence intervention, $2.5M for the task force) and, in another place, "nearly $2 million". The headline total is not itemized in what I could access. |
| 6 | 20% pay raise for State Police troopers | **Supported** by the office's materials as reported by WXYZ (troopers and sergeants). |
| 7 | More than 11,000 check-ins with felony offenders, with drugs and weapons recovered | **Supported as an official claim.** It matches the Aug. 10 release, with no start date, and drug and ammunition recoveries are not quantified. |
| 8 | Nearly a third of violent crimes involve firearms | **Matches the office's "nearly one in three" wording.** It gives no time period or source. |
| 9 | Whitmer: first responders "have the support they need" | Attributed to her. I could not verify the exact quote text. |
| 10 | Tony Gant (Safe and Just Michigan): "amazing news", but credit goes to social services, and stepping back would be unwise | Could not verify. It appears only in the WNEM summary. |
| 11 | Ronald Rampe: more police funding and responsible gun ownership help | Could not verify. It appears only in the WNEM summary, and it is a man-on-the-street opinion. |
| 12 | New gun laws pushed (universal background checks, safe storage) | **Partly misleading.** The office's release says these were already signed (April 2023 for background checks and safe storage, May 2023 for extreme risk protection orders, Nov. 2023 for the domestic-abuser ban). Describing them as new laws being pushed is inconsistent with that. |

## Materially misleading or missing context
1. **Single source.** All the numbers come from the governor's office, in a political context with an election season and criticism from Republicans. The headline says so, which is fair, but the body presents the figures with no independent check or named dataset.
2. **Cherry-picked baselines.** Murder is measured from 2021, a peak year. Robbery is measured from 2019 ("since she took office"). The one overall violent-crime figure in the office's earlier release was a modest 11% over five years.
3. **Causation.** The piece lets the governor credit her spending. Nationwide, murder has fallen sharply since 2021, and Detroit's own decline has been credited to local policing, so state policy cannot be assumed to be the cause.
4. **The $2 billion.** It is not matched by the itemized programs the office lists.

## Bottom line
The direction of the story, that violence is down, is plausible and is supported by Detroit data. The specific statewide figures are official claims I could not independently confirm. Grade: **2 Pinocchios**.

Sources: WNEM/WILX article (summary only); WXYZ and Fox17 coverage of the release; Michigan.gov release (403, not accessed); the governor's Aug. 10, 2026 release on govdelivery; ClickOnDetroit (Jan. 7, 2026) and Flint police CompStat reports for local data.
