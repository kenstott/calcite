# Fact-check: Spokesman-Review, "Democratic lawmakers in Washington state say health..." (Nov. 9, 2026)

Limits: WebFetch returned only a summary of the article, not verbatim text. Quotes are as relayed. I ran two searches, not a full primary-source audit.

## Claims and verdicts
| Claim | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| About 10,000 immigrants (refugees, asylees) lost Apple Health coverage last month | Supported, approximate | OPB (Sept 30) projected 14,000. Later reports (Seattle Times via InsuranceNewsNet) say about 11,000 lost it after state protections kept about 3,000 covered. Article's "around 10,000" is slightly low but within the range. |
| Cuts stem from the 2025 One Big Beautiful Bill Act (H.R. 1) | Supported | Washington Department of Health and community-health pages. |
| More than 1.9 million enrolled in Apple Health | Not checked | I did not look at HCA enrollment data. |
| Washington stands to lose about $3.2B over two years; at least 250,000 could lose coverage | Partly checked | Other sources say 200,000+ lose coverage by end of 2027. 250,000 is plausible as a "could" estimate, but I did not find the underlying state estimate. |
| State spends about $21B on Medicaid a year, about $13B federal | Not checked | Needs HCA or KFF figures. |
| AG filed a lawsuit to block the cuts; said at least 20% of residents could lose coverage | Unverified and possibly misleading | The AG's 2026 suits I found concern the Medicaid work-requirement rule (24 states) and Planned Parenthood defunding. I found no suit blocking the cuts broadly and no 20% figure. 20% of residents would be about 1.6 million, which is far above the state's own 250,000 estimate. Check against the complaint. |
| Jeffries: Democrats would "fight to reverse these painful Medicaid cuts" | Not checked | Quote not located in primary sources. |
| Murray and Schrier named reversing the cuts as a priority | Not checked | Plausible, consistent with their public positions. |
| Ferguson: deficit will be in the billions | Not checked | Plausible. |
| Riccelli: "I don't see ways that we can absorb billions of dollars right now." | Not checked | Attributed quote, unverified. |
| 105-day session begins in January | Plausible | Washington's long session in odd-numbered years runs 105 days. Next session is January 2027. |
| Democrats "retake Congress in January" | Needs care | Contingent on the Nov. 2026 midterm results, which I did not verify. |

## Overall rating
2 Pinocchios (provisional), "some shading". Core facts (OBBBA-driven Medicaid cuts, about 10,000 immigrants losing coverage Oct. 1, state budget pressure) hold up. The main concern is the AG's 20% figure, which conflicts with the other estimates in the same piece and which I could not source. If it is accurate, the rating drops to 1 Pinocchio.

## Misleading by omission or framing
- The 10,000 to 250,000 to 20% progression mixes already-lost coverage, projected losses, and an AG worst-case without reconciling them.
- The article does not say Ferguson's state protections preserved coverage for about 3,000 people.
- It is unclear how a lawsuit could "block" cuts already in effect.
- "Democrats retake Congress" is presented as settled.

## Not checkable here
- The verbatim quotes (Jeffries, Riccelli).
- The AG's 20% statement.
