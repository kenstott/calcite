# Is it true that FBI data shows these 2024→2025 crime declines?

**Short answer: Yes, this is accurate.** All the specific figures in the claim match the FBI's 2025 "Reported Crimes in the Nation" statistics, released around August 18, 2026 (FBI's National Incident-Based Reporting System annual release).

## Verified figures

| Metric | Claimed | Confirmed |
|---|---|---|
| Violent crime overall | fell 9.3% | Yes — largest year-to-year decline since FBI began estimating crime trends in 1936 |
| Murder/nonnegligent manslaughter | fell 18.1% | Yes — also described as the largest one-year drop in this category since 1936 |
| 2025 murder rate | 4.1 per 100,000 | Yes — tied with 1955 and 1956 for the lowest rate since FBI began national estimates |
| Robbery | fell 18.5% | Yes |
| Aggravated assault | fell 7.2% | Yes |
| Property crime overall | fell 12.4% | Yes |

Additional context from the same release that wasn't in the question but corroborates it:
- Rape/sexual assault fell about 7.6%
- Burglary fell 15.8%
- Larceny-theft fell 9.8%
- Motor vehicle theft fell 22.7%
- Estimated totals: about 1,119,768 violent crimes and 14,085 murders nationally in 2025
- Both violent crime and murder rates have now declined every year since a spike in 2022

## Notes on sourcing and caveats

- This is FBI Uniform Crime Reporting (UCR)/NIBRS *estimated* national data, which involves statistical modeling to account for agencies that don't fully report (participation is voluntary). This is normal methodology for these annual releases and doesn't undermine the headline numbers, but it means "fell 9.3%" is a modeled estimate, not a full census count.
- The FBI, Axios, CBS News, and NBC News all report the same headline percentages, and the FBI's own press release and a companion "Violent Crime Falls at Historic Rate" story use the "largest decline since 1936" framing directly.
- Preliminary/early-look data pointing to this trend had already circulated earlier in 2026 (a May 2026 Stateline piece on preliminary FBI data, and an earlier "historic early look" FBI release), and the final annual release in August 2026 confirmed the sharper, finalized numbers cited in the question.
- One minor variance across secondary write-ups: a couple of summaries listed aggravated assault as down 7.5% rather than 7.2%. I fetched the CBS News article directly, which reports 7.2% — matching the figure in the question — so I'm treating 7.2% as correct and the 7.5% figures elsewhere as a transcription variance in secondary coverage, not the FBI's own number.

## Bottom line

Every number in the question — 9.3% violent crime decline, 18.1% murder decline, 4.1 per 100,000 murder rate tied with 1955/1956, 18.5% robbery decline, 7.2% aggravated assault decline, and 12.4% property crime decline — checks out against multiple independent reports of the FBI's 2025 annual crime statistics release.
