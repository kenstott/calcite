# Fact-check: Fortune, "Corporate profits hit roughly 13.5% of GDP. Why Wall Street's bet on even more is risky" (Shawn Tully, 2026-11-28)

Method: WebFetch returned only a summary of the article (not verbatim text), so quotes are checked at summary level. BEA pages were read through WebFetch summaries. BEA level tables (NIPA 1.1.5, 6.16/1.12) were not directly retrievable.

## Claims

| # | Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Profits/GDP rose to 13.37% in Q3 2026 from 13.14% in Q2 | Direction supported: BEA second estimate (Nov 25, 2026) says profits from current production rose $206.0B in Q3 (after +$384.0B in Q2). Exact ratios not checkable here (level tables not retrieved). |
| 2 | Headline says "roughly 13.5%"; subhead 13.4% | Rounding up from 13.37% is loose. The headline overstates by about 0.1 pt. |
| 3 | Nominal GDP grew 6% y/y, $31.18T to $33.06T | Consistent with BEA: nominal GDP grew 6.2% annualized in Q3 (second estimate). The 6.0% arithmetic checks. Levels not directly confirmed. |
| 4 | Corporate earnings +23% y/y, $3.59T to $4.42T | Arithmetic checks (23.1%). 4.42/33.06 = 13.37%, so it is internally consistent. Levels not confirmed against BEA. |
| 5 | Ratio mostly 6-7% 1945-2003, ~10% in dot-com peak, ~11% average 2010-Q2 2025 | Not verified against BEA/FRED series. Plausible, but the series definition (with or without IVA/CCAdj) is not stated in the summary. |
| 6 | Strategists expect +25% profits by end-2027, implying ~15.7% | Strategist forecast is not checkable. The arithmetic is plausible (needs GDP of about $35.2T, +6.4%). |
| 7 | Reversion to 11% would cut profits to ~$3.9T, ~12% below current | Arithmetic checks (3.9/4.42 = -11.8%). It is a scenario, not a forecast. |
| 8 | Quotes: Sahil Kapoor (profitability the most mean-reverting series); Rob Arnott (voter backlash); Mark Zandi (possible permanent reset, bubble-era talk); Milton Friedman (earlier interview) | Not checkable here (private interviews or paywalled text). No contradicting public record found. |

## Overall: 1 Pinocchio (mostly accurate)

The data direction matches BEA. Possible misleading points:
- The "13.5%" headline rounds up.
- The article leans on mean reversion and a hypothetical fall to 11%. Its own source, Zandi, argues for a structural reset. Weaker union power, superstar firms and tariffs could keep margins high, so reversion is an opinion, not a certainty.
- The 11% baseline includes the post-2010 period that may already be elevated.
- BEA profits are revised across estimates (Q2 was revised down $16.9B). A 0.2 pt quarterly move is within revision noise.
- Using a 25% earnings forecast as the "Wall Street bet" frames the forecast as extreme without showing the actual consensus source.
