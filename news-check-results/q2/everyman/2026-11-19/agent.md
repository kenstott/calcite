# Fact-check: Townhall, "Republican senator obliterates the Dems' affordability narrative in under four minutes" (Vespa, 2026-11-18)

Method note: WebFetch returned only a summary of the article (not verbatim text), so claims below are those the summary reported. The video was not watched and no transcript was found. Research was limited (two searches), so most verdicts are provisional.

## Claims and verdicts
1. Republicans failed at 2026 messaging and Democrats defined the debate. Opinion, not checkable.
2. A Democratic wave occurred, with shifts among white working-class voters. Not verified (no precinct or exit-poll data pulled). Not checkable here.
3. About 35 percent of 2024 Trump voters stayed home. Not verified. The article gives it as an "estimate". The source is unknown, and the figure is implausibly high for midterm drop-off unless it comes from a specific survey. Not checkable here.
4. Democrats will hold large majorities in both chambers next year. A forecast, not a fact. Not checkable here.
5. Sen. Bernie Moreno (R-OH) appeared on Ingraham's show and made the argument. Not confirmed. Searches found no coverage of the appearance. Not checkable here.
6. Moreno: Democrats want lower gas prices but are closing refineries. Not confirmed. I found no primary source tying Democrats to refinery closures. Refinery closures in California (Phillips 66, Valero) were driven by company decisions and state policy, so "Democrats are closing refineries" is a partisan simplification.
7. Moreno: subsidies raise costs rather than lower them. Opinion and contested economics. Moreno has argued this about the EV credit (Bloomberg-reported), but economists generally find subsidies raise demand and lower buyer net cost while sometimes raising sticker prices.
8. The $7,500 EV credit "was eliminated". Plausibly true: the One Big Beautiful Bill Act ended the 30D credit for vehicles acquired after Sept 30, 2025 (Roll Call, Oct 2025, reported the credits ending). I did not re-fetch the statute.
9. EV prices fell 3 percent a year later. Partly supported. Kelley Blue Book/Cox data show the average EV transaction price in Aug 2026 at $54,813, down 2.7% year over year, so "about 3 percent" is close. However, other 2026 months showed rises (July reported +1.6% YoY by one source), and Cox says EV incentives fell 24% year on year and supply shrank, which is the opposite of a clean subsidy-removal-lowers-prices story. Cherry-picked.
10. Steve Guest social post criticizing Democratic economic proposals. Not checked.
11. Democrats blocked most cost-of-living legislation. Not checked. Vague and unquantified.

## Materially misleading
- Causal framing: a roughly 3% EV price dip after the credit ended is presented as proof that subsidies raise prices. The same data show price changes driven by discount (incentive) cuts, reduced supply and model withdrawals, and monthly YoY moves flip sign. One data point does not establish causation.
- Net cost to buyers: removing a $7,500 credit raises the buyer's effective cost even if sticker price falls 3%.
- Refinery claim is unsourced and attributes company and state-regulatory decisions to the party.
- Headline "obliterates ... in under four minutes" is characterization of a TV segment, not a fact.

## Overall rating
Mostly opinion commentary with a few checkable numbers. The one verifiable statistic (EV price about -3%) is roughly right but misleadingly framed; other claims could not be confirmed. Rating: 2 Pinnochios (Some shading of the facts), provisional given limited verification.
