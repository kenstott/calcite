# Fact-check: ABC13 (Tom Abrahams), "Bessent says economy not where Trump admin wants it, points to encouraging metrics"

Limits: WebFetch returned a summary of the article, not verbatim text, so quotes are paraphrased by the tool. I could not get the interview video or transcript. Python was not available to me in this run. BLS October 2026 CPI was read directly; other sources are secondary.

## Claims and verdicts

1. Gas prices are about $1 higher than a year ago. SUPPORTED. BLS (Oct 2026 CPI) shows gasoline +37.4% over 12 months, which is roughly $1 at about $3 a gallon.
2. Interest rates are up. PLAUSIBLE, NOT VERIFIED. September jobs coverage mentions Fed-hike odds, which fits, but I did not pull rate data.
3. Egg prices are down over twelve months. NOT CHECKABLE HERE. BLS publishes no 12-month egg change for October 2026 because October 2025 was never collected (appropriations lapse). Monthly eggs: -1.3% Aug-Sep, +0.6% Oct (seasonally adjusted).
4. Unemployment is down over twelve months. LIKELY TRUE. The rate was 4.2% in Sept 2026 and peaked at 4.5% in Nov 2025. But the September report showed it rising from August, and only 29,000 jobs were added.
5. Bessent: a "manufacturing renaissance" with manufacturing and construction jobs returning. PARTLY SUPPORTED. Manufacturing +9,000 in Sept and about +72,000 over an earlier reference period (date cut off in my source). Payroll growth overall is weak. The quote is genuine in Bessent's other interviews (Fox).
6. Bessent: real wages up 1% since the administration took office, better than the prior administration. UNVERIFIED, DOUBTFUL. Average hourly earnings are +3.0% year over year against CPI near 3.4% (August), which implies flat or negative real wages over the last year. A cumulative 1% since January 2025 could be true; I could not confirm the baseline or the comparison with the prior administration.
7. Bessent: core inflation has fallen in three of the past four months. NOT SUPPORTED BY THE DATA I FOUND. Monthly core CPI was +0.2% (Jul), +0.3% (Aug), +0.3% (Sep), +0.3% (Oct). That is flat to rising, not falling. A 12-month core measure might differ, but BLS cannot publish October 12-month changes. This is the most questionable claim, and it is Bessent's, not the reporter's.
8. Bessent: energy prices will fall when the Iran war ends. FORECAST, not checkable. Fox reports a similar claim, including oil as low as $40.
9. Bessent: oil was down 7% that day on hopes of a deal. NOT CHECKABLE HERE. It is a same-day market move; only a secondary report repeats it.
10. Bessent: food cost rate of change has dropped substantially. The piece itself notes prices still rise, only more slowly. BLS: food index +0.5% in October after 0.0% in September, so the recent trend is not clearly decelerating.
11. Voters are split on which party handles the economy, with some recent polls slightly favoring Democrats. NOT VERIFIED (I did not pull polls).
12. Bessent conceded the economy is not where the administration wants it. Reported consistently by the article and a secondary write-up; I did not see the video.

## Rating: 2 Pinocchios (Washington Post scale: 0 to 4)

The reporter's own claims (gas, food nuance, polls) are mostly accurate, and the reporter added a good caveat that slower food-price growth is not falling prices. My rating of 2 reflects that the piece passes along two unchallenged official claims (core inflation falling, real wage growth) that the available BLS data does not support, plus the ambiguity about the baseline. If the claim list is restricted to the reporter's own assertions, the piece is closer to 1 Pinocchio.

## Materially misleading

- Core inflation "falling in three of four months" is not borne out by monthly BLS data (0.2, 0.3, 0.3, 0.3). It is left unchallenged.
- Egg-price and unemployment "down over a year" comparisons rest on a baseline period. October 2025 BLS data is missing, so some year-over-year comparisons cannot be made, and the piece does not say so.
- Real wage claim is framed as a win while year-over-year wage growth trails inflation.
- Strong headline unemployment decline is paired with weak job creation (29,000 in September) that the piece does not mention.
