# Fact-check: The New American, "Census Bureau: U.S. Foreign-Born Population Stagnated or Declined in 2025-26"

Limits: WebFetch returned only a summary of the article (no verbatim text). CIS pages returned 403, so CIS figures were checked via search snippets and secondary reports. Not all primary Census tables were opened. Treat the verdicts below as partly verified.

## Claims and verdicts
1. ACS (released Oct. 29) shows foreign-born up about 174,000 from mid-2024 to mid-2025, versus about 2.4 million the prior year. SUPPORTED by Camarota/CIS ("Slowdown in Foreign-Born Growth through Mid-2025"). Primary Census table not opened. The 2.4M prior-year figure was not independently confirmed.
2. CPS ASEC shows a decline of about 790,000, March 2025 to March 2026. SUPPORTED as a CIS analysis (January 2026 CIS report per search). Primary Census microdata not checked.
3. Monthly CPS shows a decline of about 590,000 in summer 2026 versus 2025. PARTLY CHECKED. CIS reports larger cumulative drops (2.2M Jan-July, 2.9M Jan 2025-Jul 2026). The Census blog shows 53.3M to 51.9M (Jan-Jun 2025). The exact 590,000 was not located.
4. Camarota says early-2025 changes "fundamentally altered prior trends" (attributed quote). Consistent with his CIS writing. Exact wording not verified.
5. Brookings found negative net migration in 2025, the first in 50+ years. SUPPORTED. Brookings estimated -10,000 to -295,000. The estimate is an interval and is disputed; CBO's January 2026 estimate is about +400,000 for 2025.
6. CBP illegal crossings lowest since fiscal 1967. NOT SUPPORTED AS STATED. DHS/CBP say FY2025 Southwest border apprehensions (about 237.5K, preliminary) were the lowest since 1970. The 1967 date was not found in a CBP source. If the article says 1967 it is likely off. Apprehensions are also an imperfect proxy for crossings.
7. Administration actions (asylum limits, more arrests, refugee cuts, H-1B tightening, visa restrictions). Generally consistent with public record. Not checked individually.

## Misleading context
- The headline says "stagnated or declined," but the ACS shows growth (+174K), and it covers only through about July 2025. It does not capture most of the later enforcement effects.
- CPS is a survey with known weaknesses for measuring the foreign-born. A 2024 Census working paper warns against using it for population size. Camarota himself notes possible overstatement. The ACS and CPS figures are not comparable.
- Source is an advocacy organization (CIS) that favors restricting immigration. The piece is an opinion-leaning advocacy article, not independent reporting.
- Causal claim that the policies "are working" is not established by these data. The decline reflects fewer arrivals and perhaps more departures. Camarota says the shift began late in the Biden administration.
- The Census Bureau's own estimates show a large drop in net international migration (projected 321K for 2025-26), consistent with the direction of the claim.

## Rating: 2 Pinocchios (Some Shading)
The core numbers trace to real CIS analyses of Census data and the direction is corroborated, but the piece leans on the weakest data source, mixes incompatible surveys, gives an unconfirmed "since 1967" border claim, and draws policy-causation conclusions the data cannot support.

## Not checkable here
Exact article wording and quotes; the 590,000 monthly-CPS figure; the 2.4M prior ACS growth; the 1967 CBP date.
