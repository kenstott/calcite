# Fact-check: WWeek, "Violent crime rose in Portland over past two years" (2026-11-19)

Method limits: WebFetch returned only a summary of the article, not verbatim text. Search tools gave secondary reports. I could not open the PPB open-data dashboard directly. Python was not available in this session. No attributed direct quotes were visible beyond a paraphrased PPB spokesperson statement.

## Claims and verdicts
1. Person crime up 6% YTD through Oct 2026 vs 2025; 11% vs 2024. Not checkable here. It matches the article's own citation of the PPB data tracker, and I could not reach the tracker.
2. Person crime has declined monthly since July. Not checkable here (same dashboard).
3. Simple assault and intimidation make up 57% of person offenses and account for 412+ added offenses. Not checkable here (same dashboard). The figures are internally consistent with a 6% rise.
4. Intimidation up 14% (92 offenses). Not checkable here. Arithmetic is plausible.
5. Aggravated assault up about 1% (14 offenses). Not checkable here. If true, it undercuts the headline.
6. Oregon violent crime fell 7.9% 2024 to 2025, and murder fell 24.4%. Supported. A KATU report attributes these figures to the FBI Oregon field office. They are consistent with the FBI national 2025 report (violent crime -9.3%, murder -18.1%).
7. Statewide violent crime through Aug 2026 down about 16% vs 2025. Partly supported. KATU reports a decline through August, and I did not confirm the exact figure.
8. Portland property crime fell nearly 4% in 2025 and is up about 1 point in 2026. Not checkable here.
9. A PPB spokesperson said the bureau has little insight into why person crime is rising locally while falling elsewhere. Not checkable here. The paraphrase is consistent with the article's reported content.

Related context I found: PPB recorded 389 shootings through Sept 2026, down 9.7% from 2025 (KATU). Homicide is reported to have declined since 2024.

## Rating: 2 Pinocchios (mixed)
The reported numbers appear to be accurately sourced. The framing is the problem.

## Misleading elements
- The headline says "violent crime," but the data are "person crime." The increase is driven by simple assault and intimidation, which are not FBI Part I violent crimes. Aggravated assault is nearly flat, and homicide and shootings are down.
- The "rose over two years" claim rests on a 2024 baseline. The trend is down since July.
- The Portland-versus-state comparison mixes categories. Person crime is an NIBRS category, and the state figures are FBI violent crime (murder, rape, robbery, aggravated assault).
- Reporting changes could affect intimidation and simple assault counts, and the piece gives no context on that.
