## Overall grade: 1 Pinocchio (borderline 0)
Every number I could trace matches the NHFPI brief, the arithmetic is right, and the outlet labels its own calculation and notes that it did not recompute from federal files. The deduction is for framing, below.

## Materially misleading or missing context
1. The "up 6%" headline depends on how enrollment is counted. The same brief shows CC fall enrollment down 34.9% over the same decade. The piece reports both but does not explain the 40-point gap (headcount over a year including dual-enrolled and part-time students versus a fall snapshot).
2. Dual enrollment is not comparable across the decade. The brief only has data from 2022-23, so we cannot say how much of the 6% is dual-enrollment growth. Subtracting 9,364 from 24,741 gives 15,377, versus 23,325 in 2014-15, but the 2014-15 base probably also contained some dual-enrolled students, so "excluding them the system shrank" would overstate it.
3. Other counts of dual enrollment differ. CCSNH reports about 11,000 NH high school students in dual-enrollment courses in 2024-25 (course-based count), and a 2025 review put early college at about 47% of FY2024 headcount. These use different definitions from the 37.8% figure; readers should not mix them.
4. Only public USNH and CCSNH are counted; SNHU and other private online enrollment is excluded (disclosed).
5. Several figures come from one source (NHFPI, a policy think tank), so the piece verifies the brief more than it independently audits the data.
