# Fact-check: Axios Indianapolis, "Indy pedestrian deaths test Vision Zero promises" (2026-11-12)

## Limits of this check
- Axios returned HTTP 403 to WebFetch. I could not read the full article. Only search-snippet excerpts were available, so claims and quotes beyond those excerpts could not be extracted or checked.
- I did not retrieve primary data (SGA Dangerous by Design report, city Vision Zero dashboard). Verdicts rest on secondary coverage and are provisional.

## Claims found in excerpts and status
1. Nearly a year after the Vision Zero Task Force adopted a pedestrian-protection plan, pedestrian deaths in Indianapolis have "nearly doubled."
   - PARTIALLY SUPPORTED, not confirmed. Sources show 23 pedestrian deaths among 61 traffic deaths from Jan 1 to Sep 30, 2026 (task force report, via Yahoo/IndyStar-style coverage). Vision Zero Administrator LeAndre Level said pedestrian deaths and serious injuries are outpacing last year. No source I found gave a 2025 comparison figure, so "nearly doubled" is unverified. The baseline period (and whether it is a same-period comparison) matters.
2. Local leaders committed to eliminating traffic deaths and serious injuries by 2035.
   - SUPPORTED by Mirror Indy and other local coverage (2035 goal).
3. Smart Growth America ranks Indianapolis 47th of the 101 largest metros for pedestrian fatalities; 231 pedestrian deaths 2020-2024, up from 170 in 2015-2019.
   - NOT CHECKABLE HERE (primary SGA report not retrieved). Matches a March 2026 Axios piece in the search listing, but that is the same outlet, not independent.
4. Plan adopted "nearly a year" earlier.
   - SUPPORTED: Mirror Indy indicates the Vision Zero Action Plan was adopted in December 2025.
5. All other claims and quotes in the article: NOT CHECKABLE HERE (article body inaccessible).

## Rating
Provisional: 1 Pinocchio (mostly accurate; insufficient access to grade higher confidence). This is a low-confidence grade because most of the article was not read.

## Potentially misleading
- "Nearly doubled" over a short, small-number series (tens of deaths) is volatile, and the year-to-date figure is partial (through Sep 30 officially). A comparison across mismatched periods would overstate the trend. Also, one year is too early to judge a plan whose measures were still being implemented. Note the same task force reported progress on overall traffic fatalities, which headline framing about pedestrians alone may omit.
