# Fact-check: Yankee Institute, "Connecticut candidates promised lower electric bills. Now comes the hard part." (2026-11-13)

Limitation: WebFetch returned only a summary of the article, not verbatim text, so I could not extract every quote. Claims below are from that summary. Verification used web search results only.

## Claims
1. Sen. Saud Anwar said the 2025 Ratepayers First Act "cut electric bills by $800 million over three years." PARTLY SUPPORTED. Senate Democrats' releases (June 2, 2025) say "nearly $800 million" in cuts over three years. It is a projection, not realized savings. Much of it is a shift of costs to state bond borrowing (about $250 million hardship debt), so it is not a cut in total cost. Competing estimates exist (the governor's adviser said $350 million a year).
2. Eversource asked PURA for a distribution rate increase adding about $33 a month. SUPPORTED. The request is $727 million a year, about 18% on a typical bill, in docket 26-05-10, filed July 2026, effective July 1, 2027 if approved (WFSB, Hartford Business, AARP).
3. About $20 a month if storm costs are handled separately. PLAUSIBLE, NOT DIRECTLY VERIFIED. The Attorney General says storm costs from 2018-2025 drive much of the increase, and that the increase is about 11% without them. That is consistent with a smaller figure.
4. PURA hearings: Danbury Nov. 23, virtual Dec. 9. SUPPORTED (WFSB: Danbury, Nov. 23, 5:30 pm, WCSU; virtual, Dec. 9, noon). New London on Nov. 16: NOT CONFIRMED. A comment session was held Nov. 9, and I could not find a New London date.
5. Legislative session begins Jan. 6, 2027. NOT CHECKED. It matches the usual first Wednesday after the first Monday in January.
6. Three 2026 House bills would move the Public Benefits Charge to the General Fund, and none got a hearing. NOT CHECKED against cga.ct.gov.
7. An Office of Consumer Counsel report on the charge's line items is due Jan. 15, 2027. NOT CHECKED. Not checkable here.
8. Most campaign platforms had goals without specific plans. OPINION. It is not a fact claim and cannot be checked.
9. Argument that the Renewable Portfolio Standard should be loosened to allow nuclear and large hydro. OPINION. Existing nuclear and some large hydro already count toward some Connecticut Class III or other requirements. I did not check the details.

## Context a reader needs
- The piece is from the Yankee Institute, a free-market think tank, and its policy advocacy is not neutral.
- The Ratepayers First Act's "$800 million" is a political figure, and the piece reportedly attributes it to a candidate rather than adopting it. That is fair.
- The rate case is Eversource's first distribution case since 2017, and the AG has called it the largest request in state history. Much of it is storm cost and other legacy costs, not public benefits charges. Focusing on the PBC could understate this.

## Grade
2 Pinocchios is not warranted, because the piece appears mostly accurate on the checkable facts. Grade: 1 Pinocchio (mostly accurate, with one unverified hearing detail and an opinion-heavy framing). Confidence is limited because I did not see the full text.
