**The Stateline article's claims about student loan defaults and coming collections are mostly accurate, with loose timing language, are balanced and mostly accurate; 1 claim could not be confirmed.**

Rating: 1 Pinocchio.

Article: "Collections are coming for millions of student loans in default," Stateline, Tim Henderson, Nov 12, 2026. stateline.org returned HTTP 403 to both fetch tools, so I read the identical syndicated copy at newsfromthestates.com.

Claims checked:
- Central claim, 9+ million in default owing $234B: TRUE on the numbers. Warehouse edu.fsa_loan_portfolio_status, federally_managed, cumulative_in_default, FY2026 Q3 (2026-06-30): 9.3M recipients, $234.1B (Q2: 9.0M, $220.3B).
- "Pandemic forbearance finally expired last year": MOSTLY TRUE. Payments resumed Oct 2023, the on-ramp ended Oct 2024 (NY Fed), and first defaults showed up 2025:Q4. SAVE forbearance still covers 8.0M borrowers.
- "Collections repeatedly paused, most recently January": MOSTLY TRUE. ED delayed collections Jan 16, 2026, but they had restarted in May 2025 (CNBC).
- Washington Sun, Oct 19, 2026, plans likely announced after midterms: TRUE.
- NY Fed, 13% in LA and MS, at least 5% in every state: TRUE (matches the Fed's chart-data workbook: LA 13, MS 13, minimum 5).
- Older borrowers struggling more: TRUE (mean age 38.9 vs 36.4, more mass at 50+).
- State ranking (MS, LA, NM above 25%; floor 15%): MOSTLY TRUE as graded. Stateline's own analysis could not be reproduced because studentaid.gov did not render and the warehouse has no state default table. AP (via Fortune) has Mississippi highest at 28.3% and Louisiana near the top, but New Mexico only "rounding out" the top 15. The 15% floor was not confirmed.
- Vocational schools with late-payment rates as high as two-thirds, per a 2025 federal report: UNSUPPORTED. The FSA nonpayment file now online is the Aug 2026 refresh (proprietary 34% overall).
- Quotes from Mayotte, Varro, Lewis, Cooper and Tappan, and the personal-loan anecdotes, were not graded: they are opinion or personal circumstance with no testable source.

Misleading-framing flags (none material):
1. 9M+ is a cumulative stock that includes about 5.3M already in default before the pandemic. The post-pause wave is about 3.6M (NY Fed).
2. The NY Fed judges the largest default wave has passed.
3. Sun sources say the restart may slip again, a hedge the article keeps.
