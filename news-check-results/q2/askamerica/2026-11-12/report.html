Summary

Rating: 1 Pinocchio (some shading, no material falsehood). The headline numbers check out: FSA's own portfolio data show 9.3 million borrowers and $234.1 billion in cumulative default at June 30, 2026, matching 'more than 9 million ... $234 billion'. The New York Fed figures (13% of borrowers newly in default in Louisiana and Mississippi, at least 5% everywhere) match the Fed's published chart data exactly. The weak spots are timing phrasing: the pandemic payment pause did not 'finally expire last year', and collections were not simply 'repeatedly paused' (they restarted in May 2025 before the January 2026 delay). The state-by-state ranking is Stateline's own analysis; an AP analysis agrees on Mississippi and Louisiana but ranks New Mexico near the bottom of its top 15. The vocational-school figure could not be verified.

Claim-by-claim

  • 9+ million in default owing $234B (central): true on the numbers. Warehouse: cumulative_in_default, federally_managed, FY2026 Q3 = 9,300,000 recipients, $234.1B (Q2 FY26: 9.0M, $220.3B).
  • 'Since pandemic forbearance finally expired last year': mostly true. Payments resumed Oct 2023 and the on-ramp ended Oct 2024 (NY Fed); new defaults only began appearing 2025:Q4. SAVE forbearance still covers 8.0M borrowers.
  • Collections 'repeatedly paused, most recently this past January': mostly true. ED announced the delay Jan 16, 2026; collections had resumed May 2025.
  • Washington Sun reported in October that plans would likely be announced after the midterms: true.
  • NY Fed: 13% in LA and MS, at least 5% in every state: true.
  • Older borrowers struggling more: true.
  • State ranking (MS, LA, NM top; MS 28.3% per AP): mostly true. AP lists New Mexico only among the 15th-ranked group, and does not give the 15% floor.
  • Vocational schools as high as two-thirds: unsupported.

Anything materially misleading?

Not materially. (1) 'More than 9 million in default' is a cumulative stock including about 5.3 million in default before the pandemic (AEI/ED, Nov 2025); the post-pause wave is about 3.6 million per the NY Fed. (2) The NY Fed believes the largest wave of defaults has passed. (3) The Washington Sun's sources say restart may slip again, a hedge the article keeps with 'as soon as next year'.
