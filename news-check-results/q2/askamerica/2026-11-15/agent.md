Article: Jon Marcus, Hechinger Report, Nov 13, 2026. It fetched fine (no 403).

**Warehouse check**
- "$175,000 is more than double the median household income": census.acs1_income (ACS 1-year, 2025) gives $84,780, so 2.06x. TRUE. The Census CPS figure the article links ($87,460) gives only 2.0008x, so it is true but barely.
- The warehouse has no table for share of students paying sticker price (edu.college_scorecard carries net price only). The other claims were checked against primary sources.

**Verified true against primary sources**
- Middlebury: cost of attendance $97,386; tuition $72,924; non-tuition costs $24,462 (the difference); $175,000 pledge; about 630 new students (CDS first-time first-year 316+318=634).
- Thresholds confirmed on school pages: UChicago, Harvard, BU, Emory, Yale, Wellesley, Swarthmore, Colgate, Davidson, Middlebury, Tufts, Notre Dame, CMU, Brandeis (full tuition at $75k; half tuition to $200k), Wake Forest, Marist ($32,000 cap), Earlham, UNH. Prescott, Tulsa and Concordia St. Paul confirmed as price cuts.
- Sallie Mae: 79% eliminated schools on price. TRUE.
- Lumina-Gallup: 23% within $5,000, so 77% were not. TRUE, but the survey asked about annual net cost at in-state public colleges (2024), not "price".

**Mostly true, with shading**
- Levine, "already free for almost all those kids": Brookings says "many" eligible students were already covered and the policies "largely relabel" aid. "Almost all" is stronger, and students near the threshold do pay less.
- Gallup "nearly two-thirds say unaffordable": the source says 63% rate four-year colleges as doing a poor job providing an affordable education.
- Public confusion "helping drive" near-record-low confidence: Gallup shows 38% confident (57% in 2015) and cites cost (30%) and politics (31%). It does not measure confusion.

**Unverified (graded unsupported, not contradicted)**
- Strada "nearly 70 percent" find aid confusing: the Strada page says aid and costs are confusing and 77% think college unaffordable, but the 70% figure was not found.
- "445 colleges where no student pays sticker" and "fewer than 1 in 5 private nonprofit families pay it": Hechinger's own undisclosed analysis. Not reproducible from the warehouse or NCES Digest. The Sallie Mae survey the article cites found 39% of families self-reporting full advertised price (different measure). The article does not address this tension.

**Not fetched**: MIT, Johns Hopkins, Penn, Rice and Princeton pages returned 403/406. Those threshold rows are unverified, not contradicted. Quotes from Baucom, Curvin, Mark, Doughty and Valdes-Fauli are attributed opinion or anecdote and were not graded as factual claims.

**Misleading-framing flags**
1. Net-cost and affordability-rating survey results are paraphrased as "price" and "unaffordable".
2. The headline "nobody pays sticker" statistics have no disclosed methodology.
3. Confusion is implied as a driver of low confidence without evidence.
4. "Increasingly self-destructive" is author opinion.

None of these undermines the central, well-supported point that most of these pledges re-label aid already given.
