Findings

  • More than double median income: warehouse ACS 1-year 2025 $84,780 gives 2.06x; the Census CPS figure the article links ($87,460) gives 2.0008x, so true but barely.
  • Thresholds: confirmed on school pages for UChicago, Harvard, BU, Emory, Yale, Wellesley, Swarthmore, Colgate, Davidson, Middlebury, Tufts, Notre Dame, CMU, Brandeis (full tuition at $75,000; half tuition to $200,000), plus Wake Forest, Marist, Earlham, UNH. Prescott, Tulsa and Concordia St. Paul confirmed as price cuts. Not fetched (403/406): MIT, Johns Hopkins, Penn, Rice, Princeton.
  • Levine: Brookings says 'many' eligible students already had tuition covered; 'almost all' is stronger.
  • Surveys: Sallie Mae 79% true; Lumina 23% within $5,000 (so 77% not; but it concerns annual net cost at in-state public, 2024, not 'price'); Gallup 63% say poor job on affordability (article: 'unaffordable'); Gallup confidence 38%, near record low.
  • Unverified: Strada 70%; the 445-college and 1-in-5 figures. Sallie Mae's own survey, cited by the article, found 39% self-reporting paying the full advertised price (different measure), a tension left unaddressed.

Misleading-framing flags

(1) Net cost and affordability-rating findings are paraphrased as price and 'unaffordable'. (2) The 'nobody pays sticker' statistics are not reproducible from the text. (3) Attributing low confidence to confusion goes beyond Gallup, which cites cost, not confusion. (4) 'Increasingly self-destructive' is opinion. None undermines the central point that most pledges re-label existing aid.
