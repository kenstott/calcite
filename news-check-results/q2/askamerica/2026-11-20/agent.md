The Yankee Institute article's claims about Connecticut electric bills, the Eversource rate case and the Public Benefits Charge are somewhat biased and mostly accurate; 2 claims could not be confirmed.

**Rating: 1 Pinocchio.**

Report: /Volumes/main/Users/kennethstott/IdeaProjects/calcite/news-check-results/q2/askamerica/2026-11-20/report.html

**Verified**
- Typical household bill about $200 a month, second-highest in the nation. AskAmerica's `energy.state_energy_burden` (EIA, 2025) shows CT at $206.44 a month, behind only Hawaii at $207.92. MD is third at $184.46. CT ranks sixth on price per kWh (29.31 cents). The "sixth in overall residential energy costs" ranking is CT Insider's and was not recomputed.
- Eversource: about $33 a month with storm costs, about $20 without. NBC Connecticut reports $757M (18%) and $451M (11%). The hearing dates, venues and times also match NBC.
- Anwar's quote is on his site. The $800 million figure matches the Senate Democrats' own estimate, "nearly $800 million over three years".
- HB 5028, 5048 and 5080 would move the Public Benefits Charge to the General Fund and were referred to Energy and Technology.
- Class I renewable sources match the PURA definition.

**Not verified**
- Demicco's $300 million and Allie-Brennan's $250 million: the campaign pages fetched do not contain the text.
- Cabrera's quote: not found on his page.
- Fazzino's, Khan's, Kiner's and McGinnis's platform wording: the pages loaded without the quoted text, or would not load.
- "No hearing or vote" on the three bills: trackers show no action past referral, but the CGA status pages failed with a TLS error.
- The OCC report deadline extended to Jan. 15, 2027 by PA 26-68: the PDF would not load and searches found nothing.
- The rule on new nuclear plants built after October 2023: not checked.
- The Jan. 6, 2027 session start: consistent with the state constitution, not fetched from a primary source.

**Misleading framing**
- The piece says lawmakers could "stop ratcheting up" the renewable portfolio standard. PURA's schedule shows Class I falling from 30% in 2025 to 25% in 2026, then rising 1 point a year to 29% by 2030. The total falls from 39% to 34%, then rises to 37% by 2030. The mandate was recently cut. This claim is graded partially false.
- The $800 million figure is a sponsors' estimate. Sen. Gordon puts recurring savings near $200 million a year and says part comes from shifting costs to state bonding. The article does not mention the dispute.
- The piece faults Democratic candidates for missing detail while giving its own recommendations (scrap the mandate, cut programs) no costs or tradeoffs.
