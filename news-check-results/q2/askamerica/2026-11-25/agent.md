## Verified true (11 author claims, 1 Morath claim)
- **Central claim:** about 135,000 fewer students, the largest one-year drop in state history. This is a preliminary early-October TEA figure, confirmed by Fox 4 and Community Impact accounts of Morath's Nov 18 State Board of Education briefing. The article does call it preliminary.
- **COVID drop:** about 122,000 in 2020-21. The warehouse shows -122,592 (edu.ccd_districts), and TEA shows 5,371,586, -2.2%.
- **Record high:** 5,545,640 in 2024-25 (CCD, the series maximum). TEA's own figure is 5,544,255.
- **Two-year loss:** about 212,000. TEA 2024-25 (5,544,255) minus about 5.33M is about 211-214k.
- **Population growth:** Texas grew from 30.12M to 31.71M (2022-2025, Census PEP).
- **66% of districts declined 2024-25 to 2025-26:** matches Community Impact, which attributes it to Morath.
- **85% public-school share:** matches Morath.
- **Births dipped in 2020:** 364,360 in the July 2020-June 2021 data year against 378,664 in 2019.
- **Morath blamed births:** he said births are "driving the train."
- **Hinojosa to replace Morath:** confirmed.
- **Funding follows students:** confirmed by the Texas Tribune and Fox 4.

## Unsupported (4 claims, plus 1 in the source audit)
- **"Pattern across the country" quote:** not found in the Fox 4 or Community Impact accounts, and the warehouse has no national K-12 series.
- **Prosper and Princeton ISD "grew by as much as 14%":**
  - The warehouse ends at 2024-25.
  - Prosper reportedly grew about 7% in 2025-26.
  - Princeton projected about +13.5% (11,297 against 9,956).
  - The TEA district table could not be searched.
- **Pre-COVID 89% public-school share:** no source states 89%. Fox 4 and Community Impact say about 90% a decade ago, falling gradually to under 86%.
- **Immigration fears keeping students out:** the Texas Tribune offers only advocacy and anecdote, and Texas 2036 said it could not determine how much enforcement contributed. Net international migration also fell from 354,864 (2024) to 167,475 (2025), a competing cause.
- **"Most districts lost millions and millions of dollars":** no statewide total found. Houston Public Media's roughly $300M estimate covers vouchers only and comes from anti-voucher groups.
- **Source audit (not scored): "about 28,000 public school students pulled into private schools, according to Houston Public Media":** that story has no 28,000 figure.
  - It says 30% of voucher recipients were previously in public school, with more than 120,000 vouchers awarded.
  - 30% of the 85,000 confirmed by late July is about 25,600; 30% of 118,000 is about 35,000.
  - Axios reports 70% of participants were previously private or homeschooled.

## Materially misleading framing, even where the facts check out
1. **Births carry too much of the explanation.** Texas births recovered to 383,791 (2022) and 390,203 (2025), above 2019's 378,664. A 2020-21 dip cannot by itself explain a 135,000 one-year loss. Morath also cited reduced migration and non-public options, and said in May "We cannot tell you the precise cause."
2. **The 66% figure is last year's.** It describes 2025-26 but sits beside this year's headline loss. No 2026-27 district share was given.
3. **Preliminary against final.** The 135,000 compares early-October counts with final data. TEA's official snapshot is due in February. The 122,000 COVID figure is final.
4. **Voucher link overstated.** The linked story comes from anti-voucher groups, and its figures do not match the article's.
5. **Omission.** Fox 4 notes Morath did not address the voucher program's effect. The piece also does not mention his point that the senior class is larger than the first-grade class for the first time ever.
