FBI's claimed 2024-to-2025 crime decline is confirmed by independent computation, except historical superlatives which cannot be checked here

crime.cde_offenses (FBI CDE), 51 states + DC, full calendar years 2024 vs 2025

Summary

The core numerical claims in the FBI's 'First Look: 2025 Crime Data' release, as reported by CBS News, are confirmed by an independent computation from this corpus's FBI Crime Data Explorer (CDE) table. Summing monthly state-level offense rates x population across all 51 states + DC for full calendar years 2024 and 2025 gives: violent crime -9.26% (claimed -9.3%), murder -17.6% (claimed -18.1%), robbery -18.70% (claimed -18.5%), aggravated assault -7.26% (claimed -7.2%), and property crime -12.14% (claimed -12.4%). Every figure matches within about half a percentage point or less. The computed 2025 murder rate is 4.19 per 100,000, close to the claimed 4.1. The one part of the claim this corpus cannot check is the historical superlative -- 'largest decline since 1936' and 'tied with 1955 and 1956 for lowest rate' -- because this corpus's crime data only goes back to 2010.

Data and method

Table used: crime.cde_offenses (FBI Crime Data Explorer, summarized crime rates by state, offense, and month). This table has no single 'national' row, so a national estimate was built by summing offense_rate * population / 100000 across all 51 state_abbr values (50 states + DC) and all 12 months of each calendar year -- the same units the FBI's own national estimate procedure aggregates from agency-level reports. Coverage was checked first: both 2024 and 2025 have all 51 jurisdictions and 12 months present, with average population_coverage_pct of 96.5% (2024) and 96.2% (2025), and monthly coverage stable in the 95-97% range throughout both years (no anomalous low-coverage month distorting either year's total).

A separate, less reliable table was checked and set aside: crime.cde_trends carries a single live snapshot of FBI-published national year-over-year trend percentages (e.g. Violent Crime -9.0%, Murder -18.7%, Aggravated Assault -6.2%, as of a 'June 2025 - May 2026' rolling 12-month window refreshed September 15, 2026). This table's trend_pct column was flagged by this session's own query diagnostics as a high-severity 'broken_field' issue -- values reported out-of-domain for what the column name implies, with the Aggravated Assault row's -6.2% cited by the diagnostic as the example flagged value across all 10 rows. Separately, cde_trends's window is a rolling trailing-12-month comparison that keeps moving forward each month rather than a fixed calendar-year 2024-vs-2025 comparison, so it is not even measuring the same thing as the article's claim. For both reasons, no figure in this report or in the claims table above relies on cde_trends or its trend_pct column -- every cited value comes solely from the independently-aggregated crime.cde_offenses calendar-year computation.

What could not be checked, and why

crime.cde_offenses, along with every other crime table in this corpus, has a declared and observed coverage window of 2010-2026. No table here carries a national violent-crime or murder-rate series reaching back to 1936, or even to 1955-1956. The claims that the 9.3% violent-crime decline is 'the largest year-to-year decline since FBI estimations began in 1936' and that the 4.1 murder rate is 'tied with 1955 and 1956 for the lowest rate since FBI estimations began' are therefore not checkable from this corpus -- this is a genuine data-availability gap (the underlying FBI historical series predates this connector's ingestion window), not evidence the claims are wrong. A search of this corpus's catalog (search_catalog, describe_table) confirmed no substitute table covers pre-2010 national crime rates.

Small residual gaps and their likely source

The computed figures are close to but not identical to the claimed figures (typically within 0.05-0.5 percentage points, largest gap on murder at 0.5 points). This is consistent with a methodology difference rather than a data problem: the FBI's official 'First Look' release uses its own estimation procedure to project a national total from ~17,000+ reporting agencies (~96% of population), while this corpus's cde_offenses table separately estimates each state's rate and this analysis sums state-level rate x population products -- a slightly different aggregation path that will not reproduce the FBI's national estimate to the decimal point even when both start from the same underlying agency submissions. The direction and rough magnitude of every figure agree.

What This Report Does Not Answer

  • Verify 'largest decline since 1936' and 'tied with 1955/1956 for lowest rate' historical superlatives: crime.cde_offenses (the only national crime time series in this corpus) has coverage of 2010-2026 only, both declared and observed. No table here carries a crime series reaching back to 1936, 1955, or 1956, so these specific historical comparisons cannot be verified or refuted from this data. Graded 'not checkable here' rather than assumed true or false.
