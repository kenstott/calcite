**The Fortune article's claims about the defense share of the federal budget, CBO FY2026 spending and the China gap are balanced and partly false; 1 claim could not be confirmed.**

Rating: 2 Pinocchios (unchanged after regrouping).

Article: https://fortune.com/2026/11/25/u-s-defense-federal-budget-china-military-investment-gap/. Report: http://127.0.0.1:62747/a/5dc8aad84ca0c6cc059cf58c71b55042.html (report_id 20261127-063045-the-fortune-article-s-claims-about-the-d). Scores describe this article only.

Regrouping: the two quotes by named third parties are now subject_claims with a speaker set. The "$23 trillion on interest" quote is attributed to Rep. David Schweikert (Joint Economic Committee). The China-gap quote is attributed by Fortune to RAND's National Security Research Division, but it is actually from the Commission on the National Defense Strategy, July 2024. All figures Fortune reports as fact stay author_claims. Verdicts are unchanged.

**What holds**
- Defense fell from about 49% to 13% of the budget since 1962. OMB gives $52.3B of $106.8B = 49.0% for 1962; Treasury MTS gives 13.05% for FY2026. This matches Brookings, which I read.
- Defense is about 2.8-3% of GDP. CBO shows 2.94% for FY2025, and Brookings says about 3%.
- Debt is $40.7T. Treasury shows $40.79T on 11/24.
- Net interest rose about 11% per a search summary of CBO. The $1.14T level is not reproduced: the warehouse shows $1.05T on a different definition.

**What is wrong**
1. **Headline (mostly false).** "Lowest share of the federal budget since World War II" fails on OMB data: defense was 11.1% of outlays in FY2020-21 and 12.2% in FY2022, below 2026's 12.8-13%. Brookings says only that the GDP share is lowest since the 1930s.
2. **FY2026 defense outlays (partially false).** Fortune says $917B; Treasury shows $965.2B and OMB $966.4B for FY2026. $917B is the FY2025 level. The $48B increase is right; the percentage is about 5.3-5.5%, not 5.8%.
3. **China quote (partially false, subject claim).** Fortune attributes it to RAND three years ago. The quote is from the congressionally mandated Commission on the National Defense Strategy, July 2024, about two years ago. The text matches.
4. **Deficit (mostly true).** Fortune says $1.9T; Treasury receipts and outlays give about $1.99T.

**Unsupported**
- The "$3.5 trillion" for Social Security, Medicare and Medicaid, up $217B (6.7%). The warehouse has Social Security at $1.667T and Medicare at $1.073T, about $2.74T combined, and no Medicaid line. The $217B matches the reported CBO deficit increase, so the figures may be conflated.
- The Schweikert "$23 trillion" quote (subject claim). I did not locate the JEC report, so it is not checked. The JEC 2036 claim and the CRFB countdown are likewise unchecked.

**Misleading framing**
- "Squeezed" describes share, not cuts. Defense dollars rose about 5% in FY2026, and the GDP share has been near 3% since 2017.
- The "China narrows the gap" framing rests on a 2024 quote, with no new evidence in the piece.
