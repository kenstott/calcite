The Fortune article's claims about data-center electricity use in Oregon, Virginia and Iowa are balanced and mostly accurate; 4 claims could not be confirmed.

**Rating: 1 Pinocchio.** Article: https://fortune.com/2026/11/20/data-centers-electricity-use-oregon-virginia-states/ (Grist syndication). Honesty score 91, bias score 0 (no consistent lean). These scores describe this article only.

**Warehouse check (energy.eia_electricity_prices, EIA retail sales):**
- Oregon: 14.0 TWh (ECOnorthwest) / 61.54 TWh (2025) = 22.7%, matching "about 23%".
- 2024 sales: OR 59.68, VA 138.04, IA 54.93 TWh. The article's shares imply 11.1, 21.4 and 7.2 TWh, about half of the study's 82 TWh national total, so they are plausible.
- Oregon sales rose 51.02 to 61.54 TWh in 2020-25 (+10.53 TWh). With data centers at about 14.0 TWh in 2025 and "nearly quadrupled since 2020", the 96% figure is arithmetically consistent.
- 82 TWh is 2.06% of 2024 EIA retail sales. Nature Sustainability says about 1.8% of US consumption, so the article's "about 2 percent" is a mild rounding.

**Verified from primary sources:**
- Dominion 10-K: data centers were 28% of Virginia Power sales in 2025 and 26% in 2024.
- Warren-Hawley quote: matches the March 26, 2026 press release.
- EIP: 143 GW and 74 plants.
- Cleanview: about 2 GW across four projects, mostly xAI.
- Oregon DOE: the "lacks a policy mechanism" quote.

**Unsupported (could not read a primary source):**
- Oregon 96% sales-growth share. The full ECOnorthwest PDF exceeds the fetch cap, and neither OPB nor the slides state it.
- State shares of 18.6/15.5/13.1%. The numerators were not read, though the denominators are confirmed.
- "About two dozen of 74 plants permitted."
- The NYT emissions comparison to Jordan, Kenya and Guatemala, and the 10 and 325 g/kWh grid rates. The NYT page returned HTTP 403 and the Heated and EPA eGRID pages did not carry the figures.
- Not checked: West Virginia "second to Texas, more than 15 GW".

**Misleading framing:**
- The headline presents modeled, preliminary capacity-based estimates alongside metered EIA sales as if both were observed.
- Oregon's 23% (all 111 facilities, 2025) and 18.6% (verified hyperscale only, 2024) are different universes.
- Virginia's 15.5% against Dominion's 28% mixes years (2024 vs 2025), geography (statewide vs Dominion territory) and scope. The undercount inference is reasonable, but the whole gap is not undercount.
