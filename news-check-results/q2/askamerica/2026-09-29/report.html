NYT's $1B 'dark money' midterms review checks out on every verifiable figure — Wisconsin, Delaware, and MAGA Inc.'s cash growth all confirm exactly against primary sources

AskAmerica fact-check of the Aug 31, 2026 NYT dark-money review (via Political Wire / Brennan Center), cross-checked against FEC.gov and state filings

Summary

Every specifically checkable figure in the NYT's dark-money review holds up, several to the exact dollar. Trump's super PAC MAGA Inc. really did grow from $294.4 million cash on hand in its January 2026 filing to $415.8 million in its most recent report (through Aug 31, 2026) — confirmed directly against FEC.gov's own filings, with the intermediate '$403.5 million as of July 31' figure cited elsewhere matching to the dollar. Wisconsin's 'First Amendment LLC' and Delaware's Working Families Party National PAC spending figures ($13M/$9.3M and $455K respectively) are independently corroborated by multiple state-level news outlets and are consistent with each group being a state-registered, non-FEC spender — exactly the kind of vehicle the article describes as escaping federal disclosure. The one figure that cannot be independently reproduced is the headline $1 billion 'dark money' total itself: it is the Times' own proprietary tally built from ad-tracking data, filings and tax records not held in any single public dataset, so it is graded 'not checkable here' rather than true or false — though it is directionally consistent with the Brennan Center's own prior finding of $1.9 billion in dark money in the full 2024 cycle. Overall Pinocchio rating: 0 (no significant issues) on the facts checked; see the caveat below on the one structurally unverifiable figure.

Claim 2 — First Amendment LLC spent ~$13M in Wisconsin, ~$9.3M against Crowley

Verdict: True (verified against independent state-level sources; not FEC-jurisdiction data). A search of fec.committees found no federal committee named 'First Amendment LLC' — only unrelated entities ('First Amendment Alliance,' 'First Amendment Advocates, LLC Super PAC'). This is expected and correct: Wisconsin's 2026 gubernatorial race (Crowley vs. Tiffany) is a state contest, so spending on it is reported to the Wisconsin Ethics Commission, not the FEC — outside this warehouse's fec schema by design, not a coverage gap. Cross-checked against WisPolitics, Urban Milwaukee, Hoodline, and Wisconsin Public Radio, all of which independently report the same breakdown: ~$13 million total from First Amendment LLC, ~$9.3 million specifically opposing David Crowley for governor, and ~$3.7 million on state legislative races. All four outlets describe it as a dark-money express-advocacy LLC exempt from donor disclosure under Wisconsin law, and note a possible link to the group 'Make Liberty Win.'

Claim 3 — Working Families Party National PAC spent $455,000 on six Delaware candidates, no donor disclosure

Verdict: True (verified against independent state-level sources; not FEC-jurisdiction data). The WFP National PAC does have a federal committee registration (C00606962), but the $455,000 in question was spent on Delaware state legislative races and reported to Delaware's state campaign-finance regulator, not the FEC — again a genuine state/federal boundary, not a gap in FEC coverage. Independently confirmed by Spotlight Delaware: the WFP National PAC filed a long-delayed state report disclosing $455,000 spent supporting six Delaware legislative candidates (five challenging incumbent Democrats) without naming its funding source. A WFP official said Delaware regulators told the group state-level donor disclosure wasn't required as long as it filed separately with the FEC — a real regulatory quirk, not a contradiction in the reporting. Delaware state Rep. Kim Williams is quoted criticizing the lack of transparency.

Claim 4 — The $1 billion 'dark money' aggregate for 2026

Verdict: Not checkable here. search_catalog confirms no table in this warehouse (or any single public dataset) aggregates undisclosed 501(c)(4)/LLC political spending into a real-time 2026 total — by definition, 'dark money' means the funding source isn't disclosed anywhere, so it can't be summed from FEC filings (which only capture the spending vehicle, not its donors) or from IRS Form 990s (which lag years behind and don't capture a mid-cycle ad-spend estimate). The Times' $1B figure is its own proprietary tally combining ad-tracking data, campaign-finance filings, and tax records not held in this warehouse. That said, the attribution itself is accurate: Political Wire and the Brennan Center's newsletter both quote the NYT verbatim ('about $1 billion... almost certainly a low-end estimate'), and the order of magnitude is plausible against the Brennan Center's own separately published finding of $1.9 billion in dark money for the full 2024 presidential cycle (historically larger than a midterm) — a $1B midterm-cycle estimate reported roughly two months before Election Day, with money still to come, is consistent with that baseline rather than surprising.

What's misleading even though the facts check out

Nothing found is materially misleading. The framing that the two MAGA Inc. cash figures ($294M and $400M+) reflect 'real growth, not a contradiction' is not just accurate but understated — the primary FEC data shows a smooth, near-linear month-over-month build (not a one-time jump), which if anything strengthens the article's account of steady dark-money-adjacent and hard-money fundraising heading into the midterms. The one caveat worth flagging for readers: the $1 billion headline figure, while faithfully and accurately attributed to the Times, is a proprietary estimate rather than a number any outside party (including this warehouse) can independently recompute — readers should treat it as directionally credible rather than as a precise, audited total, exactly as the Times itself frames it ('almost certainly a low-end estimate').
