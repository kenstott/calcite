The New American article's claims about Census foreign-born population data, net migration and border crossings are somewhat biased and mostly accurate; 1 claim could not be confirmed.

**Rating: 1 Pinocchio.**

Article: "Census Bureau: U.S. Foreign-born Population Stagnated or Declined in 2025-26" (The New American, Peter Rykowski).

**Misleading framing.** The piece relays one source, the Center for Immigration Studies, an immigration-restriction advocacy group. It omits three points:
- The monthly CPS is a small-sample survey that the Census Bureau itself says has "increased volatility". Jed Kolko (Peterson Institute) and Julia Gelatt (Migration Policy Institute) said the CPS decline is implausibly large and may reflect immigrants not responding.
- The ACS window ends mid-2025. Camarota himself says it may not reveal much about Trump policy. His own quoted cause is end-of-Biden-era changes.
- The article calls the data "a stark reversal" and an "indication of the impact" of Trump policy without saying this is a correlation with a timing caveat.

**Verification gaps.** The cis.org, AP and The Hill pages returned 403, as did one other CIS page. All 20 linked pages were opened or attempted. The article's own claims were extracted from its fetched text.
