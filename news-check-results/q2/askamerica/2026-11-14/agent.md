The Fortune article's claims about the CBO October 2026 deficit, FY2026 totals and Bessent's Treasury buybacks are biased and partly false; 2 claims could not be confirmed.

**Rating: 2 Pinocchios.** The headline figure is right, but several supporting numbers and attributions are wrong, and most errors make the picture look worse than the data does.

Article: https://fortune.com/2026/11/10/cbo-october-budget-treasury-deficit-100-billion-weekly/ (fetched successfully). The CBO report itself (cbo.gov) returned HTTP 403 to both fetch tools. CBO-specific claims were graded on Treasury's Monthly Treasury Statement (MTS) in the warehouse, the American Action Forum's (AAF) quotation of CBO's FY2026 summary, and a search summary of the CBO report.

## Claim grades
| Claim | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| October 2026 deficit $409B, $125B above last October (central) | True | MTS gives a $404.1B deficit (receipts $368.9B, outlays $773.0B) against $284.3B in Oct 2025, a rise of $119.8B. CBO's $409B and $125B are early estimates and match what CBO published. |
| Revenues $36B lower, outlays $89B higher | True | MTS: receipts down $35.5B, outlays up $84.3B. CBO's $89B matches its own $778B vs $689B. |
| "More than $100 billion a week" | Mostly false | October has 31 days (4.43 weeks), so $409B is about $92B a week. Only a four-week month gives $102B. |
| October's rise was due to timing shifts of Nov 1 payments, which fell on a weekend in 2025 and 2026 | Partially false | The article's own parenthetical says the shift hit both Octobers, so it cancels out and cannot explain a $125B year-over-year rise. Net interest alone rose $12B ($90.9B to $103.1B). |
| FY2026 deficit 6.2% of GDP vs 5.8%; 50-year average 3.8%; matched or exceeded eight times since 1946 | Unsupported | I found no accessible copy of CBO's text with these percentages. The magnitude is plausible ($2.0T against roughly $32T of GDP), and the count of eight matches the years listed. |
| FY2026 deficit "$1.9 trillion" | Mostly false | Actual was about $2.0T: MTS receipts $5.403T minus outlays $7.396T = $1.99T. AAF, quoting CBO, gives $2.0T, $140B above the $1.9T baseline projection. |
| Revenue up to $5.4T, up $169B | True | MTS shows +$168B. AAF/CBO shows +$169B. |
| "Total outlays increased by $217 billion" (mostly Social Security, Medicare, Medicaid) | False | Outlays rose $386B (MTS $7.396T vs $7.010T; AAF/CBO agrees). The $217-218B is the rise in the deficit. The program drivers were not tested. |
| $1.14T spent on interest on the public debt | Unsupported | MTS net interest for FY2026 is $1.052T. $1.14T may be a gross-interest measure, but I could not open CBO's text. |
| Bessent described the actions as a "Treasury twist" | Mostly false | In the CNBC transcript the phrase comes from host Sara Eisen's question ("what I would call a Treasury twist"). I found no use by Bessent. |
| JPMorgan's James Sullivan: "paying your mortgage with your credit card..." | True | CNBC: "a little bit like paying your mortgage with your credit card. It can work for a while..." The article trims "a little bit like" from the quote. |
| Buybacks and other factors "brought down longer-dated yields" | Partially false | The 30-year yield (DGS30) averaged 5.10% in July, 5.22% in August, 5.37% in September, 5.47% in October and 5.30% in November so far. That is below October's peak but above August. |

## Misleading framing
- The "$100 billion a week" headline depends on treating October as four weeks.
- The timing-shift explanation is offered for a year-over-year rise it cannot explain.
- The text conflates the $217B deficit increase with an outlay increase, and shows the baseline $1.9T deficit as the actual result. Together these understate FY2026 spending growth by $169B and the deficit by about $0.1T.
- The yield-relief narrative is weaker than the data supports.
