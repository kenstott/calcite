Two Pinocchios: figures are quoted accurately, but the "Census Bureau" framing and the 1967 border claim are wrong

The article (The New American, Nov. 12, 2026) accurately relays Steven Camarota's (Center for Immigration Studies) ACS and CPS numbers, but the headline attributes the declines to the Census Bureau when the CPS declines are CIS analysis, and the CBP "lowest since fiscal 1967" claim contradicts CBP, which says lowest since 1970.

  • Grade: 2 Pinocchios.
  • Misleading: the ACS shows growth (+174,000) through mid-2025, not decline; declines come only from CPS, which CIS itself says may partly reflect immigrants' reluctance to respond. ACS ends July 2025, so it says little about Trump-era policy.
  • Brookings range (-295k to -10k) includes near-zero; CBO estimated about +400k for 2025; article omits this.
  • Not verified: I could not open CIS pages (HTTP 403) and the Census API requires a key, so ACS/CPS figures rest on CIS as quoted in a mirror post and search summaries. The policy list and the "long way to go" opinion are not factual claims I checked. I could not retrieve full article text directly (summarized by the fetch tool).
