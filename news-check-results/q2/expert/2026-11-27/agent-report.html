Fortune's defense-budget story earns 1 Pinocchio

Summary of findings: mostly accurate, with a shifted benchmark and a wrong deficit

Of 18 claims checked, 8 verified cleanly, 4 verified with shading, 2 were discrepant and 4 could not be checked. Overall grade: 1 Pinocchio. The core argument, that defense fell from 49% to 13% of the federal budget since 1962 as entitlements and interest grew, matches Brookings (Riedl, 2026-11-23; Cancian, Nov 2026), and every Brookings quote is accurate.

  • Deficit: Fortune says $1.9 trillion; CBO and Treasury report about $2.0 trillion for FY2026 (the $1.9T figure was CBO's February baseline).
  • Benchmark shifted: Riedl says defense is heading to its lowest share of the economy since the 1930s; Fortune writes "since World War II" for both budget share and GDP share. The 2.8%-of-GDP figure was not found; Brookings says about 3%.
  • Attribution: The "China has largely negated" line is credited to RAND three years ago; Riedl attributes it to a 2024 bipartisan commission. The Brookings item is a commentary, not a "report".
  • Omitted context: defense spending rose 5.8% in dollars while its share fell, and Cancian notes the FY2027 request would raise it to 3.6% of GDP, which undercuts the "squeezed to near 3% through 2030" framing.
  • Not checkable: $917B defense level and $40.7T debt (CBO site returned 403, no secondary source), and the JEC $23 trillion claim with Schweikert quotes.

What would change this: reading CBO's year-end tables directly could resolve the $917B and net-interest level; the Fortune text was read through a summarizing fetch tool, so exact wording may differ slightly.
