# Fact-check: Yankee Institute, "Connecticut candidates promised lower electric bills. Now comes the hard part" (2026-11-13)

**Grade: 1 Pinocchio (mostly accurate, advocacy framing).** Caveat: the fetch tool returned a summary, not verbatim text, so quotes and minor claims could not be checked word for word.

## Claims
1. Eversource seeks a distribution rate increase adding about $33/month. VERIFIED: WFSB and AARP cite about $32.91/month (700 kWh), about 18%. Confidence high.
2. About $20/month if storm costs are handled separately. PARTLY: AG says excluding 2018-2025 storm costs the ask is $451M and about 11% residential, which is consistent in direction. The exact $20 was not located. Confidence medium.
3. PURA hearings Nov 16 (New London), Nov 23 (Danbury), virtual Dec 9. VERIFIED via WFSB Nov 10 schedule (also Nov 9 New Britain). Confidence high.
4. Anwar: Ratepayers First Act "cut bills by $800 million over three years." PARTLY: Senate Democrats releases say "nearly $800 million over three years", a projection by supporters. No independent audit found; the article's past tense is advocacy-leaning. Confidence medium.
5. HB 5028, 5048, 5080 (2026) would move the public benefits charge to the General Fund. VERIFIED on cga.ct.gov; each referred to the Energy and Technology committee. Confidence high.
6. None got a hearing or committee vote. Consistent with "in committee" status; not exhaustively confirmed. Confidence medium.
7. OCC report on the charge due Jan 15, 2027. NOT CONFIRMED: found only SB 243 analysis with PURA study due Jan 1, 2027. Checkable at cga.ct.gov; unresolved here.
8. CT among highest household energy costs. Widely supported (EIA); not re-queried.

## Misleading context
Opinion argument that RPS should be scrapped is advocacy, not fact. Omits that PURA cut about $500M from Eversource storm-cost recovery in July 2026 and that 2026 state bonding reduced public benefit costs (ct.gov releases).
