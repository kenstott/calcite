# Fact-check: Breitbart, "Jobless Claims Fall To 57-Year Low of 189,000" (Nov 5, 2026, John Carney)

**Overall grade: 1 Pinocchio, leaning toward 0.** Every figure I could check matches the Department of Labor (DOL) release or FRED, the St. Louis Fed's database. The one real weakness is context. A raw count of claims is compared with 1969 without adjusting for the labor force, which has roughly doubled since then.

**Sources**
- Breitbart fetch: the page returned only a summary. It is John Carney, Nov. 5, 2026. I have no verbatim text, and the piece contains no attributed quotes that I could see.
- DOL release: I fetched the Nov. 5, 2026 release (dol.gov/ui/data.pdf) and read its text.
- FRED: I recomputed the history from the series ICSA (initial claims), CCSA (continuing claims) and IURSA (insured unemployment rate). FRED republishes DOL data, so it is not independent of DOL.

| Claim | Verdict | Source and confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Initial claims fell 6,000 to 189,000 in the week ending Oct. 31 | True | DOL: 189,000, down 6,000 from a revised 195,000. High confidence. |
| 189,000 ties the July 18 reading | True | FRED shows 2026-07-18 at 189,000. High confidence. |
| Lowest since 1969 ("57-year low") | True, with a caveat | The lowest values are 162,000 (Nov. 1968) and 177,000 (Apr.–May 1969). I did not check each later year for a value at or below 189,000. High confidence on 1969; medium on "nothing lower since". |
| 4-week average of 194,750, lowest since 1969 | True | DOL gives 194,750, and my recomputation from FRED matches. The only lower 4-week averages are in 1969, with 179,000 in May 1969. High confidence. |
| 4-week average was 225,500 a year earlier, a 13.6% decline | True | FRED's 4-week average for the week ending 2025-11-01 is 225,500. (194,750 ÷ 225,500) − 1 = −13.6%. High confidence. |
| 2019 averaged 217,712, with a best week of 196,000 | True | FRED's 2019 mean is 217,711.5, and the 2019 minimum is 196,000 (Apr. 6, 2019). High confidence. |
| The past month's average is below 2019's best week | True | 194,750 is below 196,000. High confidence. |
| 2026 year-to-date average of 208,068, lowest since 1969 | Average true; "lowest since 1969" not checked | The mean of 44 weekly values through Oct. 31 is 208,068.2. I did not compare it with each earlier year's year-to-date average. Medium confidence on the ranking. |
| Continuing claims were 1.718M for the week ending Oct. 24, up 23,000 | True | DOL gives 1,718,000, up 23,000 from a revised 1,695,000. High confidence. |
| Continuing claims were 244,000 fewer than a year earlier | True | FRED CCSA for 2025-10-25 is 1,962,000, and 1,962,000 − 1,718,000 = 244,000. High confidence. |
| The insured unemployment rate fell from 1.3% to 1.1% | True | DOL gives 1.1%, and FRED shows 1.3% a year earlier. High confidence. |

**Materially misleading or missing context**
1. **No adjustment for labor-force size.** The labor force is roughly twice its 1969 size, so a raw count of 189,000 is a far lower share of workers than 189,000 was then. This makes the "57-year low" more impressive than the count suggests. On the same basis, the "better than 2019" comparison is fair only if you accept the growth in the labor force. The article does not say either.
2. **Low claims do not mean strong hiring.** Claims measure layoffs. Continuing claims of 1.7 million are about 1.1% of covered workers, which fits a low-hire, low-fire market. I did not check hiring or unemployment-rate data.
3. **The figure is an advance estimate.** The previous week was revised up by 1,000 and the data is seasonally adjusted. DOL's unadjusted figure was 176,698. A later revision could change the "ties July 18" claim.
4. **Seasonal-adjustment caveat.** This is a minor point. The DOL release reports the seasonal factors expected an increase of 2,539, but unadjusted claims fell instead.
