# Fact-check: Justice in Aging "From DC" (Nov 6, 2026)

Overall rating: 1 Pinocchio (mostly accurate, with unverified figures and advocacy framing).

Limits: the page was only readable through a summarizing fetch tool, so quotes were not verbatim. The CMS/HHS press release returned 403. The final rule text and the Federal Register were not reached.

## Claims

| # | Claim | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | CMS released the final CY2027 Physician Fee Schedule rule implementing H.R. 1 immigrant Medicare eligibility limits | Verified | The HHS press release (found via search, not opened) says the rule was finalized Nov 4, 2026 and codifies the noncitizen eligibility criteria. The proposed rule cited section 71201 of the 2025 reconciliation law. |
| 2 | About 300,000 people will get preliminary termination notices in Nov 2026 | Not checkable here | No source found. The Center for Medicare Advocacy and CBO-based figures cited in search results are about 100,000. The 300,000 figure may come from the final rule's own estimate, which I could not read. |
| 3 | Terminations start Feb 1, 2027 | Partly verified | Goodsurance and MedicareFAQ cite Feb 1, 2027 as CMS's termination date. The statute's 18-month mark is Jan 4, 2027. The final rule text was not read. |
| 4 | Affected groups are refugees, asylees, TPS holders, and trafficking or domestic-violence survivors | Verified | The same search results list these groups, plus parolees and U/T visa holders. |
| 5 | The rule creates a Special Enrollment Period for people who later gain qualifying status | Not checkable here | Sources describe a process to regain Medicare after obtaining qualifying status, but none confirm an SEP. |
| 6 | Major insurers are cutting MA supplemental dental for 2027 | Verified | Leerink data in Healthcare Dive and Axios coverage show cuts at UnitedHealthcare (about 70% of members touched), Centene (45%) and Elevance (23%). The share of plans with a bundle of dental, vision, hearing and an OTC allowance fell from 59.5% to 52.7%. Preventive dental held steady at 97.9% of plans. |
| 7 | A similar trend occurred in 2026 | Not checked | Not independently verified. |
| 8 | NFHA 2026 Fair Housing Trends Report: HUD did not provide requested data, the first time | Verified | The NFHA page says "Critical federal data missing for the first time since 1996." HUD and DOJ did not provide data, and NFHA compiled DOJ figures from DOJ's public website. |
| 9 | HUD's attempts to defund nonprofit fair housing agencies forced service cuts and staff reductions | Verified | NFHA says FHIP funding was zeroed out and 78 grants were terminated. 76% of members that submitted data reported funding disruptions, and many cut services. The administration is not shown to have acted through HUD alone, but the substance holds. |
| 10 | These groups handle more than 75% of fair housing complaints | Not checkable here | The NFHA page gives only combined private-group-plus-DOJ totals (20,805 in 2025). It has no 75% breakdown. An NFHA April 2026 one-pager says only about 5% of complaints were processed by HUD, which makes a large private-group share plausible. |
| 11 | Older adults, including those with disabilities, are protected under the Fair Housing Act | Verified | The Act protects against disability discrimination, and disability is the largest complaint category (over 55%). Age is not itself a protected class under the federal Act. The claim holds only for disability. |

## Misleading or missing context

- The article is advocacy and does not say so. It presents the Medicare immigrant cuts and the HUD actions in one direction only.
- The 300,000 figure is much higher than other estimates of about 100,000 (CBO-based, per Center for Medicare Advocacy). The piece gives no basis for it.
- Age is not a federally protected class under the Fair Housing Act. "Older adults ... are protected" can mislead unless it is read as applying through disability.
- NFHA's decline in complaints (24,001 to 20,805) is partly an artifact of the missing data, since HUD and state/local agency figures are absent.
- The CMS-described stability of MA supplemental benefits conflicts with filing-level data. The piece's framing matches the filings, not CMS.

## Rating: 1 Pinocchio

The core facts hold up: the rule, the groups affected, the MA dental cuts and the NFHA data gap. The one headline number I could not support (300,000) conflicts with other estimates, and the 75% and SEP claims are unconfirmed. This is a slightly weighted advocacy summary, not a falsehood.
