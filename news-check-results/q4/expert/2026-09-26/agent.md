# Fact-check record: Federal research funding claims, 2025-2026

Question checked (4 sub-claims):
1. JHU laid off 110 employees in June 2026 after federal research portfolio declined >$500M
2. NIH issued ~25% fewer new grants in 2025 than 2024, behind pace for 2026
3. NSF new grants down 46% from Biden-era average, lowest since 1980s
4. AAU member institutions reported 10-25% declines in federal research funding, one at 32%

---

## Step 1: Johns Hopkins layoffs

**Answer:** TRUE, well corroborated.

**Key values:**
- 110 employees laid off, announced/reported June 26, 2026 (n=110)
- Affected Bloomberg School of Public Health, Carey Business School, central administration
- JHU President Ron Daniels' February 2026 letter: multiyear federal research portfolio declined by more than $500 million in calendar year 2025, driven by 43% less federal research funding and 28% fewer awards vs. prior year
- Context: separate from the ~2,200-worker layoff a year earlier tied to USAID cuts

**Sources:**
- https://thedailyrecord.com/2026/06/26/johns-hopkins-university-lays-off-employees-federal-funding-cuts/
- https://www.cbsnews.com/baltimore/news/johns-hopkins-university-layoffs-maryland-funding-cuts/
- https://www.thebanner.com/education/higher-education/johns-hopkins-university-layoffs-baltimore-federal-research-cuts-4S2I6IKVIJAING4OCDP2NJV6PU/
- https://americanbazaaronline.com/2026/06/26/johns-hopkins-university-lays-off-110-employees-amid-ongoing-federal-funding-cuts-483623/
- https://president.jhu.edu/messages/2026/06/03/research-and-resilience/ (JHU official)

**Confidence:** High. Multiple independent news outlets agree on the "110" headcount and June 2026 timing; the $500M portfolio decline is corroborated by JHU's own president's office communications, which is the primary source standard requested.

## Step 2: NIH new grants ~25% fewer in 2025 than 2024, behind pace 2026

**Answer:** PARTIALLY TRUE / METRIC-DEPENDENT — the "~25%" figure is directionally right but does not match any single official NIH metric exactly, and conflates a full-year FY25 comparison with a mid-year FY26 pace figure. The "behind pace for 2026" part is TRUE and understated if anything.

**Key values (from NIH's own "FY 2025 By the Numbers" blog, grants.nih.gov, published March 2026 — primary source):**
- Total NIH extramural awards: 55,394 (FY25) vs a higher FY24 figure — down **6.2%** (n=55,394)
- Research Project Grants (RPG), new/competing awards: 8,161 (FY25) vs 10,265 (FY24) — down **20.5%**
- R01-equivalent, new/competing awards: 5,471 (FY25) vs 7,000 (FY24) — down **21.8%**
- RPG success rate: 13.0% (FY25) vs 18.5% (FY24)

None of NIH's own official year-over-year metrics land exactly at "25%." The closest matches are RPG new/competing (-20.5%) and R01-equivalent new/competing (-21.8%), both in the same direction and same order of magnitude as the "~25%" claim but not an exact match. A secondary-source figure (STAT News, citing an outside analysis, not NIH itself) describes "the overall grant number, including new awards and ongoing payments," as "about 25% lower than NIH had typically awarded by a comparable date in the years 2021-24" — this is a mid-year PACE comparison against a 4-year average, not a same-metric FY2025-vs-FY2024 comparison, and is a different measurement than either of the above two.

**2026 pace (Science.org, citing NIH data, as of March 3, 2026):**
- New/competitive awards: **74% fewer** than the 2021-2024 average for the same partial-year period
- Monetary value of those awards: **62% below** the 2021-2024 average
- So NIH is indeed "behind pace for 2026," and by a much larger margin than the ~25% figure used for 2025.

**Sources:**
- https://www.grants.nih.gov/news-events/nih-extramural-nexus-news/2026/03/fiscal-year-2025-by-the-numbers-extramural-grant-investments-in-research (primary, NIH official)
- https://www.statnews.com/2026/03/17/nih-grant-funding-slowdown-new-awards-training-grants/
- https://www.science.org/content/article/nih-likely-award-fewer-grants-it-races-spend-2026-budget
- https://www.science.org/content/article/nih-research-grant-success-rates-plummeted-2025
- https://www.aau.edu/newsroom/leading-research-universities-report/data-show-dramatic-slowdown-nih-grantmaking (secondary aggregator, page blocked 403 on direct fetch; corroborated via search snippet only)

**Confidence:** Medium. The directional claim (large decline, worse in 2026) is solid across two independent primary/near-primary sources (NIH's own blog + Science/STAT reporting of NIH-sourced pace data). The specific "~25%" figure is a plausible rounding of one plausible metric (new/competing RPG or R01-equivalent counts, both ~20-22%) but does not exactly match any single NIH-published year-over-year number we found, and could also be a distinct mid-year pace metric describing a different comparison than "2025 vs 2024." Flagging this metric ambiguity rather than treating "~25%" as precisely verified.

## Step 3: NSF new grants down 46% from Biden-era average, lowest since 1980s

**Answer:** TRUE.

**Key values:**
- NSF projected FY2026 new grants: ~6,100 (n≈6,100), reported as **46% below** the FY2021-2024 average
- Described as the lowest number of new NSF grants issued in more than four decades (i.e., since roughly the early 1980s)
- Uneven by field: astronomical sciences down ~50%; materials research down >40% vs. 2021-2024 average
- Attributed in part to ~$1 billion of NSF's $8.8 billion FY26 budget held in a central account inaccessible to core grant-making programs

**Sources:**
- https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-02574-6 (Nature news, could not fetch full text directly — paywalled/login redirect; relied on search-result summary and corroborating secondary sources below)
- https://higheredscience.substack.com/p/nsf-set-to-issue-lowest-number-of
- https://www.aip.org/fyi/astronomy-materials-science-awards-plummet-at-nsf
- https://www.aau.edu/newsroom/nsf-making-fewer-grants-threatening-future-scientific
- https://www.aps.org/apsnews/2026/04/nsf-lags-trump-proposes-cuts

**Confidence:** Medium-high. Consistent 46% figure and "lowest since [early] 1980s" framing across multiple independent outlets (Nature, AIP FYI, AAU, APS), all reportedly drawing on the same underlying NSF award data (per the Nature exclusive). Could not directly access Nature's primary reporting (paywall) or raw NSF award tables to independently recompute the percentage, so this rests on convergent secondary reporting of one underlying dataset rather than two fully independent computations.

## Step 4: AAU institutions report 10-25% declines, one institution 32%

**Answer:** LIKELY TRUE, corroborated but not independently re-derived from primary AAU data (site blocked our fetch).

**Key values:**
- AAU (aau.edu, "Federal Research Cuts Threaten U.S. Innovation and Leadership") reportedly states some member institutions saw federal research funding declines of **10-25%** vs. 2024, with **one institution at 32%**
- Related context found in the same reporting: NIH awards described as down 29% and NSF awards down 50% in 2025 vs. recent years (per AAU framing — note these figures differ somewhat from the NIH/NSF primary-source figures in Steps 2-3 above, illustrating the same "which metric" caution)
- University of Kansas cited separately as an example: a year-over-year drop of $182 million in federal research funding, three quarters into FY26

**Sources:**
- https://www.aau.edu/key-issues/federal-research-cuts-threaten-us-innovation-and-leadership (AAU primary; page returned HTTP 403 on direct fetch attempts — content obtained only via search-engine snippet, not verified against full original text)
- https://www.aau.edu/resource-library/federal-research-cuts-threaten-us-innovation-and-leadership

**Confidence:** Low-medium. This is the weakest-sourced claim in the set: we could not directly load the AAU page (403 Forbidden on two attempts) and are relying on search-engine-generated summaries of that page rather than the primary text itself. The 10-25%/32% figures appeared consistently across repeated searches, which is reassuring, but this is not equivalent to independently verifying the primary document. Recommend treating this claim as "consistent with available reporting" rather than "independently confirmed."

---

## Overall assessment

Claims 1 and 3 are well-supported by convergent, largely primary/near-primary sourcing. Claim 4 is plausible but rests on search-engine summaries of a page we could not load directly (403). Claim 2 is directionally correct and the "behind pace for 2026" portion is solid, but the specific "~25%" figure does not exactly match any single official NIH year-over-year metric — official NIH metrics for FY25 vs FY24 range from -6.2% (all awards) to -20.5% (RPG new/competing) to -21.8% (R01-equivalent new/competing), with the "~25%" figure more likely reflecting a distinct mid-year pace-vs-average metric than a same-year, same-metric comparison. This is a textbook case of the reporting hazard flagged in the task instructions: NIH publishes several different "new grant" metrics that move by different amounts over the same period.
