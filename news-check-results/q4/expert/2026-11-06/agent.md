# Fact-check: Spectrum News, "2026 midterm election most expensive in history, $12 billion" (Nov 2, 2026, Kate Nuechterlein)

Method note: the direct fetch returned only a summary, not verbatim text (so quotes are checked as summarized). Verification used web search and secondary reports of primary data. I did not query the FEC or OpenSecrets APIs directly; I could not open the primary tables, so confidence is moderate.

| Claim | Finding | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| OpenSecrets projects ~ $12B total, most expensive election on record | Supported: OpenSecrets estimate reported as $11.9B (candidates + outside groups), "nearly $12B". It is a projection, not a tally. | High on attribution; medium on scope |
| Congressional spending exceeds any previous election, "including presidential" | Not verified. Scope comparisons vary: other outlets cite $16.7B (2022, state+federal) vs $8.9B for the same year, so the baseline depends on measure. Record claim is also made by AdImpact (ads only, $11.6B). | Low/medium |
| Texas Senate (Talarico v. Paxton) > $0.5B | Supported: ad spending alone was $500M+ (Houston Public Media, Oct 16); trackers show ~$573-579M. Note the figure mixes primaries and general, and is mostly ad spending. | High |
| GOP-aligned outside groups outspend Dem-aligned by ~ $400M | Reported with that attribution to OpenSecrets; not independently recomputed. | Medium |
| Musk sent $445M to Republican PACs, >$150M to PACs backing Paxton | Matches OpenSecrets via NPR ("more than $445M" to GOP candidates, PACs, groups). CNN puts Paxton-backing PACs at ~ $168M, consistent with ">$150M". Minor wording issue: $445M covers candidates and groups, not just PACs. | High |
| Soros ~ $100M to Democratic PACs | Reported nationally; I could not confirm it against primary data. | Low (uncheckable here) |
| Talarico raised $145M from individuals vs Paxton $22M | Close: FEC through Oct 14 shows ~ $146.3M total (one outlet: $140.3M) vs ~ $22.3M (or $21.6M). Individual donors were 99.6% / 97.8% of receipts, so "from individuals" is fair. | High |
| Quotes: Hilary Braseth (OpenSecrets), David Miller (American University) | Not verifiable beyond the article; plausible. | Not checkable here |
| Backlash/diminishing returns | Expert opinion, not a fact. | n/a |

## Context the piece lacks
- Published Nov 2, the day before the election, so figures are pre-election projections. Post-election reporting (NPR) says Paxton lost, so "Musk money" framing has since been overtaken.
- "Most expensive" depends on the measure (total spending vs ads; federal vs federal+state; inflation adjustment). The piece does not name its measure.
- Spending totals double-count transfers between committees unless deduplicated; OpenSecrets' headline is a modeled estimate.

## Grade: 1 Pinocchio
Core numbers check out against published sources. Deductions: the record claim is stated without naming its measure, the Soros figure and quotes could not be verified, and "to Republican PACs" slightly narrows Musk's $445M.
