# Fact-check: Click2Houston, "The cost of keeping the lights on" (Nov 2026)

**Rating: 1 Pinocchio.** Mostly accurate; one headline figure (about 40% rise in five years) is above what EIA data show.

| Claim | Verdict | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Hinojosa won Texas governor race (Nov 3, 2026) | Verified | AP via Fox/CBS Austin; S&P Global |
| She promised to freeze residential rates, make data centers/large users pay | Verified (PUC-directed freeze until legislation) | S&P Global, campaign pages |
| Texas electricity prices up ~40% in 5 years (Krishnamoorti) | Overstated. EIA residential: 11.71 c/kWh (2020) to ~15.4 (2025, secondary) = ~31%; to 2024 ~28%. Quote may be accurate attribution, figure is high | EIA-861 state average prices |
| Wholesale prices flat/slightly down over six years | Partly verified: ERCOT IMM shows 2019 ~$38/MWh day-ahead, 2020 $25.73; 2024 all-in fell; 2021 and 2022 spiked, so "flat" holds only endpoint to endpoint. Could not extract 2021-25 series | ERCOT IMM reports |
| Infrastructure (lines, substations) a major cost driver | Consistent with PUC/ERCOT transmission cost trends; not independently quantified | medium |
| PUC regulates key parts; large-user cost allocation may need legislature | Verified (SB 6 leaves rulemaking to PUC; PUC draft 16 TAC 25.194 Mar 2026) | law-firm summaries |
| Governor has line-item budget veto leverage | Verified (Texas Constitution) | general |
| SB 6 already passed, sets requirements for large users | Verified (signed June 20, 2025; 75 MW threshold) | Jackson Walker, Balch, Weil |
| Takes office January | Verified (Texas inauguration mid-January) | |
| Residents' quotes (Crutchfield sisters) | Not checkable here | |

Misleading flags: the 40% figure is nominal and unadjusted (general inflation was roughly 25% over the period, so real rise is small); the "wholesale flat, so wires are the cause" framing skips the 2021-22 spikes and the fact that retail plans lag wholesale. Hinojosa's freeze depends on PUC action and is legally untested.

Confidence: medium. EIA 2024-25 values came from secondary sites; the EIA file fetched ended at 2020. Wholesale annual series not retrieved. Territories not relevant.
