Mostly accurate, but the "40% in five years" figure is inflated: 1 Pinocchio

Summary of findings

The Click2Houston piece earns 1 Pinocchio. Its central figure, a roughly 40% rise in Texas electricity prices over five years, exceeds the EIA residential average (11.71 c/kWh in 2020 to about 15.4 in 2025, roughly 31%; about 28% to 2024). It is attributed to an expert and is nominal, not inflation-adjusted. The election result, Hinojosa's rate-freeze and large-user pledge, SB 6 (signed June 20, 2025, 75 MW threshold), PUC authority and line-item veto are verified. The "wholesale flat for six years" claim is only partly supported: 2019 about $38/MWh and 2020 $25.73, but 2021-22 spiked and I could not extract the 2021-25 series. Resident quotes are not checkable here.

Caveat: 2024-25 prices came from secondary EIA-based sites; the EIA file retrieved ended at 2020. A true EIA 2025 figure could move the rise by a point or two, not to 40%.

Claims

  • Hinojosa won Nov 3: verified (AP via Fox, S&P).
  • Rate freeze pledge: verified (S&P Global).
  • 40% rise: overstated.
  • Wholesale flat: partly verified.
  • SB 6 passed: verified.
  • Governor veto leverage, January inauguration: verified.
  • Infrastructure cost driver: consistent, not quantified.
  • Resident quotes: not checkable.

Misleading flags: nominal figure with no inflation context; wholesale framing omits spikes; freeze mechanism depends on PUC action. Confidence: medium.
