# Fact-Check: EPA Power Plant Carbon Rule Repeal Lawsuit (D.C. Circuit, Sept. 2026)

## Claim 1: Lawsuit basics — filing date, venue, petitioners, case number
**Verdict:** Mostly True (minor date discrepancy)
**Evidence:** EDF, NRDC, Clean Air Task Force (CATF), and APHA press releases all independently and consistently state the suit was filed **September 17, 2026** (not September 18 as stated in the claim) in the **U.S. Court of Appeals for the D.C. Circuit**. Petitioners: American Lung Association, NRDC, American Public Health Association, Clean Air Council, Clean Wisconsin (all represented by Clean Air Task Force), plus Environmental Defense Fund. Justia's docket confirms the case exists as **American Lung Association, et al. v. EPA, et al., No. 26-1238**, D.C. Circuit (docket page returned 403 on direct fetch but was independently corroborated via search results and title match). The claim's list of petitioners (ALA, NRDC, EDF, Clean Air Council, "and others") matches.
**Confidence:** High — three independent primary-adjacent sources (EDF, NRDC, CATF press releases) agree on Sept. 17; the claim's "Sept. 18" is off by one day, a minor error, not a fabrication.

## Claim 2: 2024 rule projected "up to 1,200 premature deaths and 360,000 asthma symptom cases avoided in 2035 alone"
**Verdict:** True
**Evidence:** These figures are the widely-cited headline numbers from EPA's April 2024 final Carbon Pollution Standards RIA/fact sheet (`cps-111-fact-sheet-standards-and-ria-2024.pdf`), reiterated in EPA's own 2024 announcement and repeated consistently across NRDC, APHA, and press coverage from 2024 onward as "up to 1,200 avoided premature deaths and 360,000 avoided asthma symptoms in 2035 alone." I was unable to render the original EPA PDF as text (WebFetch returned it as binary/corrupted), but the figures are corroborated by multiple secondary sources describing EPA's own RIA, and no source found suggests a different number.
**Confidence:** Medium-High — could not directly quote EPA's own PDF text due to a rendering failure, but the figures are the consistent, unchallenged consensus figure attributed to EPA's RIA across half a dozen independent outlets since 2024.

## Claim 3: 2024 rule projected "1.38 billion metric tons of carbon pollution reduction through 2047, equivalent to taking 328 million gas cars off the roads"
**Verdict:** True
**Evidence:** Search results (via press coverage summarizing EPA's 2024 RIA) confirm: "Biden administration's regulatory impact analysis projected reductions of 1.38 billion metric tons of carbon pollution overall through 2047, which is equivalent to preventing the annual emissions of 328 million gasoline cars." The implied conversion factor (1.38B tons ÷ 328M cars ≈ 4.21 metric tons CO2/car/year) is close to EPA's own Greenhouse Gas Equivalencies Calculator factor for a typical gasoline vehicle (currently 4.29 metric tons CO2e/vehicle/year, historically as high as 4.6–4.71 in older calculator versions) — consistent with an EPA-methodology-based conversion, not an outlier.
**Confidence:** Medium — matches secondary reporting of EPA's RIA; did not obtain the raw EPA RIA text directly to quote verbatim due to the PDF-parsing failure noted above.

## Claim 4: EDF's new analysis estimates the repeal "will cause up to 84,000 premature deaths by 2047" and "will add 7.5 billion metric tons of climate pollution, equivalent to the annual pollution of 1.6 billion cars"
**Verdict:** True
**Evidence:** EDF's own press release ("EDF analysis: repeal of power plant pollution standards will cause up to 84,000 early deaths, $3 trillion in damages," edf.org/media) states verbatim: repeal will result in "up to 84,000 premature deaths" by 2047, will add "7.5 billion metric tons" of climate pollution, "equivalent to the annual climate pollution from 1.6 billion cars," plus $1.2T in health harms, $1.8T in climate damages, and 28 million+ asthma attacks by 2047. All figures in the claim match EDF's release exactly.
**Confidence:** High — directly fetched and quoted from EDF's own press release.

## Claim 5: Is EDF's repeal-impact analysis built on EPA's own RIA projections, or an independent EDF model?
**Verdict:** True — built on EPA's own methodology/RIA, not an independent model
**Evidence:** EDF's press release states directly: "Environmental Defense Fund estimated the health and climate harms based on EPA's own tools and methodologies, including the emission increase projections in the Regulatory Impact Analysis, using a 2% discount rate." This means EDF took EPA's own repeal-RIA emission-increase projections and applied EPA's standard benefit-cost tools/methodology (e.g., EPA's own health-benefits-per-ton and social-cost-of-carbon type frameworks) rather than building an independent emissions or health-impact model from scratch. This is a meaningful nuance: EDF's numbers derive from EPA's inputs but represent EDF's own extrapolation/interpretation of "premature deaths" and dollar-harm totals, which the Trump EPA disputes (EPA's own repeal RIA reportedly claims net savings, per secondary coverage noting "EPA says repealing power plant emissions rules will save billions — but its estimate leaves out health and climate costs").
**Confidence:** High — directly quoted from EDF's own release describing its methodology.

## Claim 6: Car-equivalence conversions match EPA's standard Greenhouse Gas Equivalencies factor
**Verdict:** Partially True / Plausible, with caveats
**Evidence:** EPA's current published factor is 4.29 metric tons CO2e per gasoline passenger vehicle per year (per epa.gov/energy/greenhouse-gas-equivalencies-calculator-calculations-and-references). Applying this to the two claims:
- 2024 rule: 1.38B tons ÷ 328M cars = ~4.21 tons/car — close to the current 4.29 factor (within ~2%), consistent with EPA's own equivalency methodology.
- EDF repeal analysis: 7.5B tons ÷ 1.6B cars = ~4.69 tons/car — this is higher than the *current* 4.29 factor, but matches EPA's *older* calculator factor (4.6–4.71 metric tons/vehicle/year, used in earlier calculator revisions). This suggests EDF (or the underlying EPA repeal RIA) may have used an older/different vintage of the EPA equivalencies factor than the one currently published, or rounded differently. It is not evidence of a fabricated or non-EPA-standard conversion — both figures are within the range of EPA's own published factors — but the two figures in the claim are not using an identical per-car divisor, which readers should note.
**Confidence:** Medium — the arithmetic checks out against EPA's documented calculator factors (both current and prior vintages), but I could not confirm which exact calculator vintage/version EDF or EPA used for each figure.

## Claim 7: The groups call the repeal "unlawful" as their legal allegation in an unresolved case
**Verdict:** True (accurately characterized as an allegation)
**Evidence:** Press releases from NRDC, EDF, and CATF describe the petitioners' position that the repeal "violates the Clean Air Act" and is unlawful; this is consistently framed in coverage as the groups' legal claim in a newly filed, unresolved D.C. Circuit petition for review (No. 26-1238), not as an adjudicated finding. The claim in the prompt correctly frames this as "their legal allegation in an unresolved case" — accurate framing, not overstatement.
**Confidence:** High — consistent across all press releases reviewed, and the case is freshly filed with no evidence of any ruling.

## Overall Assessment

The claim is **substantially accurate**. All of the specific dollar/death/tonnage/car-equivalence figures attributed to EPA's 2024 rule and to EDF's new repeal-impact analysis check out against EDF's own published press release and consistent secondary reporting of EPA's 2024 RIA. The one factual slip is the filing date: independent sources (EDF, NRDC, CATF, APHA) all say the suit was filed **September 17, 2026**, not September 18 as stated — a minor, likely typographical discrepancy rather than a substantive error. The case number (26-1238) and venue (D.C. Circuit) are corroborated. Critically, EDF's repeal-impact methodology is confirmed to be built directly on **EPA's own RIA emission-increase projections and EPA's own valuation tools** (not an independent EDF emissions model) — an important methodological nuance the claim correctly leaves open to verify, and which checks out as accurate. The car-equivalence conversions are broadly consistent with EPA's own Greenhouse Gas Equivalencies Calculator, though the two figures cited (2024 rule vs. EDF repeal analysis) imply slightly different per-vehicle emission factors (~4.2 vs. ~4.7 metric tons/car/year), suggesting different calculator vintages were used for each — not an error, but a nuance worth flagging. The characterization of "unlawful" as the groups' legal allegation in an unresolved case is accurate and appropriately hedged.
