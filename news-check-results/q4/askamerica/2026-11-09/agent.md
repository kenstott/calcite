Verdict: 1 Pinocchio (some shading). The Click2Houston piece (Nov 5-6, 2026) is largely accurate. Its headline "about 40%" five-year electricity price rise is overstated against EIA Texas data.

Claims
1. "Electricity prices up about 40% over five years" (Krishnamoorti): PARTIALLY FALSE. Warehouse energy.eia_electricity_prices (Texas): residential 11.71 to 15.47 cents/kWh from 2020 to 2025 = +32.1%; all sectors 8.36 to 10.18 = +21.8%. Measure and geography are not stated in the article. Direction right, size inflated.
2. "Wholesale prices flat or slightly down over six years": NOT CHECKABLE HERE. The catalog has no ERCOT wholesale series. Web search found only fragments (ERCOT day-ahead about $38/MWh in 2019, $22 in 2020 per EIA), no clean 2020-2025 series.
3. Hinojosa elected governor: TRUE (AP call via Fox: defeated Abbott; preliminary until certified).
4. SB 6 passed, sets requirements for large loads: TRUE (signed June 2025; 75 MW+ loads; Bracewell, Pillsbury, Balch).
5. PUC regulates key parts of the market; changing large-user cost allocation could need lawmakers: MOSTLY TRUE.
6. Governor's line-item veto, January inauguration, Hinojosa's rate-freeze and data-center pledge: not independently tested (the freeze and data-center promises are campaign positions, not facts to grade).

Misleading flags: the 40% figure carries no unit or geography and sits in the headline. The article also does not note that a rate freeze touches only the retail portion of bills, or that Texas retail rates are largely set competitively.
