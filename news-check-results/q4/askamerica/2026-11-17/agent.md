The Dorchester Reporter article's claims about BPS enrollment, the decade-long decline and the Seattle comparison are biased and partly false; 2 claims could not be confirmed.

Rating: 2 Pinocchios.

Verified:
- 44,846 students, down 1,978 (4.2%) from 46,824. This is consistent with the paper's own Jan 2026 report (46,824, record low). The DESE/BPS Oct 1 release itself was not retrievable.
- The prior-year drop of 1,769 matches the Jan 2026 report ("about 1,700").
- The 95 buildings by 2030 and 97 if approved match the Nov 5 companion article and WBUR.
- The birth-rate and immigration explanation matches the Globe (July 2026) and the Jan 2026 report. It is the district's attribution.

Problems:
- Seattle comparison is false. The article says Seattle was "slightly lower than BPS" pre-pandemic. NCES shows Seattle 55,986 vs Boston 50,480 in 2019.
- Seattle's current figure is stale. The article says about 51,000. The Seattle Public Schools site shows 48,957 (Aug 2026); NCES 2024 was 50,773.
- Skipper's "21 percent in a decade" is partly false or at least unreconciled. WBUR, citing state data, says just over 9,000 fewer than a decade ago. NCES shows -14% from 2016 to 2024.
- "11,014 fewer than 2017-18" is unsupported. It implies about 55,860 for 2017-18; NCES shows 51,433 for 2018.
- The charter (~11,000) and METCO (~10% growth) figures are unsupported. No retrievable source carries them.
- "First time below 45,000" depends on the count used. The Globe (July 2026) already said "just over 44,000" from state data.

Misleading framing:
- The Seattle contrast is the only outside benchmark. It is backwards and stale, and it makes Boston look worse relative to a peer than the data shows.
- Differently defined enrollment counts are mixed without explanation.
- The paper's own Dec 2025 projection was a further drop of about 1,300. The actual drop was 1,978, a larger shortfall than the article notes.

Limits: the NCES CCD in the warehouse ends at 2024, so the 2026 figures were checked against the paper's prior reporting, the Globe, WBUR and the Seattle site, not DESE. Grade-band figures (6-8 down 420, 9-12 down 1,000+) and the 5-year 9% figure were not verified.
