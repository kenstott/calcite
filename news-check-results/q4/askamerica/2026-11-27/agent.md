The DTN Ag Weather Forum article's claims about Lower Mississippi drought extent, record fall warmth and dryness in Arkansas, Louisiana and Mississippi are biased and mostly accurate.

**Rating: 1 Pinocchio.**

Article: Bryce Anderson, "Drought Maintains a Tight Grip on the Lower Mississippi River," DTN, 2026-11-23. The connector fetched it successfully.

## Claim-by-claim

| Claim | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| About 94% of the region is in drought in mid-November, "more than twice" late July (central claim) | Mostly true | NIDIS (Nov 12) says 94.2% of the AR-LA-MS region is in D1-D4. The warehouse, weighted by county land area, gives 94.7% on Nov 10 and 97.7% on Nov 17. The July base is mixed up: the article's 42% is D0-D4 (the warehouse gives about 49%), while 94% is D1-D4. Like for like, D1-D4 went from about 23% to 94%, so "more than twice" understates the change. |
| About 42% had dryness or drought in late July | Mostly true | The warehouse gives D0-D4 of 48.8% on Jul 28. The article's region or date may differ. |
| D2-D4 covers more than 90% of AR, 80% of MS, 82% of LA | True | Nov 17 warehouse figures: AR 90.7%, MS 80.0% (at the margin), LA 85.0%. |
| Almost 56% of Arkansas is in D4 | True | The warehouse gives 55.7% on Nov 17, against 1.0% on Jul 28. NIDIS's "almost 50%" is from the earlier Nov 12 update. |
| Warmest August-October on record in all three states; driest ranks 3rd (AR), 4th (LA), 5th (MS) | True | NOAA nClimDiv (1895-2026): temperature rank 1 in all three states (AR 77.6F, LA 79.5F, MS 77.7F). Driest ranks 3, 4 and 5, exactly as NIDIS states. |
| Aug, Sep and Oct precipitation under 50%, under 10% and under 10% of normal | Partially false | Statewide figures against the 1991-2020 mean are Aug 50/51/60%, Sep 35/49/37%, Oct 26/30/20% (AR/LA/MS). The article's numbers may describe local hot spots, but they do not hold as stated for whole states. |
| Temperatures 5-9F above normal at all but coastal sites | Mostly true | Statewide Aug-Oct departures are AR +6.2F, MS +4.1F, LA +3.5F. Station-level figures were not verified. |
| The Nov 17 Drought Monitor summary said 20-30 inches of rain are needed | Unsupported | The USDM page loaded with garbled encoding, so the phrase could not be read. It was not contradicted. |
| NIDIS said El Nino could ease or end the drought this winter | True | The NIDIS update says a strengthening El Nino supports drought improvement or removal across much of the region by January 2027. |
| River transport is the headline impact | Mostly true | The article omits NIDIS's note that Upper Mississippi flows are temporarily giving relief. NIDIS also says the 28-day forecast keeps all Lower Mississippi points above low-flow thresholds. |

## Misleading framing

- The July comparison uses different drought categories on each side.
- The precipitation percentages are stated as statewide but only fit local areas.
- The article drops NIDIS's temporary-relief caveat on river levels.
- The 94% figure is the Nov 10-12 map, while the state shares and the quote are from Nov 17. By Nov 17 the warehouse shows about 97.7% in D1-D4.

## Caveats

- State drought shares are my own land-area-weighted county estimates, not USDM's official state figures.
- Not checked: the Southern Regional Climate Center's station-level figures and the USDM Nov 17 narrative text.
