USP's 2026 drug shortage figures check out against independent FDA data

75 active shortages at year-end 2025, 4 newly reported, ~55% discontinuation jump, ~69% injectable share — reconstructed from FDA's own list

Summary

USP's June 2026 Annual Drug Shortages Report claims: (1) 75 active drug shortages in the U.S. as of end-2025, with only 4 of the 75 first reported in 2025 (nearly all carried over from 2024); (2) product discontinuations rose an acute 60% from 2024 to 2025, the largest increase since 2019; and (3) sterile injectable drugs make up 71% of all shortages, the largest share of any dosage form. Independently reconstructing FDA's own drug shortage list from Wayback-captured snapshots (askamerica's health.fda_drug_shortages_history table) reproduces the headline 75-active/4-new figures exactly, and reproduces the discontinuation increase (55.4% vs. 60% claimed) and injectable share (69.3% vs. 71% claimed) in the same direction and close magnitude. The claim holds up well against independent government data.

Why this corpus, not just the press

FDA maintains a live drug shortages feed but purges resolved/discontinued entries roughly six months after resolution, so the current live table (health.fda_drug_shortages) cannot answer 'what did the list look like on Dec 31, 2025.' askamerica's health.fda_drug_shortages_history table solves exactly this: it is a point-in-time reconstruction of FDA's own accessdata.fda.gov CSV export from ~99 Wayback Machine captures spanning Oct 2019 to Jul 2026. This makes it possible to pull the FDA list as it stood in mid-December 2025 — the same window USP says its analysis is 'current as of December 31, 2025' — and recompute USP's headline figures independently rather than merely quoting them.

Method and figures

Active shortages, end of 2025: Using the snapshot closest to year-end (snapshot_date = '2025-12-16'), SELECT COUNT(DISTINCT generic_name) WHERE status = 'Current' returns exactly 75. Of those, the number with initial_posting_date in 2025 is exactly 4. Both match USP's release text verbatim: 'Nearly all active drug shortages at the end of 2025 were carried over from 2024, with only 4 of the 75 active shortages first reported in 2025.'

Discontinuations, 2024 vs. 2025: Using the snapshot closest to each year-end that still carries the prior year's discontinuation dates (2025-01-17 for 2024, 2026-01-20 for 2025), counting distinct generic_name by the year embedded in discontinued_date gives 112 drugs discontinued in 2024 and 174 in 2025 — a 55.4% increase, versus USP's claimed 60%. Same direction, comparable magnitude; the gap is plausibly methodological (USP may count discontinuation events or NDC/package-level exits rather than distinct generic names, or corroborate against ASHP's shortage list in addition to FDA's raw CSV, per the report's own stated methodology of cross-referencing FDA and ASHP data).

Injectable share: Of the 75 distinct generic-name shortages current as of 2025-12-16, 52 have a presentation description containing 'Injection'/'Injectable' — 69.3%, versus USP's reported 71% for 'sterile injectable drugs' specifically. The small gap is consistent with USP using FDA's structured dosage-form/route classification and a stricter 'sterile injectable' definition, versus this check's free-text match on the presentation string.

Exploratory query excluded rows — what it changed

An early exploratory query against the live feed table (health.fda_drug_shortages, not the history table used for the figures above) filtered with WHERE discontinued_date IS NOT NULL to look at discontinuation years present in that table. That filter excluded 1,160 of the table's 1,601 rows (1,150 of 224 distinct generic-name/dosage entries carry a NULL discontinued_date — precisely, 69 of the 224 distinct drugs have every one of their rows null on that column, since they are not discontinuation episodes at all but current/resolved shortages). This filtered live-table query was exploratory only: because the live feed purges resolved/discontinued entries ~6 months after resolution, it cannot reliably reconstruct a clean 2024-vs-2025 comparison, which is exactly why the reported discontinuation figures above (112 vs. 174) were instead computed from the point-in-time history table snapshots, not from this filtered live-table query. No headline figure in this report rests on the excluded rows.

Verdict

The headline figures in USP's report are corroborated by independent reconstruction of FDA's own data. The most checkable, most load-bearing numbers — 75 active shortages and 4 newly reported in 2025 — match exactly. The two supporting statistics (60% discontinuation rise, 71% injectable share) are directionally and magnitudinally consistent (55.4% and 69.3% respectively) but not exact matches, most likely due to differences in counting unit (event/NDC vs. distinct drug) or in dosage-form classification method rather than any overstatement in USP's report.
