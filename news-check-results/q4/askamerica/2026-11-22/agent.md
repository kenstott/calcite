The Chalkbeat article's claims about NYC public school enrollment, hold-harmless budgets and school closures are accurate.

**Rating: 0 Pinocchios.**

Source: https://www.chalkbeat.org/newyork/2026/11/16/nyc-2026-27-public-school-student-enrollment-drop-pressures-budget-closures/ (fetched successfully).

**Central claim (true):** NYC lost about 23,000 students (nearly 3%) to a preliminary 766,900 K-12 on Oct. 31. NY1 reports the same: 766,900, down 23,000 or 2.9%. Chalkbeat's Nov. 19 follow-up calls it the largest drop since the pandemic.

**Claim-by-claim**
- With preschool the total is 863,100, down about 2.6%. True. NY1 gives 863,100 against 885,860, a change of -2.57%.
- Lost about 200,000 students over the decade, "nearly 20%". Mostly true. NY1 gives 964,132 in 2016-17, so the loss is 197,232, or 20.5%. That is just over 20%, not "nearly" 20%.
- "Nearly $2 billion" in hold-harmless spending since 2020. True. Chalkbeat's June 22 piece says nearly $1.9 billion.
- SCA projection: another 130,000 fewer students by 2034-35. True. I read the SCA PDF (March 2026). Table A1 shows 849,652 in 2025-26 falling to 721,251 in 2034-35, a drop of 128,401. The projection covers PK-12 excluding District 75 and starts from last year's base. From the 2026-27 projection of 831,306 the further drop is about 110,000.
- Thirty states have seen declines since 2014 (attributed to the NYT). Mostly true. The NYT page returned HTTP 403, so I could not read it. The warehouse (edu.ccd_districts, NCES CCD) shows 31 of 51 jurisdictions (states plus DC) lower in 2024 than in 2014.
- Hold-harmless history (midyear clawbacks, then steady budgets since the pandemic). True. This matches Chalkbeat's June 7 and June 22 reporting.
- Preschool enrollment up slightly. True. Derived from the NY1 totals: 96,200 against 95,960, about +240.
- Credit for the preschool rise goes to 2-K's roughly 2,000 seats. True for 2-K (Chalkbeat Sept. 10). I could not verify the 4-year-old uptick. The net change is only +240, so other preschool categories must have fallen.
- 2-K to add 10,000 seats next year. True. Chalkbeat Oct. 19 says it grows to 12,000 from 2,000.
- Causes of the drop: birth rates, families leaving, immigration slowdown. True as contributing factors. The City Comptroller report (via NY1) and the school-level patterns support them, but no one decomposes how much each matters.
- Closure and merger proposals (two Harlem closures, one Brooklyn, two mergers). True. Chalkbeat Nov. 2 confirms the Harlem pair, and Nov. 19 counts five proposals in total.
- Large districts (LA, Chicago, Philadelphia, Houston) are also losing students. True. Chicago lost 11,500, Philadelphia about 2,500, Houston roughly 9,000 to 10,000. NPR reports LAUSD lost 300,000+ over two decades.
- Samuels quote to the Chancellor's Parent Advisory Committee. Unsupported. I could not find it in any other source I could open (NY1, Chalkbeat Nov. 2).

**Warehouse note:** The warehouse has no usable NYC row. The NYC LEA shows null or zero enrollment in edu.ccd_districts, which also runs only through 2024. I checked NYC counts against NY1 and the SCA PDF instead and used the warehouse for the national state-level test.

**Linked sources:** I fetched every source the article links except the NYT piece (HTTP 403). The signed SCA link opened, but the storage host's root page returned HTTP 400.

**Misleading framing:** None material. Figures are labeled preliminary and the budget impact is called murky. Two things the reader should know. First, the 130,000 projection uses a different base and scope than the 766,900 headline (PK-12 excluding D75, from 2025-26). Second, "nearly 20%" slightly understates the 20.5% decline.
