EPA repeal lawsuit claim checks out: figures match EPA's own 2024 RIA and EDF's new 2026 analysis almost verbatim

Fact-check of the September 2026 D.C. Circuit lawsuit over EPA's repeal of the 2024 power-plant carbon rule

Summary

The claim checks out. Every headline figure attributed to the 2024 EPA rule (1,200 avoided premature deaths and 360,000 avoided asthma-symptom cases in 2035 alone; 1.38 billion metric tons of CO2 avoided through 2047) is an exact, verbatim match to EPA's own April 2024 regulatory-impact-analysis fact sheet. The figures attributed to EDF's new repeal analysis (up to 84,000 premature deaths and 7.5 billion metric tons of added climate pollution by 2047, equivalent to 1.6 billion cars) are an exact match to EDF's own September 2026 press release and underlying analysis. The lawsuit's filing date (Sept 17, 2026), court (D.C. Circuit), and named petitioners (American Lung Association, NRDC, EDF, Clean Air Council, plus American Public Health Association and Clean Wisconsin under 'and others') all check out against EDF's own release. The "unlawful" characterization is correctly flagged in the claim as the groups' own legal allegation in an unresolved case, not an adjudicated fact — that framing is accurate. AskAmerica's own warehouse does not carry EPA regulatory-impact-analysis projections or lawsuit/docket data, so this check rests on primary-source documents (EPA's own PDF fact sheet, EDF's own press releases, EPA's Greenhouse Gas Equivalencies methodology page), not on this connector's tables.

The 2024 rule's projected benefits — verified against EPA's own fact sheet

EPA's fact sheet (fetched directly from epa.gov: cps-111-fact-sheet-standards-and-ria-2024.pdf) states verbatim: "The health benefits that will result from these reductions in 2035 alone include approximately 1,200 avoided premature deaths; ... 360,000 avoided cases of asthma symptoms" and "The RIA projects 1.38 billion metric tons total of CO2 avoided from 2028-2047 systemwide." Both match the claim exactly. EPA's fact sheet reports 1,200 and 360,000 as point/central estimates rather than explicitly labeled "up to" figures in this fact sheet; the RIA's full report presents ranges depending on the epidemiological model used (Pope et al. vs. Krewski et al.), and "up to" language reflecting the higher end of that range is standard in advocacy-group summaries of EPA RIAs. This is a minor stylistic imprecision, not a factual error.

The "328 million cars" and "1.6 billion cars" equivalencies

EPA's Greenhouse Gas Equivalencies Calculator methodology page (fetched directly) currently derives an average gasoline passenger vehicle's annual emissions as 4.29 metric tons CO2e/vehicle/year (based on 2022 FHWA/EPA data, calculator last updated November 2024). Dividing 1.38 billion metric tons by 328 million cars implies 4.21 tons/car/year — within about 2% of EPA's current factor, consistent with using the calculator's methodology contemporaneously with the 2024 rule (the calculator's underlying vehicle-economy inputs are updated periodically, so the exact factor used at the time of the original rule would differ slightly from today's version). Dividing EDF's 7.5 billion metric tons by 1.6 billion cars implies 4.69 tons/car/year — about 9% above EPA's current factor, plausibly because EDF's "climate pollution" figure is CO2-equivalent across all greenhouse gases (not CO2 only) or uses a different data vintage. Both conversions are within a plausible range of EPA's own standard methodology and are not evidence of fabricated or inflated multipliers.

Is EDF's repeal-impact analysis EPA's own number, or EDF's independent model?

This is the most important methodological nuance in the claim, and the claim gets it right by attributing the figures to "EDF's new analysis" rather than to EPA. EDF's own press release states explicitly: "Environmental Defense Fund estimated the health and climate harms based on EPA's own tools and methodologies, including the emission increase projections in the Regulatory Impact Analysis, using a 2% discount rate." In other words: EDF took the emissions-delta projections from the Trump EPA's own 2026 repeal RIA, then ran EPA's standard health-benefits and social-cost-of-carbon valuation methodologies itself to produce the 84,000-deaths and 7.5-billion-ton figures — because, per EDF, "the Trump EPA claims it calculated the costs of power plant pollution protections... but it completely ignored the benefits... it refused to estimate" them. So these are EDF's own modeling results, built on EPA's emissions inputs and EPA's own valuation tools, not headline figures EPA itself published. The claim's phrasing ("EDF's new analysis... estimates") is accurate on this point.
