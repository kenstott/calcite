Article: "Democrats won big. Can they lower your grocery bill?" (NPR, Joe Hernandez, via KUOW, Nov 9, 2026). The engine fetched it with no problem.

**Verified true**
- Democrats won both houses of Congress, and Annie Andrews defeated interim Sen. Darline Graham (AP/Fox projections).
- At least 28 Democratic governors: NPR's Nov 4 results piece says the same.
- No filibuster-proof Senate majority: NPR Nov 4 analysis.
- Jeffries quote: matches the NPR Nov 6 Morning Edition transcript.

- Hormuz fertilizer claim: NPR/UNCTAD say about one-third of seaborne fertilizer passed through the strait.

**Problems**
1. NCSL chamber flips: the article says ten. NCSL said 12 as of Nov 6 and later 13 (39 to 52 Democratic chambers). This figure comes from WebFetch and search summaries; the engine's own fetch of the NCSL page returned no "flipped" text. Mostly true.
2. "Grocery prices are out of control" (partially false). The warehouse food-at-home CPI (econ.food_cpi, CUUR0000SAF11) rose 1.8% to 2.9% year over year every month Jan-Sep 2026 (Apr 2.89%, Jun 2.75%, Sep 1.81%), and 1.9% to 2.7% in 2025. That is moderate, not a surge. The warehouse query gave a row_fanout warning because the table's annual M13 rows share the year key. The month-matched figures are unaffected. Oct 2025 is missing, so Oct 2026 YoY is null.
3. Tariffs and the Iran war as "two of the main causes of high food prices" (mostly true, overreach). NPR's own linked July 3 piece is headlined "High fertilizer costs unlikely to cause food price spikes". It cites TD Economics at only 0.1-0.5 point on 2027 food inflation. It also notes the US and Iran agreed in June to reopen the Strait. The article does not mention this.

**Materially misleading framing:** the piece presents a crisis narrative that its own linked reporting and BLS data only partly support. It gives no actual price figure. Its Democratic-win causation ("economic strain helped") is plausible but unquantified.
