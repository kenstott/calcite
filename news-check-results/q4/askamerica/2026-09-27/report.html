NFIP article's numbers mostly check out, but its central "September 30, 2026" deadline was already superseded three weeks before publication

Split Pinocchios: fidelity (did the article accurately transcribe NAR's attributed figures) graded separately from claims accuracy (are the underlying facts, including the article's own core premise, true)

Summary

This article's mechanics are, for the most part, careful: it correctly separates NFIP authorization from Homeland Security appropriations, correctly explains that the mandatory flood-insurance purchase requirement is suspended (not eliminated) during a lapse, and accurately transcribes eight of nine statistics it attributes to the National Association of Realtors (NAR). But two things are wrong, one attribution-level and one much more serious. First, the article's NAR-attributed state breakdown (13,460 Florida / 3,140 Texas / 1,840 California monthly NFIP-dependent closings) does not match what NAR actually published (14,870 / 3,590 / 1,680 — confirmed by fetching NAR's Economists' Outlook, March 11, 2025, directly). Second, and far more consequential: as of September 2, 2026, Congress already extended NFIP's authorization past September 30, 2026 to December 11, 2026, via P.L. 119-103 (the Continuing Appropriations and Extensions Act, 2027, signed alongside H.R.6500). This is confirmed independently by NAHB (published Sept. 4, 2026) and by CRS's own IN10835 report, revised September 11, 2026, which states plainly: "The NFIP is currently authorized until December 11, 2026." The article's entire framing — "put one date on your calendar in bold: September 30, 2026" — was superseded more than three weeks before this fact-check, and the article shows no sign of having been updated to reflect it, despite linking in its own "Related reading" section to other pieces on this same site dated September 11 and 13, 2026. A reader today acting on this article's central call-to-action is acting on stale information about the one fact that matters most for timing a closing.
