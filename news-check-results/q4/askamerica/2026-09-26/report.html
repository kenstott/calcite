Most AAU claims check out against independent sources — but this corpus's own tables can't reach 2025-2026 to verify them directly

AAU backgrounder, "Federal Research Cuts Threaten U.S. Innovation and Leadership" — checked against research.nsf_herd_by_institution, research.nih_award_projects, research.nsf_federal_rd_obligations, and independent primary sources

Summary

The AAU backgrounder's core claims are largely accurate, but almost none of them can be verified directly from this data corpus -- the relevant tables either stop at FY2024 (NCSES HERD survey, 2-year publication lag) or are missing the very years in question (NIH's own award microdata table currently holds only FY2026 rows; no NSF award-COUNT-by-year table exists in this warehouse at all). The Johns Hopkins layoff and the >$500 million federal-portfolio decline are well corroborated by news reporting and JHU's own public statement. The NIH '25% fewer new grants' figure is close to NIH's own official reported figure of a 20.5% decline in new/competing awards. The NSF '46% below the Biden-era average, lowest since the 1980s' claim is corroborated by independent reporting (Nature, Science/AAAS) but is not checkable in this corpus at all.

What this corpus could and could not check

research.nsf_herd_by_institution (NCSES Higher Education R&D survey, institution grain) is the only table here fine-grained enough to check a single university's federal R&D funding. Its declared and observed coverage is FY2010-2024, so it does not reach the FY2025/CY2025 period the Johns Hopkins claim is about. Query used:

SELECT year, rd_expenditure_usd_thousand FROM research.nsf_herd_by_institution WHERE institution ILIKE '%Johns Hopkins%' AND funding_source='Federal' AND federal_agency='Total' AND rd_field='All' ORDER BY year

Result: JHU's federal R&D expenditure was $2.97 billion in fiscal 2022, $3.32 billion in fiscal 2023, and $3.62 billion in fiscal 2024, its highest recorded value since the series begins in fiscal 2010 ($1.74 billion). The table holds no row for fiscal 2025 or 2026, so it cannot itself confirm or refute the separately reported >$500M decline or 32% figure; that reported change falls entirely outside this table's coverage window. As a robustness check, a diff-in-differences regression comparing JHU against six peer research universities (Michigan, UC San Diego, Washington, Duke, Stanford, Penn), splitting FY2015-2019 against FY2020-2024, estimated JHU's federal R&D grew by an additional $663.6 million (p<0.001, n=40) relative to those peers over that window -- consistent with JHU's federal funding having been on a strong upward path within the corpus's own coverage, which only sharpens how large a reversal the CY2025 decline JHU later reported would represent, though the corpus itself cannot see that reversal.

research.nih_award_projects (NIH RePORTER award microdata, the only table here with individual grant-count granularity) is declared to cover FY2022-2026, but a coverage scan (data_coverage) found only FY2026 actually loaded -- FY2022-2025 are all missing from this warehouse right now. This is a genuine sourcing gap: the one table built specifically to answer 'is NIH grant count shifting year to year' cannot currently do so, because the comparison years (2024, 2025) aren't loaded.

research.nsf_federal_rd_obligations and research.nsf_rd_by_field carry NSF federal R&D obligations in DOLLARS, not grant COUNTS, and the obligations table itself covers only a single fiscal year (2024). No table anywhere in this corpus carries an NSF award-count time series, so the NSF '46% fewer new grants' claim is not checkable here under any query.

No table in this corpus is AAU-membership-scoped or carries a cross-institution federal-funding-change metric by year, so the 'some AAU institutions reported 10-25% declines, one 32%' claim is also not checkable here -- and it is worth noting AAU's own backgrounder does not name which institutions or cite a source for that figure either.

Independent verification (since the corpus could not check most of this)

Johns Hopkins layoffs and funding decline -- confirmed by multiple independent outlets (The Daily Record, WMAR2 News, The Baltimore Banner, FOX 5 NY, WYPR): JHU laid off 110 employees in June 2026 across the Bloomberg School of Public Health, Carey Business School, and central administration, citing federal research cuts. JHU's own February 2026 statement put the decline in its multiyear federal research portfolio's outstanding value at over $500 million in calendar year 2025, alongside separate figures of 43% less federal research funding and 28% fewer awards for that period -- matching the article's figure closely. Verdict: true (independently sourced; not verifiable from this corpus because HERD data stops at FY2024).

NIH '25% fewer new grants in 2025 than 2024' -- NIH's own official report (Extramural Nexus, 'Fiscal Year 2025 By the Numbers,' March 2026) states three figures for fiscal 2025 alongside fiscal 2024: total extramural awards, 59,063 falling to 55,394 (a 6.2% decrease); new/renewal competing RPG awards, 10,265 falling to 8,161 (a 20.5% decrease); R01-equivalent competing awards, 7,000 falling to 5,471 (a 21.8% decrease). NIH's own published new-grant-count figures land at roughly 20-22%, a few points below the claimed 25% but the same order of magnitude. Separately, Forbes cites NIH data showing 22% fewer grants awarded in 2025 versus the 2015-2024 average (a different comparison base). NIH being 'behind pace for 2026' is corroborated by Science/AAAS reporting ('NIH likely to award fewer grants as it races to spend 2026 budget'). Verdict: mostly true -- correct direction, and the figure sits within a few points of NIH's own comparable published metric.

NSF '46% fewer new grants than the Biden-era average, lowest since the 1980s' -- corroborated by Nature ('Exclusive: NSF set to issue lowest number of new grants in four decades') and Science/AAAS reporting: NSF had issued 5,684 new grants as of August 19, 2026, tracking to roughly 6,100 for the full fiscal year, about 46% below the FY2021-24 average, a level not seen since the early 1980s. Verdict: not checkable in this corpus (no NSF award-count table exists here at all); corroborated by outside reporting.

'Some AAU institutions reported 10-25% declines, one 32%' -- this exact sentence appears verbatim in an archived (Wayback Machine, Sept. 2026) copy of the AAU backgrounder itself, confirming it is genuinely AAU's own claim and not a misquotation, but AAU does not name the institutions or cite a source for the figures, and no table in this corpus carries a cross-institution federal-funding-change metric by year to check it independently. Verdict: not checkable here (and not independently sourceable outside AAU's own unattributed assertion either).

Bottom line

The AAU article's specific, attributable facts (Johns Hopkins layoffs and funding decline) check out cleanly against independent reporting and JHU's own statement. Its aggregate NIH and NSF grant-count figures match NIH's own data and independent science-policy reporting. The one claim resting solely on AAU's own unattributed assertion (institution-level 10-25%/32% declines) cannot be checked against this corpus or any other source found. Overall, this is a case where the government-data corpus itself has a genuine, structural coverage gap on the exact period the article is about (2025-2026 grant counts and post-2024 institutional funding), not a case where the corpus contradicts the claims.
