# Fact-check: "Americans increasingly see crime declining, but fears of scams persist" (Stateline via YourNews, 2026-11-16)

Note: the YourNews page only returned a summary via WebFetch, not verbatim text, so no quotes could be checked. I did not see the full article.

| Claim | Verdict | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 46% say national crime rose in past year, down from 77% in 2023 | Supported | Gallup, Oct 1-19 2026 poll (news.gallup.com/poll/716657) |
| 33% say local crime rising, among lowest in 5+ decades | Supported (33% confirmed; "lowest" per Gallup coverage) | Gallup |
| Adults 55+ worry about identity theft 77% vs 52% of under-35 | Supported | Gallup |
| FBI: violent crime fell 9.3% in 2025, largest drop since 1936 estimates began | Supported, but preliminary data (FBI/Patel say largest since 1937 in some wording; one outlet says 9.7%) | FBI release; Fox, Axios, HSToday |

| 70% worry about identity theft; 55% about scammers; only two of ten crimes with majority worry | Not confirmed: the Gallup article I fetched gave age-group splits only. Its scam figures were 59% (55+) and 48% (18-34), which are consistent with a 55% overall figure. | Gallup topline not reached |
| Women more worried; lower-income more concerned about violence; partisan convergence | Not checkable here | Gallup crosstabs |

## Rating: 1 Pinocchio (mostly accurate)
The poll figures match Gallup. The FBI figures are right in substance, with the murder number ambiguous between count (18.1%) and rate (18.5%).

## Misleading or missing context
- The FBI 2025 figures are preliminary, not final.
- Perception improved from a 2023 peak, but 46% still say crime rose, and 46% is roughly flat versus 49% in 2025. The headline "increasingly" overstates the one-year movement.
- Gallup is a 1,200-adult poll with a sampling margin of error.
