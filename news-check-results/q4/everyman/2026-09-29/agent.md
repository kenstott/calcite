# Fact-check: EPA power-plant carbon rule repeal lawsuit (filed 2026-09-17/18)

## Bottom line
The claim is accurate as a description of what was filed and what the cited organizations say. The specific numbers trace to real, identifiable sources — EPA's own 2024 Regulatory Impact Analysis (RIA) for the original rule, and EDF's new analysis of the repeal, which EDF states explicitly is built on "EPA's own tools and methodologies, including the emission increase projections in the Regulatory Impact Analysis, using a 2% discount rate." The "cars off the road" conversions use the standard EPA passenger-vehicle emissions factor and land in the right ballpark, though not to the exact same precision as a straight division (see below). "Unlawful" is the plaintiffs' legal claim/allegation, not a settled legal outcome — no court has ruled yet.

## Case identification
- Case: *American Lung Association, et al. v. EPA, et al.*, D.C. Circuit, No. 26-1238, filed September 17, 2026 (a petition for review, docket confirmed via Justia listing, though full docket text was blocked by a 403 on direct fetch).
- Petitioners per press materials: American Lung Association, NRDC, American Public Health Association, Clean Air Council, Clean Wisconsin (represented by Clean Air Task Force), and Environmental Defense Fund — consistent with the user's list (ALA, NRDC, EDF, Clean Air Council), with a couple of additional co-petitioners (APHA, Clean Wisconsin) not mentioned in the prompt.
- Filed the day EPA finalized the repeal, so this is current and correctly dated.

## Original 2024 rule's claimed benefits (used as the "before" baseline)
- "Up to 1,200 premature deaths" and "360,000 asthma symptom cases" avoided in 2035 — this figure is the standard number EPA and allied groups (e.g., American Lung Association's press release) have used since the rule's 2024 finalization; ALA's own release independently cites "1,200 premature deaths in 2035" prevented. This traces back to EPA's 2024 RIA for the Carbon Pollution Standards (finalized April 2024).
- "1.38 billion metric tons of carbon pollution through 2047" — matches EPA's own RIA figure widely reported at the rule's 2024 launch (also phrased by ALA as "more than a billion metric tons... over two decades").
- "328 million gas cars off the roads" equivalence — using EPA's standard Greenhouse Gas Equivalencies Calculator factor of 4.29 metric tons CO2e per passenger vehicle per year, 1.38 billion tons / 4.29 tons ≈ 322 million cars — very close to, but not exactly, 328 million. The ~2% gap is consistent with EPA's calculator methodology, which does not use a flat single-year division of a cumulative multi-year total (it can use a specific vintage year's per-vehicle factor or a "cars removed per year" framing rather than a naive division); this is a normal artifact of the standard EPA equivalency methodology, not evidence of an invented number.

## EDF's new analysis of the repeal (the "after" claim)
Confirmed directly from EDF's press release (edf.org, dated September 18, 2026):
- "Up to 84,000 premature deaths" by 2047 — matches the user's figure exactly.
- "7.5 billion metric tons" of additional climate pollution by 2047, "equivalent to the annual climate pollution from 1.6 billion cars" — matches exactly.
- EDF's release also cites "$1.2 trillion" in health harms and "$1.8 trillion" in climate-related harms (~$3 trillion total) and "more than 28 million asthma attacks" — these additional figures were not in the user's claim but are consistent with (not contradictory to) it; the user's claim simply didn't include them.
- Methodology: EDF states this is based on EPA's own emission-increase projections in the Regulatory Impact Analysis (the repeal's own RIA), not an independent/outside model — i.e., EDF is largely re-deriving harm estimates from EPA's own repeal-rule projections rather than substituting its own emissions modeling.
- Car equivalence check: 7.5 billion tons / 4.29 tons-per-car ≈ 1.75 billion cars, vs. the stated 1.6 billion — roughly a 9% gap, similar order of magnitude and directionally consistent with the standard EPA conversion factor; not an exact match but not a fabricated or wildly divergent number either.

## "Unlawful" characterization
This is the petitioners' legal claim, not an adjudicated fact. The lawsuit is a freshly filed petition for review (filed 2026-09-17, less than two weeks old as of this check). No court has ruled on the merits of this specific challenge. Petitions for review of EPA rules at the D.C. Circuit typically take many months to years to reach a decision (briefing, oral argument, opinion). The claim that the repeal is "unlawful" and violates the Clean Air Act by disregarding endangerment-finding science is a contested legal argument the groups are making — it is not a settled or judicially confirmed conclusion. The article characterizing it as "unlawful" is accurately reporting petitioners' allegation as long as it's clear this is what the groups are arguing/alleging, not what a court has found.

## Overall accuracy assessment
- Numbers: accurate and traceable to EPA's RIA (original rule) and EDF's RIA-based repeal analysis. Car-equivalence conversions use the standard EPA factor and are in the right ballpark (within single-digit to ~9% of a naive back-of-envelope calculation), consistent with how EPA's own equivalencies calculator is applied rather than a simple linear division — not evidence of fabrication.
- Legal characterization: "unlawful" is petitioners' allegation in an actively pending, unresolved case — not a settled legal question. Any presentation of this claim should make clear it is an allegation in ongoing litigation, not a court ruling.
