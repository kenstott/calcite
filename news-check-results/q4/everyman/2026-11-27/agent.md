# Fact-check: DTN "Drought Maintains a Tight Grip on the Lower Mississippi River" (Bryce Anderson, Nov 23, 2026)

Method: WebFetch of the article (returned as a summary, not verbatim), then the NIDIS/drought.gov status update of 2026-11-12 and the USDM national summary of 2026-11-17. Limits: no Python or charts; no direct Southern Regional Climate Center data; Nov 17 USDM state percentages not retrieved.

## Claims
1. About 94% of the Lower Mississippi valley region is in drought in mid-November. SUPPORTED. NIDIS: 94% of AR/LA/MS (USDM, Nov 10).
2. This is more than double the roughly 42% in late July. NOT CHECKABLE HERE. NIDIS gives no late-July figure, only that drought intensified from August after a brief summer improvement.
3. More than 90% of Arkansas, more than 80% of Mississippi and more than 82% of Louisiana are in D2-D4. NOT CHECKED. NIDIS gives only D1-D4 totals (AR 97%, LA 95%, MS 92%) and no per-state D2-D4 figures. The totals are consistent with these claims.
4. Almost 56% of Arkansas is in D4. PARTLY SUPPORTED. NIDIS (Nov 10 data) says almost 50%. The article uses the later Nov 17 map, so growth is plausible, but I could not confirm 56%.
5. Aug-Oct temperatures were 5-9 F above normal (SRCC). NOT CHECKED NUMERICALLY. NIDIS confirms the warmest Aug-Oct on record in all three states but gives no departures.
6. All three states got under 50% of normal precipitation in August and under 10% in October. NOT CHECKED NUMERICALLY. NIDIS says Aug-Oct was 3rd/4th/5th driest (AR/LA/MS) but gives no monthly percentages.
7. Low river levels are the main transport concern. SUPPORTED. NIDIS: streamflows low to much below normal. It adds that the NWS 28-day forecast keeps Lower Mississippi sites above low-flow thresholds, which the article omits.
8. Other impacts: fire risk, soil moisture, livestock water, wetlands, community water supplies. SUPPORTED in general. NIDIS reports elevated fire risk, 76% of pastures poor to very poor, and lake drops of about 15 ft.
9. NIDIS says El Nino could bring enough winter rain to ease or end the drought. SUPPORTED, and the article understates it. NIDIS cites a more than 83% chance of a strong-to-very-strong El Nino and expects improvement across much of the region by the end of January 2027.
10. Quote from the Nov 17 USDM summary: "many parts of these three states require at least 20 to 30 inches of rain in a three-month span." SUPPORTED. The USDM text reads "require at least 20 to 30 inches of rain in a 3-month span", so the article's wording differs slightly from the source.

## Overall rating: 1 Pinocchio (mostly accurate)
Every claim I could check matches the primary sources. The unchecked items are the late-July 42% baseline, the per-state D2-D4 shares, the monthly precipitation percentages and the temperature departures. The Arkansas D4 figure (about 56% vs about 50% a week earlier) is the one discrepancy, and it is probably due to the later map date.

## Misleading or missing context
- Late-July baseline: the "more than double" framing relies on a figure I could not verify. NIDIS notes a brief summer improvement, so the comparison depends on the chosen start date.
- Drought categories: drought is stated in D2-D4 terms for states but D1-D4 for the region, so the two figures are not like-for-like.
- Outlook: the article is cautious ("could bring enough precipitation"). NIDIS is more optimistic, with a strong El Nino likely and improvement expected by the end of January. Navigation risk is also tempered by the NWS low-flow forecast.
