# Fact-check: Fox Business, "Down payment savings now take 10.5 years, triple the 1980s timeline" (Eric Revell, Nov 27, 2026)

Method: WebFetch returned only a summary of the article, not verbatim text. Claims below come from that summary and may miss some. No access to the Realtor.com primary report. I checked it only through syndications and secondary coverage. Python and charts were not used.

## Claims
| # | Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Saving 10% down takes a typical household 10.5 years (5.7 in 2019) | Supported as Realtor.com's finding (several outlets and a press release repeat 10.5). The 2019 figure was not independently confirmed. The model assumes savers put aside about 5.1% of income. Realtor.com's earlier estimate was about 7 years for 2025 (peak 12 in 2022). A jump to 10.5 within a year is unexplained in what I saw. |
| 2 | That is "more than triple" the 1980s | Roughly true. Secondary sources give the 1980s baseline as about 3 to 3.3 years. 10.5 / 3.3 = 3.2x. |
| 3 | 1980s home price about 3.2x income; today 4.8x | Matches Realtor.com via secondary sources. |
| 4 | Prices up more than fivefold, incomes under fourfold | Not verified. Both are nominal figures and depend on the exact period, which the summary did not state. |
| 5 | About $150,000 less buying power than four decades ago | Matches Realtor.com (about $49,000 more income needed). It is Realtor.com's model, not a government statistic. |
| 6 | Record mortgage rate of 18.63% in 1981 | Consistent with my recollection of Freddie Mac's peak (Oct 1981). I did not re-check it live this run. |
| 7 | Rate "currently" about 7.21% | Unconfirmed. Freddie Mac's latest I found was 7.28% (week of Nov 19, up from 7.11%). 7.21% may be the Nov 26 reading. Not checkable here. Another Realtor.com summary cites 6.6% for the mortgage-rate comparison. |
| 8 | Inflation 3.7% "in the most recent data" | Stale or misleading. 3.7% is the September 2026 CPI-U 12-month rate (BLS). The October release (Nov 10) exists, but BLS published no 12-month change because October 2025 data were not collected during the funding lapse. "Most recent data" is defensible but needs that caveat. |
| 9 | 43 of the 50 largest metros: payment over 30% of median income with 10% down | Attributed to Realtor.com. Not verified against the underlying data. |
| 10 | Los Angeles 85.3% of income, San Diego 64.8% | Not verified. |
| 11 | Quote, Hannah Jones: 1980s "home prices were far more attainable relative to household incomes" | Hannah Jones is a Realtor.com economic research analyst. Secondary sources show the same sentiment. Exact wording not confirmed. |

## Overall rating: 2 Pinocchios (mostly accurate, with caveats)
The piece accurately relays one trade group's analysis. I found no outright errors.

## Materially misleading or missing context
- Single-source. Realtor.com is a listings and brokerage-industry firm. The piece gives no independent check, such as Census, FHFA or Fed data.
- Modeling assumptions drive the headline. The 10.5 years rests on an assumed 5.1% savings rate and a 10% down payment. It is not an observed measurement. Realtor.com's own 2025 estimate was about 7 years.
- The comparison is lopsided. It stresses down-payment time and price/income, but 1980s mortgage rates (about 12.7% average per Realtor.com, 18.63% peak) mean monthly payments were not obviously easier. Using the peak rate as the lead comparison is cherry-picked, and the 1980s average is a fairer yardstick.
- Mixed vintages. The rate is a weekly figure, the inflation figure is from September, and the other numbers come from an unstated date.
- Headline wording ("triple") is fair but depends on a 1980s baseline that outlets report differently (3 vs 3.3 years).

Sources: foxbusiness.com article (summary); Realtor.com analysis via stocktitan.net, inman.com (11/24/2026), unitedstatesrealestateinvestor.com; Freddie Mac PMMS via tradingeconomics/streetinsider; BLS CPI releases (cpi_10142026, cpi_11102026).
