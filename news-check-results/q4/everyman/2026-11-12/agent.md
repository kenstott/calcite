# Fact-check: Gulf News, "US crude oil production hits a record 14.3 million barrels per day: EIA" (updated 2026-11-11)

Method: fetched the article (WebFetch returned a summary, not verbatim text; no attributed person quotes were reported), then checked against the EIA Short-Term Energy Outlook (STEO) page and secondary coverage. Limits: I read the EIA STEO landing page, not the full data tables, so some figures are checked only to that level.

## Claims
| # | Claim | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | US crude production "hits a record 14.3 million b/d" (headline) | Misleading | EIA STEO headline: "U.S. crude oil production reaches a record 14.3 million barrels per day **in 2027**." It is a forecast annual average. 2026 is forecast at 13.9 million b/d. Production has not hit 14.3. Argus reported the first weekly reading of 14.0 million b/d, week ending Oct 30, 2026. |
| 2 | EIA expects inventories to rise in 2027 as net imports increase | Not verified in detail | Consistent with the STEO's tone, but I did not see the inventory table. |
| 3 | Commercial crude inventories fell to the lowest October level since 2014 | Accurate | STEO: commercial crude inventories fell to 410 million barrels at the end of October, the lowest end-of-October level since 2014. |
| 4 | About 70 Bcf more gas in storage at end of November than forecast in October | Partly verified | STEO says more gas in storage than previously forecast, due to mild November weather. The 70 Bcf figure was not seen on the page. |
| 5 | Henry Hub averages $2.87/MMBtu Nov-Mar, 16% below last month's forecast | Accurate | STEO states both figures. |
| 6 | Brent $104 (Nov), $99 (Dec), $86 average in 2027 | Partly verified | 2027 average of $86 confirmed. STEO says Brent stays "near $100" for the rest of 2026. The $104 and $99 monthly figures were not found on the landing page; plausible, not confirmed. |
| 7 | Reason: more Middle East oil flows and inventory rebuild | Consistent | Matches the STEO narrative; not checked line by line. |
| 8 | US LNG gross exports rise from about 15 to about 19 Bcf/d, 2025 to 2027 | Accurate | STEO: 19 Bcf/d in 2027, up 26% from 2025 (consistent with about 15). |
| 9 | Diesel $5.03/gal (from $4.49), gasoline $3.73 (from $3.56) | Accurate, with a framing gap | STEO: diesel $5.03 "next year" (2027), up 12% from last month's forecast (4.49 x 1.12 = 5.03). Gasoline $3.73, up 5% (3.56 x 1.05 = 3.74). These are 2027 forecast averages, not current prices. |
| 10 | Shipping disruptions could cause short-term price swings | Not checkable here | Qualitative risk statement. |

## Materially misleading
The headline and lead present a forecast as an achieved fact. The record is a projected 2027 annual average. Actual production is about 13.9 million b/d for 2026 and just reached 14.0 million b/d weekly. The body's other numbers are mostly forecasts too, and a reader skimming would not know which are current.

## Rating: 2 Pinocchios
The figures are almost all correctly sourced, but the headline claim, the most prominent one, misstates a forecast as a current record. This is a significant omission of context, not a fabrication.
