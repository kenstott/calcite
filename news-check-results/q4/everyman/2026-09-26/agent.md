# Fact-check: Federal research funding cuts, 2025–2026

**Overall: Yes, this is true.** All four claims check out against current reporting, though the NIH figure depends on which metric is cited.

## 1. Johns Hopkins layoffs — TRUE
Johns Hopkins laid off approximately 110 employees in June 2026 (multiple outlets say "over 100" / "110"), citing federal funding cuts. JHU reported in February 2026 that its multiyear federal research portfolio's outstanding value had declined by more than $500 million during calendar year 2025 — driven by 43% less federal research funding received and 28% fewer awards than the prior year. This followed an earlier 2025 hit from USAID grant terminations (~$800M) that triggered furloughs and broader global job cuts.

Sources: [The Daily Record](https://thedailyrecord.com/2026/06/26/johns-hopkins-university-lays-off-employees-federal-funding-cuts/), [WYPR](https://www.wypr.org/wypr-news/2026-06-25/baltimores-johns-hopkins-to-lay-off-110-employees-due-to-federal-cuts), [FOX 5 NY](https://www.fox5ny.com/news/johns-hopkins-university-lays-off-110-workers-losing-federal-funding), [The Baltimore Banner](https://www.thebanner.com/education/higher-education/johns-hopkins-university-layoffs-baltimore-federal-research-cuts-4S2I6IKVIJAING4OCDP2NJV6PU/)

## 2. NIH grants down ~25% in 2025, behind pace for 2026 — LARGELY TRUE (metric-dependent)
NIH grantmaking clearly declined in 2025 and is running further behind in 2026, but the precise "~25%" figure lands differently depending on which measure is used:
- Total new/competing-renewal grant **dollars**: FY2025 was $8.6B vs. $10.5B in FY2024, an 18% drop.
- **R01-equivalent grant recipients**: 5,885 in 2025 vs. 7,720 in 2024 — a ~24% decline, closely matching the "~25%" claim.
- Overall new-award **counts**: some analyses put the 2025 decline at ~9% (5,564 fewer grants), while success rates fell sharply (21% in 2024 to 11% in 2025).
- For 2026: NIH is running far behind normal pace — as of March 2026 it had issued ~74% fewer competitive/new awards than the 2021–2024 average for that point in the fiscal year, later reported at roughly 30% of the normal pace. The slowdown is attributed to a White House budget office policy forcing NIH to pay upfront for multiyear grant commitments, squeezing funds available for new awards.

So "~25% fewer new grants in 2025" is a reasonable characterization if you're looking at R01-equivalent awards, though a raw grant-count comparison shows a smaller (~9-18%) decline — the claim is directionally and largely correct but the exact percentage depends on the metric.

Sources: [Science/AAAS](https://www.science.org/content/article/nih-research-grant-success-rates-plummeted-2025), [STAT News](https://www.statnews.com/2026/03/17/nih-grant-funding-slowdown-new-awards-training-grants/), [Science/AAAS (2026 pace)](https://www.science.org/content/article/nih-likely-award-fewer-grants-it-races-spend-2026-budget), [AAMC](https://www.aamc.org/media/85501/download), [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/09/24/nx-s1-5931971/trump-nih-science-funding-disruptions-charts)

## 3. NSF new grants down 46% from Biden-era average, lowest since 1980s — TRUE
NSF is issuing 46% fewer new grants than the 2021–2024 average, with FY2026 new-grant totals projected at roughly 6,100 — the lowest level since the early 1980s. The decline stems largely from about $1 billion of NSF's $8.8 billion budget being held in a central account inaccessible to grant-issuing programs. Impact varies by field: the Directorate of Mathematical and Physical Sciences is at its lowest level since the 1980s, astronomy grants are down by half, and materials-research grants are down more than 40%.

Sources: [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-02574-6), [AAU](https://www.aau.edu/newsroom/nsf-making-fewer-grants-threatening-future-scientific), [AIP.org](https://www.aip.org/fyi/astronomy-materials-science-awards-plummet-at-nsf), [Physics Today](https://physicstoday.aip.org/news/the-shrinking-nsf-support-for-the-physical-sciences)

## 4. AAU institutions reporting 10-25% declines, one at 32% — TRUE
AAU reporting confirms that some member institutions have reported federal research funding declines of 10-25% compared to 2024, with one institution reporting a 32% decline. Specific examples cited: University of Kansas down $182 million year-over-year three quarters into FY26; MIT's federally funded research activity and new federal awards both down more than 20%; Johns Hopkins' portfolio down more than $500 million (see #1). AAU also cites a 15% decline in Ph.D. admissions for Fall 2026 across 55 AAU member institutions as a downstream effect.

Sources: [AAU: Federal Research Cuts Threaten U.S. Innovation and Leadership](https://www.aau.edu/resource-library/federal-research-cuts-threaten-us-innovation-and-leadership), [AAU: University Leaders Sound Alarm](https://www.aau.edu/newsroom/leading-research-universities-report/university-leaders-sound-alarm-over-slow-release), [AAU: New PhD Admissions Data](https://www.aau.edu/newsroom/new-phd-admissions-data-show-threat-us-stem-workforce)

## Bottom line
Claims 1, 3, and 4 are confirmed as stated by multiple independent sources. Claim 2 (NIH) is directionally accurate and the "~25%" figure is defensible depending on the specific metric (it matches R01-equivalent award recipients closely), but raw new-grant-count comparisons show a somewhat smaller year-over-year decline (~9-18%) — worth noting if precision on that one number matters. NIH being behind pace for 2026 is well documented and, if anything, understates the severity (reports of 70-74% shortfalls vs. historical pace earlier in the fiscal year).
