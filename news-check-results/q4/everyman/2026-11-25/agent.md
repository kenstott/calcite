# Fact-check: WFMZ, "PA unemployment rate drops to 3.5%, matching all-time record low under Gov. Shapiro" (Nov. 20, 2026, Ally Mahoney)

Limits: WebFetch returned only a summary of the article, so I have no verbatim quotes. The primary October 2026 release (PA Dept. of Labor & Industry, due Nov. 20) and BLS October data did not turn up in my searches. Python was not used.

## Claims and verdicts
1. Preliminary October rate fell 0.1 pt to 3.5%, matching the record low. PARTLY VERIFIED. The L&I September release (Oct. 16, 2026) gives 3.6% for September, down from 3.7%. It calls 3.6% "only one-tenth above the record low set in summer 2023", which implies a record of 3.5%. So 3.6 to 3.5 is consistent. The October release itself was not found. Note: the 2023 L&I releases reported record lows of 3.4% for Sept/Oct 2023, and later releases cite 3.5%. This is presumably annual benchmark revision, so "record" depends on the current revised series.
2. Fifth straight monthly decline. NOT CHECKABLE HERE for June to July. Jul 3.9, Aug 3.7, Sep 3.6, Oct 3.5 is verified for the last four steps. I did not confirm the fifth, which depends on June 2026.
3. U.S. rate 4.1%, 0.6 pt higher than PA. NOT CHECKABLE HERE for October (BLS not retrieved). The arithmetic is right (4.1 minus 3.5 = 0.6). L&I gave the September U.S. rate as 4.2%, so 4.1% is plausible.
4. Labor force down 5,000 to about 6,658,000. Consistent. September was 6,663,000 (L&I), and 6,663,000 minus 5,000 = 6,658,000.
5. Employment up 3,000; unemployment down 9,000 to lowest since July 2023. Internally consistent. September employment was 6,423,000 and unemployment 241,000, so October would be about 6,426,000 employed and 232,000 unemployed, and 6,426,000 + 232,000 = 6,658,000. The "lowest since July 2023" part is not independently verified. It is plausible, since the September rate was the lowest in three years.
6. Nonfarm jobs up 3,300 to a record 6,220,900. UNVERIFIED, with a small discrepancy. September was 6,212.6k, and 6,212.6k + 3.3k = 6,215.9k, not 6,220.9k. Revision of September could explain the gap, but I could not confirm it. A prior record of 6,209,500 (May 2026) is cited by L&I, so "record" is plausible.
7. Jobs up 44,700 over the year. Plausible. September's annual gain was 45,000. Not verified for October.
8. Education and health services largest annual gain, +25,600. Plausible in direction, since it led in September (+32,600), but the figure is lower than September's and unverified.

No attributed quotes could be extracted from the fetched summary.

## Overall grade: 1 Pinocchio (mostly accurate, minor unverified detail)
The core claim is consistent with the official trend: 3.9, 3.7, 3.6, then 3.5 percent. The labor-force figures reconcile with L&I's September numbers.

## Potentially misleading context
- "Record low" depends on revised data. Original 2023 releases reported 3.4%. Readers comparing with older headlines may be confused.
- The headline credits "under Gov. Shapiro". The record-low rates were first set in 2023 and the rate is a statewide result of many factors. The attribution is political framing, not a verified causal claim.
- The labor force fell while unemployment fell, so part of the rate drop came from fewer people counted as unemployed or in the labor force, not only job gains. Monthly changes of 0.1 pt are within statistical noise for state estimates.

Sources: pa.gov/agencies/dli/newsroom/unemployment-rate-reaches-lowest-level-in-three-years; pa.gov L&I July/Sept/Oct 2023 releases; WFMZ article (summary only).
