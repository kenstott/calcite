# Fact-check: Spectrum News 1, "Billions spent as 2026 becomes the most expensive election in U.S. history" (Kate Nuechterlein, Nov. 2, 2026)

Method note: the page fetched, but only as a summary from the fetch tool, not verbatim text. Quotes are therefore graded from that summary. I did not check FEC filings directly. I relied on secondary outlets that cite them.

## Overall grade: 2 Pinocchios (Half True), driven by one framing problem

Most of the numbers match other outlets' reporting. The headline framing is the weak point.

## Claim-by-claim

| # | Claim | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Total spending is projected at nearly $12 billion, per OpenSecrets | Supported | OpenSecrets projects $11.9B (Reuters explainer, BBC, Bond Buyer). |
| 2 | 2026 is the most expensive election in U.S. history | Misleading | Reputable sources say "most expensive midterm" (NPR/AdImpact; OpenSecrets compares to the 2022 midterm at $8.9B). OpenSecrets' own wording is that $11.9B would exceed the total spent on *congressional* races in 2024. I could not confirm the 2024 all-in total, which I recall as roughly $15B or more. That figure is from memory and unverified. It would put 2026 below 2024 overall. The AdImpact comparison also mixes figures: Al Jazeera cites $11.21B (ads and reservations through Oct. 26) against $3.2B for the 2024 presidential season. |
| 3 | Congressional spending has surpassed any previous election, including presidential | Partly supported, overstated | OpenSecrets says only that it passes the 2024 congressional total. This is not a claim about whole presidential cycles. |
| 4 | More than $500M spent on the Texas Senate race | Supported | Al Jazeera: more than $534M from candidates and outside groups. |
| 5 | Republican-aligned outside groups outspend Democratic ones by nearly $400M | Not checkable here | I found no source for this figure. |
| 6 | Musk has given $445M to GOP-aligned PACs, the top spender | Supported | Al Jazeera: $446M, the largest donor on the right. The $1M difference is rounding or timing. |
| 7 | Soros has given about $100M to Democratic-side PACs | Supported | Al Jazeera: about $102M to left-leaning candidates. |
| 8 | Musk has given more than $150M to PACs backing Paxton | Supported | Al Jazeera: more than $150M. A Texas Tribune report relayed in a broadcast script says the pro-Paxton Texas PAC raised $158M, almost all from Musk. |
| 9 | Talarico (D) vs. Paxton (R, state attorney general) | Supported | Multiple Texas outlets. |
| 10 | Talarico raised $145M from individual donors against Paxton's $22M | Roughly supported, with caveats | Al Jazeera has $146M against about $22M. A Click2Houston/FEC script has $140.3M against $21.6M through Oct. 14. Another outlet has $136.9M against $35.6M. Cutoff dates and definitions differ. Individual donations are about 99.6% of Talarico's total and 97.8% of Paxton's. |
| 11 | Hilary Braseth, OpenSecrets executive director, said "It's not just lobbying these days," about super PACs spending on both sides | Not checkable here | I could not find the original. The WLRN/NPR piece quotes a different OpenSecrets person, Brendan Glavin. |
| 12 | David Miller, American University, said Democrats rely on small donors and Republicans on fewer, larger donors | Plausible | Consistent with the data: about 51% of Talarico's money came from donors giving under $200, against 12% for Paxton. The attribution to Miller was not independently checked. |
| 13 | Ad descriptions (Talarico calls Paxton corrupt; Paxton attacks "radical Democrats") | Not checkable here | Not checked against the ads. |
| 14 | Experts say spending may reach diminishing returns | Opinion, not checkable | Not a factual claim. |

## Misleading or missing context

1. **"Most expensive in history" is not established.** The defensible claim is "most expensive midterm". The piece's own sources use that narrower wording.
2. **Mixed measures.** OpenSecrets projects total spending and AdImpact measures ad spending. The piece does not distinguish them.
3. **Spending is lopsided.** Outside spending in Texas favors Paxton, at nearly $300M against about $80M for Talarico, according to Al Jazeera. This is despite Talarico's large lead in candidate fundraising. Setting the $145M against $22M figures without that context is misleading.
4. **Election timing.** All figures are pre-election projections, published the day before the Nov. 3 vote.

Sources: Reuters explainer via Devdiscourse; Al Jazeera, Nov. 1, 2026; WLRN/NPR, Oct. 28, 2026; Bond Buyer; Click2Houston, Oct. 29, 2026; Texas Tribune.
