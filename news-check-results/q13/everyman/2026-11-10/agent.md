## Materially misleading or missing context
- Headline "disturbing trend": the 27.6% share is only 0.3 pt above June (27.3%) and 0.5 above Aug (27.0%); it dipped to 25.5% in July. The average duration moved 24.8 -> 27.1 in one month after 26.3 in August, i.e. it is volatile (the piece does concede noise). A one-month uptick does not make a trend; the article's own source calls the series noisy.
- Not flagged: BLS says October 2025 household data were never collected because of the federal shutdown, so year-over-year comparisons of these duration series have a gap.
- Benefits-expiry explanation for low claims is speculative and presented without counter-evidence.
- Mild cherry-pick: "strong" numbers sit alongside upward revisions (Aug +53k, Sep +31k) the piece does not mention, which only strengthens the headline strength, so no distortion there.

## Rating
1 Pinocchio: core numbers are right; a few historical-rank claims and JOLTS "highest/lowest" comparisons are unconfirmed, and the "disturbing trend" framing rests on a noisy one-month move.
