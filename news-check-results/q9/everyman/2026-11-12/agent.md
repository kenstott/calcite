## Overall rating

1 Pinocchio (a few Pinocchios would be unfair).

Nearly every number matches NAR's own releases. The one clear error is the 350M population figure, which is about 8M above the Census estimate. The $35T housing-wealth figure could not be confirmed.

## Misleading context to flag

- The piece is a stenographic report of NAR's own economists. NAR is a trade group for agents, and the stats about agent use and "pent-up demand" serve its interest. The article does not say so.
- The headline "pent-up demand could be released" is a conditional on supply, affordability and confidence all improving. The body admits it is unclear whether that will happen. It is a scenario, not a finding.
- The 2000 baseline is the housing-boom run-up, and the article compares population growth with sales without adjusting for the sales-per-household ratio, so the "gap" is framed generously.
- The article does not note that median first-time buyer age is lifted by all-cash first-timers (median 54) against financed ones (38).

I could not verify claims 1, 4, 14 and 15 against any source. I did not verify the 2000 baselines (sales, jobs) from primary data.
