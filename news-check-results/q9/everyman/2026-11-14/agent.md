# Fact-check: Public Citizen, "Corporations Pour Record-Smashing $737 Million Into 2026 Midterms" (Rick Claypool, Oct 29, 2026)

Limits: WebFetch returned a summary, not verbatim text. FEC and OpenSecrets raw data were not queried directly (search snippets only). No charts were produced.

| # | Claim | Verdict |

## Overall grade: 1 Pinocchio (mostly accurate)

Reasons:
- The arithmetic and percentages are sound.
- Corporate giving is clearly at record levels and driven by crypto and tech.
- Two figures need correction or clarification: the a16z number ($72M vs $76.2M) and the MAGA Inc. denominator ($449M vs FEC-reported totals).

## Possibly misleading framing

- "Corporate" is Public Citizen's definition: for-profit entities giving $5,000 or more to super PACs and hybrid PACs. Entities used by wealthy individuals (a Soros family entity, a Musk trust) are counted, so the figure partly reflects individuals' money routed through business entities.
- The comparison is a partial 2026 cycle, through Oct 23, against full-cycle 2022 and 2024 totals. The article's claim that 2026 is higher is therefore conservative. Comparing a midterm with a presidential cycle on a 60% basis is fair only if readers know the data window.
- The totals use OpenSecrets data for 2010-2024 and FEC data for 2026. Different sources and cleaning steps can affect the comparison.
- Public Citizen is an advocacy group that favors campaign finance restrictions. Its sources are public, but its choices on what counts as corporate spending shape the headline.
