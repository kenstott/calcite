# Fact-check: Benzinga, "Social Security deteriorating rapidly ... fund deficit hit record $221 billion in fiscal year 2026"

## Access limits
- The Benzinga article returned HTTP 403 to WebFetch, and search did not surface its text. I could not read the piece, so I could not extract its claims or quotes. This is a limit of this check, not a verdict on the article.
- I did not guess at the contents. Everything below tests only the headline claims.

## Headline claims

1. **"Fund deficit hit a record $221 billion in fiscal 2026": roughly supported, about $1 billion off.**
   - Wolf Street (Nov 9, 2026), using SSA data, gives a FY2026 deficit of about $220 billion for the OASI trust fund. Income was $1.28T and costs $1.50T.
   - The trust fund fell 9.2% to $2.18T.
   - The $1 billion gap could come from rounding or a different data cut (SSA monthly trust fund data versus the Treasury statement). I did not find a primary source giving exactly $221B.
   - It is a record in dollar terms. FY2025 was $181B, and the FY2024 deficit was roughly half of that.
   - Caveat: this figure covers OASI only, not combined OASDI, and the headline calls it "the fund."

2. **"Social Security deteriorating rapidly": opinion by a commentator, not checkable as fact.**
   - Supporting data: the deficit grew from about $181B to about $220B in one year, and costs rose 5.8% against 3.5% for income.
   - The 2026 Trustees Report puts the 75-year actuarial deficit at 4.42% of payroll, up from 3.82%.
   - CRFB says the trust fund is about six years from insolvency. I did not open the Trustees Report itself.

3. **Not checkable here:** the commentator's identity and exact quotes, and any other claims in the body, because the article was inaccessible.

## Misleading-context flags (typical of this framing, not confirmed for the article)
- "Record" is nominal. Costs and income both grow with inflation and population, so a nominal record is not surprising.
- Part of the jump follows the Social Security Fairness Act (2025), which raised benefits retroactively.
- The article should say which fund the figure refers to (OASI versus OASDI) and that insolvency means a roughly 20-25% benefit cut under current law, not zero benefits.

## Rating
- **Pinocchio rating: 1 Pinocchio (provisional).**
  - The headline number is essentially right, within about $1B and probably a rounding or data-source difference.
  - The framing is opinion.
  - The rating is provisional because I could not read the body text.

Sources: wolfstreet.com 2026/11/09 Social Security deficit article; SSA Trustees summary (ssa.gov/oact/trsum); CRFB 2026 Trustees analysis; Treasury Monthly Treasury Statement, Sept 2026 (via a mirror).
