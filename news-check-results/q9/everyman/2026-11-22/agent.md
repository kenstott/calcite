# Fact-check: Hudson County View, "Jersey City crime data shows nearly 25% average decline, officials say"

Method: WebFetch of the article (returned as a summary, not verbatim), WebFetch of impact.jcnj.org, and one web search. Limited depth.

## Claims
| Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Shots fired 47 to 28 (-40%) | Supported: impact.jcnj.org, YTD through Nov 14, 2026 vs 2025 |
| Theft from vehicles 832 to 452 (-46%) | Supported (same dashboard) |
| Burglaries 597 to 391 (-35%) | Supported (same dashboard) |
| Homicides 11 to 9 (-18%) | Supported (same dashboard) |
| Data is public 24/7 at impact.jcnj.org | Supported. The portal launched June 11, 2026 (News12, city release). |
| Overall crime down about 25% citywide; listed decreases average about 24% | Not verified. The dashboard figures I read do not reproduce either number. The four items I could read average about 35%, so the 24% average rests on rows I did not see. Not checkable here. |
| Ambrose and Kearns leave at year end; Roldan and Ragland succeed them | Not checkable here. My search found no confirmation of Kearns, Roldan or Ragland. |
| Solomon quote, "Every resident deserves to feel safe in their neighborhood." | Not checkable here (attribution only; no transcript found). |
| Credit to intelligence-led policing, JC IMPACT and a new Domestic Violence Unit | Attribution to officials, not independently verifiable. Causation is unproven. |

## Misleading context
- The figures are preliminary, and the dashboard says they are subject to revision.
- Comparison base: a June 2026 city audit released by Mayor Solomon said more than 3,250 crimes went unreported under the prior administration. If the 2025 baseline was recounted or the 2026 methodology differs, a year-over-year drop is hard to interpret. The piece does not address this.
- Small counts: the 18% homicide drop is 2 incidents, and shots fired fell by 19.
- Averaging percentage changes across categories of very different size is not a crime rate. Two unrelated search results (MyTownView, 12 months) show a more modest 9% decline in total offenses, though that covers a different period and source.
- Source: all data comes from officials, and the article is framed as "officials say".

## Rating
2 Pinocchios (leaning toward 1). The individual numbers match the city dashboard, but the headline 25% figure was not independently confirmed, and the piece omits the baseline and audit caveats and the small-number caveat. This is an incomplete verification, not a finding of falsehood.
