Limits: WebFetch returned only a summary of the article, not the full text, so claims below are those in that summary. I did not obtain full quotes or surrounding context. I ran three searches and did not reach the primary sources (EGLE Integrated Report, Illinois/Indiana reports, the Hauck quote).

## Rating: 1 Pinocchio (mostly accurate, with caveats)

Every claim I could check holds up. The main problems are in framing, not in the facts.

## Potentially misleading
- The 75% headline is an extrapolation from partial monitoring. It is not a measured share of river miles.
- The Indiana and Illinois comparison is shaky. State monitoring designs and standards differ, and Indiana's figure is of assessed miles while Michigan's is an estimate across all waters. Michigan Public flagged this.
- The "300 at any time" wording conflicts with how EGLE applies the standard (daily geometric mean in season). Exceeding 300 on one grab sample is not necessarily a violation.
- The home-price loss is temporary and is a model estimate.
- Not stated in the summary: exceedance of the partial-body standard is much rarer than of the swimming standard. "Dangerous" in the headline applies mainly to swimming-level limits.
