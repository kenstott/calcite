# Fact-check: Political Wire, "Federal Employees Look to Democrats for a Lifeline" (Nov 15, 2026)

Note: Political Wire's post is a short summary of a New York Times article (Nov 15, 2026). I could not read the NYT original directly (via WebFetch), only the summary and search snippets of the Times piece (republished in the Boston Globe/DNYUZ). The Times' own text and quotes could not be fully checked.

## Claims
1. Trump has cut the federal workforce since the start of his second term, "shearing 17 percent of employees" (quote from NYT). **Disputed / not matched.** Sources I found: OPM data shows about -264,000 employees since Jan 20, 2025 (Aug 2026 data); AIP and CBPP put it near 10%; GAO says the largest agencies are down 11.3%; Our Public Service says ~12% from the Sept 2024 base; OPM director 249,000 (~10.7%). I found no source for 17%. It may use a different base or definition (for example, including separations only at some agencies, or counting projected exits), which I could not verify. Direction is right; the magnitude looks high.
2. Trump has politicized jobs meant to be nonpartisan. **Characterization / opinion attributed to NYT.** Not testable as a fact here.
3. Current and former workers are turning to Democrats. **Supported.** Federal News Network (Nov 2026) reports employee advocates see midterm results as an opportunity; the Times article lists a wish list including reconstituting USAID.
4. Democrats gained power after Republican midterm losses. **Supported, with timing nuance.** AP called the House for Democrats after the Nov 3, 2026 midterms; about 25 Republican seats flipped. Some outlets report a Senate win, which I did not confirm. They take office Jan 3, 2027; until then Republicans hold Congress, so "gained power" is slightly early.
5. Democrats face competing priorities (affordability, health care, the war in Iran), so reversing cuts may have to wait. **Supported.** The Iran war exists (Reuters/Al-Monitor explainer Nov 12, 2026); the Times piece says a Democratic Congress can do only so much while Trump is president.

## Rating
2 Pinocchios (half true) on the 0-4 scale, driven by the 17% figure, which is higher than every measure I found (about 10-12%). Everything else checks out.

## Misleading context
- The piece is a three-bullet aggregation of another outlet's reporting, so it inherits that outlet's numbers without checking them.
- Doesn't note that Democrats lack power over the White House (veto) and that a cut may be partly reversible only via appropriations.
- The 17% figure is unqualified; the base and period are unstated.
