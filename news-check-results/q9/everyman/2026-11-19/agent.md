# Fact-check: WTKR "Hampton Roads bridges show improvement, air travel surges in 2026 transportation report"

Method: WebFetch of the article (returned as a summary, so quotes are as relayed by the fetch tool), HRTPO news pages, and web search. The full report PDF (hrtpo.org DocumentCenter 15070) exceeded the fetch size limit, so figures that appear only inside the PDF could not be read directly. WAVY coverage returned 403.

## Claims

| # | Claim | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | HRTPO released a "State of Transportation 2026" report | Mostly true, naming off | HRTPO titles it "State of Transportation in Hampton Roads 2025" (doc T26-01, released around Jan 2026). "2026" is WTKR's label. |
| 2 | 2.7% of roughly 1,300 bridges are in poor condition | Supported | HRTPO newsflash: 34 bridges (2.7%) poor in 2025. 34 / 2.7% implies about 1,260 bridges, consistent with "roughly 1,300". |
| 3 | Down from more than 5% in 2017 | Supported, with a baseline wrinkle | HRTPO says 68 bridges in 2016, which is half of 34. 68 of about 1,260 is about 5.4%. Another outlet reported 5.5%. The article's year is 2017 and HRTPO's is 2016. |
| 4 | Nichols (HRTPO principal engineer) credited state funding, a state-of-good-repair program, and a prioritization methodology | Not checkable here | The quote is only in the video or article. No transcript found. |
| 5 | HRBT is "decades and decades old" and widening will improve condition | Not checkable here | Same as claim 4. |
| 6 | Traffic fatalities higher than a decade ago but declining over the last few years | Not checkable here | The report PDF was too large to fetch, and the HRTPO summary page gives no fatality figures. |
| 7 | Air travel out of Hampton Roads up 46% from 2015 to 2024 (Norfolk and Newport News) | Not verified | HRTPO says more than 2.5M passengers boarded in the region and that Norfolk set a record in 2024. The 46% figure did not appear anywhere I could access. |
| 8 | Norfolk added international service (Cancun) and a new arrivals terminal | Not checkable here | No primary source retrieved. |
| 9 | Norfolk Amtrak added a third daily train | Not checkable here | HRTPO gives only about 421,000 Amtrak boardings and alightings in FFY2024, 70% above FFY2022. |
| 10 | Travelers lost an average of 17 hours to congestion in 2023 | Not checkable here | Not on the pages retrieved. It is probably in the PDF. |
| 11 | Traffic about 4% below pre-pandemic levels | Supported | HRTPO: roadway travel about 4% below 2019 levels. |
| 12 | Work-from-home rates higher than 2019 | Not checkable here | Plausible, but I found no primary source. |

## Misleading or tension points

- Headline says air travel "surges". The only metric reported is a 9-year cumulative change (2015 to 2024) that includes the 2020 collapse. It is not a recent surge. If 46% is right, that is about 4.3% per year.
- A headline about "improvement" sits beside a line saying traffic is about 4% below pre-pandemic levels. A search snippet of the HRTPO report says congestion has passed pre-pandemic levels by some measures. The piece does not resolve the two, and I could not read the underlying PDF to see which is right.
- Report naming and year (2025 vs 2026) are inconsistent with HRTPO's own title.

## Rating

Two Pinocchios by the Post scale is too harsh and one is nearly right, so: **1 Pinocchio (minor omissions or framing problems)**. The verifiable core numbers (2.7%, the 2016/2017 baseline, 4% below 2019) match HRTPO. The weak points are the "surges" framing, the 2025/2026 naming, and the unverified 46%, 17-hour, and third-train claims. A reader should treat those as probably drawn from the report but unconfirmed here.

Not checkable here (limit of access, not evidence of error): claims 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 10, 12, and 7 for the 46% value.
