Bridge Michigan fecal-bacteria story: 2 Pinocchios

Findings: every checkable number holds up; the headline 75% could not be independently reproduced

Standards, the $13,000 home-price study, the Environment America 69%/17-state figure and the CDC cite all check out. The 75% estimate (attributed to EGLE's Molly Rippke), the one-employee and $40,000 funding claims, the Indiana 74% and Illinois 83% figures, and the lawmaker quotes were not verified. The "50 million GI illnesses" figure was not found. Rating: 2 Pinocchios overall, reflecting unverifiable core claim and limited checking, not found falsehoods.

  • Caveat: I could read the article only through a summarizer, not verbatim; quotes were not checked verbatim.
  • Misleading risk: 75% is an extrapolation from about half of waterways tested; the 69% figure means unsafe on at least one day, and Indiana/Illinois use river-mile assessments, so comparisons are not like for like. Headline "fecal levels befoul 3 of 4 rivers" states an estimate as fact. The home-price effect was temporary (during outbreaks) per the study.
  • CDC: study estimates 5.61M recreational-water illnesses (CrI 2.97-9.00M) in 2014, 79% ear infections, so "could top a million" is consistent but GI-specific figure is not in the paper.
