Benzinga's Social Security story: 1 Pinocchio. The numbers hold, but they describe only the retirement fund

Summary of findings: accurate figures, unlabeled scope

Rating: 1 Pinocchio (mostly accurate). The article (Benzinga, Tanya Rawat, Nov 16, 2026, relaying an X post by the Kobeissi Letter) reports a record $221 billion FY2026 deficit, up $39 billion, with costs up $83 billion to $1.5 trillion and income up $44 billion to $1.3 trillion. An independent analysis of SSA data (Wolf Street, Nov 9) gives $220 billion, 181 to 220 (+$39B), costs +$83B to $1.50T, income +$43.5B to $1.28T, reserves $2.18T, down $640B from the 2017 peak. These match within rounding.

  • Main problem: scope. Every one of these numbers is for the Old-Age and Survivors (OASI) fund alone; Wolf Street states the Disability Insurance (DI) fund is excluded. The article says "Social Security" and "the fund" without saying so. DI ran a surplus of about $40B in 2025 (SSA Table 4.A2), so the combined OASDI deficit is smaller. Calendar 2025: OASI -$200.0B, DI +$39.8B, combined -$160.2B. Combined reserves were $2.56T at end-2025 versus $2.34T for OASI.
  • "585% since FY2022" is a small-base effect and is not checkable here. It implies an FY2022 deficit of about $33B (internally consistent with $221B and $188B). I could not get SSA's monthly FY2022 series. For reference, calendar 2022 OASI was -$40.7B. A 585% rise from a small base overstates the scale of change; the dollar rise is the meaningful figure. Drivers omitted: the Social Security Fairness Act (Jan 2025, retroactive payments), lower income-tax-on-benefits revenue from the 2025 tax law, and demographics.
  • "Lowest since 2007" is approximately right for OASI: year-end reserves were $2.02T in 2007 and $2.20T in 2008, so $2.18T is the lowest since 2008 (late-2008 level).
  • Trustees and AARP context is accurate. OASI depletion Q4 2032 with 78% of benefits payable; combined 2034 with 83% (2026 Trustees Report). AARP: taxable payroll 41% of GDP in 1983 vs 34% in 2025, $10.8T extra 1984-2034, 2059 (via Newsweek quoting AARP; AARP site blocked to me).
  • Not checkable here: the Kobeissi X post itself (unreachable) and the FY2022 base. The exact FY2026 deficit (221 vs 220) could not be resolved against SSA's monthly table, which returned 403; a $1B difference is within rounding.
  • What would change the rating: if SSA monthly data show an FY2026 OASI deficit materially different from $220-221B, or an FY2022 base far from $33B, the numeric claims would move. "Deteriorating rapidly" is opinion, but supported by the trend (OASI net change -$22B in 2018 to -$200B in 2025).

Step 1: Core deficit, income and cost figures

Answer: Supported, OASI only. Key Values: FY2026 (Oct 2025-Sep 2026) deficit $220-221B; +$39B; costs $1.50T (+$83B, +5.8%); income $1.28T (+$43.5B, +3.5%). Sources: Wolf Street (SSA data). Confidence: medium, secondary source, primary monthly table blocked.

Step 2: Reserves and peak

Answer: Supported for OASI. Key Values: OASI $2.18T Sep 2026; 2017 year-end $2.820T; drop $640B (22.7%). Combined OASDI year-end 2025 $2.561T; combined peak $2.908T (2020). Sources: SSA Tables 4.A1/4.A3; Wolf Street. Confidence: high for history, medium for Sep 2026.

Step 3: 585% claim

Answer: Not checkable here; arithmetic internally consistent. Key Values: implied FY2022 deficit about $33B. Sources: none primary available. Confidence: low.

Step 4: Trustees and AARP

Answer: Supported. Key Values: OASI 2032/78%; OASDI 2034/83%; AARP 41%/34%, $10.8T, 2059. Sources: SSA 2026 Trustees Report; Newsweek quoting AARP. Confidence: high (Trustees), medium (AARP).

Step 5: Misleading framing

Answer: Omitting that the figures are OASI-only, using a percentage from a small base, and presenting a long-projected, Trustee-anticipated shortfall as a sudden "rapid" change are the material framing issues. The article does hedge with "reported". Confidence: medium.
