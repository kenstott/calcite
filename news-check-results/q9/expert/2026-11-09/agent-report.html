Fact-check: Fox45/Baltimore Sun on Mayor Scott and Baltimore's image

One factual error, otherwise sound: 1 Pinocchio

The report (Chevall Pryce, Baltimore Sun, posted on Fox45 Oct. 29, 2026) is largely accurate, but it states that 65% of city residents trust city government never or only sometimes, citing an April 2026 UMBC poll. That 65% is the April 2025 figure; the April 2026 release (n=666, MOE +/-3.8%) says 62%, and trust rose slightly (35% most/always vs 32%). The error also overstates the distrust the article uses as backdrop.

  • Poll items 61% trust local news, 41% right direction, 27% less neighborhood crime (44% same, 21% more per Fishbowl/Fox45 coverage; not found in the Part 1 PDF I read): confirmed or consistent.
  • Homicides 102 vs 108 and nonfatal shootings 259 vs 254 as of Oct. 28: not found in a primary dashboard. Consistent with city statements (83 vs 98 on Oct. 1) and JNS (101 vs 105 and 253 vs 247 about Oct. 20), but the gap narrowed sharply during October, so "down" is accurate yet fragile; shootings flipped from down 4% (Oct. 1) to up.
  • Vacants from over 16,000 to just over 11,000: supported (about 11,813 in April 2026, 11,558 later; count tracks vacant-building notices, with disputed completeness).
  • Downtown lost about $1 billion in value: supported only by secondary reports ($1.07B assessed loss since 2020, not "last year"). Timing in the article ("last year") is not confirmed.
  • Harborplace PILOT awaiting Board of Estimates: consistent with Aug-Sept 2026 reporting. West Lexington PILOT approved for a $160M project: supported by Banner reporting; the project is variously quoted as $160M, $263M or about $300M.
  • Scott's quotes and his blaming of media and counties: not checkable here (no transcript or recording found); matches the headline summary from WBFF only.

Misleading context: the mayor's "falling homicides" claim is true but the 2026 lead eroded from -26% (Aug.) to about -6% (Oct. 28). Poll perception items are opinion, not crime measures. Caveat: if the 65% figure appears in a different UMBC table I did not see, the grade could drop to 0-1.
