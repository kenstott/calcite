## Statistical caveats
- Census starts changes of this size are typically inside the 90% confidence interval. Monthly starts moves of -4.6% or -5.2% are often not statistically distinguishable from zero. I could not read the intervals from the PDF, so I cannot confirm significance either way. The article presents them as definite declines.
- The comparison vintage is the revised August figure (900k), which the article states correctly.
- Rates (weekly, Oct 15) are set against monthly starts (Sept) and the NAHB October survey. Rates and starts moving together does not establish that rates caused the drop. The article's framing ("squeeze") implies causation. Supply costs, tariffs, and labor also matter, and the NAHB itself cites several of them.

## Misleading or sloppy points
1. The first-time-buyer age is attributed to the wrong source, called an average when it is a median, and not flagged as a year-old figure.
2. The headline "takes another hit" frames monthly changes that may be within sampling error as a clear deterioration. The NAHB and mortgage-rate facts, however, do support a weak market.
3. Existing-home sales rose 1.5%, which cuts against the "hit" framing. The article does mention it.

## Grade
1 Pinocchio. Nearly all of the headline numbers are accurate and traceable to Census, NAR, NAHB and Freddie Mac. The deductions are for the misattributed, stale age statistic, the unverified Dietz quote, and the causal framing without any caveat about statistical significance.
