# Question 9 — Verification Record

**Claim:** "The EIA forecasts U.S. crude oil production will average 13.8 million barrels per day (b/d) in 2026, surpassing the previous record of 13.7 million b/d set in 2025, and that marketed natural gas production will average 122.5 billion cubic feet per day (Bcf/d) in 2026, surpassing the previous record of 118.5 Bcf/d set in 2025."

**Check date:** 2026-09-26

---

## Step 1 — Identify the current STEO edition

**Answer:** The current (most recent) STEO edition as of 2026-09-26 is the **September 2026 edition**, released September 9, 2026, with the forecast completed September 3, 2026. No October 2026 edition exists yet (STEO is released monthly, typically first/second week of the month; next release would be early October).

**Key values:** Edition date = Sept 9, 2026; forecast-completed date = Sept 3, 2026.

**Sources:**
- https://eia.gov/outlooks/steo/report/petro_prod.php (states "September 9, 2026 edition, forecast completed September 3, 2026")
- https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/pdf/steo_full.pdf (cover/header text: "September 2026")

**Confidence:** High — directly stated on EIA's own pages.

---

## Step 2 — Crude oil production, current STEO vs. claim

**Answer:** The current STEO's U.S. total crude oil production table gives 2025 = 13.66 million b/d (rounds to 13.7) and 2026 forecast = 13.83 million b/d (rounds to 13.8). This matches the claim exactly, and 2025 is confirmed as a record year (2024 = 13.27 million b/d, lower).

**Key values:** 2025 = 13.66 million b/d; 2026(f) = 13.83 million b/d; 2024 = 13.27 million b/d (for record comparison). n = 1 annual average value per year, derived from 4 quarterly figures each (Q1–Q4).

**Sources:**
- STEO full PDF, petroleum supply table, p.37 ("U.S. total crude oil production (a)" row): 2024=13.27, 2025=13.66, 2026=13.83, 2027=14.26.
- EIA Today in Energy, Sept 10, 2026, https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=68125 — states "We forecast U.S. crude oil production will average 13.8 million barrels per day (b/d) in 2026, surpassing the previous record of 13.7 million b/d set in 2025" — consistent with the live STEO table.

**Confidence:** High. Cross-checked in two independent primary EIA artifacts (PDF table and Today in Energy narrative), both from the current edition, in full agreement.

---

## Step 3 — Marketed natural gas production, current STEO vs. claim

**Answer:** The claim's natural gas numbers (122.5 Bcf/d for 2026, 118.5 Bcf/d for 2025) do **not** match the current (September 2026) STEO. They match the **prior (August 11, 2026) STEO edition** instead. The current STEO revised the 2026 marketed natural gas production forecast upward to **123.0 Bcf/d**. The 2025 figure is unchanged at 118.4 Bcf/d in both editions (claim's "118.5" is a minor rounding discrepancy, not a vintage issue).

**Key values (from EIA's own edition-comparison file, compare.pdf):**
| Edition | 2025 (Bcf/d) | 2026 forecast (Bcf/d) |
|---|---|---|
| August 11, 2026 (previous) | 118.4 | 122.5 |
| September 9, 2026 (current) | 118.4 | 123.0 |

n = 1 annual average per year per edition, each built from 4 quarterly values; 2 editions compared directly against each other via EIA's own official comparison file (not independently recomputed).

**Sources:**
- STEO edition-comparison PDF: https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/pdf/compare.pdf — "Current Forecast: September 9, 2026; Previous Forecast: August 11, 2026," "Total Marketed Production" row: Current 2025=118.4, 2026=123.0; Previous 2025=118.4, 2026=122.5.
- STEO full PDF, natural gas supply table, p.41 ("U.S. total marketed natural gas production" row): 2024=113.1(approx from total supply context), 2025=118.4, 2026=123.0, 2027=127.6 — confirms the current-edition figure independently of the comparison file.
- https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/report/natgas.php (returned dry gas figures, 2025=107.64, 2026=111.70 — a related but distinct series used as a secondary sanity check; dry gas is consistently lower than marketed gas by construction, and the ratio is consistent with the marketed-gas figures above).

**Confidence:** High that the claim's number is stale (directly confirmed via EIA's own official month-over-month comparison document, the most authoritative possible source for "which edition is this number from"). Medium-high on the exact current value given rounding (123.0 vs raw table value; both PDF table and comparison file agree at 123.0, so confidence is high).

---

## Step 4 — Overall verdict

**Answer:** Mostly true. Crude oil figures (13.8M b/d 2026 vs. 13.7M b/d 2025 record) are current and accurate per the live September 2026 STEO. Natural gas figures are directionally correct (2026 will set a new marketed-production record above 2025) but the specific number cited (122.5 Bcf/d) is one STEO edition out of date; the current STEO puts the 2026 forecast at 123.0 Bcf/d, a 0.5 Bcf/d upward revision from the vintage the claim appears to be quoting.

**Confidence:** High overall, based on direct primary-source retrieval and cross-checking of the current STEO PDF against EIA's own official prior-vs-current edition comparison file — no reliance on secondary "Today in Energy" summaries for the gas figures (those were only used for the crude oil corroboration, and were themselves current-edition and correct).
