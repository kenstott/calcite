## Assessment
- **Correct verdict: 1 Pinocchio.** The vote, its procedural nature, the sponsors, the Trump, Jeffries and Cortez Masto statements and the XLU/GRID prints check out. But the article's only quantified consumer-benefit figures (Brattle $35B per decade, Rhodium $50-$78 lower bills in 2040) have no link and no source I could find, and the Rhodium figure does not match anything Rhodium published that I found. The article's own text confirms none of these passages carries a hyperlink. The EEI "supports the bill" line is unconfirmed (EEI's CEO was reported as only "reviewing" and later "appreciating" Congress's work). The headline overstates: the bill assigns data centers of 20 MW or more the incremental grid-infrastructure costs they cause, which is not paying "their own power bills". "The market barely responded" sits oddly beside GRID up about 4.3% and XLU up 1.5% the same day. These are shading and unsourced precision, not fabrication proven, so 1 and not 2. It would rise to 2 if Brattle and Rhodium are confirmed invented.

## Recipe check
Partly. A recipe for news-story fact-checks would help in three ways:
- **Unlinked quantified claims.** Each number attributed to a named study (Brattle, Rhodium) must be traced to the study or marked untraceable, and untraceable items belong in the rating, not in a separate unscored audit. askamerica's 0 came from excluding these.
- **Rating consistency.** If the findings list contains unsupported items and misleading-framing flags, the rating cannot be 0. A recipe step that compares the rating to the flags would have caught this.
- **Price claims.** The warehouse stock table ends May 2026, so the web was the right route. Coverage of the "barely responded" judgment needs the day's move, not only the close.
