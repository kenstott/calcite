# The Hudson County View article's claims about Jersey City's 25% crime decline, the JC IMPACT dashboard and the police leadership change are mostly accurate.

Rating: 1 Pinocchio. Local report: http://127.0.0.1:63768/a/887da752d1258e8133ecc92070e152c2.html (saved at /Users/kennethstott/.askamerica/reports/20261122-073728-the-hudson-county-view-article-s-claims.html). The scores below describe this article only.

## Verdict
Every figure I could check matches the city's own JC IMPACT dashboard (fetched 2026-11-22), and the percentages are computed correctly. The headline claim is looser than it reads. "Nearly 25% decline in overall crime" is an unweighted mean of nine categories the city chose (23.7%). The nine categories' pooled incidents fell 22.8% (4,930 to 3,804). The list omits aggravated assaults (765 YTD, the largest violent category on the dashboard) and shoplifting.

## Findings
- The percentages all check: shots fired -40.4%, vehicle thefts -45.7%, burglaries -34.5%, stolen cars -18.5%, homicides -18.2%, robberies -14.4%, thefts -14.1%, shooting hits -13.8%, sexual assaults -12.8%.
- The simple mean weights 11 to 9 homicides (2 fewer) the same as 316 fewer thefts.
- The "thefts" figure of 1,926 differs from the dashboard Thefts card (1,044). 1,926 equals 1,044 plus 327 stolen bikes plus 555 package thefts, which the card says it already includes. The dashboard's own property total (3,701) is built the same way. The 2025 base of 2,242 could not be verified.
- Dashboard values match the article for homicides (9), shots fired (28), shooting hits (25), robberies (274), burglaries (391), theft from auto (452) and stolen cars (624). Sexual assaults show 74 against the article's 75.
- Omitted context: the administration's own audit found at least 3,251 cases from 2021-2024 improperly entered in NIBRS and said only 2025 is a reliable baseline. The article's "seemingly supporting" line is the reporter's arithmetic, not independent verification. The article does not say aggravated assaults were left out.
- The 3,251-case figure is accurate as an audit finding, which Fulop disputes.
- The leadership change (Ambrose and Kearns leaving; Roldan and Ragland succeeding) and the Traffic Division expansion (10 to 19) are confirmed by the companion articles. The other linked pages were fetched and are consistent with how the article describes them.
- "For the first time" public 24/7 data is Solomon's assertion. I did not verify or grade it separately.
