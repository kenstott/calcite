The Axios article's claims about the House and Senate freshman classes being the largest in decades (article text not read) are mostly accurate.

Rating: 1 Pinocchio.

Important limitation: the Axios page returned HTTP 403 and no syndicated copy of that story loaded, so I did not read its text. I checked only the headline thesis and the claims that follow from it, using the outlets and datasets below. Nothing else in the piece was checked.

Warehouse check: officials.members covers Congresses 117-119 only. It has no 120th Congress rows, so the class sizes could not be computed there, and I verified them externally.

Claims
1. Central: both the House and Senate freshman classes are the largest in decades. Mostly true.
2. Nineteen new senators, the largest Senate class in almost five decades. True.
   - CBS News reports 19 new senators.
   - The Wilson Center / Vital Statistics table shows 20 freshman senators in 1979, 18 in 1981 and 17 in 1977, so 19 is the most since 1979, about 47 years.
   - The count depends on definition. The Wikipedia list shows 17 taking office Jan 3, while CBS adds Sherrod Brown and Kevin Hern, who take their seats earlier.
3. The House class is the biggest since the 1992 election brought 108. Partially false as stated.
   - Cronkite News reports 105 chips at the Nov 17 lottery. That is below 1992's 108.
   - House History puts the 1993 class at 108 pre-convening, or 116 including post-convening members, and the 2011 class at 89 pre-convening, or 99 total.
   - Cronkite News itself says the class is the biggest since 1992 and may be the biggest since 1922 (117) once pending contests are settled.
   - One tracker counts 109 or more. The "since 1992" claim holds only if the final count reaches 109 or more.

Framing caveats: the House figure is still moving while contests are pending, and "freshman" counts differ by source (pre-convening versus total, 105 versus 109 or 110). The Senate claim is solid. The House claim is directionally right but depends on the final tally.
