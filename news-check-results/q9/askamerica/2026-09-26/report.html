Crude oil claim matches EIA's September 2026 STEO exactly; natural gas figures are one release-cycle stale

Checked against EIA's own September 2026 Short-Term Energy Outlook (primary source) and askamerica's energy schema actuals

Summary

The crude-oil half of the claim is accurate: EIA's own September 2026 Short-Term Energy Outlook (STEO), read directly from its Table 4a, forecasts 2026 U.S. crude oil production at 13.83 million b/d (rounds to 13.8) versus a 2025 average of 13.66 million b/d (rounds to 13.7) — exactly matching the claim and EIA's own September 9, 2026 press release.

The natural-gas half is one release-cycle stale: the numbers in the claim (122.5 Bcf/d for 2026, 118.5 Bcf/d for 2025) are EIA's AUGUST 2026 STEO figures, not the September 2026 STEO the article cites. The actual September 2026 STEO (Table 5a) revised 2026 marketed natural gas production up to 123.0 Bcf/d and the 2025 figure to 118.4 Bcf/d. The direction of the claim (2026 sets a new record above 2025) still holds, and the magnitude is close (within 0.5 Bcf/d, under 0.5%), but the specific cited numbers do not match the vintage of report attributed to them.

Step 1 — Which tables can speak to this

askamerica's energy schema carries EIA API v2 actual production data — energy.eia_crude_oil_production (petroleum/crd/crpdn) and energy.eia_natural_gas_production (natural-gas/prod/sum, filtered to process_code='VGM' for 'Marketed Production' at the national area code 'NUS'). These tables carry only realized monthly production through the data's observed coverage (crude oil and gas both loaded through June 2026 at the time of this check), not EIA's STEO forecast series itself. So this corpus can verify (a) whether the claimed 2025 'record' figures match actual production, and (b) whether the 2026 actual-to-date trend is consistent with the forecast direction/magnitude — but the forecast numbers themselves (13.8 mb/d, 122.5 Bcf/d) had to be checked against EIA's own STEO report directly, which was fetched as a primary source.

Step 2 — Computed actuals from askamerica

Crude oil (query: SELECT production_year, AVG(production_volume) FROM energy.eia_crude_oil_production WHERE eia_area_code='NUS' AND fuel_type='Crude Oil' AND production_unit='MBBL/D' GROUP BY production_year):

  • 2024 full year: 13,267 MBBL/D (13.27 mb/d)
  • 2025 full year: 13,660 MBBL/D (13.66 mb/d — matches STEO's 2025 historical value and rounds to the claim's cited 13.7 record)
  • 2026 Jan–Jun (only months loaded): 13,712 MBBL/D (13.71 mb/d) — already running above the 2025 full-year average, consistent with a full-year 2026 landing near 13.8

Marketed natural gas (query: summed monthly MMCF from energy.eia_natural_gas_production where eia_area_code='NUS' AND process_code='VGM', converted to Bcf/d by dividing by ~30.4 days/month):

  • 2024 full year: ~113.3 Bcf/d
  • 2025 full year: ~118.3 Bcf/d (closely matches both STEO vintages' 2025 figure of 118.4–118.5)
  • 2026 Jan–Jun: ~121.6 Bcf/d, trending toward, but below, either STEO's full-year 2026 forecast (122.5 or 123.0) since production typically continues rising through the year

Step 3 — Verifying the forecast numbers against the primary source

Because the actual data tables don't carry STEO's own forecast series, the September 2026 STEO PDF (steo_full.pdf, completed by EIA on September 3, 2026) was fetched and read directly:

  • Table 4a (U.S. Petroleum and Other Liquids Supply): annual 'U.S. total crude oil production' = 13.66 (2025), 13.83 (2026), 14.26 (2027) million b/d. This matches the claim (13.7 / 13.8) and matches EIA's own September 9, 2026 press release table exactly.
  • Table 5a (U.S. Natural Gas Supply): annual 'U.S. total marketed natural gas production' = 118.4 (2025), 123.0 (2026), 127.6 (2027) Bcf/d — NOT 118.5 / 122.5 as the claim states.

Tracing the 122.5 / 118.5 figures: they appear verbatim in EIA's 'Today in Energy' article published August 12, 2026 ('United States on track for record natural gas production in 2026'), which explicitly attributes them to the AUGUST 2026 STEO, not the September edition. Between August and September, EIA revised the 2026 marketed natural gas forecast up by 0.5 Bcf/d and the 2025 figure down by 0.1 Bcf/d.

Step 4 — Verdict

Crude oil claim: TRUE. Confirmed directly against the primary source named in the article (September 2026 STEO), and cross-checked against askamerica's own production actuals.

Natural gas claim: STALE VINTAGE. The direction (2026 record exceeding 2025) and approximate magnitude are correct, and the numbers are real EIA STEO output — but they are the prior month's (August) forecast, not the September 2026 STEO the article cites as its source. The September STEO's actual numbers are 123.0 Bcf/d (2026) and 118.4 Bcf/d (2025), each about 0.1–0.5 Bcf/d different from what's quoted.
