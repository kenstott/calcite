The WTKR article's claims about Hampton Roads bridges, air travel and congestion are somewhat biased and mostly accurate.

**Rating: 1 Pinocchio.**

Source audit: the article relays HRTPO's "State of Transportation in Hampton Roads" (2025 edition). I read the report itself (73 pages) and checked the key figures against the warehouse.

| Claim | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 2.7% of bridges in poor condition, vs more than 5% in 2017 | True | transport.bridges (FHWA NBI): 34 of 1,271 bridges in 16 localities = 2.68% in 2025; 62 of 1,240 = 5.0% in 2017. HRTPO p.22 says 34 (2.7%) vs 69 (5.5%). |
| Roughly 1,300 bridges | True | 1,271 primary NBI records. |
| Air travel up 46% 2015-2024 (ORF and PHF) | True | transport.t100_segments: 1,733,373 to 2,541,047 domestic departing passengers = +46.6%. HRTPO p.11 also gives +46%. |
| 17 hours lost to congestion in 2023 | Mostly true | HRTPO p.32 (TomTom) gives 17 hours for 2024, not 2023. |
| Fatalities higher than a decade ago, with some improvement | True | HRTPO p.37: 153 in 2024, down from the 2021 record of 179, still up 26% over the decade. |
| Third daily Amtrak train added | Mostly true | HRTPO p.20 says the third Norfolk-DC train will be temporarily replaced by a bus during construction. |
| International flights "like Cancun" | Mostly true | Breeze's weekly Cancun service starts January 2026, one destination (HRTPO p.14). |
| Nichols quote: traffic is "about 4% lower than prior to the pandemic" | Partially false | HRTPO pp.26-28 say vehicle-miles traveled has returned to pre-pandemic levels (VDOT) and per-capita VMT is -0.4% vs 2019. Count-station volumes fell 0.6% in 2023-24, which also conflicts with "increasing each year". No 4% roadway figure appears in the report. |

**Misleading framing**
- The headline says air travel "surges in 2026". All data end in 2024, and 2026 is the report's publication year.
- The 46% growth is entirely Norfolk (ORF). PHF departures fell from 202,509 to 69,342 (T-100), while ORF rose about 61%. The article blends the two airports.
- The article omits that the fatality rate is up 20% over the decade, and that the poor-bridge share ranks Hampton Roads 32nd among 41 comparable metros.
- Not checked against a primary source: the "state of good repair" program history and the new arrivals terminal construction. Whether the quotes were actually spoken was not tested.

No warehouse table carries regional VMT or the TomTom congestion measure, so those claims were graded from the HRTPO report. The bridge and air figures were computed from AskAmerica tables (transport.bridges, transport.t100_segments).
