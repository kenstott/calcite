My own assessment: about 3 Pinocchios on the Washington Post scale. The engine's scoring left its rating blank, and the 3 is my judgment, not an engine score.

Article: WDBO/WFTV, "Traffic fatalities drop significantly across Florida and the nation" (2026-11-25).

- National claims are TRUE. The article says 16,750 deaths in H1 2026 against 17,175 in H1 2025 (-2.5%), with vehicle miles traveled up 0.8%. NHTSA DOT HS 813 875 says the same. These are estimates, and I read the report directly.
- The Florida claim is FALSE or misleading. The article says 2,155 deaths this year against nearly 3,000 last year, "834 fewer." NHTSA's own state table gives Florida 1,474 in H1 2025 and 1,439 in H1 2026, a 2.4% drop of about 35 deaths.
  - The article implies a roughly 28% drop. It appears to compare a year-to-date count with a full prior year.
  - I could not locate a source for 2,155.
  - The warehouse (FARS) shows Florida at 3,548 in 2022, 3,375 in 2023 and 3,138 in 2024. 2025 is not loaded.
- The Central Florida claim is UNSUPPORTED. The article says 550 deaths in the six counties in 2025 and 375 in 2026 through Nov 14. FARS for those counties shows 487 in 2024, and no 2025 or 2026 county source was found. This looks like the same partial-year against full-year mismatch.
- The "significant" Florida decline framing is misleading, since NHTSA's own state estimate shows only about a 2% drop. The comparison is not like-for-like.

The national facts check out. The Florida headline numbers overstate the decline roughly tenfold against NHTSA's own state estimate.
