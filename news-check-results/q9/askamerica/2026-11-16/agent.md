The Newsweek article's claims about Mamdani's SNAP deal with Trump, the USDA retailer stocking rule and SNAP enrollment are very biased and mostly accurate.

**Rating: 1 Pinocchio.**

Report: /Volumes/main/Users/kennethstott/IdeaProjects/calcite/news-check-results/q9/askamerica/2026-11-16/report.html

**What checks out**
- Mamdani negotiated the nationwide delay, and the White House and USDA confirmed it (NYC Mayor's Office, amNY, Politico via Yahoo).
- The delay is 90 days ("at least"). Mamdani first asked for six months, and USDA first refused.
- The rule's details match amNY and law-firm summaries: 7 varieties in each of 4 categories, up from 3. Perishables are required in 3 categories, up from 2, with 3 units each. 7 x 4 x 3 = 84 items. Butter, jerky, snack bars and fruit spreads no longer count. Specialty stores are exempt.
- "More than 36 million rely on SNAP" is true. The warehouse (`fiscal.snap_benefits_by_geography`) shows 36.09M persons in July 2026, the latest loaded month. That matches the 36.1M from the Center on Budget and Policy Priorities (CBPP) in Newsweek's own sibling article.
  - My query left out the fiscal-year "Total" rows (month IS NULL). That excluded FY2026 average is 38.04M. The claim holds on monthly data, not on that average.
- Florida's restrictions began April 20, 2026 (WLRN). The February Sunnyside Yard pitch ($21B, 12,000 homes) and the September Gracie Mansion meeting are confirmed (CBS NY, The Real Deal).

**What is misleading**
1. **Framing.** The section heading ("Deal to Delay SNAP Cuts") and the URL slug ("snap-benefits-cuts") call a retailer stocking rule a "cut". The article's own text says the rule does not cut benefit amounts. The actual benefit cuts, the work requirements, are separate and untouched by the deal. I graded the heading mostly false.
2. **Nov 4 as a cliff.** Nov 4 was the compliance date, and the rule took effect July 7. USDA told amNY that compliance is assessed at authorization or reauthorization, generally every five years. Stores were not set to lose SNAP on Nov 4. Newsweek omits this, and so does Mamdani's "risked losing" quote.
3. **Trimmed USDA quote.** The statement opened by blaming the convenience-store lobby for "spending millions" fighting the policy. Newsweek dropped that.
4. **Section label.** The section "Changes Coming Under USDA Proposal" calls a final rule a proposal.

**Unverified**
- The "more than 250,000 retailers" figure is corroborated only by trade press (Grocery Dive: more than a quarter million). I did not get the Food and Nutrition Service (FNS) retailer count.
- usda.gov returned HTTP 403 to both fetch tools. The rule details come from amNY, the NYC Mayor's Office release and law-firm summaries surfaced by search, not the USDA text.
