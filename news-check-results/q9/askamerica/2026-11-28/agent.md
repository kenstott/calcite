**The Brownfield Ag News article's claims about NOPA's October soybean crush, the 27% four-year gain and RVOs are too few to rate.**

Article: https://brownfieldagnews.com/news/nopa-reports-record-october-soybean-crush/ (Nov 25, 2026, single-source interview with NOPA CEO Devin Mogler)

**Pinocchio rating (my judgment, not an engine score): 1 Pinocchio.** The core facts check out; deductions are for an unsourced statistic, the unverified "all-time" scope, and one-sided framing. The engine's scorer returned too few claims to rate, so the rating is mine.

Claims
1. "234 million bushels... all-time record": true. OleoScope (citing NOPA, Nov 18, 2026) gives 234.2 mbu, a record, up 19% from September (196.3 mbu). Oct 2025 was 227.65 mbu. Not confirmed against NOPA's own release. "All-time across all months" was not independently checked. A government series for 2026 was not found.
2. "27% increase vs four years ago": true. Oct 2022 NOPA was 184.464 mbu, and 234.19/184.464 = +27.0%.
3. Slow western Corn Belt harvest hurt September: mostly true. Warehouse (ag.nass_crop_progress): US soybeans were 17% harvested by Sep 27, 2026, vs 19% in 2025 and 26% in 2024. This is modestly behind, but I did not test the western Corn Belt specifically or the effect on crush.
4. EPA expanded RVOs earlier this year: true. EPA finalized RFS "Set 2" on Mar 26, 2026, the highest volumes in program history (EPA release read).
5. 10% of the distillate pool is bio-based diesel: unsupported. No source found. The interview audio (mp3) could not be parsed as text.

Warehouse: no NOPA crush table (search_catalog, unmatched terms: nopa, crush). The only warehouse figure used was the NASS harvest progress.

Misleading context
- The piece relays the trade association's own figures from its CEO without independent confirmation.
- "27%" uses a low 2022 base. Growth versus October 2025 is only +2.9%.
- "Further proof the industry can meet RVOs" is advocacy. One record month does not establish capacity.
- USDA NASS crush is a different, larger series (237.06 mbu for Oct 2025) and should not be compared directly with NOPA figures.
