The CNBC article's claims about the frozen housing market, mortgage rates and permits are balanced and mostly accurate; 2 claims could not be confirmed.

**Rating: 1 Pinocchio.**

**Method.** CNBC fetched fine. I checked the claims against AskAmerica's warehouse first (econ.fred_indicators) and then against primary releases. I also fetched the CNBC pages the article links.

**Verified true**
- October existing-home sales fell to a 16-year low. Warehouse: 3.75M SAAR, down from 3.97M in September. NAR calls it the lowest since August 2010.
- Pending sales hit a record low on NAR's index. NAR cites two consecutive months of record-low pending contracts.
- Prices are up well over 50% from pre-pandemic. Case-Shiller rose from 215.0 (Jan 2020) to 334.2 (Aug 2026), about +55%.
- Builder sentiment is the lowest since April 2012 (NAHB index at 25).
- Cotality's housing turnover rate is 3.8% against 5.0% for 2011-2019.
- Median homeowner net worth is about 43 times renters' ($458,900 vs $10,600, 2025 Survey of Consumer Finances, via NAHB).
- Redfin's active buyers fell for a sixth straight month, to the lowest in its data since 2013.

**Mostly true**
- The 6.60% to 7.61% rate move is a Mortgage News Daily figure. MND daily readings were 7.54% on Oct 1 and 7.56% on Oct 6, and 7.61% appears only as a range figure. Freddie Mac's weekly rate rose about 0.7 to 0.8 points (6.58% on Jul 23 to 7.28% on Oct 1, peak 7.40%). "A full percentage point" is therefore the high end. I could not confirm MND's July level.
- "Nearly $35 trillion" in housing wealth: Cotality's report gives about $34 trillion in homeowner equity. I did not test the "record" part.

**Partially false**
- "Single-family building permits down 13% y/y." CNBC's own linked report says total building permits fell 12.9% y/y and 8.7% m/m. Its single-family figure is for starts (-3.2% y/y). The warehouse shows total permits at -8.7% y/y on the current vintage (1,295 vs 1,418). The 13% looks like the all-permits figure with the wrong label.

**Unsupported (not shown to be wrong)**
- The mobility rate of 8.3%, down from 11.1%, was not found in any page I read. Harvard's household mobility rate from the American Community Survey is 11.2% for 2024. The article also defines the rate as "region to region", but it counts all residential moves.
- Mortgage-industry jobs "down 38% since Q2 2021" (MBA). MBA's own chart, using BLS data, shows -36% through Q2 2025. I did not find a later MBA update.
- The BLS job-loss counts (about 2,000 real estate and leasing jobs, 4,400 residential construction jobs) were not verified because the warehouse has no matching BLS series. The report lists them as not checked rather than claims.

**Misleading framing.** The headline rate shock rests on one private index (MND) whose October peak runs above Freddie Mac's, so the move looks larger than the standard weekly series shows. The permits label overstates what the single-family data show. Neither changes the main story: October sales, pending contracts and builder sentiment are all at or near multi-year lows. Interviewee anecdotes (Deighan, Welch, Bull, Banosian) cannot be checked.

**Limits.** One warehouse query for pre-2026 existing-home sales failed on a storage error. The "lowest since 2010" ranking therefore rests on NAR's release, not my own series check. Three linked CNBC pages (the Oct 20 housing article, the Zillow video page, the Nov 18 mortgage-demand article) loaded but did not contain the figures checked here.
