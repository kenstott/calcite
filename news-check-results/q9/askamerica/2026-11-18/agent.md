The Benzinga article's claims about Social Security's record FY2026 trust fund deficit, reserves and depletion dates are mostly accurate.

**Rating: 1 Pinocchio.**

Article text: Benzinga returned 403. I read the Yahoo Finance syndication of the same story (Benzinga, Nov 17, 2026).

**Claims checked**
1. Deficit up $39B to a record $221B in FY2026 (Kobeissi Letter, X post; central). Graded true, consistent. CRS gives OASI calendar-year balances of -$103B (2024), -$200B (2025) and -$243B projected (2026). The fiscal-year figure itself was not confirmed because Treasury and SSA pages returned 403/404.
2. Reserves $2.18T, lowest since 2007. Graded true. OASI reserves were $2.02T at end-2007 and $2.20T at end-2008 (SSA historical table via search snippet), and $2.34T at end-2025 (CRS).
3. Trustees: OASI depleted 2032, 78% payable. True. Warehouse: econ.social_security_trustees_projections, 2026 report.
4. Trustees: OASDI depleted 2034, 83% payable. True, from the same table.
5. AARP: payroll-taxed wages fell from 41% of GDP (1983) to 34% (2025). True per secondary coverage of the AARP report. The AARP page returned 403, and one source dates the 41% to 1984.
6. AARP: $10.8T extra revenue 1984-2034, trust fund life to 2059. True per Newsweek and PlanAdviser summaries. Not read from the AARP primary.
7. Headline framing of "the fund" and "deteriorating rapidly" (author claim, central). Mostly true, with one flaw. The $221B is the OASI fund's annual shortfall, not all of Social Security. CRFB puts the combined 2026 cash deficit at $270B. Trustees depletion dates are unchanged at 2032 (OASI) and 2034 (OASDI).

**Materially misleading or missing context**
- The headline and lede say "Social Security fund" when the figure covers only OASI.
- The deficit is a drawdown of reserves still around $2.2T, not debt. The piece does not say that depletion dates did not move.
- A single X post is presented as "breaking" news, and the "deteriorating rapidly" quote is opinion.
- Calendar-year and fiscal-year bases differ, and the piece does not flag that.

**Not verified**
- +585% since FY2022.
- $83B rise in costs and $44B rise in income.
- $640B drop from the 2017 peak.
- Fiscal-year-end OASI reserves from the Treasury Monthly Treasury Statement.
