The WGME article's claims about Portland's warm start to November and Maine drought are biased and mostly accurate; 1 claim could not be confirmed.

Rating: 1 Pinocchio.

Report: http://127.0.0.1:57787/a/81154c83a0f1b06ac1f0470c104703f4.html (report_id 20261117-124816-the-wgme-article-s-claims-about-portland)

Findings (ACIS threaded Portland record PWMthr 1874-2026; warehouse GHCND 2010-2025; USDM county table, week of 2026-11-10):
- True: 48.0 F average for Nov 1-15; 73 F on Nov 4; 57 F average high; 1.13 in precipitation; 2022 warmest at 50.6 F; unusually dry October (Portland 1.67 in).
- Rank is off. The URL says second-warmest and the title says third. On the full record 2022 (50.60), 1938 (50.17) and 1975 (48.23) are warmer; 2026 ties 1969 at 48.00, so tied fourth. It is third only on a post-1941 record.
- Misleading framing: 1.13 in is set beside a full-month 4.25 in normal. The Nov 1-15 normal is about 2.0 in (1991-2020), so to-date is roughly 56% of normal, not 27%. Measurable-precip days were 5 (two more were trace), not six.
- "Nearly every day warmer than average" is an overstatement: Nov 1 and Nov 12-15 were at or below normal, so the warmth was front-loaded.
- Drought: USDM Nov 10 shows extreme drought (D3) over 82% of Lincoln, 70% of Knox, 68% of Sagadahoc and 19% of Aroostook counties; severe (D2) in York and Aroostook. Mostly true.
- Unverified: the CPC outlook claim (map image unreadable). The polar-vortex remark is a hedged forecast, not checkable.
- Caveat: a first drought query joined to geo.counties and fanned out; I discarded it and used the single-table query.
