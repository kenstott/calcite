Fact Check: 2026 Measles Cases and Kindergarten MMR Coverage

Checked 2026-09-26 against CDC primary sources, cross-checked with independent secondary reporting.
VERDICT: TRUE — both figures confirmed against CDC's own data

The claim

"The CDC reports 3,659 confirmed measles cases in the United States in 2026 (as of September 24, 2026), and MMR vaccination coverage among U.S. kindergartners has decreased from 95.2% during the 2019–2020 school year to 92.4% in the 2025–2026 school year."

1. Measles case count

CDC's own measles data-and-research page (updated September 25, 2026, reflecting counts through Wednesday, September 24) reports 3,659 confirmed measles cases for 2026. This matches the claim exactly.

Methodology note (what we checked, not just the label): CDC explicitly states this figure is confirmed cases only — "CDC is aware of probable measles cases being reported by jurisdictions. However, the data on this page only includes confirmed cases." We found no indication CDC changed its confirmed/probable case definition during 2026, so the count is internally comparable across the weeks reported this year. CDC does flag that "2025–2026 case counts are preliminary and subject to change" (routine backfill/revision, not a definitional shift).

Cross-checked against two independent secondary sources reporting the same CDC feed: Contagion Live's September 25, 2026 update and news coverage of CDC's first 2026 measles death confirmation, both citing the same 3,659/September 24 figure.

2. Kindergarten MMR coverage: 2019–20 vs. 2025–26

CDC's SchoolVaxView / MMWR national kindergarten vaccination coverage series confirms both endpoints in the claim:

U.S. kindergarten MMR coverage, school years 2019–20 through 2025–26. Source: CDC SchoolVaxView / MMWR.

The decline is monotonic across all seven years — not a two-point comparison masking a rebound — and every year from 2021–22 onward sits below the ~93–95% coverage generally considered necessary for measles herd immunity.

Comparability caveat (observed, not inferred): CDC's 2019–20 report itself flags limitations on cross-year comparability: state-level differences in vaccine requirements, data-collection methods, exemption policy, and assessment timing (school-year start through April 30), plus COVID-19-related disruptions to the 2019–20 assessment itself. These are the standard caveats CDC attaches to this series every year, not a data problem specific to this claim — the reported national topline numbers are still what's cited as "the" CDC estimate for each year and are what the claim quotes correctly.

Bottom line

Both figures in the claim match CDC's own published numbers, retrieved directly from CDC's measles data page and CDC's MMWR/SchoolVaxView kindergarten vaccination series, and independently corroborated by multiple news outlets summarizing the same CDC releases (Axios, CNBC, KFF, The Hill, Forbes). No methodology shift, mid-year redefinition, or column-name/value mismatch was found in either series that would undermine the comparison.
