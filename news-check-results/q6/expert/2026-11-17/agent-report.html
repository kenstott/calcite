Mostly accurate, with a wrong rank in the title tag: 1 Pinocchio

Summary of findings

The WGME piece (Hannah Sheehy, Nov 16, 2026) rates 1 Pinocchio out of 4. Its core numbers check out against NOAA data: Portland averaged 48.0 F for Nov 1-15, 2026, average highs were 56.7 F against a 51.2 F normal, the Nov 4 peak was 73 F, and precipitation was 1.13 in. The drought paragraph matches the US Drought Monitor map valid Nov 10, 2026.

  • Rank is soft. 2026 ties 1969 at exactly 48.00 F. That is tied 3rd/4th on the post-1941 record and tied 4th/5th on the full 1871-2026 record, where 1938 (50.17 F) ranks second. The URL slug and HTML title tag still say "second-warmest", which no record supports; the displayed headline and body say "third".
  • "Nearly every day warmer than average" is overstated. 11 of 15 days were above the NWS 1991-2020 normal; Nov 1 and Nov 12-14 were below.
  • "Six days" of measurable rain does not reproduce. I count 5 days with at least 0.01 in (plus 2 trace days).
  • Misleading framing, not false. The article sets 1.13 in against the whole-month 4.25 in normal. The to-date normal is 2.07 in, so the deficit is about 0.9 in (55% of normal), not the 27% that the pairing suggests. The "typical" 43.1 F average also matches a recent 20-year mean (43.2 F), not the NWS 30-year normal (42.6 F); the base period is undisclosed.
  • Not checkable here: the polar vortex, Thanksgiving-week and "active stretch" statements are hedged forecasts or undefined.
  • What would change this: a different station record or tie-break moves the rank; Nov 1-16 observations are preliminary. My drought percentages are my own overlay of USDM polygons on Census 20m county outlines (about 2-3 points of coastal error), not an official USDM statistic.

Step 1: Temperature claims

Answer: Supported, with the rank caveat above.

Key Values: Nov 1-15, 2026 mean of daily (max+min)/2 = 48.00 F (n=15 days); 2022 = 50.60 F; 1975 = 48.23; 1969 = 48.00; 1938 = 50.17. Record n=152 Novembers (1871-2026; 1871-73 and 1940 incomplete).

Sources: NOAA RCC-ACIS StnData, station PWMthr (Portland Area threaded record); ACIS 1991-2020 normals.

Confidence: High for values; medium for rank because of the exact tie and the record choice.

Step 2: Precipitation claims

Answer: Totals supported; "six days" off; comparison framing misleading.

Key Values: Nov 1-15, 2026 = 1.13 in; 5 days of 0.01 in or more; trace on Nov 3 and 12; 1991-2020 mean for Nov 1-15 = 2.02 in (NWS daily normals 2.07 in); November mean 4.25 in (n=30 years); October 2026 = 1.67 in vs 5.25 in normal, 23rd driest of 155.

Sources: NOAA RCC-ACIS PWMthr.

Confidence: High.

Step 3: Drought claims

Answer: Supported. The Nov 12 release (data valid Nov 10) was the latest when the article ran.

Key Values (% of Maine land area, own overlay): D1+ 96.4 (Oct 27), 96.4 (Nov 3), 89.4 (Nov 10); D2+ 53.4, 57.9, 49.1; D3 3.5, 8.2, 7.7. Downeast: Washington D1+ 93 to 43%, Hancock 79 to 46%.

Sources: US Drought Monitor shapefiles USDM_20261027, 20261103, 20261110; Census cb_2023_us_county_20m. The USDM statistics API returned empty arrays, so the overlay is mine.

Confidence: Medium-high; qualitative statements are clear, percentages are approximate.

Step 4: Outlook claims

Answer: Temperature outlook supported; precipitation "near-average" is a fair but loose reading.

Key Values: CPC 8-14 day (Nov 24-30, issued Nov 16): temperature Above at 50% over Maine; precipitation Above at the 33% tier. The 8-14 day product itself ends Nov 30.
