Mostly accurate: one Pinocchio for the Inside Climate News land-sale report

Summary of findings: 1 Pinocchio, with several imprecise details

Of 26 claims and quotes extracted, 11 verified accurate, 7 verified with imprecision, and 8 could not be checked here. No claim was found false. The core story holds: Interior's FY2026-2030 plan says it will "decrease the size of the federal estate" by offering lands to states, localities and Tribes, and it stops using the LWCF to expand holdings.

  • Judge overstated: the Boulder City halt was a stay by an Interior Board of Land Appeals administrative judge (Sept 1, 2026), preliminary, not a federal court ruling.
  • Pitkin County permit: county commissioners approved the permit Sept 23; Forest Service/USDA sign-off was reported pending, and the Forest Service keeps ownership. Calling it a precursor to land transfer is critics' opinion; it is a management permit.
  • 2,500-acre rule: the statute requires notice to Congress and a 90-day wait with congressional disapproval power, not affirmative approval.
  • Numbers: 3.2 billion offshore acres is not supported (published figures about 1.7 billion); 17% workforce loss is gross, with OPM net near 9% through Nov 2025; Boulder site acreage reported 80, 81 or 88.5.
  • Misleading by framing: the headline's "untold acres" is accurate only because the plan names no acreage or parcels; AP says officials gave no details. The plan does not bind successors and does not name data centers, as the article says. Sales to state/local/tribal buyers are not sales to industry; resale is a critics' prediction.
  • Could not check: the primary plan PDF returned 404 for me, so plan quotes were confirmed via AP, Outdoor Life and E&E only; the Alaska 28 million acre claim, Interior non-response, and the Groetzinger/O'Neill quotes were not independently verifiable. If the plan text differs from those reports, plan-quote grades would change.
