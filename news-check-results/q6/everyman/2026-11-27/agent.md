## What the primary and secondary sources say about the underlying story
Sources: KFF quick insight (Nov 14, 2026), Georgetown CCF explainer, Fierce Healthcare, Route Fifty, Healthcare Dive, Stateline (all found via search).

- On Nov 13, 2026, CMS (letter SMD #26-006) offered states an optional "hold harmless" delay in enforcing the Medicaid work requirement. It runs to June 30, 2027. Confirmed by KFF and others.
- States must notify CMS by Dec 11, 2026 to opt in. Confirmed.
- The statutory start date was Jan 1, 2027. Confirmed.
- During the delay, states must still check work and exemption status. They pause denials and disenrollments of otherwise eligible people. They must keep working toward full compliance. Confirmed, per KFF.
- A late-October Massachusetts federal court ruling vacated the medical-frailty regulations. Reported by secondary sources only.
- Wyden and Pallone called the delay a "small step in the right direction, but it does not avert the disaster ahead." Reported by search summaries only. The wording is approximate.
- The delay is optional and applies only to states that opt in. KFF gives no state count. Stateline reports states still face challenges. A 26-state lawsuit was reported by search summary only.
- The separate statutory good-faith exemption allows delays up to Dec 31, 2028. The rule caps an initial exemption at six months, and CMS has not granted any.

## Possible misleading framings to watch for
These are risks in any headline like "states delay work rule cuts to 2027". I could not check whether this article commits them.
- The delay is not automatic or universal.
- Nobody's eligibility checks stop during the delay. Only the loss of coverage is paused.
- Calling it a "cut delay" could blur the difference between the January 2027 statutory date and the June 30, 2027 hold-harmless end date.

## Verdict
Article accuracy: not rated, because the text was inaccessible. The underlying event is real and its main dates are corroborated.
