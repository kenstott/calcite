# Fact-check: Inside Climate News, "California endures historic coastal flooding, waves over Thanksgiving" (2026-11-26)

Limits: WebFetch returned only a summary of the article, not verbatim text. Quotes could not be checked word for word. NOAA's tide page returned no data. Python was not available, so there is no chart.

## Claims and verdicts
| Claim | Verdict | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| King tides plus a strong El Nino caused flooding in SoCal and the Bay Area | Supported in general | Fox Weather, AP, PPIC and Fast Company describe record king-tide flooding on the California coast in late October 2026. PPIC links high tides to a strong El Nino. |
| A Kelvin wave added to sea level | Weakly supported | Only Fixr mentions Kelvin waves, and Fixr is a low-quality source. Not confirmed by NOAA or the NWS. |
| Thanksgiving-week tides were extreme | Forecast confirmed, outcome not | Fast Company forecast a Nov 22-28 round. I found no post-event report. |
| Pacifica lost part of its pier | Not checkable here | No source found. |
| San Diego closed boardwalks; waves hit La Jolla houses | Not checkable here | No source found. |
| LA tide peaked at 8.19 ft, matching last month's record | Not checkable here | NOAA station data was not retrievable. A tied record is plausible given the October coverage. |
| Swain: overlap of king tides and a climate-amplified super El Nino, not storms | Plausible | His field fits the quote, but the quote is unverified. Coverage does say the cause was tides, not an atmospheric river. |
| Lucey-Renteria (Cal State LA): December worse, January similar | Opinion or forecast | Fast Company's Dec 22-26 round is consistent with it. |
| Long Beach Naples: 90-year-old seawalls, $125M retrofit slowed by falling oil revenue | Not checkable here | Not verified. |
| Newport Beach raised about 70 seawalls; stormwater crews grew from about 20 to about 60 | Not checkable here | Not verified. |
| Craig Auger: "a lot of infrastructure would have to be rebuilt" | Not checkable here | Not verified. |

## Rating
Provisional 1 Pinocchio, with low confidence. The central story matches independent reporting. Most specific figures and quotes could not be verified.

## Possibly misleading
- "Climate-amplified super El Nino" attributes the event to climate change without a cited attribution study. Expert opinion is presented as established.
- Calling it "historic" depends on the unverified record claim. A tied record is not a new one.
- The article reads as if a Kelvin wave was a measured contributor. Public sources barely mention it.
