# Fact-check: TPR "One in three Texas students leaves college without a degree, new study says" (2026-11-18)

Piece: Texas Tribune (Sneha Dey) story republished by TPR. Study: Philanthropy Advocates + Jacob Kirksey (Texas Tech), "Stopouts" report.
Limits: the report PDF exceeded the fetch size limit, so I could not read it directly. Checks rely on the Tribune text and Fox 4's coverage of the report. WebFetch returns model summaries, not verbatim text.

## Rating: 1 Pinocchio (minor shading)
The core numbers match the study as reported elsewhere. Concerns:
- "Average" versus "median" for the $6,250 figure.
- The "nearly 30% had debt" figure appears inconsistent with Fox 4's borrower count and was not confirmed.
- The "just above 1.0" GPA wording differs from "half had 1.0 or lower".
- The debt window ("last five years") is ambiguous.
- The headline's "one in three" understates 38%.

## Materially misleading?
- The $1.1B debt is a total of about 331k borrowers. It says nothing about the per-student burden, which is small (about $6k median).
- The wage gap is a correlation between completers and non-completers. It is not causal, since the groups differ in preparation and background. Non-completers still earned about $2,700 more than people who never enrolled (per Fox 4).
- The piece does not disclose that the study's sponsors are advocates. It does disclose Texas Tech and Philanthropy Advocates as Tribune funders.
