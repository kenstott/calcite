# Fact-check: "Federal Government Could Sell Off Untold Acres of Public Lands" (Inside Climate News, Lisa Sorg, 2026-11-13)

Limitation: WebFetch returned only a condensed summary of the article, not verbatim text, so I checked the claims in that summary rather than every sentence. Primary-source checks were done through search snippets and news coverage. I did not open the Interior plan PDF (doi.gov/sites/default/files/documents/2026-11/doi-strategic-plan-2026-11-10.pdf), though search confirmed it exists.

## Overall rating: 1 Pinocchio (leaning accurate, with caveats)

The core claim is well supported. Interior's plan does say it will shrink the federal estate and move land to other governments. The headline's "could sell off untold acres" is hedged and accurate, since the plan gives no acreage and no binding commitment (AP notes future administrations are not bound).

## Materially misleading or imprecise points

- "Federal judge" for the Boulder City stay. It was an Interior administrative appeals judge, and the stay is temporary.
- Framing Pitkin County as a land-transfer trial run. It is a permit to manage a recreation area, not a transfer of ownership, and it is not final. The Forest Service cites cost reasons.
- "Untold acres" is the nature of the plan, not a hidden number. Disposal is conditional on future actions and, for large tracts, on Congress.
- Data center developers, miners and energy companies are named as beneficiaries. This is a critics' inference, not something the plan states.
- The workforce figure and the Alaska acreage were not verified, so treat them as unconfirmed.
