# Fact-check: Fortune, 2026-11-19, "Social Security 22% cut / Ron Johnson / Stop Scaring Seniors Act / $190 trillion (CRFB)"

Method: WebFetch returned only a summary of the article, not verbatim text, so the claims below come from that summary. The claims were checked with WebSearch and by fetching the CRFB analysis of the 2026 Trustees Report. I did not open the Trustees Report itself, the Johnson committee report, or CRFB's $190T analysis. Checks therefore rest on secondary or CRFB sources.

| # | Claim | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Retirement trust fund (OASI) runs dry in under six years | Supported | CRFB's analysis of the 2026 Trustees Report gives 2032. Other coverage says Q4 2032 (about 6 years from Nov 2026). "Under six" is borderline but fits a Q4 2032 date. |
| 2 | Automatic cut of about 22% at depletion | Supported | CRFB's 2026 Trustees analysis gives 22%. It grows to about 38% by 2100. Earlier 2025-era figures were 24%, so the 22% is the current number. |
| 3 | Johnson proposed on Nov 18 letting Treasury move general-fund money into the trust fund once reserves are gone; CRFB calls it the "Stop Scaring Seniors Act" | Partly checked | The Nov 18 date and Johnson's Budget Committee report ("Legal Ponzi Scheme" framing) are confirmed by several outlets. I found no primary text of the bill. The nickname and mechanism rest on Fortune and CRFB only. |
| 4 | Johnson's report projects a shortfall of about $511 billion in 2032 | Supported | Multiple outlets repeat the $511B figure from the report. I did not read the report itself. |
| 5 | CRFB estimates the bill adds $190 trillion to debt over 75 years, inflation-adjusted, or 165% of GDP | Not checkable here | I could not locate CRFB's underlying analysis. The figure is plausible only as a total of 75 years of shortfalls. It is far above the roughly $31T present-value shortfall in the Trustees Report (4.42% of payroll). The gap is explained only if the $190T is an undiscounted, real, cumulative sum. |
| 6 | The cut would cost the average beneficiary about $500 a month and affect about 70 million people | Not checkable here | CRFB's Trustees analysis gives no monthly average or count. It says "nearly all" beneficiaries. It cites $18,400 a year for a couple retiring in 2033. About 70M beneficiaries is in the plausible range, but I could not verify it. |
| 7 | Earlier CRFB analysis: general-revenue funding could lift 10-year yields from 4% to 6.6% | Not checkable here | I could not find the CRFB source. It is a modeled CRFB estimate, not an observed outcome. |
| 8 | MacGuineas called the bill "a cynical pander"; said fixing the deficit as a whole is "a reasonable position" | Not checkable here | No primary statement found. The quotes are attributed to CRFB's president. |
| 9 | TD Securities expects the $41.1T debt limit to bind in January, with room running out around September 2027 | Not checkable here | I found no source. |
| 10 | Johnson argues Americans have been misled about how Social Security is funded | Supported | Reported by several outlets (the "scammed" and "Ponzi" framing). |

## Overall rating: 2 Pinocchios (leaning toward 1)
The Fortune summary matches the figures I could check: the 22% cut, the 2032 depletion and the $511B shortfall. The most dramatic numbers ($190T, 165% of GDP, 6.6% yields) are CRFB projections and are presented as such. Four of the ten claims (5 to 7 and 9) are unverified, and the quotes in claim 8 were not located.

## Materially misleading or missing context
- **Advocacy source.** The piece depends heavily on CRFB, a deficit-hawk group opposed to the bill. Johnson's side is given less space than CRFB's.
- **Scale.** $190T over 75 years in real terms is a cumulative figure that sounds enormous. The Trustees Report's 75-year shortfall is about $31T in present value. Without that comparison, readers may not see the gap.
- **Cut versus debt.** A 22% cut and a $190T debt addition are both consequences of the same shortfall. The bill does not create the gap. It changes who bears it.
- **Modeled yield effect.** The 4% to 6.6% yield rise is a model output, not a forecast.
- **Johnson's rationale.** The motive for the bill (the "misled seniors" argument) is thinly represented.

Sources: CRFB, Analysis of the 2026 Social Security Trustees' Report (crfb.org/papers/analysis-2026-social-security-trustees-report); Fortune, 2026-11-19 article; coverage of the Johnson Budget Committee report (political.org, thepostmillennial.com, dallasexpress.com, local TV syndication).
