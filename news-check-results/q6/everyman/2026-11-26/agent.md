# Fact-check: Boston Foundation, 2026 Greater Boston Housing Report Card press release (Nov 10, 2026)

Method: WebFetch of the release; WebSearch/WebFetch of independent coverage (WGBH, Axios, Boston.com, WBUR, Globe). The underlying report PDF and Census tables were not opened, so most claims are checked against secondary coverage of the same report, not primary data.

## Claims
| Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Region grew ~9,000 in 2025, slowest since 2011 excluding COVID | Supported (WGBH: ~9,000, down from ~49,000). "Slowest since 2011" not independently checked. |
| Net international in-migration halved to just over 30,000 | Not independently checked. WGBH says overseas arrivals no longer offset domestic losses. |
| ~32,000 left for other U.S. regions | Supported (WGBH). |
| Census address-count units fell by more than half, Jul 2025 to Jul 2026 | Partly supported. Boston Indicators coverage says "nearly 50%" down and cites 15,241 units added. "More than half" vs "nearly 50%" is a wording discrepancy. |
| 2025 permits lowest in over a decade | Supported (multiple outlets). |
| Ranks 9th of 11 large metros for permits per 1,000 residents | Not checkable here (needs the report data). |
| Only six communities with median home price under $500K, down from 42 a decade ago (inflation-adjusted) | Not checkable here. WGBH names four such single-family communities (Ashby, Avon, Brockton, Shirley); the difference may be the home type or the definition. Unresolved. |
| $181,000 income needed in a low-priced community; one in six renters has it | Roughly supported. Axios headline says $181K; WGBH says about $180K; 17% of renter households (about 1 in 6). |
| 20% down on $600K cuts monthly costs by nearly $1,000 | Not checked; plausible arithmetic, depends on rate assumptions. |
| 2024: over 48% of renters cost-burdened, nearly half of them severely | Roughly supported. Boston.com says about 49%. Census 2023 ACS showed 27.1% severely burdened, i.e. about 55% of the burdened, which is "over half", not "nearly half". Not verified against the 2024 ACS. |
| Quotes from Luc Schuster, Chris Herbert, Keith Mahoney | Not checked against recordings. Schuster's remarks are consistent with coverage (federal policy, a weaker economy). |
| Social housing examples (BHA Building F at Bunker Hill, East Boston Neighborhood Trust) | Not checked. |

## Grade
1 Pinocchio (minor issues). The release is an institutional press release summarizing its own research. The main numbers match independent reporting, with small wording gaps and a few figures I could not reach.

## Misleading or context flags
- The release is self-reported by the report's author. Nothing in it is independently audited.
- The Boston Foundation's own earlier report notes that ACS cost-burden figures may overstate burden for renters in subsidized housing. The release does not mention this caveat.
- "Fell by more than half" versus "nearly 50%" should be checked against the data.
- "Price increases have leveled off" sits alongside a rising income threshold. The release gives no clear comparison to show that the two fit together.
