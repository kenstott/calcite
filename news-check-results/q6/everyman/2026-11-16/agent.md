# Fact-check: Yahoo/AccuWeather, "Is the 2026 Atlantic hurricane season over? El Nino suppressing late-season storm risk" (Emilee Speck, Nov. 9, 2026)

Method limits: WebFetch returned a summary of the article, not verbatim text, so quotes were checked only as paraphrased. Wikipedia (secondary, citing NHC reports) was the main source for storm data; NOAA and AccuWeather pages were checked for forecasts. NHC's own reports were not fetched. Python was not available as a tool in this run.

## Claims

| # | Claim | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | AccuWeather declared Atlantic activity over, three weeks before Nov. 30 | Consistent (attribution, not checkable independently) | Article date Nov. 9; 21 days before Nov. 30 |
| 2 | 10 named storms, fewest since 2014 | True | Wikipedia lists 10 (Arthur to Josephine). 2014 had 8; later seasons had more. |
| 3 | Only one hurricane, Isaias; first time since 1925 with one | Partly checked | One hurricane confirmed. The "since 1925" comparison was not verified. Wikipedia frames the record differently, as the latest first hurricane (see Misleading). |
| 4 | DaSilva quote: "broke a 121-year-old hurricane record" | Misleading | Wikipedia: Isaias formed Oct. 8, which tied the 1905 season for the latest first hurricane. 2026 - 1905 = 121. A tie is not a break. The quote is attributed to DaSilva, so it is accurately reported but inaccurate. |
| 5 | March forecast 11-16 named storms | True | AccuWeather Mar. 25 forecast: 11-16 named storms. It was later lowered to 8-11. NOAA's Aug. 6 outlook was 7-13. |
| 6 | Strong El Nino developed, adding shear | True | NOAA: El Nino emerged in June and is the dominant factor. Wikipedia notes record July Caribbean shear. |
| 7 | Arthur, Bertha, Edouard caused Gulf Coast flooding/erosion | Plausible | All three made Texas/Louisiana landfalls as tropical storms. Damage: $0.7B, $0.4B, $0.14B. The flooding/erosion wording was not verified. |
| 8 | AccuWeather est. $4-6B for Arthur | Not checkable here | Wikipedia shows $700M (Gallagher est. $1.3B). The AccuWeather figure was not found. It is far above other estimates, but it is AccuWeather's own "damage and economic loss" metric, which is broader. |
| 9 | Isaias Cat 2 at landfall near Destin on Oct. 9 after reaching Cat 3 | True, with a date caveat | Landfall near Destin at 01:30 UTC Oct. 10 (evening Oct. 9 local), 105 mph (Cat 2). Peak 120 mph (Cat 3). |
| 10 | AccuWeather prelim. $16-20B for Isaias | Not checkable here | Wikipedia lists more than $2B in damage and 9 deaths. The AccuWeather figure was not found. |
| 11 | Josephine, subtropical, Oct. 21, 10th named storm | True | Classified late Oct. 21, 65 mph, no landfall. |
| 12 | E/C Pacific: 24 named storms, 13 hurricanes as of Nov. 3 | Roughly consistent | Wikipedia: combined basin 25 named storms and 13 hurricanes (page current to Nov. 12). Hurricanes match. The storm count differs by one, possibly because of the date. |
| 13 | DaSilva: U.S. should avoid further direct tropical impacts | Not checkable here (forecast opinion) | NHC had no active Atlantic systems in the sources found. |

## Misleading or missing context
- "Broke" a 121-year-old record is wrong. The season tied 1905 for the latest first hurricane.
- AccuWeather's $4-6B (Arthur) and $16-20B (Isaias) are 5 to 8 times or more the figures in the reference tables ($0.7B and over $2B). They are presented without noting that AccuWeather's metric includes broad economic loss and is not insured or reported damage.
- The article is AccuWeather-sourced and does not mention that the season's nine deaths from Isaias and 12 season-wide are the more concrete impact.
- Isaias was the first U.S. hurricane landfall since Milton (2024), which the article leaves out. A season with "only one hurricane" still had a major hurricane (Cat 3) landfall at Category 2.

## Rating: 1 Pinocchio (Some shading)
Core facts check out: storm count, single hurricane, Isaias landfall, Josephine, forecast, El Nino. The "broke" a record claim overstates a tie, and the AccuWeather loss estimates could not be confirmed and are well above other estimates.
