# Fact-check: "School districts with fewer students are delaying painful budget decisions" (AP via ABC News)

Limits: WebFetch returned only a model-written summary of the article, not verbatim text. Attributed quotes could not be extracted and are not checked here. I ran three web searches. Checks rest on secondary sources, not primary data.

## Claims
1. Enrollment is down more than 1.4 million since the pandemic, per a Stanford study, driven by lower birth rates and school choice. PARTLY VERIFIED. The 1.4 million figure matches federal data (49.4M in fall 2020, about 1.4M below the prior fall, per Chalkbeat). A Stanford/AP analysis (Thomas Dee) put the loss at about 1.2M. Different periods and datasets give different totals, so the attribution to "a Stanford study" is loose. Causes: falling births, immigration drops and school choice are plausible, but I did not verify the "mainly" weighting.
2. Staffing and school counts have barely changed. NOT CHECKABLE HERE. No primary data was pulled.
3. Seattle: a 2024 plan to close up to a fifth of schools was dropped after opposition. LARGELY VERIFIED. The 2024 plan proposed closing up to 21 schools, was scaled back to four, then withdrawn. "A fifth" is roughly right, depending on the denominator.
4. Seattle: a new plan to close nearly a dozen campuses draws resistance. PARTLY VERIFIED. Axios (2026-11-02) reports Seattle considering merging up to 21 schools. "Nearly a dozen" may reflect an AP-specific figure or a narrower phase. Not confirmed.
5. San Francisco: $100M+ deficit, 2024 closure proposal shelved, $26M deficit next year, drawing down reserves. NOT CHECKED. My search returned no SFUSD results.
6. More than $190B in pandemic relief. NOT CONFIRMED. ESSER I-III is commonly cited at about $190B, but my search did not surface that total. ESSER III obligations reached about $107M of... (87% of ESSER III, roughly $107B). The relief money is spent, which is consistent with the deadlines (spend by Jan 2025, extensions to Mar 2026).
7. Payroll is about 80% of district budgets. NOT CHECKED. Education Department data show about 53% of ESSER spending went to salaries and benefits, a different measure.
8. At least 21 state takeovers since 2023. NOT CHECKED. Brookings counts 139 takeovers over three decades, but no source found for the post-2023 count.
9. School closures yield modest, temporary savings. Consistent with the research literature, but I did not review it here.

## Rating
Provisional: 1 Pinocchio (mostly accurate, with the checked facts largely holding). This is a low-confidence grade because most numeric claims were unchecked.

## Possible misleading framing
- The headline implies districts are "delaying" decisions, but Seattle has repeatedly proposed and withdrawn closures and has made other cuts. That is political resistance, not simple inaction.
- The "Stanford study" attribution blends different enrollment totals. Reardon's Stanford work is about achievement, not enrollment.
