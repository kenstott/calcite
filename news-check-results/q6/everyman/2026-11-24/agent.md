# Fact-check: Pew, "Democratic wave sweeps in more split Senate delegations" (2026-11-23)

Limits: WebFetch returned only a summary of the Pew piece, not verbatim text. No direct quotes were extracted. I could not open the Biographical Directory or the Pew charts.

## Claims and verdicts
1. More states will have split Senate delegations than at any time since 2019. NOT CHECKED HERE. This needs the historical series.
2. 10 states will have one D and one R senator in the 120th Congress. PARTIAL. Wikipedia's 2026 Senate elections page and NBC's tracker both show 9 Democratic flips: AK, IA, KS, ME, MS, NC, OH, SC, TX. Wikipedia's inferred split list (AK, IA, KS, MS, NC, OH, SC, TX) is 8 states, and it is the page's own inference. Pew's 10 is plausible but unconfirmed; it could include VT, ME or others depending on how the independents are counted.
3. 23 unified D, 17 unified R, 10 split. CONSISTENT. 23x2+10 = 56 D-caucus seats, and 17x2+10 = 44 R seats. Both match the 56-44 Senate that Wikipedia and NBC report.
4. First time in 14 years that unified D delegations outnumber unified R. NOT CHECKED HERE.
5. Split delegations ran 10-20 per Congress from the 1910s to the early 1970s, peaked at 27 in the 96th Congress (1979-81), and the 119th had 3. NOT CHECKED HERE (not verified against the Biographical Directory).
6. The trend since 1980 is toward single-party delegations. NOT CHECKED HERE.
7. Method: 64th Congress (1915-17) onward, Biographical Directory, start-of-Congress figures. Not verifiable. It is a description of method.

## Sourcing caveats
- NBC's tracker was live (8:02 PM ET snapshot, some races incomplete). It is not a certified result.
- Wikipedia's 100,000-character read was truncated.
- Search snippets contained garbled text, so I treated them with caution.

## Rating
Preliminary 1 Pinocchio on the 0-4 scale. Counts that could be checked (control, flips, totals) are consistent with Pew. Most historical claims are unchecked, so this is provisional rather than a confirmed "Geppetto Checkmark".

## Possible misleading framing
- The headline calls this a "Democratic wave", but split delegations are an artifact of Democrats flipping seats in Republican-held states. A rise in splits signals Democratic gains, not bipartisan convergence.
- Independents caucusing with Democrats are counted as Democrats, which affects the 23/17/10 breakdown.
