# Fact-check: CFR, "The 2026 Election by the Numbers" (James M. Lindsay, Nov 10, 2026)

Method: WebFetch returned only a summary of the article (not verbatim; no direct quotes were available beyond "historic victory for Democrats and an epic defeat for Republicans" and "dummymander"). Corroboration came from WebSearch and one WebFetch of a secondary Ballotpedia-citing page. No primary result source (AP, Clerk of the House, state certifications, FEC, UF Election Lab final data) could be opened directly, so most claims are graded on secondary corroboration only.

## Claims

| Claim | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Democrats won both chambers; "historic victory" | Supported | Multiple outlets (Cox/WDBO, GMA, CBS) report Democrats captured House and Senate. The "historic" wording is opinion. |
| House: D 254, R 165, 16 undecided at publication | Not checkable here | No snapshot found at that date. Early reports (Nov 4) show D ahead 228-139, consistent in direction only. |
| NBC projects final 259 D / 176 R; biggest D margin since 1990 | Not checkable here | NBC projection and the 1990 comparison not found. Note 259+176 = 435 (internally consistent). |
| Neither reaches 290 for a veto-proof majority | Accurate arithmetic | 290 is two-thirds of 435 (rounded up). |
| Democratic popular-vote lead about 13 pts, second-largest midterm margin since 1942 after 1974 | Not checkable here | No national popular-vote margin found in any source. |
| Redistricting: Democrats won all 6 seats leaning their way; Republicans won only 2 of 15 targeted | Partly supported | Democracy Docket and WJLA say GOP gerrymanders "largely backfired" and Democrats held nearly every seat. The exact 6/15 and 2/15 counts were not verified. Ballotpedia (Nov 4) estimated new maps netted about 9 R-favorable districts on paper, so the framing depends on measurement. "Dummymander" is an opinion label, and the term was already in use in April 2026 coverage. |
| Senate: D 54, R 44, Alaska and Mississippi uncalled but leaning D | Largely supported | GMA/AP-derived reporting: Democrats at 54 on Nov 4, Alaska and Mississippi too close to call. One report had Sullivan (R) holding a slim edge over Peltola in Alaska, which cuts against "leaning Democratic" for Alaska. Flag as questionable. |
| Net gain of 9 Senate seats | Questionable | Early reports and Ballotpedia say Democrats flipped 7 (at least). 9 would require winning Alaska and Mississippi, which were uncalled. Possible if those and others resolved, but the figure could not be confirmed. Also unclear how it squares with 54 seats, given the baseline. |
| 60 needed to overcome a filibuster | Accurate | Cloture threshold is 60. |
| At least 159 women in the next Congress, a record | Contradicted/unverified | Newsweek reports at least 155 projected, beating the prior record of 152 (2024). The record claim holds; the 159 figure does not match what I found. Final CAWP counts were not available. |
| New senators average about 55 years old; new Senate "younger" | Not checkable here | No source found. |
| At least 123.5 million voted, 49.1% of VEP; second-highest midterm turnout since 1914 behind 2018 | Not checkable here | No final turnout figure found. Early-vote data (UF Election Lab: more than 53 million early ballots by Nov 1 vs 45.9 million in 2022) is directionally consistent with high turnout. 2018 (about 50% VEP, about 120 million) being the modern record is correct from known data, but 123.5 million would exceed 2018's total votes, so "second-highest" is about rate, not count; the piece should say so. |
| Polls underestimated Democrats more than they underestimated Trump in 2016/2024; one aggregator had D +9 | Not checkable here | No polling-error analysis located. |
| Democrats flipped governorships in 5 states; at least 28 governorships, first majority in 16 years | Close, slightly high | Ballotpedia (Nov 4): at least 27 Democratic governorships, first majority since 2010 (about 16 years: consistent). 28 vs 27 may reflect later calls; unverified. |

## Overall rating

Provisional: 2 Pinocchios (Some shading / needs context), with low confidence. Reason: the direction (Democratic sweep, 54 Senate seats, redistricting backfire, governor majority) is corroborated, but the headline numbers (popular-vote margin, turnout, 159 women, +9 Senate, exact redistricting counts) could not be verified, two are in tension with sources found (women 155 vs 159; Senate +7 vs +9), and the Alaska "leaning Democratic" characterization conflicts with a report showing Sullivan ahead.

## Materially misleading flags
- Mid-count figures: the piece mixes counted totals with projections (NBC) and "leaning" calls for uncalled races, which can read as settled results.
- "Backfired" redistricting: Ballotpedia and AP-syndicated coverage in June said the maps netted GOP-favorable seats on paper; the backfire reflects the wave, not the maps themselves.
- Turnout "second-highest since 1914" mixes rate and raw count if read casually.

## Limits
Most claims graded "not checkable here" because I could not access primary results (AP, House Clerk, certified returns) or the article verbatim.
