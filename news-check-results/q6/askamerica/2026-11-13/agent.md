The Council on Foreign Relations article's claims about the 2026 midterm results, historical comparisons and Senate demographics are balanced and mostly accurate; 1 claim could not be confirmed.

**Rating: 1 Pinocchio.**

Source: https://www.cfr.org/articles/2026-election-by-the-numbers (James Lindsay, Nov 10, 2026). The fetch worked but the text cut off at the National Popular Vote paragraph, so the last paragraphs were not read.

## Verified
- Democrats won both chambers, with a 54-44 Senate count and Alaska and Mississippi uncalled (AP via Cox, Nov 4). The central claim holds.
- The 13-point House margin is second only to 1974 since 1942. Ballotpedia: 1974 was D+16.6, 1958 D+11.9, 1982 D+11.8, and 2010 R+6.6. The 13-point figure is an early, uncertified estimate. The warehouse (officials.house_race_results, 1976-2022, contested races only) shows no later midterm above 12.5.
- Democrats' biggest House majority since 1990 holds. Ballotpedia has 267 in 1990, 258 in 1992 and 257 in 2008. The projected 259 is NBC's estimate. The warehouse shows 270, 260 and 257 on a decisive-round count.
- Turnout of 49.1% (123.5M), second since 1914 behind 2018, matches the UF Election Lab first estimate and the Brennan Center.
- The "9-point" poll lead matches the final Silver Bulletin average of 8.8.
- "Seven changes in 1960-1998" and "twelve of fourteen elections 2000-2026" check out by count.

## Errors
- **False:** "The youngest Senator, Democrat Jon Ossoff... will be thirty-seven." Ossoff is 39 in January 2027 (Roll Call). Incoming senator James Talarico is 37 (NYT) and will be the youngest.
- **Mostly true:** Hinojosa is "first Democrat to win the Texas governorship in thirty-two years." Ann Richards last won in 1990, which is 36 years ago. Thirty-two years counts only from her 1995 exit.
- **Mostly true:** "thirteenth time in sixteen tries." The elections from 2000 to 2028 number fifteen.
- **Mostly true:** Democrats lost "127" House seats in 1894. House History shows 218 to 93, which is 125.

## Not confirmed
- At least 28 and possibly 30 Democratic governorships, and the first Democratic majority in 16 years. No post-election tally page was found; only the Texas flip was confirmed.

## Misleading framing
- Alaska and Mississippi are treated as near-certain Democratic wins. Alaska was a 637-vote lead on Nov 6 with about a quarter of ballots outstanding and possible ranked-choice tabulation.
- The 13-point margin and the poll-miss comparison rest on an early, uncertified tally.
- The state-legislature paragraph is duplicated, and 23 D + 22 R + 2 split = 47, not the 49 partisan legislatures.

## Not graded
NBC, CNN, CAWP and multistate pages were not fetched. Demographic-swing figures, women-in-Congress counts, state-legislature counts and most of the "firsts" were not checked. The NYT Upshot link the article cites loaded only as a preview. It confirmed 222 electoral votes in the National Popular Vote compact and five new trifectas reaching 283.
