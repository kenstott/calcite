**The CNBC property tax revolt article's claims about ballot results, home prices, tax bills and Florida Amendment 3 are accurate.**

Rating: 0 Pinocchios.

Source: CNBC, Nov. 16, 2026 (fetched in full).

**Method.** The warehouse has no table for NAR prices, ACS B25103, or ballot results. It does carry FRED MSPUS (Census/HUD median sales price), used as context. Every claim was therefore checked against primary sources.

| Claim | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Florida Amendment 3 passed on Nov. 3 (central) | True | About 62% yes against a 60% threshold. Homestead exemption rises to $150,000 in 2027 and $250,000 in 2028. |
| NC approved its amendment; Oklahoma and Wyoming rejected theirs | True | NC passed with 60.4%. Wyoming Initiative 1 and Oklahoma SQ 847 failed. |
| Single-family median about $300,000 in 2020 vs about $419,000 in 2025 (NAR) | Mostly true | NAR's 2025 single-family median is $419,300. $296,500 is NAR's 2020 all-types median; single-family was nearer $310,000. The rise is slightly overstated. Warehouse MSPUS (Census/HUD, not NAR) ran 317,100 to 338,600 across 2020 quarters and 410,100 to 423,100 across 2025 quarters. |
| Median property tax bill about $3,300 last year (ACS) | True, with a caveat | The 2024 ACS median was $3,211, so about $3,300 for 2025 is consistent. I could not retrieve the 2025 national figure because the Census API needs a key. |
| At least 168 state measures in 40 states and DC, Jan 2020 to Jun 2026 (NACo) | True | NACo says "at least 168 property tax actions in 40 states and DC". |
| Tax Foundation: Amendment 3 costs $4.9B, $8.7B and $11.8B | True | Matches the Tax Foundation report. These are estimates, and the report says the full impact is uncertain and could exceed them. |
| Property taxes about 28% of local funding in 2024 (Lincoln Institute) | True | The Lincoln Institute figure is 28.4%. I read it from a search snippet because the direct fetch failed. |
| Bills under $250 in some rural counties, over $10,000 near big cities (Tax Foundation) | True | 11 counties are under $250 and 16 are over $10,000. |
| Ohio abolition amendment missed the ballot; supporters aim for 2027 | True | Reported by WOSU and Signal Cleveland. |
| Some NY counties are paying more for rock salt | Mostly true | The linked WKBW piece covers one town, Tonawanda, buying through an Erie County contract. Price rose from $53 to $83-93 per ton. |
| Walczak: "no realistic way to replace property taxes" | Not checkable | A policy judgment no data can test. |

**Framing.** The article does not mislead. It notes that the Tax Foundation's cost figures are estimates, quotes both proponents and critics, and gives the NACo point that relief reduces revenue. It omits that NACo's own report finds real property tax bills rose only 9% from 2010 to 2024 once inflation is removed. That makes the "rising home values drive rising taxes" framing somewhat stronger than the NACo data supports. This is a minor omission and does not change the rating.

**Limits.** The ACS 2025 national median was not retrieved directly. The Lincoln Institute page was read through search rather than a direct fetch.
