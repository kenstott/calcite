The WGME article's claims about Portland's warm start to November 2026 and Maine drought are somewhat biased and mostly accurate; 1 claim could not be confirmed.

Rating: 1 Pinocchio.

Report: http://127.0.0.1:54890/a/8b0f85fda00da825ff0590737f084162.html (saved at /Users/kennethstott/.askamerica/reports/20261117-101325-the-wgme-article-s-claims-about-portland.html)

Verified (true): 2022 warmest at 50.6 F (warehouse 50.62; ACIS 50.60); average high 57 F (IEM 56.7); warmest day Nov 4 at 73 F; average temperature 48.0 F; 1.13 in precipitation; severe drought (D2) from York to Aroostook, extreme (D3) in Midcoast and parts of Aroostook, per the Drought Monitor (no D4).

Mostly true, with differences:
- Third-warmest rank: on the Jetport record since 1941 (ACIS), 1975 (48.23) and 1969 (48.00) are at or above 2026 (48.0). So 2026 is third to fourth, tied with 1969. The URL slug still says "second-warmest".
- "Nearly every day warmer than average": Nov 12-14 highs were 44-46 F and Nov 1 and 14 lows were 25-28 F, so roughly 10-12 of 15 days were above average.
- "Typical average 43.1 F": the 1991-2020 ACIS mean is 42.2 F.
- "Six days of measurable precipitation": IEM shows five days at or above 0.01 in (seven counting trace).
- Downeast drought eased "to abnormally dry": on Nov 10 only 53.9% of Washington County and 41.6% of Hancock County were D0, the rest still moderate drought.

Unsupported: the CPC outlook (warmer than average, near-average precipitation). The CPC page returned a stale 2014 cache and I could not read the Nov 2026 outlook.

Not checkable: "Maine will need several days of steady, soaking rain" is a forecaster's judgment.

Flags: the headline/slug mismatch; the record length is not stated; the ranking is a near-tie, with 2015 only 0.1 F behind.
