The Boston Foundation press release on the 2026 Greater Boston Housing Report Card: population, permits, affordability and renter cost-burden claims are accurate; 1 claim could not be confirmed.

**Grouping correction:** the Census Address Count claim ("fell by more than half") is now graded as a `fidelity` claim, since the release relays a named outside source. The other claims name no outside source and stay as author claims. The server did not require the Address Count claim to be duplicated as an author claim.

The server restarted partway through, so I re-ran the key fetches, catalog searches and queries before resubmitting. The figures did not change.

**Claims**
- **Population (central claim):** true. About 9,000 added in 2025, the slowest since 2011 excluding the pandemic. Report p.13 says so, and the Boston Globe repeats it. It is an estimate.
- **Permits:** true. In the warehouse (Census permits, five Massachusetts counties), units fell from 15,019 in 2021 to 11,823, 9,525, 8,993, then 7,746 in 2025, the lowest of 2015-2025.
- **Rent burden:** mostly true. The release says over 48% of renters were cost burdened in 2024. The warehouse (ACS 2020-24, five counties) gives 51.2%, and 50.6% of those severely. The release's 48% is about 3 points lower. The cause is probably a different ACS product or geography.
- **Entry-level affordability:** true. $181,000 income needed and one in six renters (about 17%) have it, per report p.37. These are model outputs.
- **Six communities under $500,000:** true. Report p.32 says six towns versus 42 in 2016, inflation-adjusted. These are single-family medians.
- **20% down payment:** true. Report p.40 says it cuts the monthly payment by almost $1,000.
- **Address Count "more than half" (fidelity):** mostly true, but overstated. WBUR reports 15,241 net new units in Greater Boston from July 2025 to July 2026, versus 29,024 the year before. That is a 47.5% decline, slightly under half. I did not confirm the statewide figure.
- **Ninth of 11 metros on permits per 1,000:** unsupported. The Globe confirms the direction (about two permits per 1,000, below other major metros) but gives no rank.

**Not checked:** the roughly 30,000 international in-migration and 32,000 domestic out-migration figures.

**Framing flags**
- The "more than half" drop compares a spike period with the latest period. The report itself warns that six-month periods are not precise counts. It also says Massachusetts still largely exceeded its 22,000-per-year production goal, which the release leaves out.
- The release says price increases "leveled off" and also says lower-cost communities saw the largest increases. Both are in the report, but they pull in different directions.
- The Boston Foundation sponsors the report, so matching the release to the report only shows it is faithful. The permit and rent-burden figures are confirmed independently from Census data.
