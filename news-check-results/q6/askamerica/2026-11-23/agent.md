**The Texas Tribune/TPR article's claims about Texas college stopouts, student debt and the completer earnings gap are mostly accurate and cut against its own case.**

**Rating: 1 Pinocchio.**

Source note: the TPR page (a Texas Tribune republication) and the Tribune original were fetched. The Philanthropy Advocates report PDF exceeds the 25MB fetch cap, so report figures come from FOX 4's article quoting the report, not the primary document. The warehouse has no Texas stopout data (search_catalog: "stopout" matched nothing). I used census.acs_earnings_by_education (Texas, ACS 2024) as a context check on the earnings gap.

| Claim | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| About one in three Texas students left without a degree; $1.1B debt (central) | Mostly true | Report via FOX 4: 38% (347,061) left; debt is "at least $1.09B" for the 2017-2022 cohorts. Cohort is Texas public high school graduates who enrolled within a year, not all Texas students. |
| 900,000 students tracked | True | 913,443; rounded. |
| 49% completed, 38% left, 14% still enrolled | True | Matches the report as relayed. The three shares sum to 101% from rounding. |
| Most who left did so in year one | True | 51% in year one, 78% by end of year two. |
| Grade averages "hovered just above 1.0" | Partially false | The report says half of first-year departures had a GPA of 1.0 or lower. It does not say averages were above 1.0, or that this holds for all leavers. |
| Nearly 30% had debt; "average" borrower owes $6,250 | Mostly true | $6,250 is the median balance per FOX 4's relay, not the average. The 30% share could not be verified. |
| $20,800 annual wage gap | True | Report: $60,500 vs $39,700 ten years after high school. ACS 2024 Texas medians: bachelor's $70,006 vs some college/associate $46,890, a gap of $23,116. Same magnitude. The gap is correlational, not causal. |
| Abbott set a 60% working-age credential-of-value goal | True | The governor's office announced 60x30 on Nov 16, 2015 (60% of ages 25-34 with a certificate or degree). The working-age, credential-of-value wording is from the later Coordinating Board plan (ages 25-64). |

**Misleading framing.** The headline says "one in three" while the study finds 38%, closer to two in five. The "average" label on a median understates how skewed debt may be. $1.09B is a floor. The report's sponsor discloses financial ties to the Tribune; the article discloses this.

**Not done.** I did not read the primary report PDF, so the 30% borrower share and the GPA wording rest on FOX 4's summary.
