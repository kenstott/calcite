The Yahoo/AccuWeather article's claims about the 2026 Atlantic hurricane season, its records and Isaias are biased and mostly accurate; 1 claim could not be confirmed.

**Rating: 1 Pinocchio** (engine score from the graded claims).

**Source note.** The Yahoo page loaded in full. It is AccuWeather's own content, and all six AccuWeather pages it links returned HTTP 403. I checked claims against Wikipedia season pages (which cite NHC), WGCU and Noonsite. I did not read NHC itself. The warehouse has no NHC/HURDAT table; FEMA declarations (disasters.disaster_declarations) cover only part of the storm set.

**What checks out**
- 10 named storms, the fewest since 2014 (8). Seasons 2015-2025 all had 11 or more.
- Isaias details: landfall near Destin at 8:30 pm CDT Oct 9 as Category 2, peak Category 3 at 120 mph, Okaloosa County evacuations, 24 tornadoes (the article says "more than 20"), 9 deaths (the article says "as many as eight" indirect), 5.51 ft surge at Panama City, 8.06 in of rain at Niceville.
- No Atlantic hurricane through Sept 21, the first such season since 1914 (112 years).
- Pacific: 13 hurricanes and 11 major hurricanes, a record tied with 2015. The article's 24 named storms as of Nov 3 is consistent with Wikipedia's current 25.
- FEMA declarations exist for Arthur (LA, MS), Hurricane Lala (HI) and Nolo (HI).

**What is wrong or shaky**
1. **Central error (partially false, stated three times).** The article says Isaias "broke a 121-year record" for the latest first hurricane. Wikipedia says Isaias reaching hurricane status on Oct 8 only tied the 1905 season. Noonsite also says it ties 1905. It is an outright record only for the satellite era (the prior mark was Sept 11).
2. **Arthur landfall.** The article says Matagorda Bay, Texas. Wikipedia says near Galveston. Minor.
3. **"First time since 1925 with only one hurricane."** Unsupported. 1925 did have one hurricane, but I could not confirm that no season in between had exactly one.
4. **Arthur damage of $4-6B.** Unsupported. The AccuWeather page returned 403, and Wikipedia cites a far lower insurer estimate of $1.3B.
5. **DaSilva's forecast that the US will avoid further direct impacts.** Unsupported. It is a prediction, and the season runs to Nov 30.

**Misleading framing**
- The $16-20B Isaias figure is AccuWeather's own "total damage and economic loss" estimate, presented without context. Wikipedia lists at least $2B in damage. That figure is correctly attributed, but the article never says other tallies are about an order of magnitude lower.
- The record language rounds "tied" up to "broke" in the lede, the quote and the body.
