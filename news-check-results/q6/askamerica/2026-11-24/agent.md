**Warehouse check (officials.members).** The table covers Congresses 117-119 for this purpose. **Claims**
- **Central claim, true:** the 120th Congress will have 10 split delegations, the most since the 115th. The 116th had 9, so the claim holds. AP reports a 56-44 Democratic majority, which fits 23 unified D, 17 unified R and 10 split (23x2+10=56; 17x2+10=44).
- **True:** 23 unified Democratic vs 17 unified Republican is the first Democratic edge since 2013. The 113th had 19 vs 14, and Republicans were at or above Democrats in every Congress from the 114th on.
- **True:** the 96th Congress had 27 splits, and every Congress from 1973 to 1994 had 20 or more. This matches Pew's 2021 table; it is not in the warehouse.
- **True:** the 84th Congress had 9 splits and a 49-47 Democratic majority. The Senate Historical Office confirms Morse joined the Democratic Conference, giving Democrats a slim majority, so the "thanks to Morse" cause is supported.
- **True:** Klobuchar and Blackburn were elected governors (Klobuchar 55.5% to 42.6%; Blackburn 45.5% to 43.0%).
- **True:** the 2020 Daines/Tester water compact (NBC Montana/AP) and the 2006 Nelson/Martinez drilling bill (PolitiFact) both check out.
- **Unsupported:** Johnson as 13th-most conservative and Baldwin as sixth-most liberal by DW-NOMINATE. I did not extract Voteview rankings. The Johnson (R) and Baldwin (D) pairing is confirmed in the warehouse.

**Framing.** Not misleading. Pew discloses that it counts independents with their caucus, counts at the start of each Congress, and assumes same-party appointees fill Klobuchar's and Blackburn's seats. The 120th figures are January 2027 projections, and the piece says so.
