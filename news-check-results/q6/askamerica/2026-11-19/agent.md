The Grist article's claims about the 2026 El Nino, Midwest September heat and the 450,000 heat-death estimate are balanced and mostly accurate; 1 claim could not be confirmed.

**Rating: 1 Pinocchio.**

Article: https://grist.org/extreme-weather/a-monstrous-el-nino-has-arrived-what-it-means-for-where-you-live/ (fetched successfully, published Nov 9, 2026). Local report: /Volumes/main/Users/kennethstott/IdeaProjects/calcite/news-check-results/q6/askamerica/2026-11-19/report.html

**Warehouse check.** search_catalog found no ENSO or Nino 3.4 table and no state-level September temperature-rank table. environment.ocean_sst_monthly covers only U.S. marine ecosystems, so it cannot test the headline. Every claim was therefore graded against primary sources that were fetched.

**Claim grades**
- Central claim, "already the strongest El Nino on record": mostly true. Carbon Brief reports a daily Nino 3.4 record of 3.11C on Sept 21 against 3.08C in 2015. But on the relative index (RONI) it was about 0.7C below the 1982 record in mid-September, and the August monthly value (about 2.45C) was below the 2015-16 peak (2.75C). Grist gives no qualifier. This is the main framing issue.
- 450,000 heat deaths: true. The Climate Impact Lab projects 451,000 for June-February against a normal year. It is a projection and not peer reviewed.
- Illinois and Missouri warmest September on record: true. NCEI lists Arkansas, Illinois, Kansas, Missouri and Oklahoma as September record-setters. The contiguous U.S. also had its warmest September (69.4F, 132-year record).
- "Midwest region highest in 132-year record": unsupported. The NCEI text I read gives the national and state ranks but no Midwest-region rank. It is plausible, but I could not confirm it.
- USGS "3F to more than 8F warmer" winters: mostly true. USGS does say that, but it also says the signal is weaker toward Missouri and Michigan, and most models lean near or below normal for Missouri. Grist applies the range to the whole region.
- Newsom state of emergency (Sept 21): true, per the governor's proclamation release.
- Sinlaku, 17 deaths: true. Grist's own earlier story gives a region-wide tally of 17 (Guam plus Micronesia), citing the Pacific Daily News, which I did not open. The storm hit in April, before the period the paragraph describes.
- Art Miller's "1,000 times more ability to hold heat": unsupported. The quote is confirmed only in Grist's own earlier story, and the Scripps page does not contain the figure.
- LA stormwater enough for 106,000 households: true, per Grist's linked story.
- "Every three to five years": mostly true. Imperial's Grantham Institute says two to seven years on average.

**Misleading framing**
- The "strongest on record" claim is stated flatly although RONI and monthly measures are still below earlier peaks.
- The intro says the Southeast "could get colder and wetter", while the article's own outlook map says Arkansas to Virginia leans warm.
- The Midwest is described as "drier" in the intro, but the Midwest section says winter "might not be" dry.

**Not graded.** The Washington Post "coldest winter in a decade" link timed out. KTOO, AccuWeather, Stanford and PPIC returned 403. The Texas2036 PDF exceeded the fetch size cap. Most regional forecast statements are expert predictions and were not graded individually.
