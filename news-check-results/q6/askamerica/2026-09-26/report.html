Neither half of the claim can be independently computed from this connector's own data — this is a genuine sourcing gap, not a defect in a table that exists. No discrepancy was found in any of the four sources.

Step 1 — Searching the corpus for a measles case-count table

Ran search_catalog with the terms 'measles cases', 'NNDSS notifiable disease', and 'infectious disease outbreak surveillance'. No table or column in any schema matched 'measles', 'NNDSS', 'notifiable', or 'outbreak'. The health schema's disease-surveillance tables are: cdc_wonder_std_morbidity (STI cases, 1984-2014, frozen), cdc_wonder_tb (tuberculosis, 1993-2024), and the cause-of-death tables (categories of death, not active-infection case counts). None carry measles. A direct list_tables call on the health schema (68 tables) was also reviewed and confirms no measles-specific or general notifiable-disease case table exists anywhere in the corpus.

Step 2 — Searching the corpus for a kindergarten/school vaccination coverage table

Ran search_catalog with 'kindergarten vaccination coverage MMR', 'school immunization exemption rate', and 'vaccination coverage children'. No table or column matched 'kindergarten', 'immunization', or 'exemption' anywhere in the corpus. The only vaccination-related table found is health.cdc_covid_vaccinations — COVID-19 dose/booster coverage, national-level only, and explicitly documented as frozen at 2023-05-10 with no updates since. This table cannot speak to MMR coverage, kindergartners, or any period after May 2023, and is unrelated in both disease and population to the claim.

Step 3 — Attempting to read CDC's own pages directly

Fetched the article's cited source, CDC's measles data page, and separately CDC's SchoolVaxView data page. Both requests returned HTTP 403 — neither page could be read directly as a primary source in this session. The verification below therefore rests on independently fetched secondary reporting that explicitly cites and quotes CDC's own releases, not on a direct parse of CDC's page itself.

Step 4 — Independent verification via four fetched, CDC-sourced outlets

Because no warehouse computation was possible and the primary CDC pages 403'd, four independent secondary sources were fetched in full (not just search snippets) and cross-checked against each other and against the claim:

  • USAFacts (fetched in full, updated Sep 25, 2026, sourced directly to CDC): "As of September 24, 2026, 3,659 cases have been confirmed in 2026" — matches the claim exactly.
  • CIDRAP (fetched in full, dated Sep 24, 2026 reporting on the Sep 17 CDC weekly update): reports 3,471 cases as of that week's CDC update, one week's data point earlier in the same fast-rising trajectory that reaches 3,659 by Sep 24 — consistent with, not contradicting, the claim (case counts were climbing by 150-190/week at the time).
  • Cronista (fetched in full, published Sep 25, 2026, explicitly sourced to CDC's measles data page): states verbatim, "coverage of the MMR vaccine... fell from 95.2% in the 2019-2020 cycle to 92.4% in 2025-2026 among kindergarten children" — an exact match to both halves of the claim, from a source that read CDC's release directly.
  • KFF (fetched in full, issue brief published Aug 21, 2026, sourced to CDC's SchoolVaxView release): "92.4% of kindergarteners had been vaccinated against measles, mumps, rubella (MMR) and polio... for the 2025-2026 school year. This is down from 95% across all three vaccines for the 2019-2020 (pre-pandemic) school year" — confirms the same trend and rounds to the same baseline (the CIDRAP/USAFacts/Cronista figures give the more precise 95.2%).

All four sources, fetched and read independently rather than taken from search-result snippets, agree with each other and with the claim. No contradiction was found across any of them.

Why this is a genuine data gap in AskAmerica, not a defect

This is being logged as a sourcing gap: measles is a nationally notifiable disease tracked by CDC's National Notifiable Diseases Surveillance System (NNDSS), and kindergarten vaccination coverage is tracked by CDC's SchoolVaxView program — neither pipeline is currently ingested into this warehouse. Both would need a new ETL source (NNDSS weekly measles counts, or CDC's SchoolVaxView annual coverage/exemption tables) to become directly checkable here in future.
