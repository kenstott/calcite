The Industrial Cyber article's claims about Bridewell's Q3 2026 ransomware victim count and manufacturing as top target are biased and mostly accurate.

Rating: 1 Pinocchio.

Access: the article returned HTTP 403 to both the engine fetch and WebFetch, so its text was never read. Claims are reconstructed from the headline and search excerpts of the article, then graded against Bridewell's own report (https://www.bridewell.com/insights/blogs/detail/the-state-of-ransomware-q3-2026, read in full). Warehouse: search_catalog found no ransomware leak-site table (only cyber_vuln.kev_catalog's known_ransomware_use flag: 361 Known of 1,779), so grading is from independent primary sources.

Claims
1. "80% surge in global ransomware victims" (central): TRUE. Bridewell: 2,889 vs 1,609 in Q3 2025 = +79.6%. Caveat: victims are Ransomware.live leak-site postings, not attacks.
2. Manufacturing top target, 474 victims (16.4%): MOSTLY TRUE. Bridewell has professional services at 392 and technology at 363. CyberMaxx (457 attacks) also ranks manufacturing first. ReliaQuest says professional, scientific and technical services still led on its data.
3. Manufacturing up from 274 (12.4%) in Q2: MOSTLY TRUE. The article repeats Bridewell's figure. 274 of Bridewell's Q2 total of 2,299 is 11.9%, not 12.4%, so the share is inconsistent in the source itself.
4. 111 groups, Qilin 362, Thegentlemen 360, US 34.7%: TRUE. All match Bridewell, and the 362 for Qilin matches CyberMaxx.
5. Monthly 970 / 1,064 / 855: TRUE. They sum to 2,889.
6. "About 30% above Q2": PARTIALLY FALSE. Against Q2 2026 (2,299) the rise is 25.7%. The 30% figure is the rise over the six-quarter average (2,229). Bridewell's own analyst note conflates the two.

Misleading framing
- "Victims" are public leak-site postings. Bridewell itself says they undercount incidents.
- ReliaQuest finds 93% of the quarter's growth came from 43 groups with no prior posting history, and says leak-site counts are an increasingly unreliable proxy for attacks. The "surge" may partly reflect posting behavior, rebrands and unverified claims rather than more attacks.
- Manufacturing's lead depends on the tracker.

Not checked: the article's own wording and any quotes. The Bridewell "30% above Q2" error is Bridewell's, not necessarily the article's.
