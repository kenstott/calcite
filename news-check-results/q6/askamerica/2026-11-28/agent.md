The Inside Climate News article's claims about Thanksgiving-week California king tides, record water levels and Naples seawalls are accurate; 1 claim could not be confirmed.

**Rating: 0 Pinocchios.**

Article: "Southern California Endures Historic Coastal Flooding and Devastating Waves Over Thanksgiving" (Inside Climate News, Nov 26-27, 2026). The direct fetch returned HTTP 403, so I read the Wayback Machine capture of the same URL. Every claim below is graded against that text.

**Checked against NOAA's own gauge data**
- **Los Angeles tide record: true.** The article says the tide peaked at 8.19 ft and tied the record from last month. NOAA's 6-minute gauge (station 9410660) peaked at 8.194 ft MLLW on Nov 25 at 08:36. The warehouse (weather.coops_high_low, which ends Oct 2026) shows a prior maximum of 8.17 ft. That is a tie within rounding. The Nov 25 value is preliminary. "Average low tide" loosely stands in for MLLW, which is mean lower-low water.
- **La Jolla tide record: true.** The warehouse's prior maximum is 7.96 ft. NOAA's Nov 25 peak (station 9410230) was 8.114 ft, so the record was broken. The value is preliminary.
- **Peak on Wednesday with records in parts of Southern California: true.** weather.com reports six California gauges tied or set records on Nov 25. KTLA relays NOAA's report of new or broken records at Los Angeles, San Diego, La Jolla and Santa Barbara.
- **Pacifica lost part of its pier: true.** Supported by weather.com's caption on its king-tide report.
- **Kelvin wave exacerbated the flooding: true.** NOAA, as relayed by KTLA, says Kelvin waves raise baseline water levels by 6 to 12 inches.

**Quotes and attributed claims**
- **Swain, on baseline levels and the king tide / El Niño overlap: true.** Storms added swell on top of that baseline, so his point is consistent with the weather.com account. The article's sentence is garbled, but the substance holds. I did not verify the "super El Niño" label independently.
- **Lucey-Renteria, "worse in December, similar in January": true.** NOAA's Greg Dusek, quoted by weather.com, expects more king-tide flooding in Christmas week and late January.
- **Auger, more 7-plus-foot tides than the early 2000s: true on direction, baseline untested.** Los Angeles highs of 7 ft or more averaged 13.5 a year in 2010-19 and 16.3 a year in 2020-25, with 41 so far in 2026. The warehouse starts in 2010, so the early-2000s baseline is untested. The gauge is Los Angeles, not Newport Beach.

**Weaker or unconfirmed**
- **$125M Naples seawall project "on hold briefly" over ballooning costs: mostly true.** The Long Beach Post piece says the city needs $125M to finish the wall, tied to an oil-revenue decline. LAist says officials halted reconstruction. Neither mentions ballooning phase costs or a brief hold.
- **"Tidelands Operations Fund with a severe budget deficit": unsupported.** The Long Beach Post page the article links never mentions the fund. It does say oil restrictions could cost the city up to $20M a year and projects a $23.5M general budget deficit. I found no page stating a Tidelands fund deficit.

**Misleading framing**
Nothing materially misleading. The "historic" framing rests on gauge records that NOAA data and weather.com support. Two cautions: the records are preliminary, and the Naples budget detail is thinly sourced.
