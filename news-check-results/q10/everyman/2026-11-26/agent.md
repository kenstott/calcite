# Fact-check: White House release "Gas Prices Plunge Ahead of Thanksgiving — Just as President Trump Predicted" (Nov 24, 2026)

Method note: WebFetch returned only a summary of the release, not verbatim text, so claims below are those the summary reported. I could not read the exact "predicted" quote or its date.

## Claims and verdicts

1. National average at lowest level in eight months (attributed to Bloomberg). SUPPORTED. CNN reports $3.72 on Nov 19-20, "lowest since March 16", about eight months. I did not open the Bloomberg piece itself.
2. "Biggest three-week dive since 2008" (attributed to CNN). SUPPORTED, with a qualifier. CNN (Matt Egan) reports a 50.5 cent fall (about 12%) from Oct 29, the largest three-week drop since 2008 in AAA data back to 2000. NBC Palm Springs (Nov 19) repeats it.
3. Local drops: Birmingham AL down 11.8 cents in a week; Missouri down 19.3 cents; Wyoming down nearly 18 cents; some Oklahoma and Texas stations under $3; roughly 30 states covered. NOT CHECKABLE HERE. These are local news items I could not retrieve. They are plausible given the national trend.
4. "Just as President Trump predicted." NOT VERIFIED. I found no Trump forecast of this decline. The closest items are Energy Secretary Wright saying in spring that prices had "likely peaked", after which new 2026 highs were set.
5. Causal claim that Trump policies delivered the relief. UNSUPPORTED. Sources tie the 2026 surge and the fall mainly to the Iran conflict, Strait of Hormuz disruption, oil prices and refining margins. No source credits administration policy for the drop.

## Material omissions and misleading framing

- The prices are falling from a very high level. October 2026 averaged a record $4.31 for that month, and more than half of states were at $4 or higher. At about $3.72 the price is still well above last Thanksgiving's roughly $3.07 (AAA). GasBuddy projects about $3.62 for Thanksgiving Day, and CNN notes this could be the most expensive Thanksgiving on record (prior record $3.55-3.58, 2022). "Plunge" is accurate about the direction, but the release implies cheap gas and never says prices are higher than a year ago.
- The "lowest in eight months" line implies a low. It only means prices are back to where they were in March, before the spike.
- The release leaves out that the preceding spike happened under the same administration, in an Iran-related supply shock.
- Pairing a real decline with a credit claim is the main flaw. A three-week record drop also partly reflects the record peak it fell from.

## Rating

Two Pinocchios. The cited price statistics hold up. The causal credit and the "predicted" framing are unsupported, and the omission of year-over-year and record-level context makes the piece misleading.
