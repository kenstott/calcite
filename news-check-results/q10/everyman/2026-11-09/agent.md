# Fact-check: theGrio, "Birmingham homicides / Mayor Woodfin violence prevention" (2026-10-15)

Method: WebFetch returned only a summary of the article, not verbatim text. WebSearch only. I did not open primary documents such as the BPD Q3 report or the ARPA report, so every verdict rests on secondary reporting.

## Verdict: 2 Pinocchios (Some Pinocchios)
The 2026 headline is well supported. The prior-year baseline figures are mislabeled and do not match the police figures or year-end reports.

## Claims
| # | Claim | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 34 homicides in 2026 as of early Oct | Supported | BPD Q3 report, via ABC 33/40 (Oct 1): 34 through end of Q3. |
| 2 | 46% down vs the same point last year | Supported (approximately) | 34 vs 61 is about 44%. The mayor's figure is 46%, and another report says 45%. A rounding or cutoff-date difference. |
| 3 | 74% down vs 2024 | Supported (approximately) | 34 vs 129 is about 74%. |
| 4 | On track for fewest homicides in 75 years (since 1951) | Plausible, but a projection | The mayor's claim, repeated by theGrio. I found no independent historical series back to 1951. Also, ABC 33/40 reported a violent weekend and a spike after the Q3 cutoff, so "on track" is not final. Not checkable here for the 1951 comparison. |
| 5 | 2025 had 63 homicides, down about 51% from 130 in 2024 | Not supported (contradicted) | Year-end reports (AL.com via NNPA) give 88 for 2025 and 152 for 2024, a record. The FBI-reportable count for 2025 is about 72. The article's 63 and 130 are close to the year-to-date figures for Q3 (61 and 129). They look like partial-year numbers presented as full-year totals. The article's attribution to the "Birmingham state legislature" is odd and I could not confirm it. The real 2025 to 2024 drop is about 42%, not 51%. |
| 6 | 31 of 34 cases closed | Not checkable here | No primary clearance data found. |
| 7 | Pickett: "did not think that we would have this much of a reduction this year" | Not checkable here | I could not read the source interview. Chief Pickett's general comments are consistent with it. |
| 8 | $5,512,378 for CVI in the city's 2025 ARPA report | Not checkable here | I did not retrieve the ARPA report. |
| 9 | Programs named: Offender Alumni Association, VIPP, One Hood, Everybody WENS, GVRS, Urban League street outreach | Not checked | I did not look up these programs individually. |
| 10 | Four pillars; 10% annual reduction goal | Supported | The 5-year plan (ABC 33/40, WBHM, Alabama Reporter) sets a 10% annual cut in homicides and nonfatal shootings. |
| 11 | Woodfin: "34 lives were still lost" and the work is not finished | Consistent | Matches the 34 count. |

## Materially misleading
- The 2025 and 2024 baselines are wrong or mislabeled, which inflates the 2025 progress. The 2026 improvement stands regardless.
- "Lowest in 75 years" is the mayor's projection. The article's summary gives it as a likely outcome, not a certainty. The count after Q3 is not the final count.
- The piece is mostly mayoral and city self-reporting. ABC 33/40 has raised transparency questions about the city's violence data. I did not see that context in the summary I got.
- Crediting CVI programs for the drop is asserted and not shown causally. The funding figure by itself is not evidence of effect.

Sources: abc3340.com (Q3 update, 5-year plan, transparency stories); nnpa.org / AL.com year-end 2025; wbhm.org; alreporter.com.
