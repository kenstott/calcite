## Not checkable here

## Context and possible misleading points (apply if the article mirrors the report)
- Headline "tax breaks outpaced community spending" rests on Lown's definition of community spending, which excludes Medicaid shortfall, research and medical education. The AHA and hospitals dispute this, saying the omitted items are real community benefit. Lown itself says that counting all revenue streams hospitals mostly break even. An article that omits this contested definition would mislead.
- Tax break values are Lown's estimates (not realized payments), and the earlier Lown release (2022) counted a narrower set of community spending. Year-over-year comparisons are therefore sensitive to method changes.
- "52%" is roughly half, not "most" in a strong sense; the headline's "most" is accurate but marginal. Lown's earlier reports gave higher shares (80% in a 2021-data report; 54% in the 2025 state report using 2020-2022 data), so methodology changes matter.
