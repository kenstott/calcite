# Fact-check: KFF Health News / Paula Span, "The New Old Age" (early Social Security claims spike)

Limits: WebFetch returned a summary, not verbatim text, so exact quotes were not re-read word for word. Only a few claims were checked against primary sources, and no charts were made.

## Claims and verdicts
1. Early claiming at 62 fell from 31% (2019) to 26% (2023) for men and from 34% to 27% for women (Boston College CRR). Not independently checked. Not checkable here.
2. Urban Institute (Smalligan and a colleague) found about 267,000 more claims in Oct 2024 to Apr 2025, a 13% rise, against typical swings of 2-3%. SUPPORTED. Multiple outlets (CBS, Axios, NPR) report 267,000 and 13%. One AOL piece says 276,000, which looks like a transposition. The Urban Institute said late-boomer growth does not fully explain the rise.
3. Claims fell by about 235,000 (about 10%) from Oct 2025 to Apr 2026, "almost an exact reversal" (Smalligan). Corroborated only by a KFF-derived summary. The 10% figure is internally consistent, since 235,000 is roughly 10% of an elevated base. Plausible, not primary-verified.
4. Turmoil at the Social Security Administration (SSA), including DOGE, staff cuts and false fraud claims, drove the spike, and headlines about fear made people say they would claim about a year earlier. This is a causal inference, and the researchers frame it as likely rather than proven. Rival factors include the Social Security Fairness Act and Peak 65. Partly supported.
5. The retirement trust fund runs out in late 2032, with benefits up to 22% lower. The 2026 Trustees Report (June 9, 2026) puts OASI depletion in Q4 2032. The combined OASI and DI date is 2034, with about a 19% cut. The 22% figure was not found and is unverified. "Retirement trust fund" is accurate for OASI alone.
6. Sens. Warren and Moreno are preparing bipartisan legislation to lift the payroll tax cap above $184,500. The $184,500 cap was not confirmed in my search. Not checkable here.
7. SSA says it provides its best customer service ever and did not answer questions on the spike. This is an attributed statement and not checkable.

## Rating
1 Pinocchio (minor issues, essentially accurate). Verified figures match. The main weaknesses are the causal framing and the unconfirmed 22% cut.

## Misleading or context flags
- Causation: the spike followed DOGE-era news, but the 2025 reversal could also reflect pulled-forward claims, which would explain the "reversal." The piece suggests the cause without proving it.
- The 2032 and 22% figures apply to the retirement fund alone. Combined-fund figures (2034, about 19% cut) are less alarming and are not mentioned in the summary I saw.
