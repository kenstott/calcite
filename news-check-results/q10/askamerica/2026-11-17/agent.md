The Iowa Farm Bureau November 2026 Crop Production article's claims about USDA corn and soybean yields, state forecasts and market reaction are accurate: 0 Pinocchios are balanced and accurate.

**Rating: 0 Pinocchios.** I found no wrong number in the article.

**What I checked.** I compared every figure against the USDA-NASS Crop Production report of November 10, 2026 and the October 2026 report (state tables, pages 6 and 10 of the PDF). I also used the warehouse tables ag.nass_crop_estimates_current, ag.nass_crop_production and ag.nass_crop_progress. I recomputed every percentage from the published bushel levels.

- **National figures:** corn yield 179.6 bu/ac (October 181.2, 2025 186.5); production 15.893 billion bushels (down 140.5 million, 0.9%, from October and down 6.3% from 2025); harvested area 88.5 million acres, unchanged from October. All match.
- **Soybeans:** 4.574 billion bushels (+7.3%), 53.3 bu/ac, 85.9 million acres (+6.8%). All match.
- **Iowa:** corn 219 bu/ac, which is one of the seven record-yield states USDA names (Arkansas, Iowa, Minnesota, Mississippi, Ohio, Tennessee, Virginia). Production 2,803 million bushels, 17.6% of the U.S. crop. Second by yield behind Washington's 241. Soybeans 65.0 bu/ac, a record. All match.
- **Tables 1 and 2:** all 2025, October and November values and all percentage changes reproduce. The twelve states shown are the twelve largest producers, and the corn production ranks did not change from October.
- **Other USDA statements:**
  - The reliability statement (90% chance the final estimate lands within 2.3%) is in the NASS text.
  - Pod counts are down in 6 of 11 states, with Kansas the most.
  - Corn harvest was 74% on Nov 1, 3 points behind the five-year average. That average (77%) came from a DTN summary, not an NASS page.
  - The soybean harvest figure matches the warehouse.
- **Market:** Dec corn $4.49 (up 9¾ cents) and Jan soybeans $12.28 (up 39¾ cents) match Brownfield's closing prices. I did not open the USDA AMS page the article cites for these.

**Quotes.** The piece has no direct quotes. Its paraphrases of USDA match the USDA text.

**Context the article leaves out (not errors).**
- USDA raised the corn yield 2.7 bu/ac in October, so November's 1.6 cut gives back part of that gain.
- The rally was modest, about 2% in Dec corn.
- USDA cut its projected corn farm price by 10 cents to $4.60, and part of the soybean gain came from lower ending stocks and higher exports.
- The "good one for Iowa" framing is favorable but rests on accurate numbers.
