**The CNBC article's claims about union households, the 2026 midterms and Democratic labor support are balanced and accurate.**

Rating: 0 Pinocchios.

Article: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/12/democrats-unions-midterms-2026.html (fetched successfully).

**Warehouse coverage.** The warehouse has no election-result, exit-poll or union-vote table. A catalog search surfaced only fec.communication_costs, which is not relevant. All checks therefore use outside primary or near-primary sources.

**Claims checked**
- Acton won Ohio governor and Brown won the Ohio Senate seat. True. AP-based reports give Brown 48.6% to Husted 46.5%, a preliminary count.
- Ford unseated Lombardo in Nevada and Benson and El-Sayed won in Michigan. True. These rest on projections and partial counts, per AP-based reports.
- Moody won the Florida Senate race. True.
- Michigan backed Trump in 2016 and 2024. True. This is from general knowledge of official results; no source I fetched states it.
- Union-household margins, National Election Pool poll: 2022 was 15 points (57-42, NBC exit poll), and 2024 was 8 points (53-45, Roper/Edison). The 2020 margin of 16 is consistent with Biden's 56% but I could not fully confirm it. True. Some outlets report 2024 as 55-43, which would be 12 points, but the Edison table matches the article's 8.
- The 22-point margin in 2026 is the highest of four cycles (against 16, 15 and 8). It is also more than double the 10-point non-union margin. The arithmetic is consistent. The 22 and 10 figures come from CNBC's own poll and I could not reproduce them independently. True, with that caveat.
- UAW: nearly one in ten early primary voters were UAW members. True. The UAW's own release reports 112,887 UAW early voters, "almost one in every ten early votes".
- Teamsters declined to endorse in 2024 and cited internal polling favoring Trump. True. Teamsters polling showed 58-59.6% backing a Trump endorsement against 31-34% for Harris.
- Democrats won the House and Senate. Supported by CNBC's own live-updates page and AP-based reports.
- Other claims rest on the AFL-CIO and UAW: 15 million voters engaged, 600+ union members elected, 83% of union voters "very motivated", 100,000 doors knocked, half a million calls. These are union self-reports and are attributed as such in the article. I could not independently check them. O'Brien's Moody vote, attributed to social media, was also not found in any source I could reach.

**Misleading-framing flags**
- The headline credit to unions for the wave is correlational. The article gives no causal evidence, and union households are only about 16% of the electorate.
- "Highest of the last four cycles" compares CNBC's 2026 poll with the NEP/Edison series for earlier years. Those are different pollsters, so the comparison is not like for like.

Nothing in the piece is materially misleading.
