The Insurance Journal article's claims about FEMA individual assistance, Texas disaster funding and Hill Country flood relief are partly false.

# Rating: 2 Pinocchios

Article: "FEMA Won't Help Texas After July 2026 Floods" (Insurance Journal magazine, Nov. 9, 2026; a Texas Tribune story by Emily Foxhall, originally Oct. 21).

## Key finding
Figures and quotes match the Tribune original, but the headline and lead are stale. FEMA denied Individual Assistance (IA) around Oct. 21. Texas (TDEM chief Nim Kidd) appealed Oct. 27 for 20 of 30 counties, and FEMA approved IA for 20 counties, including Kerr and Uvalde, on Oct. 30 (Fox 26 Houston; Texas Tribune Oct. 28). The Nov. 9 piece says the governor is "reviewing the decision and the process for appealing it" and never mentions the reversal. The hazard mitigation appeal was still pending. Materially misleading: a reader would conclude individuals have no federal aid.

## Author and fidelity claims
- FEMA denied IA for July 2026 floods (central): true Oct. 21, reversed Oct. 30; mostly false as of publication.
- Abbott/TDEM reviewing appeal process: false, stale.
- Abbott's September announcement for local governments, over six weeks after the flood: true. Warehouse disasters.disaster_declarations: DR-4935-TX declared 2026-09-01, incident began 2026-07-12 (~7 weeks), 26 counties; ih_program_declared false in snapshot, predating the reversal.
- July 2025 flood killed 100+: true (Fox 26 says more than 130).
- FEMA Review Council May report favors state-led response: matches Tribune; DHS PDF not opened.

## Attributed quotes (each graded as its own claim)
- Cameron Hamilton (FEMA), states "must have the appropriate financial reserves": true. Other outlets quote "Every state must have..." at the NEMA conference Oct. 20 per Politico; Politico original not opened; the article drops "Every state".
- Nim Kidd: $37M of $1.9B for 2027 (2%): true per Tribune, 37/1900 = 1.95%; not in warehouse. His "very different situation this time next year" is a prediction: not checkable here.
- Allison Brim-Estelle (Rebuild): "big thing people were counting on" and "Governor Abbott owes Texas families... an explanation and a plan": opinion, not checkable here.
- Austin Dickson / Community Foundation: $1.3M, 947 households, 58 housing: true per Tribune. "Families are continuing to recover without FEMA individual assistance": mostly true when spoken; overtaken by the Oct. 30 approval.
- Andrew Mahaleris (Abbott spokesperson): "Texas will deliver every necessary resource": pledge, not checkable here.

## Sourcing note
The warehouse covers FEMA declarations only and does not yet show the Oct. 30 IA designation, so the reversal rests on Fox 26 Houston and Texas Tribune reporting. Catalog searches found no table for Texas emergency budgets or philanthropy. The two Insurance Journal topic pages linked by the article were fetched and bear on none of the claims.
