The Farm Policy News article's claims about the shrinking US inland barge fleet, grain exports and farm margins are accurate.

**Rating: 0 Pinocchios.**

The article is a roundup of coverage of one CoBank report (Nov 10, 2026). Every figure matches the CoBank report, and the arithmetic holds.

**Checked and true**
- The fleet is 19,668 barges. Average age is 17.6 years, up from 13.8 in 2016. The fleet is down 1,617 barges, or 7.6%, in 10 years. The calculation 1,617 / 21,285 gives 7.6%.
- The projection is 16,240 barges by 2035. That is 17.4% lower, or 3,428 fewer ("about 3,400"). It is a projection that assumes current trends hold, and CoBank says so.
- About 410 barges are built a year against 500 to 1,000 retired. Replating covers 100 to 200 barges a year and extends life by 12 to 15 years.
- The steel and Section 232 tariff cost explanation is CoBank's own causal claim.
- USDA projects grain and oilseed exports up 9% by 2035, as relayed by CoBank. I did not parse the USDA OCE-2026-1 tables, so this is graded on CoBank's statement.
- The warehouse (ag.fas_export_sales) shows soybean shipments peaking at about 60.6 Mt in the 2020/21-era year and staying below that since. That is consistent with "below the 2020/21 record".
- Barges carry about half of corn and soybean exports and a third of wheat. USDA AMS gives 48%, 48% and 32% for 2022. This figure is in CoBank's text but is not quoted in the article.

**Minor issues (mostly true)**
- The article says CoBank gave "$1 million or more, up from roughly $650,000 a decade ago". The CoBank report cites Opentug for the $1M figure and does not state $650,000. Opentug gives a hopper-barge range of $500,000 to over $1 million. The $650,000 appears only in secondary copies (Maritime Executive, IndexBox, World Grain).
- The Water Resources Development Act of 2026 is described as "recently proposed" and as one that "would provide more than $6 billion". The bill authorizes funds rather than appropriating them. Sources range from over $6B to nearly $7B. It has since cleared Senate committee (July) and passed the House (September), so "recently proposed" is stale.

**Framing flags**
- The fleet counts come from a single commercial database (Current Data LLC). The warehouse has no barge data (no "barge" term in the catalog), so I could not cross-check them against a government series.
- The article relays one study through five outlets. The repeated quotes are not independent confirmation.
- The 17.4% decline is a trend extrapolation. Higher freight rates would likely spur new builds, which CoBank acknowledges. CoBank also says outcomes are not driven by fleet age alone.
- Corn shipments already reached about 69 Mt in 2025, so the USDA forecast of "a new record by 2035/36" is a near-term claim.
