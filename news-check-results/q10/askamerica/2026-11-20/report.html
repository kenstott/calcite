Claim checks

  • Fleet 19,668 barges, avg age 17.6 vs 13.8 in 2016, down 1,617 or 7.6% in 10 years: matches CoBank (Current Data LLC). 1,617/(19,668+1,617)=7.6%. True.
  • 410 built vs 500-1,000 retired; fleet to 16,240 by 2035, down 17.4%, about 3,400 fewer: arithmetic holds (3,428 fewer). A projection assuming current trends hold, as CoBank states.
  • New barge about $1 million or more, up from about $650,000 a decade ago: CoBank cites Opentug for $1M and does not state $650,000; Opentug gives a hopper range of $500,000 to over $1 million.
  • USDA projects exports up 9% by 2035: matches CoBank; USDA tables not parsed. Soybean record in 2020/21: warehouse soybean shipments peak about 60.6 Mt, above all later years. Corn shipments already reached about 69 Mt in 2025, so the corn 'new record' forecast is near-term.
  • Barges carry about half of corn and soybean exports and a third of wheat: USDA AMS reports 48%/48%/32% for 2022. Consistent (in CoBank, not quoted in this article).
  • WRDA 2026 over $6 billion, 'recently proposed': a House committee said over $6B, ARTBA says nearly $7B; it authorizes rather than appropriates, and has since cleared Senate committee (July) and passed the House (September).

Misleading-framing flags

  • Fleet counts come from one commercial database (Current Data LLC); no government barge fleet series exists in the warehouse to cross-check.
  • The whole piece is one study relayed through five outlets; repeated quotes are not independent confirmation.
  • The 17.4% is a trend extrapolation; higher freight rates would likely spur new builds, which CoBank acknowledges. CoBank also says outcomes are not driven by fleet age alone.
