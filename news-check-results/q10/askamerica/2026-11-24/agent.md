# The KFF Health News article's claims about the early Social Security claims spike, its DOGE-era cause and the 2026 reversal are balanced and accurate.

Article: https://kffhealthnews.org/aging/social-security-early-claims-spike-doge-new-old-age/ (Paula Span, Nov. 19, 2026)

**Rating: 0 Pinocchios** (engine rating from claim scores). Scores describe this article only.

## Verdict
The headline numbers hold up against SSA's own monthly retirement-application data. A few blemishes are worth correcting.

## Verified
- Surge, Oct 2024-Apr 2025: 2,087,629 to 2,364,201 = +276,572 (+13.25%). Urban reported 276,000+ and 13%.
- Reversal, Oct 2025-Apr 2026: 2,364,201 to 2,128,993 = -235,208 (-9.95%). Matches "235,000, about 10%".
- CRR claiming-at-62 and 67+ shares (31 to 26, 34 to 27, 19 to 26, 20 to 25) match CRR Figure 1 data exactly.
- 2026 Trustees Report: OASI depleted Q4 2032, 78% payable, so up to 22% lower. Cap $184,500. FRA, 8% delayed credits, 3,118 experiment participants confirmed. Musk's Ponzi remark confirmed (Rogan podcast, Feb 2025).

## Problems
1. Surge printed as 267,000; the data show about 276,572 (digits transposed). The 13% is right.
2. "$3 trillion ... according to the Social Security trustees" is misattributed. The Warren/Moreno op-ed says "according to one estimate" (linked to the Peterson Foundation); the Trustees Report highlights contain no such figure. Graded mostly false.
3. "Almost an exact reversal": about 85% of the surge was recovered; FY2026 Oct-Apr is still about 41,000 (2%) above FY2024.
4. Causal framing: the DOGE-turmoil explanation is Smalligan's inference and is presented as the cause. Urban's own post also lists aging, spousal-notification changes (about 50,000 extra claims at 71+) and Social Security Fairness Act confusion, which inflated the FY2025 base. AARP's survey supports a fear mechanism (self-reported).
5. The 2021 experiment: a full year earlier applied only to the "insolvency" headline; "reserves depleted" gave half a year (not significant). Outcome was intended, not actual, claiming.
6. Brookings raises the taxable maximum to cover 90% of wages; it does not eliminate the cap.
7. SSA's series counts retirement applications, not awards; the article says "claims" with no caveat.

## Unverifiable
Turner's $5,000/yr and Mansson's $300/mo are personal figures (unsupported, plausible against SSA's reduction table).
