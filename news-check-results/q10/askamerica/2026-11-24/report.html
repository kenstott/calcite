What checked out

  • Surge: SSA CSV, Oct-Apr FY2024 = 2,087,629 vs FY2025 = 2,364,201, +276,572 (+13.25%). Urban reported 'more than 276,000' and 13%.
  • Reversal: FY2026 Oct-Apr = 2,128,993, down 235,208 (-9.95%).
  • CRR figures (62: men 31% to 26%, women 34% to 27%; 67+: men 19% to 26%, women 20% to 25%) match CRR's Figure 1 data exactly.
  • 2026 Trustees: OASI depleted Q4 2032; 78% of scheduled benefits payable, so up to 22% lower.
  • Cap $184,500 (2026); FRA 67 for 1960+ and 66 and 6 months for 1957 births (SSA table); 3,118 experiment participants; Musk's Ponzi remark and CPAC chainsaw are documented.

What did not check out or is misleading

  1. 267,000 vs 276,572. Urban and SSA data give about 276,000-277,000; the 13% is right.
  2. $3 trillion 'according to the Social Security trustees'. The Warren/Moreno op-ed says 'according to one estimate' and links a Peterson Foundation page; the 2026 Trustees highlights contain no such figure.
  3. 'Almost an exact reversal'. About 85% of the surge was recovered; FY2026 Oct-Apr is still about 41,000 (2%) above FY2024.
  4. Single-cause framing. Urban lists aging, spousal-notification changes (about 50,000 extra claims at 71+) and SSFA confusion besides DOGE-related fear; the article does not say these inflated the FY2025 base.
  5. Headline experiment. A full year earlier applied to the 'insolvency' headline; 'reserves depleted' moved intended claiming age half a year, not statistically significant. Intended, not actual, claiming.
  6. Brookings. The plan raises the taxable maximum to cover 90% of wages; it does not eliminate the cap as the senators propose. The article's 'calls for that, too' is loose.
  7. Scope. SSA's series counts retirement applications, not awards; the article calls them 'claims' without caveat.

Provenance: NYT-hosted links returned HTTP 403, so the NYT pieces themselves were not read. The warehouse has no monthly claims table, so figures are my own computations from SSA's published CSV.
