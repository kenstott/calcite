Step 1 -- Which tables could speak to this claim

search_catalog surfaced several Census income tables: census.acs1_income (ACS 1-Year, state-level, most current single-year Census income estimate in this corpus), census.acs_income (ACS 5-Year, smoothed, ~2 years behind), census.income_summary (a view over acs_income), and census.saipe_poverty (Small Area Income and Poverty Estimates). None of these carry the Current Population Survey Annual Social and Economic Supplement (CPS ASEC) -- the specific survey the Census's national 'real median household income' headline figure is drawn from every September. search_catalog's own unmatched-terms flag confirmed "asec" matches nothing in this corpus. This is an important distinction: the Census's annual income/poverty/health-insurance press release (the one behind this claim) is always CPS ASEC-based, not ACS-based, and the two surveys produce different point estimates for the same concept by design (different sample, different collection method, different weighting).

Step 2 -- Querying the data directly (data_coverage + query)

data_coverage on census.acs1_income confirms the table's actually-loaded window is 2005-2024 (2020 is an interior gap -- no ACS 1-Year collection that year due to COVID), with 2025 not yet loaded (the ACS itself publishes ~1 year behind, so 2025 ACS 1-Year data would not be expected until roughly September 2026 at the earliest, and evidently is not in this corpus yet). The table is also state-level only, with no national 'United States' row (confirmed empty on a direct query).

As a contextual proxy (NOT a recomputation of the claimed CPS ASEC figure), a simple unweighted average of ACS 1-Year median household income across all 50 states + DC by year:

SELECT year, avg(median_household_income) AS avg_state_median
FROM census.acs1_income
WHERE year IN ('2019','2021','2022','2023','2024')
GROUP BY year ORDER BY year

Results: 2019 = $64,645; 2021 = $68,339; 2022 = $73,477; 2023 = $76,589; 2024 = $80,424 (nominal dollars, not inflation-adjusted, and a simple state average rather than population-weighted, so it is directionally informative but not a national point estimate). The direction -- steady increases each year -- is consistent with the Census's own reported 2024-to-2025 increase, but the level and the exact percentage cannot be checked against this corpus because it is a different survey, a different weighting, and does not yet reach 2025.
