**Access limit.** The Fierce page returned HTTP 403 and no copy of it loaded, including Becker's. I could not read its sentences or quotes. I graded the claims that same-day coverage of the Lown report carried (GBH, Boston Globe, Ideastream) against Lown's own page and press release. Only two claims were formally graded. Every other sentence in the Fierce piece is ungraded.

**Warehouse.** The catalog has no table with hospital tax-exemption value or Form 990 Schedule H community benefit, so Lown's deficit cannot be recomputed here. The only partial check was the CMS cost-report extract (health.hospital_cost_report_financials). Its fiscal_year 2024 rows show Mayo Rochester net income of about $1.23B, MGH about $373M and NYP about $1.58B. That shows the named hospitals are very profitable. It does not test Lown's deficit.

**Graded claims (both true against Lown's page and press release, FYE 2023):**
- More than half of hospitals gave back less than they received in tax exemptions: 52% of 3,037 hospitals. This is the central claim.
- Mass General ranked fifth with a $219M deficit.

**Figures that also match Lown (not formally graded):**
- Deficit hospitals received $27.5B and gave back $12B, a $15.5B net deficit.
- Surplus hospitals received $11.3B and gave back $25.1B, a net benefit of $13.8B.
- Total exemptions were $38.8B, with $240B in real estate.
- Mayo Rochester's deficit was $366M, the largest.

**Misleading by omission, if the Fierce piece omits what the other outlets reported:**
- Lown's "community spending" excludes Medicaid shortfall, health professions education and research. Medicaid shortfall is nearly half of reported community benefit. Mass General Brigham, Mayo, Cleveland Clinic and the AHA dispute this. MGB says its IRS-defined benefit was $507M, more than $150M above its tax exemptions.
- Tax-exemption values are Lown's estimates, built partly from property assessments. Lown excludes Medicaid disproportionate share payments and 340B discounts. High-real-estate areas such as Boston, New York and California are structurally more likely to show deficits.
- The dollar picture is less lopsided than "half" implies. Surplus hospitals' $13.8B net benefit offsets most of the $15.5B net deficit.
- The data are FYE 2023, three years old at publication. Lown is an advocacy think tank, and its 2025 report used 2021 filings, so year-to-year comparisons are not like for like.

**Rating: 1 Pinocchio, provisional.** This rating is not in the published report, which was refused when it stated a rating. The figures relay Lown accurately. The one point against it is that the "tax breaks outpaced community spending" framing treats a contested, Lown-defined metric as settled. Confidence is limited because the Fierce text was not read.
