The CIDRAP article's claims about newborn hepatitis B vaccination rates, the ACIP vote and the Truveta study are somewhat biased and mostly accurate.

**Rating: 1 Pinocchio.**

Sources read: the article, the NEJM Evidence/CIDRAP Public Health Alerts PDF (full text), CIDRAP's ACIP-vote, HHS-schedule and court-ruling stories, the VIP report (first pages) and the Public Health Alerts index. The NEJM Evidence editorial and index pages returned HTTP 403. The warehouse has only national births (health.cdc_wonder_natality: 3,596,017 in 2023; 3,628,934 in 2024), which confirms the study's 3.6M denominator. It has no birth-dose coverage or EHR table, so study figures were graded against the primary paper, not recomputed from independent data.

**Verified as true (matches the paper):** 1,338,961 infants / 1,075,767 mothers; 90.5% with an HBV test; 0.18% HBsAg-positive; no-test share 15.6% (2018) vs 7.6% (2026); 1,016,149 (75.9%) vaccinated within a day; 76.4% (2018), 79.8% (2022); 96.9% / 75.9% / 76.8% / 75.4% by maternal status; 97-98% falling to 93.9% in Jan-Jun 2026 among HBsAg-positive mothers; ~360,000 and >640 (authors' estimates; 10% x 3.6M, 0.18% x 360,000 = 648); ACIP vote Dec 2025 ending the 1991 universal recommendation; schedule cut 17 to 11 and the March 2026 court block; VIP report (Dec 2, 2025) preceded the vote and CIDRAP leads VIP (disclosed).

**Errors and weaknesses:**
- HBsAg is called an antibody; it is the surface antigen (mostly false label, correct 0.18% figure).
- Article says 'median' of 80.0% for 2020-22; the paper says 'mean'.
- Lede says the fall came 'after' the ACIP downgrade. The paper says the decline began in mid-2023 and predated the vote (Table 1 implies 77.9% in 2023, 75.4% in 2024, 69.0% in 2025, 65.6% Jan-Jun 2026). The article mentions mid-2023 only deep in the text. Causal framing is overstated.
- A Truveta EHR sample, with vaccination counted only if recorded within 1 day of birth, is presented as 'the rate of babies' nationally.
- 'Without presenting data to support the decision' is CIDRAP's characterization. Its own vote story quotes Hibbeln saying no science was presented on the timing point only, and shows the meeting did include risk-benefit discussion.
- 'Unscreened' mothers means no test evidence in the EHR, not proven no screening. Typo: 'aren't screen'.
