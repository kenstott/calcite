Verdicts (9 claims: 8 true, 1 mostly true; honesty 97/100)
- Central: excess mortality among Black adults fell 56% (men) and 68% (women), 1993-2023. TRUE to the ACS press release of 2026-11-23. This is fidelity only: the Annals paper returned HTTP 403, a web search found no copy, and the warehouse holds no race-specific 1993-2023 all-cause series. The figure rests on the sponsor's own release.
- Cancer, heart disease and HIV drove progress: true (release).
- Cancer now ranks sixth and HIV tenth for Black men: true (release).
- Homicide gap fell in the 1990s, rose sharply after 2010, erased about two decades of progress; COVID nearly doubled the overall gap in 2020: true (release).
- "Planned to mark" the Heckler Report's 40th anniversary: mostly true. The release says it was planned in 2025 and "coincides with" the anniversary.
- JAMA Oncology (July 2026): lung 31.1% plus prostate 25.8% = 56.9% of the narrowing among Black men: true.
- Yale/JAMA 2023: 1.63 million excess deaths and more than 80 million years of potential life lost, 1999-2020: true (Yale release). The article correctly notes the methods differ.
- "A large gap remains": true. Warehouse health.chr_premature_death (CHR 2025 release, a different measure and vintage): Black premature-death rate 13,698 vs white 8,038 per 100,000, ratio 1.70.
- OTC naloxone: true (FDA approval, March 29, 2023).
- Not independently checked: "often free through local health departments" and the age-45 colorectal screening guideline.

Framing concerns (the facts hold, but note these)
- The headline "Overdose Gaps Persist" sharpens the release's category "accidents (including drug overdoses)". Overdose is not separately quantified.
- The lede "excess deaths fell 56%" can read as deaths falling. The article clarifies this later.
- The 1993 baseline is near the gap's peak, which flatters the percentage reduction. The article does not say so.
- All study content comes from the sponsor's release, with no independent expert comment.
