Summary

Rating: 1 Pinocchio (mostly accurate, with caveats). Recomputed from BLS state payroll (SM) data, Nevada had the nation's highest year-over-year job growth in each of the 11 months July 2025 to May 2026 (May 2026: +2.0%, next best West Virginia +1.23%). The 1.6 million jobs and every unemployment-rate comparison check out. Problems: the release says "total employment" and links the household-survey (LAUS) page, but on the LAUS household employment measure Nevada was NOT first in April or May 2026 (South Carolina led; May: 2.65% vs Nevada 1.97%). The "$6 billion in private investment" is the governor's own figure; it has appeared as $5 billion and as a five-year incentive projection. The causal link to Lombardo's policies is asserted, not shown.

Idaho led June 2025, so the streak starts July 2025: eleven months through May. The warehouse holds the current benchmark-revised, not-seasonally-adjusted vintage; the release used the then-published seasonally adjusted figure (1.8%). LAUS household employment: Nevada first Jan-Mar 2026, South Carolina first Apr-Sep 2026. May 2026 unemployment: DC 6.1, CA 5.3, WA/OR/NV 5.2, CT/DE/MI/IL 5.1, an exact match. Nevada payroll first reached 1.6 million in Sep 2025 (1,603.5K); no earlier month in the loaded 2022-2025 data did.

Misleading framing

The headline streak is true for payroll jobs only; the release calls it "total employment" and cites the LAUS page. A reader checking household employment would find it fails for April and May. The leisure and hospitality softness DETR's economist concedes is absent from the governor's text.

Data-quality notes and exclusions

Fanout warning: my first-pass self-join queries on econ.regional_employment and econ.state_industry returned duplicated rows (the tables' keys include type and frequency). Rankings and growth rates are ratios computed per state, so duplication does not change them; the final figures were re-run with DISTINCT. Exclusion: the SM queries left out annual-average rows (period M13) so only monthly values are compared; including them would not change any monthly figure. An attempt to compute supersector (industry) growth returned no rows because the series-code positions were wrong, so industry claims were not verified.
