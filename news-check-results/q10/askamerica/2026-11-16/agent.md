**Problems**

2. Tariffs "ruled unconstitutional" is loose. In Learning Resources v. Trump the Supreme Court held IEEPA does not authorize the tariffs, a statutory ruling. Graded mostly true.
3. Causal attributions (corporate tax "tax cuts", EPA cuts, food-stamp cuts) are unsupported. The amounts reproduce but Treasury totals do not attribute them to those causes.
4. Misleading framing: receipts are adjusted for inflation and growth while outlays are adjusted for inflation only. On the same baseline the "$152bn spending increase" would be about -$9bn. The article does disclose an $11bn growth comparison. Also, nominal customs duties fell ($195bn to $173bn), so the "large increase in tariffs" is visible only gross of refunds.

6. The 3.3% inflation and 2.3% growth assumptions are ICAEW's own. September 2026 CPI-U was 3.7% year over year, so they are not checkable as a single published figure.
