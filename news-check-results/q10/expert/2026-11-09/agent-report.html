Birmingham homicide claims: 2 Pinocchios

Summary of findings

The 2026 decline is real (34 vs 61 vs 129 thru Q3), but the article presents year-to-date figures as full-year 2025 (63) and 2024 (130); actual year-end totals were 88 and 152.

Per step: Answer / Key values / Sources / Confidence.

1. Claim: 34 homicides in 2026 as of October; 31 closed. Answer: supported. BPD: 34 thru Q3 2026; 91% clearance (31/34 = 91%). Source: ABC 3340 2026-10-01. Confidence: medium-high.

2. Claim: 63 homicides in 2025, down ~51% from 130 in 2024. Answer: WRONG as full-year labels. Year-end 2025 was 88, 2024 was 152 (AL.com via NNPA). BPD thru Q3: 2025=61, 2024=129. The article's 63/130 are year-to-date same-point figures. Confidence: high.

3. Claim: down 46% vs last year, 74% vs 2024. Answer: 74% checks (34 vs 129). 46% matches 34 vs 63; vs BPD's 61 it is 44%. Minor. Confidence: medium.

4. Claim: fewest homicides in ~75 years (1951). Answer: Woodfin's own claim, relayed by AL.com; no independent historical series found. Mark as attributed, not independently verified.

5. Claim: Pickett quote "did not think that we would have this much of a reduction". Not found in sources I could access (ABC 3340 article has only paraphrase). Not checkable here.

6. Claim: ARPA report $5,512,378 to CVI incl. Offender Alumni Association and VIP2 hospital program. Programs real; BirminghamWatch reports about $5.5M CVI allocation; exact figure not confirmed to the city report. Confidence: medium.

7. Claim: four pillars, 10% annual reduction goal, One Hood, NICJR, Urban League. 10% annual goal for five years confirmed (BirminghamWatch/WBHM 2026-08-31 plan). Other program names not individually verified.

8. Misleading context: omits that 2025 was itself a big drop from record 2024 (152); omits domestic violence offenses up 18.3% and a reported recent spike in homicides/shootings (ABC 3340); attributes decline to CVI while BPD attributes to enforcement; correlation not causation.
