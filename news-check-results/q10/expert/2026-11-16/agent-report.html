Fact-check: ICAEW 'Chart of the week: US federal deficit' (12 Nov 2026)

Headline numbers check out; line-item changes are real-terms but read as nominal: 1 Pinocchio

Every headline figure matches Treasury: FY2026 deficit $1,992bn (FY2025 $1,775bn), receipts $5,403bn, outlays $7,396bn, debt $40.17tn ($32.43tn public, $7.75tn intragovernmental). Grade: 1 Pinocchio (minor issue, not materially deceptive).

  • The per-item changes (corporate tax -$96bn, customs -$33bn, health +$57bn, interest +$49bn, Social Security +$34bn) do not match nominal Treasury changes (-$71bn, -$22bn, +$123bn, +$81bn, +$86bn). They match exactly once FY2025 is inflated by 3.3%, so they are real-terms deltas. A reader comparing with the Treasury statement would find them wrong unless this is stated clearly. I could read the article only through a summarised fetch, so I cannot confirm how prominently the basis is stated.
  • The CBO comparison uses the February 2026 baseline ($1,853bn); actual came in $139bn higher, so it is stale, not wrong. A later CBO estimate was about $2.1tn per search results.
  • Not verified: 3.3% inflation, 2.3% growth, GDP share ratios (6.2%, 125%), exact CBO $3,115bn and $46.7tn, a December CBO options report (no source found).
  • Deficit explanation (inflation/growth/receipt/spending) is a stylised bridge; it reconciles arithmetically but is the author's construction.

Method and confidence

Answer: as above. Key values: FY ended 30 Sep 2026, $ millions from MTS. Confidence: high for MTS/MSPD matches (read directly from the Treasury statement PDF); medium for the real-terms explanation (my reconstruction matches within $1bn on all seven items). Treasury's Fiscal Data API was unavailable (outage page), and cbo.gov returned 403, so CBO figures come via Reuters/CRFB/CBO search summaries. No quotes attributed to named people appear in the piece as summarised.
