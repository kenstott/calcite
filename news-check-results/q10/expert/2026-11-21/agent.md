# Fact-check: Fierce Healthcare, "Most nonprofit hospitals' tax breaks outpaced their community spending in 2023: Lown Institute"

## Summary of findings
Overall: 1 Pinocchio (mostly accurate; headline figures match Lown's own release). Caveat: Fierce's page returned HTTP 403 (Cloudflare) to every fetch, so I could NOT read the article text. Claims below are those reported for it via search excerpts and checked against Lown primary pages. Treat per-sentence grading of the article as unverified.

- 52% of nonprofit hospitals receive more in tax breaks than they spend on charity care and community investment: confirmed (Lown, 3,037 hospitals, FYE 2023).
- Combined deficit $15.5B: confirmed ($27.5B exempted, $12.0B given back).
- $38.8B total tax exemptions, ~$13M per hospital, $240B real estate: confirmed; $38.8B/3,037 = $12.8M.
- Mayo Clinic Hospital Rochester largest deficit, -$366M, then Tisch (-$294M), Stanford (-$255M): confirmed.
- Saini quote ("Tax exemptions aren't a gift - they're an expectation"): confirmed in Lown release.
- Misleading context: 48% of hospitals had surpluses totaling $13.8B (net -$1.7B nationally, derived: $37.1B returned vs $38.8B exempted). "Outpaced" headline is true for most hospitals but aggregate gap is small. Lown excludes Medicaid shortfall, research, medical education (AHA/Mayo dispute this); the figure is Lown's definition, not an IRS-style total community benefit. Tax-break values are Lown estimates, not reported amounts.
- Not checkable: the exact Fierce wording, and any quotes/figures in the piece beyond the above.
