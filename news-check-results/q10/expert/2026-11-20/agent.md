# Fact-check: barge fleet article

Verdict: 1 Pinocchio. All checkable figures match CoBank (Nov 10 2026): 19,668 barges, 17.6 yrs vs 13.8, -1,617/7.6%, 410 built, 500-1,000 retired, 16,240 by 2035 (-17.4%), replating 100-200/yr +12-15 yrs, $1M vs $650k.

- Fleet 19,668 barges in 2026: Supported (CoBank, Current Data LLC)
- Avg age 17.6 vs 13.8 in 2016: Supported
- Down 1,617 / 7.6% in 10 yrs: Supported; arithmetic consistent (1,617/21,285=7.6%)
- ~410 built/yr; 500-1,000 retired: Supported
- 16,240 by 2035, ~3,400 fewer (17%): Supported; 19,668-16,240=3,428 = 17.4%. It is a projection
- Replating 100-200/yr adds 12-15 yrs: Supported
- New barge ~$1M+, ~$650k a decade ago: Supported ($1M in CoBank; $650k in Maritime Executive; not on the CoBank page text I retrieved)
- Steel prices elevated partly due to Section 232 tariffs: Supported as CoBank attribution; causal share not quantified
- USDA: grain/oilseed exports +9% by 2035: Plausible, matches CoBank; I could not retrieve the USDA 2035 baseline table
- WRDA 2026 would provide >$6B: Roughly supported (committee version); article calls it proposed, but the House passed H.R. 9497 415-9 on Sept 16; it is an authorization, not funding
- 'structural tightening' (CoBank analysts): Supported via Maritime Executive; not found on the CoBank page by my fetch tool (limited reliability); AgWeb/World Grain returned 403
- Aging locks and dams vulnerable: Not independently checked (general; Corps data not retrieved)

Confidence: high on fleet figures; medium on USDA 9% and quote (secondary only). See agent-report.html.
