# Fact-check: KCUR, "Kansas City 4th-warmest fall ever, electricity bills higher" (2026-11-16)

## Summary: Weather claims hold up; the bills link is unverified and thinly supported. Rating: 1 Pinocchio (of 4)

- Fall ranking: from NOAA RCC-ACIS daily mean temps for the Kansas City area (MCIthr, 136 complete seasons), Sep 1-Nov 15 2026 averaged 66.0 F, 5th warmest (1963, 1931, 1956/1897 ahead). Through Nov 24 it ranks 3rd (63.9 F). "4th" is within the window/station ambiguity. Anomaly vs 1991-2020 is +6.8 F (Sep1-Nov15), close to the article's "7-8 F". Confidence medium-high.
- Latest first freeze on record Nov 24, 1931: CONFIRMED (min <=32 F; next 1918-11-22). 2026 first freeze was Nov 12. High confidence.
- "139 years" of records: consistent with the 1888 start. High.
- NOT CHECKABLE here: BPU Nov bills $94.96 -> $117.21 (+23.4%), 23% usage rise over three years, United Way 211 changes (gas -~20%, electric -~5.5%, homelessness prevention +~130%). No public source found. Quotes (Spencer Mell, Zachary Leasor, Kera Mashek) could not be independently confirmed.
- Misleading: the headline ties a warm fall to higher bills, but the piece's own data show electric/gas bill-help requests fell, and a 3-year usage trend, a partial/preliminary month and possible rate changes are not separated from weather. Correlation is not shown.
- Would change the conclusion: BPU data confirming the figures, or a rate change explaining the bill rise.

Sources: KCUR article; NOAA RCC-ACIS StnData (data.rcc-acis.org); web searches found no BPU/United Way confirmation.
