Fact-check: "Since 2014, Minnesota's median household income growth ranks 49th out of 50 states"

Piece: John Phelan, Center of the American Experiment. Checked 2026-11-23.

Accurate numbers, but a fragile headline rank: 1 Pinocchio

Every figure in the piece reproduces from primary data. Real median household income in Minnesota rose 7.3% from 2014 to 2025 (US +23.6%), 49th of 50 states on point estimates. The GDP-per-capita premium over the US went from +$4,658 (2014) to -$2,767 (2025), 2017 dollars.

  • All nine checkable claims verified: the GDP gap values, the family-of-four multiples, the one-year exception (2022), the 2023 sign flip, ranks 17th and 46th for GDP per capita, 16th and 49th for median income, and the significant correlation (r=0.52, n=50, p=0.0001).
  • Materially misleading by omission: the 49th rank comes from the Current Population Survey, whose state medians carry large sampling error. Minnesota's standard error is about 5.5% of its median in 2025 (4.8% in 2014). A simulation using the published standard errors puts Minnesota's rank at 49 or worse only about 25% of the time (median 45; 5th-95th percentile 28-50). Using 3-year averages at both ends (2013-15 vs 2023-25), Minnesota ranks 45th (+7.1%). The honest statement is "bottom decile", not "49th".
  • Minnesota's level is still high: median income ranked 6th in 2014 and 17th in 2025 ($95,160, versus $87,460 for the US, 2025 dollars). Slow growth from a high base is partly convergence.
  • The start year is a choice; 2014 was a high point for Minnesota's GDP premium. The 1997-2014 comparison crosses the 2013 CPS income-question redesign, so that half is a different series.
  • The GDP and income relationship is a state-level association (ecological inference); it does not show that GDP growth causes income growth or explain Minnesota's slowdown.
  • Not done: the ACS 1-year check (the Census API requires a key, which I did not use), and the earlier posts the piece links to.

Method and sources

Growth rates are computed in 2025 dollars from the Census table block, excluding DC and the US. GDP per capita is BEA real GDP in chained 2017 dollars divided by BEA population (SAINC1 line 2). All 50 states, n=50 each. Quotes: the piece contains none. Series gaps: none among the years used.

  • BEA Regional SAGDP (real GDP, chained 2017$): https://apps.bea.gov/regional/zip/SAGDP.zip
  • BEA SAINC (population): https://apps.bea.gov/regional/zip/SAINC.zip
