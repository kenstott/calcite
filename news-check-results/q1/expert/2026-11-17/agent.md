# Fact-check: MPA "Existing-home sales fall to their lowest level since 2010" (12 Nov 2026)

**Rating: 1 Pinocchio (mostly accurate; one materially incomplete framing).** All NAR figures match NAR's 12 Nov 2026 release. The mortgage-rate framing ("highest since 2000") omits NAR's own exception: one month in 2023.

## Claims
| Claim | Result |
|---|---|
| Sales -5.5% m/m to 3.75M SAAR; -8.8% y/y | Verified (NAR) |
| Lowest since Aug 2010 / "16 years" | Verified (NAR, Yun) |
| West -9.7%, South -6.5%, Midwest -3.2%, Northeast flat | Verified |
| First-time buyers 29% (31% Sep, 32% yr ago) | Verified |
| Investors 20% vs 16% yr ago | Verified (NAR: investors and second-home buyers) |
| Inventory +1.3% to 1.59M; 5.1 months vs 4.7 | Verified |
| Median $416,700, +0.4%, 40th straight y/y gain | Verified |
| Affordability index 100.7 vs 107.3 | Verified |
| Freddie Mac 30-yr avg 7.33% in October | Verified via NAR; my weekly readings (7.28, 7.40, 7.32, 7.24; Oct 8 missing) average about 7.31%, consistent but not exact |
| Yun quote "Home buying is difficult when mortgage rates have climbed to their highest levels since 2000" | Verified, but NAR's text adds an exception for one month in 2023 |
| Rate "highest since 2000" | Misleading: Oct 2023 weekly PMMS readings 7.49/7.57/7.63/7.79 (mean 7.62%) exceed 7.33% |
| Shraybman (Innovative Mortgage Brokers) quotes | Not checkable here (original interview) |

## Caveats
Source pages were summarised by the fetch tool, not read raw. Oct 2023 mean is my calculation from weekly values. Freddie Mac monthly archive not directly accessible.

Sources: nar.realtor/newsroom/existing-home-sales-decreased-5-5-percent-october-nar-report; freddiemac.com/pmms; freddiemac.gcs-web.com (Oct 2023 releases); tradingeconomics.com; the MPA article.
