# Fact-check: Roll Call, "White House Cancels $810M in Health, Education Funds" (Aris Folley, 2026-09-28)

Method note: no AskAmerica connector or prior-run data used. Research conducted directly via web
search/fetch against primary and secondary reporting (Roll Call, CNBC, Fox News, Time, NPR/OPB,
NBC News, Newsweek, ABC News, NLIHC, Hoodline) and the 2025 Roll Call/CBS/NPR/JURIST coverage of
the Supreme Court order in *Department of State v. AIDS Vaccine Advocacy Coalition*.

---

## Claim 1: Total rescission is $810 million, announced days before FY end via "pocket rescission"

**Verdict:** True.

**Evidence:** Confirmed by every outlet surveyed (CNBC, Roll Call, Time, Fox News, NBC News,
Newsweek, ABC News, NLIHC). OMB Director Russell Vought's office submitted the package Friday,
September 26, 2026 — roughly five days before the September 30 fiscal year-end — explicitly
timed so Congress could not complete the Impoundment Control Act's 45-day review window. This is
the second such "pocket rescission" maneuver by this administration (first used in August 2025
for ~$4.9B, separately from the $4B foreign-aid case in Claim 7).

**Confidence:** High — figure and timing corroborated by numerous independent outlets and directly
attributed to OMB.

---

## Claim 2: Itemized breakdown — $567M HHS/ORR, $25M Education migrant students, $70M
foreign student/faculty exchange, $28M health research, $56M HUD housing counseling

**Verdict:** True, but incomplete — these five items are real and accurately sourced, but they are
not the entire package, and the article does not disclose that.

**Evidence:** All five figures are independently corroborated:
- $567M HHS/Office of Refugee Resettlement (attributed to reduced border crossings/caseload) —
  confirmed by CNBC, Fox News, Time, NLIHC.
- $25M Education Department migrant-student program — confirmed by Time, Fox News.
- $70M foreign student/faculty exchange programs — confirmed by Time, OPB summary (described by
  critics as targeting "woke international education").
- $28M HHS research funds, characterized by the administration as wasteful/duplicative —
  confirmed by CNBC.
- $56M of HUD's ~$58M FY2025 housing-counseling appropriation, with the administration citing
  DEI-related grantee activity — confirmed by NLIHC and a Hoodline report quoting the same $56M
  figure and DEI rationale.

These five sum to **$746M**, leaving a **$64M gap** to the stated $810M total, exactly as the
prompt notes. Multiple other outlets identify additional line items the Roll Call article omits:
a **$15M** Justice Department office focused on reducing racial/ethnic tensions, a **$10M** cut to
the Minority Business Development Agency (Commerce), and a **$5M** cut to the HHS Office of
Minority Health — identified across Fox News and Time coverage of the same OMB package. Adding
those three brings the running total to **$776M**, still roughly $34M short of $810M; remaining
outlets (OPB, Time) describe an additional unspecified "tens of millions" in climate- and
minority-focused research/nonprofit grants that no outlet, including Roll Call, itemizes by exact
dollar amount. OPB's report references "a detailed accounting provided by the administration"
(i.e., an OMB-published list) as the source for the fuller breakdown, confirming a fuller official
accounting exists beyond what any single news article — Roll Call included — reproduces in full.

So: the reported five items and their dollar figures are accurate as far as they go, but they are
a partial breakdown of a package that also touches DOJ, Commerce/MBDA, and HHS Office of Minority
Health, among other lines. Roll Call's article does not flag that its own itemization is partial,
which is a completeness problem worth noting even though every individual figure it does report
checks out.

**Confidence:** High on the five reported figures being accurate individually; high on the
existence of additional unreported line items (corroborated by two independent outlets); medium on
the precise composition of the remaining ~$34M gap, since no single source (including OMB's own
materials as summarized second-hand) provides a penny-perfect public reconciliation to exactly
$810M in the reporting surveyed.

## Claim 3: Sen. Susan Collins (R-Maine, Senate Appropriations Chair) called it an "unlawful
cancellation," said "I will work with my colleagues to address these illegal actions," and cited
OMB intentionally withholding funds for months

**Verdict:** True.

**Evidence:** Both quotes are corroborated in Roll Call's own article text and echoed in wire
coverage (lmtribune/AP wire pickup of the same Roll Call piece). Collins separately was quoted
elsewhere (Newsweek) making a related but distinct statement — that the package arrived "without
warning or consultation" and represented "the most recent attempt by this ... OMB to undermine
Congress's Constitutional power of the purse" — confirming a consistent, sustained pattern of
Collins criticism across multiple public statements that week, not a single quote taken out of
context.

**Confidence:** High — direct quotes appear in the primary article and are consistent with
Collins's other contemporaneous statements on the same package.

## Claim 4: Rep. Rosa DeLauro (House Appropriations ranking Democrat) called it "illegal" and said
the administration had "unlawfully impounded funds all year"

**Verdict:** True.

**Evidence:** Confirmed directly from the article text: "Not only is freezing the money in this
proposal illegal, but the administration has also clearly been unlawfully impounding funds all
year" is attributed to DeLauro in the piece itself.

**Confidence:** High — direct quote extracted from the primary source.

---

## Claim 5: The administration called the action "historic" spending elimination

**Verdict:** Unconfirmed / likely conflated with a different event.

**Evidence:** I could not independently verify that the administration used the word "historic" to
describe this specific $810M September 2026 package in the sources checked. Time's coverage does
use "historic pocket rescission package" — but that phrase describes the prior, separate ~$4.9
billion rescission package from **August 2025**, not the $810M action at issue here. It is
plausible the administration reused similar rhetoric for the new package (OMB/Vought have
repeatedly framed these moves as historic assertions of executive spending authority), but none of
the sources reviewed here directly quote an official calling *this* $810M move "historic." This
should be treated as unverified rather than confirmed false — it may simply not have surfaced in
the outlets checked — but it is flagged because there's a concrete, similar-sounding claim in the
record about a *different* dollar figure and date that could be the source of confusion.

**Confidence:** Low — this is an absence-of-evidence finding based on the outlets surveyed, not a
confirmed contradiction; the administration may have used this language in materials not indexed
by the search results obtained.

## Claim 6: Combined breakdown context — 2025 precedent of ~$4 billion in foreign aid cancelled via
the same pocket-rescission tactic

**Verdict:** True.

**Evidence:** Confirmed by Roll Call's 2025 coverage and CBS/NBC/NPR/JURIST: in August–September
2025, the administration used the same end-of-fiscal-year timing loophole to withhold roughly $4
billion in foreign aid appropriations, triggering litigation (*Department of State v. AIDS Vaccine
Advocacy Coalition*).

**Confidence:** High.

## Claim 7 (highest-scrutiny item): "The Supreme Court 'upheld'" the administration's cancellation
of the ~$4 billion in foreign aid

**Verdict:** Misleading / needs significant qualification — but note the article's own actual
wording is more careful than the prompt's paraphrase.

**Evidence:** On checking the literal text of the Roll Call article (not a paraphrase), the
sentence used is: "However, the Supreme Court ultimately ruled that the administration had the
right to cancel the money" — using "ruled," not "upheld." Even so, this framing substantially
overstates what happened:
- The Supreme Court's September 26, 2025 action was an **unsigned, single-paragraph order on the
  emergency (shadow) docket**, granting a **stay** of a lower-court injunction that would have
  required the funds to be paid out before they expired on September 30, 2025. It was **not a
  ruling on the merits**.
- The order stated only that the administration was "likely" to prevail and that the ICA "likely"
  barred the recipients' suit — explicitly provisional, predictive language, not a final holding.
- The Court gave **no reasoning** beyond that one paragraph. Justice Kagan's dissent (joined by the
  other liberal justices) specifically objected to deciding a major separation-of-powers question
  "on a short fuse... with scant briefing, no oral argument, and no opportunity to deliberate in
  conference," and noted that because the funds would expire by the time of any final ruling, the
  practical effect was permanent even though the legal question was left open.
- The underlying merits litigation was **not resolved** by this order; the case returned to the
  lower courts, and the money's expiration mooted the practical dispute without the underlying
  legal question — whether pocket rescissions are lawful under the ICA — ever being decided on the
  merits.
- The independent, non-partisan Government Accountability Office (GAO) has separately and
  repeatedly opined that pocket rescissions of this kind are **unlawful** under the ICA — a
  position squarely in tension with the "Supreme Court upheld/ruled the administration had the
  right" framing, since GAO's legal position was never overturned by a merits decision, only
  practically sidestepped by a preliminary stay plus the funds' expiration.

So: "ruled that the administration had the right to cancel the money" is true in the narrow sense
that the Court's order let the cancellation stand and predicted an eventual win for the
administration — but a reader would reasonably take "ruled ... had the right" to mean a final,
reasoned merits determination establishing pocket-rescission legality as settled law. That is not
what happened. This is the most legally consequential imprecision in the piece, even though it is
a comparatively soft one (using "ruled" rather than the more clearly wrong "upheld").

**Confidence:** High — the shadow-docket/stay-not-merits distinction is directly documented in
contemporaneous, mainstream legal-press coverage (Roll Call's own 2025 piece, CBS, NPR, JURIST),
including Justice Kagan's dissent explicitly objecting to exactly this kind of characterization.

---

## Overall assessment

The article is **largely accurate on its verifiable, checkable facts**: the $810M total, the
timing and mechanics of the pocket rescission, the five itemized dollar figures it does report,
and both the Collins and DeLauro quotes all check out against primary/wire sourcing. The
2025-precedent claim (~$4B foreign aid, same tactic) is also accurate.

**The single most significant problem** is the characterization of the Supreme Court's role in the
2025 foreign-aid case. Even though Roll Call's actual text says "ruled ... had the right" rather
than the more egregious "upheld" framing in the prompt, both convey a false sense of legal
finality: what actually happened was an unreasoned, predictive stay on the emergency docket that
left the core legal question — whether pocket rescissions violate the Impoundment Control Act —
formally undecided, while GAO's contrary legal position stands unrebutted on the merits. A reader
relying on the article would likely walk away believing the Supreme Court settled the legality of
this exact maneuver; it did not, and that gap matters directly for how much weight to give
Collins's and DeLauro's "illegal"/"unlawful" characterizations of the new $810M action, since the
Court has never actually held pocket rescissions lawful.

A secondary, lower-severity issue is completeness on the dollar breakdown: the five reported line
items are correct but only account for $746M of the $810M total, and the article does not
acknowledge that a fuller official breakdown exists (it does, per OPB's reference to "a detailed
accounting provided by the administration") or that other reporting identifies at least three more
line items (DOJ $15M, MBDA $10M, HHS Office of Minority Health $5M) plus additional unspecified
tens of millions. This is a completeness gap rather than an inaccuracy — nothing reported is wrong,
but the reader is left with the false impression that the five cited items constitute the whole
package.
