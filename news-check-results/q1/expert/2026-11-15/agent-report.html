Fact-check: Texas Tribune, "Parents of kids with disabilities fear losing Texas school voucher" (Nov 13, 2026)

Mostly accurate: 1 Pinocchio, with one unresolved statistical discrepancy

Nearly every checkable fact holds, including election results, program dollar amounts, the Huffines funding ask, the $227 million loss estimate and the Bernal quote. The piece earns 1 Pinocchio of 4 (minor issues) because its participant demographics look low against comptroller-sourced figures.

  • Disability share: article says 18,800 students, about 17%. Community Impact, citing comptroller data, gives about 24% (of ~110,000), and NCR reported 28% in May. I could not find 18,800 anywhere; the comptroller dashboard failed to load, so the gap is unresolved.
  • Low-income share: "at least 60%" is technically true but sources say about 80%.
  • Families vs students: sources count ~110,000 students; the article says families.
  • Eckhardt audit: sources report a pledge to audit, not a formal announcement.
  • 910,000 special education students: TEA shows 903,072 for 2025-26.
  • Materially misleading? Not seriously, but the sourcing is one-sided: every parent quoted is a pro-voucher family; the 24%/80% profile and the 30% who left public schools are absent. The Tribune notes some parents returned vouchers only via Bettencourt. This is a framing limit, not a factual error.
  • Not checkable here: private parent interviews and prices; Bettencourt post; the West bill sponsor count.

What would change the verdict: if the comptroller dashboard shows 17% for a defined subgroup (for example, students with IEP on file), the discrepancy disappears and the piece becomes 0 to 1 Pinocchio.
