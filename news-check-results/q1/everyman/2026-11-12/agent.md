## Overall rating: 1 Pinocchio (provisional)
Every checkable number matches the primary sources. Two figures (the 5.2% baseline and the income-gap pair) could not be verified.

## Materially misleading context to flag
- Headline framing "record high as Americans struggle": the record is nominal and a record is expected as nominal balances grow. Balances rose 4.2% YoY (CNBC), and the NY Fed notes card limits rose $115B.
- Card distress indicators were flat to better: flow into serious delinquency fell to 6.87% from 7.05%; NY Fed says early and serious transitions improved slightly for cards. TransUnion 90+ day card delinquency was 1.99% vs 2.05% a year earlier and charge-offs flat ($15.79B to $15.76B) per search results.
- Aggregate delinquency did worsen (5.0% of all debt, +0.3pp), but that is driven by mortgages (1.28% to 1.60% flow) and not cards. A reader could wrongly attribute the deterioration to cards.
- NY Fed economist Donghoon Lee describes overall credit quality as strong; an August 2026 Liberty Street post (per search summary) argues the rising stock delinquency rate reflects stale charged-off debt, not new worsening.
