# Fact-check: MPA "Existing-home sales fall to their lowest level since 2010" (Nov 12, 2026)

Primary source: NAR release "Existing-Home Sales Decreased 5.5% in October" (Nov 12, 2026), https://www.nar.realtor/newsroom/existing-home-sales-decreased-5-5-percent-october-nar-report
Secondary: Freddie Mac PMMS weekly readings (Oct 1 7.28, Oct 15 7.40, Oct 22 7.32, Oct 29 7.24), consistent with NAR's cited 7.33% monthly average. Cross-checks: RISMedia, NAHB, investingLive.

## Misleading or missing context
- "Highest since 2000" is imprecise. NAR's own release notes an exception in 2023, when rates peaked near 7.8% (my recollection, not re-checked this session), above the 7.33% October average. Repeating the phrase without that qualifier overstates it. As a monthly average, October's 7.33% is below late-2023 peaks.
- Sales fell even though the Freddie Mac rate in October was a rise from 6.86% in September. The headline "lowest since 2010" is a monthly annualized estimate, not a level of actual transactions. It also missed the roughly 3.85M consensus. Both are accurate, but the piece frames it as a pure rate story. RISMedia's headline says rates are only partly to blame.
- Prices still rose, and inventory rose 4.6% y/y. This is a weak-demand market, not a collapsing one.

## Rating
**1 Pinocchio** (minor imprecision, mostly accurate). Every number checks out against NAR. The one flaw is the "highest since 2000" framing, which omits NAR's 2023 exception, plus the investor-category simplification.
