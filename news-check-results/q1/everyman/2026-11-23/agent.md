# Fact-check: "Since 2014, Minnesota's median household income growth ranks 49th out of 50 states" (John Phelan, American Experiment, 2026-11-17)

Method limits: WebFetch returned only a summary of the article (no verbatim text, no quotes). WebSearch gave secondary sources only. I could not open BEA or Census tables, so no numeric claim below is verified against a primary table.

## Claims and status
1. MN GDP per capita was $4,658 above the US average in 2014 ($18,632 for a family of four, 2017 dollars). NOT CHECKABLE HERE. BEA real GDP per capita by state is the source, and I could not reach it.
2. The premium was gone by 2023, and MN was $2,767 below the US average in 2025 ($11,068 for a family of four). NOT CHECKABLE HERE. Arithmetic is internally consistent: 4 x 4,658 = 18,632 and 4 x 2,767 = 11,068. A USAFacts page lists MN real GDP per capita 2025 at about $69,600 and ranked 16th of 50 (secondary source). That sits oddly with "below the US average" unless the US figure is higher. It is unresolved, not a refutation.
3. 1997-2014: MN per capita GDP growth ranked 17th and median household income growth ranked 16th. NOT CHECKABLE HERE.
4. 2014-2025: per capita GDP growth ranked 46th and median household income growth ranked 49th. NOT CHECKABLE HERE. The headline claim depends on which Census series is used (ACS 1-year vs CPS ASEC), the deflator, and the endpoint years. Secondary sources show MN median household income of about $92,297 in 2025, ranked 14th of 50, against a US figure of $84,780. I found no 2014 figure.
5. Attributed sources are BEA, Census and American Experiment itself. This is consistent with the claims, but I could not confirm the table-level match.
6. No named quotes were found other than the author's own conclusion, "Minnesota needs more of it." That is opinion.

## Rating
Provisional, 2 Pinocchios (mixed, framing-dependent). The data may be accurate. The unverified rankings are a sensitive function of design choices, and the framing is selective.

## Materially misleading or missing context, even if the facts check out
- Rank of growth is not rank of level. MN remains about 14th of 50 in median household income. A high starting base and rank-based reporting amplify small differences in growth among states.
- Endpoint sensitivity. 2014 to 2025 spans the pandemic, and 49th of 50 can change a lot with one year's sampling error. Census state median income has wide margins of error, so adjacent ranks are often statistically indistinguishable.
- The article cites "revised data" from its own earlier piece, and the author works for an advocacy think tank. The piece is advocacy, with no policy causation shown. Nothing in it ties the ranking to any specific state policy.
- Per capita GDP mixes in corporate profits and other items that do not reach households, so the GDP and income results should not be treated as one finding.

Recommended follow-up: pull BEA SAGDP/SARPP real GDP per capita and Census ACS table B19013 (2014 and 2025, inflation-adjusted) and check the ranks and confidence intervals.
