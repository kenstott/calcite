# Fact-check: Fox Business, "Holiday airfares hit highest levels in a decade as jet fuel prices surge" (Eric Revell, Nov 20, 2026)

Limits: WebFetch returned only a summary of the article, not verbatim text. Argus index data is paywalled. Hopper's own report was not opened; I relied on Fox-syndicated coverage of it.

## Claims
| # | Claim | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Holiday airfare is the most expensive in at least 10 years (Hopper's Hayley Berg, quote) | Supported (secondhand) | Fox Weather, Fox News and Fox 13 report Hopper's 2026 Holiday Travel Index: Thanksgiving domestic round trip about $402 (+31%), Christmas about $452 (+23%). All are Fox syndications of the same Hopper release. |
| 2 | Jet fuel is about twice its level of a year earlier | Plausible, not directly verified | A 2025 Argus index reading was about $2.44/gal. A late-October 2026 reading of $4.90 would be roughly 2x. The Argus 2026 YTD average is about $2.97. I could not see the Nov 2026 value. |
| 3 | Argus index peaked at $4.88 in April | Not checkable here (partly corroborated) | Argus reports a US Gulf Coast all-time high of $4.73/gal on April 2. That is a different series; the $4.88 index figure is paywalled. |
| 4 | Fell below $3 over the summer, rebounded to $4.90 in late October | Not checkable here | Argus daily data is paywalled. The EIA outlook of $3.39 average for 2026 and $2.59 for 2027 fits a spike-and-fall pattern. |
| 5 | Surge linked to the Iran conflict | Supported | Argus and EIA coverage tie the jet fuel price increase to the Iran war. |
| 6 | Fuel is 15-30% of an airline's cost of operating a flight | Consistent with industry figures | This is the commonly cited range. I did not retrieve a primary source in this session. |
| 7 | Travelers are booking earlier than usual | Not checkable here | Hopper advice was to book before Halloween. No booking-curve data was seen. |
| 8 | Relief unlikely until fuel falls; Hopper hopes for early 2027 | Forecast, not checkable | EIA projects $2.59/gal in 2027, which is directionally consistent. |
| 9 | Pre-pandemic holiday fares were record low | Not checked | No source retrieved. |
| 10 | Drivers are strong demand, slow supply recovery, volatile fuel | Opinion/attribution | Attributed to Berg. |

Side note: the byline spelling "Hayley" vs "Haley" is inconsistent in the article. Minor.

## Context check
BLS CPI airline fares: September 2026 was +22.4% year over year (via Airways Magazine, citing BLS). BLS could not publish most October 2026 year-over-year changes because October 2025 data was never collected. So the "decade high" claim comes from Hopper's index, not from government data.

## Rating
1 Pinocchio (mostly accurate). The core claim is supported by Hopper's index, and the jet fuel linkage is credible.

## Potentially misleading
- "Highest in a decade" is a Hopper figure, not adjusted for inflation as far as I could see. A decade-high nominal fare is a weaker statement than a real-terms one.
- The "twice last year" fuel comparison uses a late-2025 baseline that was depressed. The $4.88 and $4.90 peaks sit near the all-time high.
- Fuel is 15-30% of costs, so a doubling of fuel price cannot alone explain a 23-31% fare rise. The article's demand-and-supply explanation does matter here.
- All fare evidence is one vendor's index (Hopper), so "highest" is not independently checked.

Sources: Fox Weather, Fox News, Fox 13 (Hopper index coverage); Argus Media; EIA STEO via Argus; BLS CPI releases; BTS; Airways Magazine.
