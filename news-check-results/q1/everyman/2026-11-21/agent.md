# Fact-check: OPB, "Poor school attendance persists in Oregon, remains among lowest in nation post-COVID-19" (Nov 19, 2026; Oregon Capital Chronicle, Alex Baumhardt)

Method: WebFetch returned only a summary of the article (not full text), plus three web searches. I did not reach ODE's raw data files, so most checks rest on secondary reports of ODE data. Limits are flagged below.

## Pinocchio rating: 1 (mostly accurate)
The numbers match other reporting. The problems are about framing and ambiguity, not wrong facts.

## Potentially misleading or missing context
- The headline stresses "persists" and "lowest in nation," yet the data show improvement in nearly every metric (9th-grade chronic absence down about 2 points, on-track rate at a record high). That is also the third consecutive year of improved attendance by the state's own account.
- Statewide figures are inconsistent across sources (69%, 64.5%, 32%, 33.5%) and depend on definition. The article presents one set without that caveat.
- The record on-track rate rose only 0.2 points, while the raw count fell. Calling it a record is true but a small change.
- The SB 817 passage is a one-sided policy argument. The cause of absenteeism is contested. Malheur County's local fines are reported to show gains, but other evidence is mixed. Oregon's chronic absenteeism rose across all states after COVID, so attributing it to SB 817 is unproven.
- Not checked: the national 21% comparison and the "ten years ago" benchmark.
