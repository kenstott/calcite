# Fact-check: "ASCE gives New York infrastructure a C-" (Construction Dive via Yahoo, Nov. 19, 2026)

Method: WebFetch of the article (returned only as a summary, not verbatim), then comparison to ASCE's Nov. 16, 2026 release and the infrastructurereportcard.org New York page. No dashboard or chart was produced.

| Claim | Verdict | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Overall C- on the 2026 NY report card | Confirmed; down from C in 2022 | ASCE release; ASCE state page |
| Dams, parks, solid waste dropped; 8 categories unchanged | Confirmed (the release names the 3 that fell; prior grades not stated) | ASCE release |
| Bridges C- unchanged, national C | Confirmed | ASCE |

I did not check the original ASCE report PDF's underlying agency data (NYSDOT, FHWA, MTA). Those figures are checked only against ASCE's own summary.

## Not checkable here
- Anything in the article beyond what the fetch summary returned.
- The report's underlying primary datasets (see above).

## Context and caveats
- The ASCE state page has internal inconsistencies: transit trips of 4.3B vs 3,405M, and driver cost of $718 vs $732.53. Neither appears to be repeated by the article.
- The "grade drop" is one step (C to C-), and 8 of 11 compared categories were unchanged. The ASCE outlook is "generally stagnated" (the article's tone is not alarmist).
- The IIJA expired at the end of September and Congress has not passed a successor. If the article omits this, it is a missed context point rather than an error.
- The $74.5B is an ASCE estimate.

## Rating
1 Pinocchio on the 0-4 scale is the nearest fit for a "mostly accurate" piece, but a more accurate reading is 0 Pinocchios (the "Geppetto" checkmark): every checkable figure matches ASCE. Verdict: 0 Pinocchios. No materially misleading framing found.
