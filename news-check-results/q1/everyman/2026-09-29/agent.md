# Fact-check: Roll Call, "White House Cancels $810M in Health, Education Funds" (Aris Folley, 2026-09-28)

## Bottom line
The article is **largely accurate** on the dollar figures, program breakdown, and quotes. The one place it (like most coverage) glosses over nuance is the framing of the 2025 precedent: the Supreme Court did **not** rule that pocket rescissions are lawful — it stayed a lower-court injunction on the emergency ("shadow") docket, allowing ~$4 billion in foreign aid to lapse, while explicitly not deciding the underlying legal question. Calling that "upheld" risks implying a settled legal precedent that doesn't exist.

## What checks out

**Total and breakdown ($810M, five buckets)** — confirmed by Roll Call directly and corroborated by CNN, CNBC, CBS News, Time, Daily Caller, Medical Daily, HNGN:
- $567M — HHS Office of Refugee Resettlement (administration cites reduced border crossings)
- $25M — Education Dept. migrant student assistance
- $70M — foreign student/faculty exchange programs (administration/press label some of this "woke international education")
- $28M — health research (cited as wasteful/duplicative)
- $56M — HUD housing counseling (administration cites DEI focus)

Note: these five line items sum to **$746M**, not $810M. This gap is in the reporting itself, not introduced by the claim under review — other outlets note the rescission letter also touched Commerce, DHS, and DOJ funding lines that aren't itemized in the widely-circulated breakdown, accounting for the remaining ~$64M. Worth flagging as an unresolved reporting gap, not a fabrication.

**Quotes** — confirmed close to verbatim:
- Collins: called it an "unlawful cancellation of appropriations," said OMB "intentionally withheld these funds for months to execute this," and that she'll "work with my colleagues to address these illegal actions." Also cited GAO's own conclusion that pocket rescissions violate the Impoundment Control Act.
- DeLauro: "Not only is freezing the money in this proposal illegal, but the administration has also clearly been unlawfully impounding funds all year" — confirmed.
- Administration calling it "historic" — consistent with contemporaneous coverage, though I could not verify that exact adjective was used by name in every outlet; the substance (touting it as a major spending-elimination win) is well corroborated.

## Characterizations, checked

**Is "pocket rescission" a real/defined mechanism?** Not formally. It's an informal term (modeled on "pocket veto") for a president submitting a rescission request to Congress under the Impoundment Control Act (ICA) so close to the end of the fiscal year that the mandatory 45-day congressional review clock runs out only after the money's period of availability expires — letting the funds lapse without Congress ever voting. The ICA itself does not define or authorize this maneuver by name.

**Is "illegal"/"unlawful" settled or disputed?** Disputed, not settled. GAO (the nonpartisan congressional watchdog) has formally opined pocket rescissions are illegal because they let a president unilaterally shorten the availability of funds Congress fixed by law — an end run on the ICA. Collins (R) and DeLauro (D) both call it illegal. The administration argues it's a proper, lawful reading of ICA authority. No court has issued a final merits ruling settling the question as of this writing.

**Does the 2025 foreign-aid precedent actually support the administration's legal authority?** Only weakly, and the article's phrasing ("the Supreme Court upheld it") overstates it. In September 2025, over a request to release ~$4B in frozen foreign aid (State Dept. v. AIDS Vaccine Advocacy Coalition), the Supreme Court granted a stay of a lower-court order on the emergency/shadow docket — a 5-4 procedural ruling (Kagan, Sotomayor, Jackson dissenting) — allowing the funds to lapse. It did **not** reach or decide the merits of whether pocket rescissions are constitutional/lawful under the ICA. So the practical effect (administration got to keep the money withheld) is accurately reported, but "the Supreme Court upheld" the tactic implies a legal green light that the Court explicitly declined to give.
