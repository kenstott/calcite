# Fact-check: KCUR, "Kansas City 4th warmest fall ever, electricity bills higher" (2026-11-16)

Limits: WebFetch returned only a summary of the article, not verbatim text. Two searches found no primary NWS, BPU or United Way data. Primary-source checking was minimal.

## Claims and verdicts
1. Fall about 7-8 F above normal, 4th warmest in a 139-year record (NWS, meteorologist Spencer Mell). Partly corroborated. Only KCUR attributes it to NWS. No NWS climate summary found. The figure is "so far" and provisional. Not checkable here against NWS data.
2. Latest first freeze on record was 1931 (Nov 24, per NWS via KCUR); this year is not far off. Consistent with KCTV5, which reported the first freeze around Nov 13, the coldest morning since March. Record date not independently verified. Unverified.
3. Missouri State Climatologist Zachary Leasor: flowers bloomed earlier, leaves stayed longer, longer growing season; "There's a lot more nuance to the data." Quote not independently confirmed. Plausible.
4. United Way of Greater Kansas City, Sept 1 to Oct 31: gas-bill help requests down nearly 20% vs 2025, electric help down about 5.5%. Not checkable here (no public data found).
5. BPU: average residential use up about 23% over three years for Oct/Nov. Not checkable here.
6. BPU: average November bill $94.96 (2024) vs $117.21 (2026, preliminary). Not checkable here. Arithmetic: +23.4%, close to the 23% usage figure.
7. United Way homelessness-prevention requests up nearly 130% in October year over year. Not checkable here.

## Misleading or context flags
- The headline says "electricity bills higher" and ties it to warmth. Assistance requests for electricity and gas actually fell, which cuts against the framing. The piece does report this.
- Bills rose about 23% while BPU rate increases (2.5% a year per earlier KCUR reporting) are also in play. Usage and rate effects are not separated.
- A "so far" ranking is presented as a settled record. The 2026 bill figures are preliminary.
- BPU covers Wyandotte County (Kansas City, KS), while the weather ranking is for Kansas City, MO area. The piece mixes the two geographies.
- The 130% homelessness-prevention jump is a small-base percentage with no counts given, and is not tied to weather.
- One warm fall does not establish climate causation. The climatologist says as much.

## Rating
2 Pinocchios, provisional. Claims are plausible and attributed, and nothing found contradicts them. Most numbers are unverified. The weather-to-bills framing is somewhat loose and the sources are partly preliminary.
