# Fact-check: "'Affordability'—Really?" (Victor Davis Hanson, American Greatness, 2026-11-26)

Limitation: WebFetch returned only a summary of the article, not verbatim text, so no attributed quotes could be extracted. Verification used web search of BLS/BEA/AAA-reported figures; I did not open the primary tables directly. Python, charts and image dashboard were not produced.

## Claims and verdicts
| Claim | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 165,000 October jobs | True | BLS Employment Situation, released Nov 6, 2026: +165,000 (vs ~65,000 expected) |
| Unemployment 4.1% | True | BLS: fell 0.1 pt to 4.1%. Omitted: U-6 rose 7.6% to 7.9%; wage growth 3.1% y/y |
| Q3 GDP growth 4.3% | Likely wrong | BEA advance estimate (Oct 29) is 3.4% annualized. 4.3% looks like a transposition. A Nov 25 second estimate was scheduled; I could not confirm any revision to 4.3% |
| Cumulative price rise under Biden 21.4% | Essentially true | Jan 2021 to Jan 2025 CPI is about 21.5% (PERC/BLS); other sources say 21.2% to 21%. Depends on endpoints |
| Cumulative price rise in Trump's first term 7.7% | Plausible, not located | Not found in search. My own rough calculation gives about 7.4% to 7.9% depending on endpoints |
| Inflation went from an inherited 1.9% to 5.1% when Biden left | Misleading or unverified | Year-over-year CPI was 1.4% in Jan 2021 and 3.0% in Jan 2025 (2.9% in Dec 2024). 5.1% is not the exit rate. It might be an average of some kind. Unverified |
| Gas prices spiked since late February, then fell | Partly checkable, directionally true | AAA: national average $3.91 on Nov 12, down about 45 cents from a month earlier, first time under $4 since July. Still the most expensive November on record. I did not check the February start |
| Democrats won the 2026 midterms on "affordability" | Not checked | Not verified against election results |
| Democrats prioritize impeachments, subpoenas and investigations over prices | Opinion or prediction | Not checkable as fact |
| Planned entitlement expansions will raise inflation and middle-class taxes | Opinion or prediction | Not checkable as fact |
| Clinton-Gingrich deficit reduction as a model | Opinion | Not checkable as fact |

## Overall rating: 2 Pinocchios (half true)
Most economic figures check out. The GDP figure is overstated by about a point, and the inflation framing is shaky.

## Misleading by omission or framing
- The piece cites the strong October jobs and GDP data but omits that U-6 rose and that unemployment has stayed between 4.1% and 4.3% since March.
- Gas prices are called falling, but they remain at record levels for a November.
- Cumulative-inflation comparisons use different spans (Biden's four years versus Trump's first term) and different endpoints. They cover a pandemic and supply-shock period for Biden, and the piece does not say so.
- The Democratic agenda and the inflation effects of their plans are speculation presented as likely fact.
