The KCUR article's claims about Kansas City's fall warmth, freeze timing and BPU electricity bills are balanced and mostly accurate; 4 claims could not be confirmed.

**Rating: 1 Pinocchio.**

**Weather claims (checked against the ACIS threaded Kansas City record, MCIthr, 1888 on)**
- "4th-warmest fall, records back 139 years": mostly true. Same-span ranking (Sep 1 to the same day each year) puts fall 2026 5th through Nov 15, behind 1963 (68.88F), 1931 (67.14F), 1956 (66.18F) and 1897 (66.15F), against 65.99F for 2026. Through Nov 24 it is 3rd (63.92F, behind 1963 and 1931). No year is within 0.1F. The 139-year record length matches. The "7 to 8 degrees above normal" figure was not recomputed.
- Latest first freeze on record, Nov 24, 1931: true. The 1931 low was 28F on Nov 24 and 36F on Nov 23.
- "Not very far off from that record this year": mostly true but generous. The first 32F low was Nov 12, 12 days before the record.
- Climate experts use first-freeze timing as an indicator: true, per the USDA Climate Hubs and MRCC Freeze Date Tool page.

**Not confirmable (graded unsupported)**
- BPU residential usage up about 23% over three years, and the average November bill of $94.96 (2024) against $117.21 (2026). The BPU homepage has no such data, and the warehouse EIA tables are state-level only. The two bill figures imply +23.4%, the same as the usage rise, which implies no rate change.
- United Way 211 figures: gas-bill requests down nearly 20%, electric requests down about 5.5%, homelessness-prevention requests up nearly 130%. The United Way homepage and the linked KCUR pages do not publish them.

**Misleading framing**
- The headline says the warm fall made bills higher. That causal link is not shown. The article's own data show assistance requests fell, the November bill figure is preliminary (the month was half over), the bill comparison skips 2025, and late-November cold would raise heating use.
- BPU serves Wyandotte County, Kansas, while the weather ranking is for the Missouri-side airport.
- "4th" is a rank that moved from 5th to 3rd within days.
