The Urban Milwaukee article's claims about Wisconsin utility rate hikes, ownership and residential electricity costs are mostly accurate.

**Rating: 1 Pinocchio.**

Article: John D. Johnson, Urban Milwaukee, 2026-11-11. The connector's fetch succeeded.

**Central claim.** Investor-owned utilities (IOUs) raised rates about twice as much as municipal and cooperative utilities since 2020. This is true on the years I could check.
- Source: warehouse table energy.eia_utility_annual (EIA-861), which covers 2012-2023. I used Wisconsin bundled-service utilities that reported in both years, and computed residential revenue divided by kWh sold.
- 2020 to 2023: IOUs rose 20.3% (14.64c to 17.61c per kWh). Cooperatives rose 6.7% (14.33c to 15.28c). Municipals rose 10.9% (11.15c to 12.37c).
- The article's 2020 base levels match ours: about 14.8c, 14.3c and 11.2c.
- The article's 2025 figures (29% IOU, 15% co-op, 14% muni) continue the same pattern.
- The 2024-2025 endpoints are not in the warehouse and were not checked independently.

**Other claims checked.**
- **PSC approval:** True. WPR reports the commission voted Nov 6 for We Energies increases of 3.9% (2027) and 3.5% (2028), and WPS increases of 0.3% and 2.9%. These were smaller than requested, and the allowed return was cut from 9.8% to 9.5%. The article omits both the trimming and the cut.
- **IOUs matched co-ops until 2020:** True. In 2020 IOUs were at 14.64c and co-ops at 14.33c, and the gap widened to 2.3c by 2023.
- **Customer shares (86.6% IOU, 8.1% muni, 5.3% co-op):** True. The 2023 panel gives 86.4%, about 8.1% and about 5.5%. The non-IOU share including the EIA adjustment record is about 19%, as the article says.
- **Arithmetic (115%, 148%, $1,513, $1,313, $1,025, $488, Cedarburg $567):** Internally consistent. The 2023 IOU-muni gap of 5.24c per kWh is $419 per 8,000 kWh, the same direction and order of magnitude as the article's $488 for 2025.
- **Large-IOU bill growth ($391 We Energies, $408 Alliant, $276 WPS, $177 Xcel):** Consistent with 2020-2023 rises of 3.3c, 2.8c, 2.8c and 2.7c, but 2025 is unverifiable. Xcel's implied 2025 rate (about 15.5c) would be below its 2023 rate of 16.0c, which is odd.
- **Average usage "just over 8,000 kWh":** Roughly consistent. 2023 usage was 7,246 to 8,667 kWh for the large IOUs; We Energies was 7,500.
- **Cedarburg ($0.1344) and We Energies ($0.2053) 2025 rates:** Unverified, since 2025 is not loaded. 2023 values were $0.1271 and $0.1896.
- **"Costs affect all utilities more or less equally":** Partially false. Ownership is confounded with scale, density, generation and transmission ownership, taxes, and allowed return on equity. The author hedges with "presumably".

**Materially misleading framing.**
- The headline and structure imply that for-profit ownership causes the gap. The data compare aggregate revenue per kWh for a few very large IOUs against about 45 small municipals, with no controls.
- The Cedarburg versus We Energies comparison is not like-for-like.
- The more apt comparison is IOUs against cooperatives. That divergence is real in the data, but the article does not identify its cause.
- The piece never mentions that the PSC trimmed the requested hikes.
- Revenue per kWh is an average realized price, not a rate-case rate.

**Limits.** The warehouse ends at 2023, and EIA publishes about 3 years behind. The 2024-2025 figures and the 2001-2019 history were not independently verified. The "10%" in the page text appears only in a reader comment and was not graded.
