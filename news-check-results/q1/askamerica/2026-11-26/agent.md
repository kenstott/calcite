The Honolulu Star-Advertiser article's claims about Hawaii's October 2026 unemployment rate, labor force and state rankings are balanced and mostly accurate; 1 claim could not be confirmed.

**Central claim: true.** The warehouse (BLS LAUS, econ.regional_employment) shows Hawaii at 2.8% in October 2026 and in September 2026. The rate was 2.8% from January through September 2024 and 2.7% in October 2024. The BLS state ranking page (last modified November 20) confirms Hawaii tied with Nebraska for 5th at 2.8%, and the rest of the ranking matches.

**Checked and true**
- Employed: 677,807 in October (article 677,800), highest since June 2018 (679,382).
- Unemployed: 19,665 (article 19,650), highest since March 2023 (19,761).
- Labor force: October 697,472 (article 697,450), highest since June 2017 (698,244).
- U.S. rate: 4.2% in September, 4.1% in October.
- Fed expectation: PIMCO's most likely next move is a 25bp hike in December (also J.P. Morgan and CME FedWatch via web search).
- October county rates, the +1,000 payroll jobs, leisure and hospitality +600 and construction +500, and the Maui County island figures, per Maui Now relaying the state Department of Business, Economic Development and Tourism (DBEDT).
- Statements by DBEDT economist Seth Colby: the market is pricing a 0.25% December hike (true), and Kauai has the highest county rate at 4.5% (true).

**Errors and problems**
1. The September labor force is given as 694,350. BLS shows 694,853, and the article's own September components sum to 694,850. The rise was about 2,600, not 3,100. Grade: partially false.
2. The data table shows Hawaii's September 2025 rate as 2.4%. Current BLS shows 2.1%, though DBEDT's own July 2026 release chart also shows 2.4% for that month. The other cells match. Grade: mostly true.
3. The lead says more people entered the labor force "and squeezed out others looking for work." That contradicts the article's own figures, because the number unemployed also rose, and Colby calls it a statistical quirk. Grade: mostly false. This is the materially misleading framing.
4. Unsupported: the September prior values for Hawaii County (3.5%) and Kauai (5.6%). The warehouse has no county data. Maui Now says Hawaii County "held at 3%", and I could not locate DBEDT's September release.
