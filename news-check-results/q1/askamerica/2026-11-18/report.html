The Morning Ag Clips article's claims about China's $17 billion US farm purchase pledge, soybean targets and the Trump-Xi summit are accurate.

Article under reviewhttps://www.morningagclips.com/china-unlikely-to-fulfill-17-billion-us-ag-purchase-pledge-this-year/

Summary

The piece is a roundup of quoted wire reports and every quote and figure I could read matches its source (Bloomberg via Yahoo, Agri-Pulse, Investing.com copy of the Reuters trade-council story). Warehouse USDA FAS data is consistent: China corn purchases fell from 14.3 MMT (2022) to 0.03 MMT and 0. Reuters' $7.2 billion could not be read at Reuters (HTTP 401); it matches the republished quote. econ.trade_exports ends in 2025, so it cannot check Jan-Sep 2026. For scale only, US-reported exports to China of HS chapters 01-24 excluding soybeans for Jan-Sep were $3.6B (2019), $9.1B (2023), $7.8B (2024) and $4.2B (2025). That is a US export (Census) basis, not Chinese customs imports, so it neither confirms nor contradicts $7.2B or the 'lowest since 2019' ordering. Framing caveat: the headline states failure more firmly than Bloomberg ('could get an extended timeline'), and the piece omits that Beijing never confirmed the deal and its scope is unclear.
