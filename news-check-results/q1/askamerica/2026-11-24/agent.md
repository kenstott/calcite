Article: Fox Business, "Holiday airfares hit highest levels in a decade as jet fuel prices surge" (fetched successfully). Most claims are Hopper economist Hayley Berg's.

Warehouse check: search_catalog found no jet fuel price table and no airfare CPI table (energy, econ and transport were searched). I queried WTI crude from econ.fred_indicators ($89.53 on 2026-11-17) for context only. Every claim was therefore graded on outside primary or wire sources.

Claim grades:
1. Peak of $4.88 in April, and below $3 in summer. **True.** AP reports the Argus index fell from an early-April peak of $4.88 to a June low of $2.70.
2. Rebound to $4.90 in late October (Argus). **Unsupported.** Argus data is paywalled. AP reported $4.53 on Sept 17 and $4.30 on Sept 26. The Airlines for America page showed only $3.47 on Nov 23. $4.90 is plausible but I found no source for it.
3. Berg: jet fuel is "two times higher than last year." **Mostly true.** IATA put global jet fuel up 111% year over year in the week ending Oct 9, and AP said Argus was nearly twice the 2025 average on Sept 26. It is stale now, with Argus at $3.47 on Nov 23.
4. Berg: "most expensive it's been in at least 10 years" (central claim). **Mostly true.** Hopper reports Thanksgiving fares of $402 (+31%) and Christmas fares of $452 (+23%). BLS airline fares were +22.4% year over year in September and AP cited +23% for August. The "decade" claim rests on Hopper's own search-fare index, and no government series covers holiday fares. October's year-over-year change cannot be computed because BLS did not collect October 2025 data during the shutdown.
5. Berg: fuel is "15% to 30%" of flight operating cost. **Mostly true.** IATA expects fuel to be nearly one-third of airline operating expenses in 2026, up from about a quarter in 2025. That is above the top of Berg's range.
6. The headline causal claim: fuel prices drove the highest airfares in a decade. **Mostly true.** The mechanism is well supported by AP, which cited airlines and analysts saying fuel costs are passed through with a lag. Hopper also cites capacity cuts and added fees as contributors.

Misleading framing:
- Present tense. The Argus index was $3.47 on Nov 23, about 29% below the claimed $4.90, so "prices today are more than double" is outdated. The article never says fuel has fallen.
- Single interested source. The whole piece rests on Hopper, a booking company, for the "decade high" claim.
- Hopper's index page was not fetched, only a syndicated Fox local article relaying it, so Hopper figures are checked for fidelity only.
- The article's six linked pages were fetched, and none changed a grade.
