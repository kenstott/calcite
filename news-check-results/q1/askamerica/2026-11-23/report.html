Misleading-framing flags

  • Single-survey rank: the CPS state medians are noisy. Minnesota moved from $91,890 (SE $2,013) in 2024 to $95,160 (SE $5,191) in 2025. A 49th rank on two noisy endpoints is fragile. The ACS 1-year, with a much larger sample, gives 38th.
  • Start year: 2014 is Minnesota's GDP-premium peak in the data shown, so the window begins at the state's best relative point. The article does not say why 2014.
  • Figure 2 is credited to 'Census Bureau' with no table named. The related post links H-8.
  • The correlation (r=0.557) is offered as support that GDP growth raises incomes. It is an association across states.
  • The 1997-2014 per capita GDP rank (17th) could not be tested: the warehouse population series starts in 2007.

Method and exclusions

Per capita GDP: BEA SAGDP9 line 1 real GDP (chained 2017 dollars) divided by SAINC1 line 2 population, Minnesota against the US row (00000, excluded from state rankings as it is not a state). Ranks cover 50 states. The District of Columbia (FIPS 11) was excluded from all rankings because the article ranks 50 states. The filter a.state IS NOT NULL excluded the ACS national row (null state), which is not a state. The predicate on Alabama and Alaska (01000, 02000) only re-added them and removed nobody. The ACS ranking had 51 units with Puerto Rico, where Minnesota was 39th; without Puerto Rico (50 states) Minnesota is 38th. The CPS ranking, without DC, has 50 states and Minnesota 49th. The 2023 gap is -$312, so the premium vanished in 2023 as claimed. The 2022 uptick is the 'one year' exception.

What This Report Does Not Answer

  • 1997-2014 GDP per capita rank 17th: Warehouse population (SAINC1) starts 2007, so 1997 per-capita GDP cannot be built; not independently verified.
