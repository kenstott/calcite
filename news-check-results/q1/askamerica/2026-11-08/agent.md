# Fact-check: White House release "President Trump Was Right: Tariffs and Tax Cuts Are Reshoring Manufacturing, Creating Jobs" (Nov 6, 2026)

**Rating: 3 Pinocchios.** The headline jobs numbers are accurate, but the manufacturing "renaissance" claims that carry the piece are inflated or false.

## Checks passed
- 165k October jobs, versus a 59k forecast: TRUE. Warehouse econ.fred_indicators PAYEMS 159,293 minus 159,128; CNBC confirms the forecast.
- Aug revised to 186k and Sep to 60k (about double the first print of 29k): TRUE.
- Unemployment fell, 4.2% to 4.1%: TRUE.
- 1.25M private jobs since Jan 2025: TRUE. BLS CES0500000001 is +1,250k, Jan 2025 to Oct 2026. Measured from Dec 2024 it is +1.17M.
- Q3 GDP of 3.4%: TRUE. GDPC1 shows 3.35% annualized.
- Claims at a 57-year low: MOSTLY TRUE. 189k is the warehouse low since 1986 but ties the July 18 reading. The 57-year framing was not independently checked.
- Average hourly earnings beat expectations: TRUE, per CNBC.

## Failed or misleading
- "More than 100,000 manufacturing jobs since return": FALSE. BLS CES3000000001 went from 12,673k (Jan 2025) to 12,681k (Oct 2026), about +8k.
- "19k, strongest monthly manufacturing gain since 2023": FALSE on current data. July was +20k and August +21k.
- "Rising for the first time in three years": MISLEADING. It has risen every month since June 2026, and employment is still about 194k below January 2024.
- "Biden-era loss of >200k factory jobs": the Jan 2024 to Jan 2025 decline was 202k, but this is a peak-to-end window. January 2021 was not checked, and the framing omits any net gain from 2021.
- "Trade deficit dropped over 12% in September": FALSE. BEA/Census shows $105.0B versus $105.7B, down 0.7%.
- "Goods exports record, +15% y/y": NOT SUPPORTED. Goods exports were $211.0B, +$5.1B m/m, and no record was found. The y/y figure was not verified.
- "Wage growth highest for the year": FALSE. AHE growth was 3.1% y/y in October, against about 3.7% in Jan-Feb and 3.2% in July.
- Tariffs and tax cuts caused reshoring: UNSUPPORTED. Manufacturing employment is roughly flat since January 2025, and no causal test is offered.

## Not checked
These were not checked: the 120k factory construction jobs; capital goods at 46% of imports; the 36%/63% reshoring survey shares; ISM expansion for a 10th straight month (ISM page returned empty); core capital goods orders +14% y/y. The Reuters link returned HTTP 401.

## Omitted context
Unemployment is down from a 4.5% peak, but long-term unemployment rose sharply (27.1 weeks average duration) and U-6 rose to 7.9%, per CNBC.
