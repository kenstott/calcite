Verified true against warehouse data
- Cumulative CPI: +21.4% Jan 2021 to Jan 2025 and +7.7% Jan 2017 to Jan 2021; the $1 to $1.21 gum arithmetic follows.
- June 2022 inflation was 9.06%; current (Sept 2026) is about 3.7%, so "two and a half times" holds.
- Inflation-adjusted gas in Nov 2022 (about $4.30 in 2026 dollars) exceeded Nov 2026 ($3.64 to $4.15). The 2022 peak was $5.01 weekly (EIA) against the article's $5.03 (AAA-style daily).
- Gas fell about 69 to 71 cents over six to seven weeks (EIA weekly), consistent with 72 on daily data.
- Q3 GDP is 4.3% annualized in the warehouse (24,408.0 to 24,665.2). BEA's advance estimate was 3.4%, so this is a revised estimate.
- October payrolls +165,000 and unemployment 4.1%.
- Median household income $87,460 (2025 dollars, record; prior high $85,320 in 2019). Poverty 10.2%, lowest since 1959 (prior low 10.5% in 2019).

Errors
- Central claim: "5.1 percent annual average inflation" when Biden left office. The 2021-24 annual rates average about 4.9%, and December 2024 inflation was 2.9%. Partially false. The "inherited 1.9%" is the Trump-term average; January 2021 year-over-year was 1.4%.
- National debt "$41 trillion": Treasury shows $40.79 trillion on Nov 24. Rounded up.

Misleading framing
- The Hegseth impeachment line implies Democrats are driving the lame-duck push. The November floor action was by Republican Rep. Massie. Democrats did file articles in April.
- The GDP "strangely ... adjustments right before and after the midterms" insinuation has no evidence. Revisions are routine and the 4.3% figure matches the data.
- The piece credits "the Trump economy" for gains and blames Democratic past policy for inflation, without noting the 2026 gas spike it concedes began in late February.
- Predictions about Democratic behavior and the characterizations of their plans (Medicare expansion fivefold, reparations) are opinion and were not graded.

Not tested: the claim that food, fuel, housing and insurance rose more than 21.4%.
