The OPB/Oregon Capital Chronicle article's claims about Oregon chronic absenteeism, ninth-grade on-track rates and SB 817 are balanced and partly false; 4 claims could not be confirmed.

Rating: 2 Pinocchios.

Method: The article (OPB republishing Oregon Capital Chronicle, Nov 19, 2026) loaded. I checked the warehouse first. edu.crdc_schools carries chronic absenteeism only through 2022, and nothing carries Oregon 2024-26 attendance or on-track rates, so claims were graded from independent sources. I did not retrieve ODE's At-A-Glance data itself.

Claim grades
- Ninth-grade on-track rate rose from a record 86.6% to 86.8%: TRUE. OPB's Nov 2025 report gives 86.6% for 2024-25, and news coverage gives 86.8%.
- 36,512 of 41,713 on track in 2024-25, then 36,200 in 2025-26, 312 fewer, with 473 fewer ninth graders: PARTIALLY FALSE. The counts imply 87.5% and 87.8% (36,200 / 41,240), not the stated 86.6% and 86.8%. The 312 and 473 differences are internally consistent, but the counts and rates do not reconcile.
- About 69% regular attendance in 2025-26: PARTIALLY FALSE. Rep. Wise's Aug 14, 2026 release gives 64.5%. The two sources conflict, and the 2024-25 figure in OPB's 2025 article was 33.5% chronic absence.
- National chronic absenteeism about 21% versus Oregon about 32%: UNSUPPORTED. The linked Future-Ed tracker has no national figure and no Oregon entry. It says 28 states reported for 2025-26, with New Mexico highest at 31.3%.
- 35% of ninth graders chronically absent in 2024-25, with a 2-point improvement: UNSUPPORTED. OPB's 2025 article gives 33.5% for all students and no ninth-grade figure.
- About 43% of 11th graders and more than half of seniors chronically absent: UNSUPPORTED. No grade-level 2025-26 source was found.
- Over 80% regular attendance ten years ago: MOSTLY TRUE. The 74 says roughly four in five in 2018-19 and gives no 2015-16 figure.
- Oregon's 160-day year is among the shortest: UNSUPPORTED. No source found.
- Chris Wells says SB 817 (2021) neutralized schools' ability to penalize parents: TRUE as to the law. The enrolled bill has a "Truancy fees" section that removes the Class C violation for non-attendance. Whether this caused the attendance decline is his opinion and was not tested.

Misleading framing
- The "about 13 points" improvement since the pandemic has no stated base year.
- SB 817's bill title concerns juvenile-delinquency fees. The article does not say it was a broader juvenile-justice bill with a truancy section.
- Oregon Education Director Charlene Williams's non-answer on social promotion is reported fairly.
