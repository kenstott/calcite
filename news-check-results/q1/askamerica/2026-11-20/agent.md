The Construction Dive/Yahoo article's claims about New York's ASCE 'C-' infrastructure grade, bridge conditions and IIJA funding are somewhat biased and mostly accurate.

**Rating: 1 Pinocchio.**

**Method.** The article was fetched. Its source, ASCE's 11/16/2026 release, and the 2025 national report coverage were read. The warehouse (transport.bridges, FHWA National Bridge Inventory, record_type=1) was queried for the bridge figures. The ASCE grades themselves are not in the warehouse (search_catalog found no match for "asce"), so they were checked against the ASCE release.

**Claims**
- NY got a 'C-' on its 2026 report card: true (central claim).
- Poor-bridge rate rose from 9.5% to 9.9%, versus 6.7% nationally: mostly true. Warehouse counts: NY 1,611 of 17,579 (9.2%) in 2022 and 1,741 of 17,690 (9.8%) in 2025; US 41,685 of 624,862 (6.7%) in 2025. ASCE's 2022 figure is about 0.3 points above my count, likely a method difference.
- $18B bridge investment and $74.5B need: mostly true. ASCE says "more than $18 billion".
- Roads (17% to 24% excellent; 10% to 8% poor), transit (85% ridership, $50B planned), water (40% of pipes pre-1940, up to 40% water lost): true, all match the ASCE release.
- US earned a 'C' in 2025, the most recent national scoring: true.
- IIJA put $30B into NY: mostly true. ASCE says "more than $30 billion".

**Misleading framing.**
- The lede and the definition of C- as "adequate, stable performance" omit that the overall grade fell from 'C' in 2022. ASCE's own headline is the drop, and it calls C- "fair condition".
- The piece omits that the IIJA expired at the end of September with no successor law.
- It adds a new stormwater category (D), which weakens comparison with 2022, without saying so.

**Limits.** The full ASCE report page was not read, so the road and transit figures rest on the press release. The ASCE grades are ASCE's own judgments and were not independently tested.
