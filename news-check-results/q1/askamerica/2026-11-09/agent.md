# The NBC News Meet the Press Nov. 8, 2026 transcript's claims about the midterm results, gas prices and inflation are mostly accurate.

Source: https://www.nbcnews.com/meet-the-press/transcripts/meet-press-november-8-2026-rcna608110 (fetched successfully; Welker, Hamilton, Sanders, Donalds, Shapiro and the panel were all read via full-text find).

**Rating: 1 Pinocchio.** The transcript reproduces what was said, and the checkable facts hold. The one materially misleading item is Donalds presenting the 9% inflation peak as the Biden-era rate and the "lion's share" cause of today's prices.

## Checked against the warehouse
- DeSantis, "$3 a gallon a year and a half ago, now $4": TRUE. BLS regular gasoline was $3.31 in May 2025 and $3.30 in June 2025, then $4.20 in Aug 2026 and $4.48 in Sep 2026. EIA weekly was $4.15 on 2026-11-02, down from $4.48 on 2026-09-21. Tables: econ.average_prices, energy.eia_retail_gasoline_prices. The transcript has DeSantis saying "Iraq war", which looks like a misstatement or transcription slip for Iran.
- Donalds, "9% year-over-year inflation from the Biden years" as the lion's share of today's prices: PARTIALLY FALSE and misleading. CPI-U not-seasonally-adjusted year-over-year peaked at 9.06% in June 2022, was 6.5% by Dec 2022 and 3.35% by Dec 2023 (econ.inflation_metrics). The peak is accurate but it was one month, not the era's rate. The causal "lion's share" attribution is untestable here.
- Kansas Senate history: officials.senate_election_results (1976 onward) shows only Republican winners 2008-2022, so it cannot test "94 years". Checked against outside reporting instead.

## Checked against outside sources
- Hamilton winning the seat Republicans held "94 years": MOSTLY TRUE. KCTV reports Republicans won every Kansas Senate race since 1932; AP/DDHQ has Hamilton at 51.0% to Marshall's 46.0%. The phrase "that seat" is loose because Kansas has two seats.
- Seven Senate pickups, 54 seats: MOSTLY TRUE. NPR/AP list seven flips (NC, SC, OH, TX, KS, IA, ME) and Alaska and Mississippi still open. I found no source stating the 54 total.
- Five Black governors in 2027 (Moore, Donalds, Bottoms, Ford, Crowley): TRUE.
- Short, Trump "31-32% approval": MOSTLY TRUE. Reuters/Ipsos early Nov put him at 32%; NYT/Siena in Oct had 36% of likely voters.
- Donalds, "15 years since the ACA": MOSTLY TRUE. It was signed March 23, 2010, so 16 years 7 months.

## Not checkable here (flagged, not graded false)
- House "up to 259 seats": NBC's own projection. The linked NBC data-graphics page carried no 259 figure, and early AP/NYT counts were 228-139 with races open.
- Sanders: 66% support Medicare for All (no poll table in the catalog; support varies with wording), 20% of seniors on $15,000, and the $20 minimum wage (a policy preference, not a fact).
- Hamilton: 60,000 Kansans losing insurance, 20,000 children losing SNAP (ACS insurance data ends 2024; the SNAP table has no child split), "$250 a month" in fuel and tariff costs, and Trump making "$1 billion" in his first year back.
- Shapiro: Trump put "$2 billion in his pocket"; nine of the ten biggest states have Democratic governors, covering 75% of the population. Newsweek supports roughly three-quarters of the population, but I could not confirm nine of ten.
- A panelist: "90% of the country" said corruption was the most critical issue in NBC exit polls. This looks implausible as stated, but I could not access the exit poll.
- Welker: exit polls showing "nearly six in ten" saying immigration policy has gone too far (not tested).
- The "$5,000" in the page text is a sidebar teaser headline, not a claim.

## Misleading even where facts check out
- Donalds' use of the 9% peak, and his claim that no Republicans blamed Biden for gas prices. Welker correctly notes Republicans on the show did.
- Several guest claims (insurance losses, Trump's income, the 90% figure) are presented as facts but were not verified. They lean toward each speaker's thesis.
