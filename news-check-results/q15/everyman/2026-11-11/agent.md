# Fact-check: WFTV, "How data centers affect power bills" (Shipley Energy syndicated explainer)

Limit: WebFetch returned only a summary of the article, not verbatim text. I checked the claims in that summary. Any attributed quotes I could not see are not graded.

## Verdict: 1 Pinocchio (mostly accurate, minor imprecision)

## Claims checked

| Claim | Result | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Data centers used about 4.4% of US electricity in 2023; LBNL projects 6.7% to 12% by 2028 | TRUE | LBNL/DOE Dec 2024 report (176 TWh; 325-580 TWh in 2028) |
| PJM 2028/29 auction cleared at $325/MW-day, 6,831 MW short of the reliability requirement | TRUE | PJM release 14 Jul 2026 |
| Market monitor attributes about 46% of capacity costs over the last four auctions to data centers | TRUE, with a caveat | Monitoring Analytics: $29.4B of $63.6B. It is the monitor's own estimate, PJM has not endorsed the split, and the method is unpublished. |
| Dallas Fed: existing data centers raised wholesale prices 2% to 6% | PARTLY ACCURATE | Dallas Fed WP 2606 abstract says 3-5%. The revised version (wp2606r1) says 6.9% nationally. The 2-6% range appears only in press coverage. |
| Wholesale is about half of a retail bill, so increases reach customers at roughly half size | PLAUSIBLE | Consistent with Dallas Fed researchers' comment that energy is roughly half of retail price. This is a simplification. |
| EIA: residential price rises from 17.3 to 18.2 cents/kWh in 2026 | TRUE, depends on edition | EIA STEO. Figures shift between editions (Aug 2026 STEO: 17.30 for 2025, 18.27 for 2026). |
| Ohio AEP tariff: pay for 85% of subscribed capacity for up to 12 years | TRUE | PUCO order, 9 Jul 2025. It applies above 25 MW, with a ramp period and an exit fee. |
| Oregon: data center rates up about 29%, residential and commercial slightly down | TRUE | Oregon PUC PR 202615. PGE: data centers +29%, residential -1.3%, commercial -2.1%. |
| Oregon class for facilities above 20 MW | TRUE | POWER Act (HB 3546) / PGE Schedule 96 |
| Utility forecasts of about 90 GW new data center load by 2030 vs about 65 GW from analysts; 24 GW to 166 GW peak forecast jump; NERC 13 of 23 areas at elevated or high risk; Georgia Power 69,000 MW pipeline; MISO price fall; ERCOT audit order; Virginia 25 MW class; North Dakota; Winter Storm Fern; turbine lead times of 4-5 years | NOT CHECKED | I did not verify these against primary sources within this session. |

## Misleading or missing context
- The 46% share is a market monitor's attribution, not an established cause. The Dallas Fed paper does not separate demand-driven price effects from supply constraints.
- Capacity prices were hitting a federal cap, so the 46% figure partly reflects the cap's effect. The monitor estimated the cap and floor lowered costs by $13.1B.
- The Oregon result applies to one utility's rate case, and a small number of facilities fall into the class. It should not be read as typical.
- The piece cites mixed evidence on load growth and prices (the North Dakota example), so it is fairly balanced.
- The piece ships from an energy retailer (Shipley Energy), which has a commercial interest in advising businesses on demand response.

## Grade
1 Pinocchio. Core figures check out. The Dallas Fed range is a press-derived paraphrase that differs from the paper (3-5%, revised to 6.9%). Several figures were not checked.
