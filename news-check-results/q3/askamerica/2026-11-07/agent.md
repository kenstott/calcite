# The 51st article's claims about D.C. evictions, ERAP cuts and cost of living are balanced and mostly accurate.

Article: "D.C. evictions hit 12-year high as safety net shrinks and cost of living soars" (51st.news, Nov 5, 2026). The connector's fetch worked, so no substitute outlet was needed.

**Rating: 1 Pinocchio.**

## Verified against the warehouse
- Washington-area CPI +4.1% year over year and about +28% since 2019: **true**. In econ.metro_cpi (CUURS35ASA0), Sep 2026 is 338.73 against 325.312 in Sep 2025 (+4.12%) and 265.5 in Sep 2019 (+27.6%).
- D.C. unemployment of 5.9% in August, almost 2 points above the national rate: **mostly true**. The warehouse's BLS LAUS series shows 5.7% for August 2026, against national UNRATE of 4.1%, a 1.6-point gap. The cited CFO report page had no searchable text.
- About 45% of renters spend more than 30% of income on rent: **mostly true**. ACS 2024 renter cost burden for D.C. is 46.6% in census.acs_congressional_district.

## Verified against primary sources
- 13,051 landlord-tenant filings in CY2025: **true** (D.C. Courts CY2025 Statistical Summary; 2024 was 13,697). The "less than half of 2019" comparison could not be checked because the 2019 summary URL returned 404.
- 2,420 evictions in FY2025 and about 1,600 pre-pandemic: **consistent** with UPO (2024 was nearly 2,000 and 18% above the pre-pandemic five-year average). The OTA source data and the 2,313 figure for FY2026 were not retrievable.
- ERAP cut to about $7M: **true** (Street Sense, a $1.6M cut from FY2026). The "more than $60M in 2024" figure is consistent with UPO's account of a 60% cut to $27M in FY2025. More than $20M requested in under six hours in Nov 2024: **true**.
- CLCPP (legal-aid network) Jan-Jun 2026 report:
  - ERAP used in 73% of payment dismissals before 2025 and 32% now: **true**.
  - 1,487 active cases: **true**.
  - 12% of closed cases fully represented and 17% partially: **true**.
  - Landlord attorney rate: the report gives 92% (1,363 of 1,487). The article's 93% is the cumulative 2019-2026 rate. This is a minor error.
  - Share of clients who are Black: the report gives 81%, not 82%. Women are 67%, which fits "nearly two-thirds".
- **Partially false:** the article says "among tenants represented by a CLCPP attorney for their entire case" the share staying housed fell from 77% to 63%. In the reports, both figures cover all closed cases with known outcomes, not only fully represented tenants. The numbers match, but the population is misdescribed.
- Over 80% of cases for nonpayment: consistent with CLCPP (81% of its cases). January Advisors' own page had no matching text.
- HFA arrears of $21.5M (2024) and $26.8M (2025): the 2024 figure appears in the outlet's own earlier article; the 2025 figure could not be confirmed. Not independent.

## Not checkable here
- EPI's $203,000 family budget: the EPI page returned HTTP 403.
- DCFPI's analysis and the BLS release pages could not be fetched (HTTP 403 or fetch failure).
- Quotes and opinions from Legal Aid DC, Bread for the City, SMOA and Councilmember Frumin are advocacy or opinion. The Jenkins causal quote ("record-high evictions") is graded unsupported: the article's own data show a 12-year high, and its own landlord source disputes the ERAP-cut cause.

## Misleading-framing flags
- The URL and one quote say "record"; the data support only a 12-year high.
- CLCPP statistics describe legal-aid clients, not all tenants.
- Falling filings alongside rising completed evictions is read as renters having fewer options. That interpretation is plausible but untested.
- The article presents both sides of the ERAP-cause dispute.
