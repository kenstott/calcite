The San Antonio Current (Texas Tribune) article's claims about Texas maternal mortality, severe maternal morbidity and the 2026 MMMRC report are balanced and accurate.

**Source.** The article is a Texas Tribune story republished by the San Antonio Current. The connector's own fetch of the article worked. The warehouse has no Texas maternal mortality, SMM or MMMRC table (search_catalog flagged "maternal" as unmatched). A query on health.cdc_state_vital_provisional returned only 2023-2024 state birth and death counts, which do not bear on the claims. All claims were therefore graded against the primary source, the DSHS/MMMRC 2026 Joint Biennial Report (56 pages, searched in full), plus the Tribune's earlier stories and the Texas Legislature bill histories.

**Verified (true)**
- Central claim: the 2024 maternal mortality ratio was 20.5 per 100,000 live births, which the report calls "a similar rate as before 2020" (2013-2019 range 17.0-20.7).
- 23.5 in 2021 excludes COVID deaths. The all-in 2021 ratio was 39.1.
- 77% (2021) and 76% (2024) of pregnancy-related deaths were potentially preventable. The previous report's cohort figure was 80%.
- Black women's pregnancy-related mortality rate was about 2.4 times that of white women.
- Infection was the leading cause in 2021 (55%, 91% of it COVID). Mental health was the leading cause in 2024 (20%, 12 of 59 deaths).
- Violence as a contributing circumstance rose from 5% to 19%. Obesity fell from 63% to 29%.
- Medicaid share fell from 57% to 53%. Out-of-pocket or no payor was 22% in both years.
- The report has 11 recommendations. The article's claim that most repeat the 2024 report was not compared line by line.
- Report delay: due Sept. 1, released Nov. 13, a gap of about 2.4 months, consistent with Rep. Howard's "more than 2 months".
- Bills: HB 286 was filed 11/09/2026. HB 3339 (2025) passed the House on 5/16/2025 and went no further after reaching the Senate.

**Errors**
- The article says severe maternal morbidity "increased from 80.4 to 83.5". The report says 83.9 in 2024, up from 80.4 in 2023 (not 2021).
- The article gives the unit as "per 100,000 births". The report's denominator is delivery hospitalizations. Black SMM of 146.2 vs 65.7 for white women is 2.2 times, so "more than double" holds.
- "Quadrupling" (5% to 19%) is 3.8 times, and it is a share of deaths, not a count. "Every other factor nearly doubled" understates nicotine (3% to 19%) and mental disorders (8% to 19%).

**Misleading-framing flags**
- The 2021-to-2024 "drop" compares against a COVID-excluded figure. The all-in 2021 ratio was 39.1, so most of the decline reflects the end of COVID deaths.
- "Slightly above 2019": 20.5 vs 17.2 is about 19% higher, though within the 2013-2019 range.
- The mental-health finding rests on 12 of 59 reviewed deaths, a plurality on small counts.
- 2022-2023 case reviews were skipped, so the report cannot speak in case detail to post-abortion-ban years. The article discloses this and DSHS's explanation for it.
