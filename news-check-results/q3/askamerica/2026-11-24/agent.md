**The 74 article on Alabama's reading gains: headline NAEP rank claims are mostly false.**

**Rating: 3 Pinocchios.**

**Central claim (headline and lede): "In 2015, Alabama ranked 45th on 4th-grade NAEP, rose to 18th in 2019 and 13th in 2024." FALSE.**
- The Fordham Institute report that the article summarizes gives those ranks (45th in 2015, 18th in 2019, then "another five spots") for **Mississippi**, not Alabama. The 74's headline, subhead and lede put them on Alabama.
- Warehouse (edu.naep_scores, grade 4 reading, 51 jurisdictions): Alabama 40th in 2015, 48th in 2019, 33rd in 2024. Fordham itself says Alabama ranked last in 2019.
- A governor's-office release linked from the Vaites post says Alabama's reading rank moved from 49th to 34th during Gov. Ivey's term.
- Alabama did improve (211.7 in 2019 to 213.2 in 2024; one of two states that gained over 2019-2024, with Louisiana). It is still about 33rd, not 13th.
- The article's correction note ("misidentified Alabama as The Magnolia State") suggests the Mississippi/Alabama mix-up was partly caught, but the headline ranks were not fixed.

**Other claims**
- **Alabama's 2024 score "just two points below the national average": mostly true.** Warehouse: 213.2 vs. national public 214.3 (about 1 point); Fordham says two points. Below-average from 2013 to 2022 matches.
- **Funding and history figures: true, to the Fordham report.** $1.5M raised in 1998, $6M from the legislature, ARI $58M (2013) to $40.7M (2018), $152M this year, $85,000 per school for a coach. These are fidelity to Fordham, not independent data; the warehouse has no state literacy funding table.
- **"Nearly 1,000 people are employed by the state to just focus on literacy": unsupported.** This is a statement by the interviewee, Liana Loewus (Thomas B. Fordham Institute), graded under her name as a subject claim, not an assertion by the article's author. The figure is not in the Fordham report (the only "1,000" there is a $1,000 teacher stipend), and I found no other source.
- **Not verified:** Massachusetts "almost 400 districts," the "42-page" length, and "Dr. Mackey nearly nine years" (Fordham says eight). "Gov. Kate Ivey" is a name error; she is Kay Ivey.
- Her explanations for why Alabama gained (coaching, steady leadership, top-down approach) are judgment, not testable here. Fordham itself says the gains reflect "multiple forces."

**Misleading framing.** The interview body is mostly faithful to the Fordham report, but the headline claim inflates the result. A rise from 45th to 13th reads like a miracle; Alabama's actual rank is mid-table and improving. It also carries Mississippi's ranks, which makes Mississippi's gains look like Alabama's. The statement that Alabama was "making gains when no one else was" ignores Mississippi and Louisiana, whose gains were larger.
