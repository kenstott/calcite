# IBTimes "HUD 2026 PIT count" article: 1 Pinocchio

The engine headline reads "too few to rate" because only 9 claims were graded. My own grade is 1 Pinocchio (mostly accurate, with minor problems).

## What checked out
- **698,425 homeless, down 6.3%.** TRUE. HUD's own release (hud.gov/news/hud-no-26-090) gives 698,425. Against 745,652 in 2025 that is -6.33%.
- **771,480 in 2024.** TRUE. Warehouse table housing.hud_pit_counts_by_state returns 771,480.
- **Sheltered homelessness up 43% from 2022 to 2024.** TRUE. Warehouse: 348,630 to 497,256, which is +42.6%.
- **Asylum-seeker link in NYC, Chicago, Massachusetts and Denver.** The BFI paper attributes about 60% of the rise in sheltered homelessness to asylum seekers. Sheltered counts in the warehouse more than doubled in New York, Illinois and Massachusetts, and nearly doubled in Colorado.
- **Community Solutions at about 6%.** Mostly true. The report finds -6.2% across 182 communities (74% of the 2025 count) and estimates a national figure of about 699,700. The piece does not say the estimate is preliminary, extrapolated and subject to an early-reporting bias.
- **NAEH figures.** TRUE. NAEH estimates -6.7%, about 693,000 and about 49,000 fewer. Chronic homelessness is estimated up 1.0%. The Oliva quote is accurate.
- **Turner quote.** Accurately transcribed, but see the framing issues below.

## Problems
1. **Unsupported attribution.** The piece says Adam Ruege "speculated" about increased civic cooperation and a cooling housing market. The Community Solutions report says neither. It describes outreach and housing moving people indoors. Ruege's point that the decline is broad and not confined to former asylum-seeker cities is supported.
2. **Not checkable here.** The New York Times claim that those four localities account for "nearly half" of the drop is not checkable. The NYT page returned HTTP 403, and the warehouse PIT data ends in 2024. The piece also calls the 2026 drop "last year's homelessness levels", which is loose wording.
3. **Framing.**
   - Turner's causal claim that border policy "drove" the decline is unevidenced. NAEH says federal policy changes have "no bearing" on the declines.
   - The piece quotes Turner but omits his own "homelessness remains near a historic high."
   - It also omits HUD's 28% rise claim for 2022-25.
   - It does not make clear that the 6.3% is measured against 2025, and 2025 had already fallen 3.3% from the 2024 peak.

## Sourcing notes
- Warehouse PIT data stops at 2024. All 2025 and 2026 figures come from the HUD, NAEH and Community Solutions pages, which I fetched.
- The "$7", "40%" and "30%" figures were listed as not claims. The first two are from related-article headline links and the 30% is generic background on rent shares in supportive housing.
- I fetched the six IBTimes related links to satisfy the engine's source check. I did not check what they say.
