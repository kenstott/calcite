## Summary
Every MCCA figure recomputes exactly from the cited PDF. The statewide DOJ-based rates match the FBI CDE series in warehouse table crime.cde_offenses. The framing misleads. The headline claims "RECORD LOW CRIME RATES", but only homicide is a record low.

## Claim results
- **Nine CA jurisdictions, Jan-Sep 2026 vs 2025: violent -6%, homicide -12%.** True. Summing the MCCA agency rows gives violent 52,724 to 49,383 (-6.3%) and homicide 504 to 444 (-11.9%). MCCA labels the data preliminary.
- **City bullets.** True: Oakland violent -19.1%; Sacramento violent -15.5%; homicide Sacramento -44.4%, LASD -29.5%, Oakland -21.6%, LA -12.8%.
- **Statewide homicide 3.5 per 100k in 2025, lowest since 1966.** True. The warehouse gives 3.48, and every year 2010-2024 is higher. The 1966 rank rests on DOJ via CalMatters because the warehouse starts in 2010.
- **Since Newsom took office: homicide -20%, robbery -31%, property -24%, vehicle theft -19%, violent -3%.** Mostly true. The figures reproduce only from a 2018 baseline, the year before he took office (warehouse: -20.9%, -30.9%, -24.3%, -19.2%, -3.0%). From 2019 the declines are smaller: homicide -18%, robbery -28%, property -23%, vehicle theft -11%, violent -1.5%.
- **Central claim, "RECORD LOW CRIME RATES".** Mostly false. The CA violent crime rate was 434 per 100k in 2025, against 391 in 2014, 396 in 2013 and 412 in 2011. It is only about 3% below 2018 and rose about 20% from 2019 to 2023 before falling.
- **"2023 largest-ever retail-theft investment, more than $242M to 38 agencies."** Partially false. The Governor's own Sept 14, 2023 release says $267,118,293 to 55 agencies.
- **$107M violence-intervention grants (Feb 2026).** True.
- **$350M and "prevent more than 30,000 violent incidents".** Mostly true. The Governor's Feb 2026 release calls the 30,230 incidents "interrupted, as reported by grantees", so they are a self-report and not an independent count.
- **$2.1B investment since 2019.** True as the Governor's own total. No itemized independent budget was found.
- **1,000 new CHP officers (2024).** True.
- **Fentanyl 39,644 lbs, 54.6M pills, $534M; naloxone 460,000 reversals; hemp/kratom counts and compliance rates.** True. These match the Governor's Sept 2, 2026 releases and are self-reported. The DHCS naloxone page loaded empty.
- **CDC 2024 state homicide rates (CA 5.1 to 4.5; MS 19.7, etc.).** Unsupported. The CDC page returned 403 to the connector and an empty table through WebFetch, and the warehouse has no CDC state homicide table.
- **Gun law rank #1 from Giffords and Everytown.** Unsupported. Both organization pages loaded without ranking tables. The only support is the Governor's own March 2026 fact sheet.
- **"$9 million-a-year program grew roughly eightfold".** Unsupported. No page read states it.

## Materially misleading framing, even where the facts check out
1. The city bullets list only the largest declines. In the same MCCA table homicides rose in San Diego (31 to 45), Fresno (15 to 22), Long Beach (23 to 29) and San Jose (20 to 21).
2. There is no national context. MCCA national totals show violent crime down 6.3%, the same as the nine CA jurisdictions, and homicide down 17.3%, more than CA's 11.9%. CalMatters reports that analysts see no clear cause and that the decline tracks the national trend, yet the release credits the Governor's strategies ("no accident").
3. The "since taking office" baseline is 2018.
4. "Prevent 30,000 incidents" rests on grantee self-reports.
5. The "record low" and "lowest in recorded history" phrasing applies to homicide only. Violent crime was lower in 2011-2014 and property crime was lower in 2021.
