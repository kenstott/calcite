American Immigration Council report, published Jan 14, 2026 — validated against TRAC, FactCheck.org, and the report's own PDF; AskAmerica corpus has no matching table

Summary

The AskAmerica corpus has no table covering ICE detention, ICE arrests, or any DHS immigration-enforcement statistic — confirmed live via three separate search_catalog queries and a full listing of the crime schema, all of which came back with zero matches on "detention," "ICE," "immigration," "deportation," or "removals." This is a genuine sourcing gap, filed as kenstott/govdata-ops#754. Because the corpus could not check this, the claim was instead validated directly against primary sources fetched from the originating organizations themselves: the American Immigration Council's own report PDF (published Jan 14, 2026), TRAC's separate Nov 24, 2025 report, and FactCheck.org's Jan 2026 analysis of Deportation Data Project microdata.

Finding: the 2,450% figure is accurately and faithfully quoted from AIC's report (fidelity is high — it is not a distortion or exaggeration of what AIC said), and the underlying trend it describes — a large, real shift toward detaining and arresting people with no criminal record — is independently corroborated in direction and rough order of magnitude by TRAC's own separate reporting and by FactCheck.org's independent analysis. However, the 2,450% figure applies to a narrow, specifically-defined subgroup (people arrested by ICE in the interior of the country, excluding CBP/border arrests, tracked January through November 29, 2025 only) growing off a very small January 2025 base (about 6% of that population), which is exactly the kind of base-rate effect that produces a very large percentage even for a real and substantial shift. I could not independently reproduce the precise 2,450% figure because TRAC's underlying daily table renders via JavaScript and returned no data through a direct fetch.

Step 2 — What the AIC report actually claims (read from the primary PDF)

Fetched the report PDF directly (americanimmigrationcouncil.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/immigration-detention-report.pdf, published Jan 14, 2026, 57 pages). The report states, verbatim, on page 10:

"From January through the end of November, the percent of people held in detention after an ICE arrest without a criminal record rose from six percent to 41 percent… In total, the number of people with no criminal record arrested by ICE and sent to detention increased by 2,450 percent from January through the end of November."

Key methodological details the headline number omits: (1) this is specifically people arrested by ICE in the interior of the country — CBP/border arrests are excluded; (2) the window is January 2025 through November 29, 2025 (not a full year, and not extending into the December 2025–January 2026 Operation Metro Surge period covered by AIC's later April 2026 follow-up blog post); (3) the report's own source is TRAC's "ICE Detainees" tracker (tracreports.org/immigration/detentionstats/pop_agen_table.html, accessed by AIC on Nov 19, 2025) combined with Deportation Data Project FOIA microdata (deportationdata.org). The separately-cited "600 percent increase in at-large arrests" figure (also in the report) is a related but distinct statistic about arrest location/method, not criminal history.

Step 3 — Independent corroboration from other primary sources

Two other primary sources, using overlapping but not identical data, corroborate the direction and rough scale of the shift, without independently reproducing the exact 2,450% figure (their window/population definitions differ from AIC's):

  • TRAC's own "Taking Stock" report (Nov 24, 2025) — for the total ICE detained population (broader than AIC's interior-arrest-only subset): no-criminal-conviction detainees rose from 42,755 (Sept 21, 2025) to 47,964 (Nov 16, 2025), while criminal-conviction detainees were nearly flat (17,007 → 17,171). 97% of the net population increase in that eight-week window was people with no criminal conviction.
  • FactCheck.org's Jan 2026 analysis, using ICE's own public detention statistics and Deportation Data Project microdata: the number of ICE detainees with no convictions or pending charges rose from 3,165 (Feb 2025) to 25,193 (Jan 2026) — roughly a 696% increase, over a similar-but-not-identical window (Feb 2025–Jan 2026 vs. AIC's Jan–Nov 2025) and a broader population (all ICE detention, not just interior ICE arrests). By comparison, December 2024 (Biden's last full month) had just 869 detainees with no convictions or pending charges.
  • TRAC's live Quick Facts page (checked 2026-09-28): 70.6% of the total ICE detained population currently has no criminal conviction, consistent with the trend continuing well past AIC's November 2025 cutoff.

None of these three sources reproduces AIC's exact 2,450% figure, because none uses the identical population definition (interior ICE arrests only) and window (Jan–Nov 29, 2025) that AIC used. But all three independently confirm a real, large, and directionally consistent shift toward detaining people with no criminal record — this is not a fabricated or cherry-picked statistic.
