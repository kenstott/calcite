The Newsweek article's claims about mortgage-free homeownership, mortgage rates and housing prices are balanced and mostly accurate; 3 claims could not be confirmed.

**Rating: 1 Pinocchio.**

Source: Newsweek, "Map Shows States Where Homeowners Are Most Likely to Be Mortgage-Free" (fetched successfully).

**Verified true**
- Freddie Mac 30-year rate: 7.11% (week of Nov 12, 2026), 7.18% the prior week, 6.24% a year earlier. Matches warehouse table econ.fred_indicators (MORTGAGE30US) and the Freddie Mac PMMS page.
- "Mortgage rates doubled in 2022" after Fed hikes: autumn 2021 range 2.86-3.14%, 2022 peak 7.08%. The Fed link is the standard explanation.
- Existing home sales of 3.75 million (October 2026, annualized): matches econ.fred_indicators (EXHOSLUSM495S). "Lowest since August 2010" comes from a second Newsweek article relaying Realtor.com, a secondary source.
- Mortgage-free share rose from 34.4% (2010-14) to 39.4% (2020-24): confirmed by the Census Bureau story of Jan 29, 2026.
- National median listing price of $415,000 for October: confirmed via the second Newsweek article, which relays Realtor.com.

**Problems**
1. Unit error: "56.6 percent ... is 15.6 percent more than the national average of 41 percent." The gap is 15.6 percentage points. The relative gap is about 38%.
2. "Just as expensive": Maryland's $415,000 equals the article's own national median and sits about 21% below DC's $528,000. This is partially false by the piece's own figures.
3. The article puts 41% (2025 national share) beside 39.4% (2020-24 five-year share) without noting they are different ACS products and vintages.

**Not verifiable, graded unsupported**
- The 2025 state shares for DC 26.5%, MD 30.6% and CO 31.6%, and the WV 56.6%, MS 52.7% and NM 50% figures. The warehouse has no state-level mortgage-free table, data.census.gov returned an empty page, and Realtor.com returned HTTP 429.
  - The direction matches the Census 2024 five-year data: WV highest at 53.9%, DC lowest at 24.3%, MD next at 29.0%.
- State listing prices: WV $259,999, MS $292,000, NM $399,000. Only the national figure was confirmed.
- West Virginia's 18-34 population down 5% from 2015 to 2025 (versus +3.8% nationally). No source found.

**Attributed quote (subject claim, speaker Jiayi Xu, Realtor.com senior economist):** "Areas with a higher share of outright ownership are less exposed and may feel smaller effects from rate shocks because residents who own their homes outright are likely indifferent to changes in borrowing costs." This is a hedged causal judgment. No study or data found tests it, so it is graded unsupported, though the logic is plausible. It is the only quote of a named person in the piece; other attributions are to unnamed "experts."

**Overall:** The core story and the national rate and sales data hold up. The deductions are for the percent versus percentage-point error and the "just as expensive" framing. The Realtor.com state figures remain unconfirmed.
