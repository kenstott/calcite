**The Blaze article's claims about post-election gas prices, the NBC exit poll and seasonal patterns are balanced and accurate.**

## Findings
- Price path: GasBuddy $4.24 (Oct 26), $4.12 (Nov 2), $3.96 (Nov 9), $3.74 (Nov 16) were each read on GasBuddy's own pages. The warehouse series (EIA weekly, energy.eia_retail_gasoline_prices) gives 4.269, 4.153, 3.995, 3.805. These are different surveys but follow the same path.
- AAA: $4.2926 a month ago and $3.6366 on 11/23. The article's "$3.72 on Thursday" is consistent with AAA's week-ago figure of $3.81 and its continued fall.
- NBC exit poll (read from NBC's page): 87% very or somewhat concerned, 59% very concerned, 67% of the very concerned voted Democratic. All correct.
- De Haan quote: verbatim on GasBuddy's Nov 9 release. His claim holds in EIA data. In even election years 2006-2024 the price fell in the month before Election Day except 2010 (2.732 to 2.806). 2022 also fell (3.912 to 3.796).
- "Over 70 cents higher than before the Iran strikes": EIA Feb 23 $2.937 vs Nov 16 $3.805 is +87 cents. GasBuddy Feb 23 $2.88 vs $3.74 is +86 cents.
- "12 states fell more than 25 cents" and the "lowest since March" forecast: both are in GasBuddy's Nov 16 release. TT/Bloomberg on Nov 17 reported $3.78, the lowest since mid-March.
- Hormuz at about 20 million barrels a day: EIA gives 20 million b/d for 2024 and flat for 1Q25. The IEA page returned 403, so the IEA's 2025 figure rests on a search summary.
- Trump's quote that oil "dropped like a rock the day after the election" is mostly true. WTI fell about 8% on Nov 4 ($99.64 to $91.73 in FRED; NBC says $88.12). It had already fallen from $107.56 (Oct 30) to $100.06 (Nov 2), before the vote.

## Misleading-framing flags
- The headline and lede imply a post-election plunge. The decline began before Election Day: EIA peaked at $4.478 on Sept 21, and $4.269 on Oct 26 fell to $4.153 on Nov 2. The article itself rebuts the election-timing theory, citing seasonality, refinery switchover, Hormuz flows and inventories.
- The causal weight of each factor (seasonality, Hormuz flows, Trump's softer stance) is attributed to outlets and not tested.
- Gas is still about 87 cents above pre-war levels.
