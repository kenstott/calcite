## Potentially misleading
1. Headline and framing: it says costs in battleground states are rising "faster than the national average." The summary I saw supports this for gas (state increases above 40%). For groceries and housing, the evidence is mostly rankings (WalletHub burden, NTU tariff exposure, Redfin levels), which show high levels or exposure, not a faster pace. Alaska's and Ohio's price changes are cited, but the comparisons are uneven.
2. Cause attribution: the piece links price increases to tariffs and the Iran war. That is plausible and partly supported, but the state-level data show correlation, not causation. The Great Lakes refinery issue is an independent, regional cause.
3. Selection: the states are chosen because they are Senate battlegrounds, then shown with the largest increases. Without a full state comparison, readers cannot tell whether the "battleground" label means anything.
4. Mixed dates and baselines: gas is from Feb. 28 to Nov. 2, wages and CPI are September, purchasing power is the three months to August, and housing is September. Combined, this overstates a single "now" snapshot.
5. Yale tariff figure: the estimate depends on which tariffs are in force after the February court ruling. It needs a stated scenario.

## Limits of this check
Only the summarized NBC text was available, so the quote wording and some context are uncertain. Redfin, WalletHub, NTU, the polling, and per-state gas data were not independently accessed.

## Rating
1 Pinocchio: the core numbers are accurate, but the headline overreaches for groceries and housing and the causal framing is stronger than the data supports.
