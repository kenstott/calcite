# Fact-check: "Record low crime rates, crime drops again in major cities across California" (gov.ca.gov, 2026-11-17)

Note: WebFetch returned only a summary of the release, not verbatim text, so the quotes were not checked word for word. Claims below are as summarized.

## Claims and verdicts

1. Homicide rate 3.5 per 100,000 in 2025, lowest since statewide reporting began in 1966. VERIFIED. The CA DOJ 2025 report gives 3.5, down from 4.3 in 2024 (-18.6%); 1,666 to 1,374 homicides. DOJ cautions about comparing 2025 to prior years because of the transition to CIBRS.
2. California's 2024 homicide rate was 4.5 per 100,000 (CDC), compared with states 2-4x higher. PARTLY CHECKED. DOJ's own 2024 figure is 4.3; the CDC figure uses a different method, so the gap is plausible but I did not pull the CDC table or the state comparisons. Mixing the CDC 2024 rate with the DOJ 2025 rate is apples to oranges.
3. Since Newsom took office, homicides -20%, robbery -31%, property crime -24%, motor vehicle theft -19%, violent crime -3% (DOJ through 2025). NOT CHECKABLE HERE in full; I did not retrieve the DOJ baseline-year tables. Note the baseline choice (2019 vs 2018) matters, and violent crime down only 3% is the weakest result in the list and sits next to much larger local numbers.
4. MCCA report, first nine months 2025 vs 2026: nine California jurisdictions combined, violent crime -6%, homicides -12%. NOT CONFIRMED. I could not locate the MCCA Jan-Sep 2026 California table (the Q3 PDF was not reachable). Press coverage says the national MCCA numbers are down (homicides about -15% to -17%), so direction is consistent, but New York City is an outlier on the rise nationally.
5. Violent crime -19% Oakland, -15% Sacramento; homicides -44% Sacramento, -30% LA Sheriff, -22% Oakland, -13% LA. NOT CONFIRMED / PARTLY CONTRADICTED by earlier data. Oakland police: violent crime -17% through Aug 9 (ABC7), and Q1 homicides -39% (CBS), consistent with a decline. For Sacramento, the MCCA midyear report shows homicides 23 to 17 (about -26%), and Q1 showed 6 to 11 (an increase), so a -44% nine-month figure would need a very large Q3 drop. Small counts make percentages volatile.
6. Over $2.1 billion invested since 2019; $242M+ retail theft investment in 2023; CHP partnerships; top-ranked gun laws (two advocacy groups); National Guard counterdrug fentanyl seizures worth $534M; about 15,300 illegal hemp/kratom/7-OH products removed. NOT CHECKABLE HERE (state's own budget and operational figures, not independently published in sources I reached). Gun law ranking by Giffords and Everytown is well known.

## Misleading context
- Source is the Governor's office press release: self-congratulatory framing, causal credit given to state spending without evidence. Crime fell nationally in the same period (MCCA national homicides about -15 to -17%), so California's decline is not unique and cannot be attributed to state policy from this data.
- Headline says "major cities across California" but only nine selected agencies are covered; MCCA membership is a self-selected group, and 2026 data are preliminary and unaudited.
- Preliminary 2026 city-level data are mixed with finalized 2025 DOJ data; the record-low statewide claim covers 2025, not 2026.
- Violent crime since 2019 is down only 3%, and the release leads with homicide.
- Percent changes in small-count cities (Sacramento) are volatile.

## Rating
2 Pinocchios (Some Omissions and/or Exaggerations). The headline statewide fact is accurate; the city-level figures and causal credit are unverified or doubtful, and the framing omits the national trend.
