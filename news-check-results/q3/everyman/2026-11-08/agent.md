## Flags
- **Dateline and timing.** The article is dated Nov 5, but the same AAA page now shows a Nov 8 average of $4.0203 in its header. Anyone reading it on Nov 8 sees stale figures. This is not an error in the article, but it is potentially confusing.
- **EIA vintage.** The EIA numbers are for the week ending Oct 30, released Nov 4. The article's wording suggests they are current to Nov 5, which is a normal lag.
- **Gasoline demand framing.** A weekly demand jump of 450 thousand b/d is noisy. Any implied "demand is recovering" reading would overstate what one week shows. I could not tell whether the article draws that inference.
- **Record claim.** The "highest ever for November" line is the one headline-grade assertion I could not confirm.

Confidence: the verified numbers match primary data exactly. The overall grade is held at 1 Pinocchio because several claims (WTI, five-year-average, record, state tables) could not be checked, not because any claim was found false.
