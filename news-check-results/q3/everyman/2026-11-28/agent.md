# Fact-check: Fox Business, "Amtrak hits record 37.1M riders amid high flight, gas prices" (Nov 26, 2026)

Method: I fetched the article (the fetch tool returned a summary, not verbatim text) and searched for corroboration. I could not open Amtrak's own release; I relied on trade-press and aggregator reports of it. Treat the "Supported" verdicts as secondary-source confirmation.

## Claims
| # | Claim | Verdict | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Record 37.1M riders, up 7.4% | Supported | Fact sheet reports 37,067,685 vs 34,528,695 in FY25 (+7.4%). Railway Age, American Rails. |
| 2 | Third straight record year | Supported | Same sources. |
| 3 | FY (12 months to Sept 30) operating revenue $4.1B, up 4.9% | Mostly supported | One source gives $4.1B and 4.9%. Another says it passed $4B for the first time. FY25 was $3.9B, so +4.9% is consistent. |
| 4 | Adjusted net loss $492.7M, 19% better | Supported | Matches reported figures. FY25 adjusted operating loss was $598.4M, so the 19% improvement is plausible. |
| 5 | Interim President Byl Herrmann said high airfares and gas prices likely pushed people to rail; credited NEC investment and new trainsets | Not checked directly | I could not read the release. The causal attribution appears in secondary coverage. Quote wording unverified. |
| 6 | Hopper's Hayley Berg: holiday airfares highest in at least 10 years, jet fuel about double last year, Iran conflict | Partly supported | Hopper's late-Sept 2026 analysis shows 10-year-high Thanksgiving ($402, +31%) and Christmas ($452) fares and cites Iran-related jet fuel costs. I found no source for the "about double" figure or a Berg quote. Unverified. |
| 7 | Northeast Regional trainsets replaced by 2028; Acela replacement begun | Not checked | No source found. |
| 8 | NY-Washington Acela cut from ~3 hours to 2.5, possibly by 2028, pending approval | Not checked | No source found. |
| 9 | About $2.4B/yr recent funding, plus $22B from the 2021 law over five years | Not checked | The 2021 infrastructure law gave Amtrak $22B in advance appropriations. I did not verify the $2.4B/yr figure. |

## Context the piece may under-weight
- Growth was concentrated in state-supported corridors, which gave more than half of the increase. The article's gas-price framing is Amtrak's "likely" explanation, not a demonstrated cause.
- Figures are preliminary. Adjusted and gross ticket revenue differ, which complicates year-over-year revenue comparisons.
- The headline implies a causal link between gas prices and ridership. The ridership trend is a three-year run that began before the 2026 fuel spike, so the link is a plausible hypothesis, not a finding.
- The Berg quote could not be located. A claim of "double" jet fuel cost is a large one.

## Rating
**1 Pinocchio (mostly accurate, with minor caveats).** The headline figures check out. The causal story (gas and air prices drove ridership) is Amtrak's own assertion and the article presents it with appropriate hedging ("likely"). The jet-fuel "double" claim and several secondary claims (fleet timelines, funding) are not verified.
