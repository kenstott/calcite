# Fact-check: "A Deep Dive Into Alabama's Reading Gains" (The 74, Jessika Harkay, Nov 2026)

Limits: WebFetch returned summaries, not verbatim text. I did not reach NAEP Data Explorer directly, so rank figures rest on state, Fordham, and press sources.

## Verdict: 3 Pinocchios (headline numbers likely wrong)

## Claims
1. FALSE / not supported. "Alabama ranked 45th on 4th-grade NAEP in 2015, 18th in 2019, 13th in 2024" (subtitle and body).
   - The Alabama governor's office and the state education department report Alabama at 49th in 2019 and 34th in 2024. PARCA gives 33rd for 2024.
   - Fordham's report says Alabama was last in 2019, with a 2024 average of 213, two points below the national average.
   - Secondary write-ups attach the 45th, 18th and 13th sequence to Mississippi, not Alabama. This looks like a Mississippi-to-Alabama mix-up or an unlabelled adjusted ranking. I could not tell which.
   - Alabama's score rose only 212 to 213 from 2019 to 2024, which NCES treats as not statistically significant per 1819 News. Fordham calls Alabama one of two states that improved. The claim is disputed, not settled.
2. Supported. Katherine Mitchell launched ARI in 1998 with 16 schools, backed by the A+ Education Partnership and the Eagle Forum (Fordham). The article's "Catherine" is a spelling variance.
3. Supported. ARI funding fell from $58M (2013) to $40.7M (2018), and is $152M for 2026-27 (Fordham, other coverage).
4. Supported. $85,000 per school for a coach (Fordham, Aldeman interview). Coaches are barred from substitute duty by an MOU.
5. Supported. The 2019 Alabama Literacy Act was cosponsored by Rep. Terri Collins (the article spells it "Terry"), who is retiring.
6. Supported. K-3 students are assessed three times a year. Good-cause exemptions cover English learners and students with disabilities (IEPs). Fordham gives 3,454 of 57,098 third graders promoted for good cause in 2024-25.
7. Misattribution/typo. "Gov. Kate Ivey" should be Kay Ivey. The superintendent is Eric Mackey, in office since 2018, and the article calls him only "Dr. Mackey." Ivey is leaving office (term-limited).
8. Attributed opinions (coaching is the biggest factor, federal role is minimal, Reading First had problems, watch older grades and leadership transitions). These are Loewus's views as reported. I could not confirm the exact quotes against the verbatim text. Not checkable here.

## Misleading even where facts are right
- The headline rank jump (45th to 13th) is far above any official ranking and carries no adjustment label. The real 2024 position is mid-30s.
- Alabama's 2019-2024 gain is statistically insignificant, and 8th-grade reading has fallen (Chalkbeat). The piece offers a causal story (coaching, the 2019 law) from one advocacy-think-tank report, with little skepticism.
