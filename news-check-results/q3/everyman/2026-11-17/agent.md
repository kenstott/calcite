# Fact-check: "Texas maternal death rate drops to pre-pandemic levels as mental health becomes the leading cause" (SA Current, republished from Texas Tribune, Nov 2026)

Method and limits: I read the SA Current piece, the original Texas Tribune story (2026-11-13), the Tribune's 2026-09-25 delay story, and the DSHS MMMRC page, which links the "2026 Joint Biennial Report" (Nov 2026). The report PDF came back as unreadable binary through WebFetch, and I did not work around that. So no figure below was checked against the report text itself. They were checked against the Tribune original and repeat coverage (US News, Click2Houston, Washington Post syndication, Yahoo), which all trace to the same report. That is secondary corroboration, not primary verification.

## Misleading or weak points
- **Headline overreach.** "Pre-pandemic levels" depends on a 2019 comparison that is not quantified. The text only says "slightly above." The title also overstates by implying the rate "dropped to" the old level.
- **Mixed measures.** The report gives a maternal mortality ratio (a death-certificate-based measure) and, separately, pregnancy-related deaths from committee review. The "leading cause" finding applies only to reviewed pregnancy-related deaths, which are a sample. The piece does not explain the method, so readers may take "mental health is now the leading cause of maternal death" as a statewide count.
- **Not like-for-like.** 2021 excludes COVID deaths, but the 2024 figure is not described the same way. 2024 data were partial: Dec 2025 minutes covered only three quarters. Comparing 2021 with 2024 is therefore fragile.
- **Missing years.** 2022 and 2023, the first years after the abortion ban, are absent. A "drop" over a two-point comparison says nothing about the post-ban trend. The piece notes this, but the headline does not.
- **Big shifts in contributing factors** (violence 5% to 19%, obesity 63% to 29%) may reflect changed review methodology or small samples rather than real change. This is not caveated.
- **Counter-trend buried.** Severe maternal morbidity rose, and the Black-white disparity persists. The headline is only positive.
- Attribution: the story is Texas Tribune reporting republished by SA Current. The article should be judged as the Tribune's.

## Rating
**1 Pinocchio (some shading).** The individual numbers match the Tribune and the other coverage of the report, and attributions are fair. Points off for the unverified 2019 "pre-pandemic" comparison, the unexplained measure and sampling differences, and the thin 2021-vs-2024 framing. My own checks stopped short of the primary PDF, so this rating carries that limit.
