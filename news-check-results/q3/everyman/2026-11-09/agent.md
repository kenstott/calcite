# Fact-check: IBTimes, "HUD 2026 PIT count homelessness decline" (ibtimes.com/hud-2026-pit-count-homelessness-decline-3809530)

Overall: 2 Pinocchios (some shading). Headline numbers match HUD; the baseline is muddled and the causal framing is HUD's, not established.

Method note: only web search and page fetch were used. The IBTimes page returned only a summary, not verbatim text, so the quote wording is as relayed. HUD's AHAR full report is not yet out.

## Claims

| # | Claim | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2026 PIT count: 698,425 people homeless on a single January night | TRUE | HUD press release HUD No. 26-090 (hud.gov/news/hud-no-26-090) |

## Misleading or missing context

- Mixed baselines (2024 vs 2025) in one sentence. The correct comparison for 6.3% is 2025.
- The headline-level framing (the piece's title says "the government is claiming credit") is fair, but the causal story (border enforcement) is asserted by HUD without evidence. PIT counts are single-night estimates that vary with CoC methods, weather, and counting effort; the count alone does not establish cause. The piece does include the asylum-seeker and nonprofit counterpoints, which is to its credit.
- The "record" decline is partly a rebound from a 2022-2024 surge, so the level remains near historic highs (HUD itself says so). Chronic homelessness rising is correctly noted.
- Nonprofit estimates (NAEH, Community Solutions) are preliminary and independent of the HUD figure; the piece reports them accurately.

## Rating: 2 Pinocchios

Core numbers are right and quotes attributed to HUD match. Deduction for the inconsistent baseline that makes the decline look like either 6.3% or 9.5%, and for several secondary claims (43% shelter rise, NYT share, Ruege/Oliva quotes) that I could not verify.
