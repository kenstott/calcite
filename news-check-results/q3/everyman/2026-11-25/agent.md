## Rating: 2 Pinocchios (mostly accurate, with a misleading framing)

The verified facts hold up: the record count, the 7%+ mortgage rates, the 16-year saving figure and the NAR age of 40.

## Material problems
1. The "record/historic high" framing applies to the headcount, not the share. The share is slightly below 2020. Population growth drives part of the count.
2. The Great Depression comparison is unsupported by anything found. It conflicts with the 2020 level and likely refers to a longer historical series.
3. The piece combines different age bands (18 to 34, 25 to 34, 30 to 34) and multi-year pooled windows (2023 to 25 vs 2020 to 22), which makes headline comparisons hard to follow.
4. It attributes the trend mainly to housing costs. Realtor.com's own reasoning supports that, but marriage timing, immigration and culture also contribute. Roughly 70% of those living at home are employed, which is consistent with an affordability story but does not prove it.
5. The metro and regional figures could not be checked here, which is most of the article's numbers.
