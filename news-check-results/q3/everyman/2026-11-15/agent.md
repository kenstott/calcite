# Fact-check: Inside Climate News, "Global warming remains unchecked, experts warn at COP31" (Bob Berwyn, 12 Nov 2026)

Method: WebFetch of the article (returned as a summary, not verbatim text), plus web searches against the Climate Action Tracker (CAT) Nov 2026 update, Copernicus (C3S), and wire coverage of COP31. I could not read the CAT PDF directly or the Express & Star wire story (403), so several verdicts rest on search-result summaries of those sources.

## Claim-by-claim

| # | Claim | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | CAT projects warming of nearly 5°F by 2100 on current trajectory | Supported | CAT Nov 2026 update: 2.7°C (2.72°C) current policies, up from 2.64°C in 2025. 2.72°C is about 4.9°F. |
| 2 | Under the most optimistic scenario, about 3.4°F by 2100 | Supported, wording loose | 1.9°C is about 3.4°F and is CAT's optimistic scenario. But the article ties it to "full implementation of existing policies and pledges". CAT's pledges-and-targets case is about 2.1°C (3.8°F), and 2030/2035 targets alone give 2.6°C. The 1.9°C case assumes more than existing policies and pledges. |
| 3 | Copernicus says warming has accelerated since Paris 2015 | Supported | C3S article of 29 Oct 2026 says the acceleration appears across the main datasets. |

## Overall rating: 1 Pinocchio (on the Washington Post 0-4 scale)

The headline numbers are right: CAT's ~4.9°F and 3.4°F figures and the Copernicus 2029 date all match primary sources. The Thunberg and co-host facts check out. The weaknesses are small:

- The "most optimistic scenario" is described as "existing policies and pledges", which is more pessimistic than what CAT's 1.9°C case actually assumes.
- The Copernicus "projects" language drops C3S's own warning that the app is a trend extrapolation, not a forecast.
- The Paris-era comparison date (2045 versus 2042) is inconsistent in C3S's own page.
- Two direct quotes (Thunberg, possibly Heck) could not be matched verbatim.

## Materially misleading?

Not materially, but two framing points matter:

1. The headline ("Global warming remains unchecked") and the "2029" claim give a stronger impression of a scientific forecast than the sources support. The CAT 4.9°F figure is a modeled end-of-century estimate with wide uncertainty (about a 33% chance of 3°C or more), and the Copernicus date is a straight-line extrapolation.
2. The piece is quote-heavy and one-sided by design: all voices are climate analysts and advocates, with no response from Turkey, Australia, or the EU on the "clean" wording and speech criticisms. Those are allegations that are unverified here.

## Limits

Claims 9, 12, 15 are graded "not checkable here" because I found no accessible primary record (negotiating text, speech transcript, or press coverage) for them. The article text itself came through a summarizing fetch, so attributions may differ slightly from the original.
