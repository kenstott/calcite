# Fact-check: Fox 17, "Nashville mayor says crime is down 17% this year as more residents report feeling safe" (Nov 13, 2026)

Method: WebFetch of the article (summarized by the tool, not verbatim) plus WebSearch of other outlets. I did not reach MNPD's raw data or the survey PDF for 2026, so most findings rest on secondary reporting.

## Claims
| Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Mayor O'Connell said crime is down 17% this year | Accurately attributed. It is his claim, repeated by outlets. Not independently recomputed from MNPD data. |
| Declines in every precinct | Consistent with the Fox 17 first-half MNPD report (earlier article). Not re-verified for Nov. |
| North Nashville crime down 13% | Attributed to the mayor. No second source found. Not checkable here. |
| Auto thefts down 30% | Conflicts with other figures: WKRN (July) said 40%, MNPD first-half summary said 37%. Differences could reflect time windows, but the drop shrinking from 40% to 30% is unexplained. Unverified. |
| September update: crime down 16%, homicides down 44% | Matches the September mayor's office update and MNPD first-half summary. Q1 homicides were down 20% (WSMV), so the 44% reflects a different window. |
| Survey: Nashville Police + Public Safety Alliance, Impact Research, 1,000 residents, Sept 8-15 | Confirmed by WSMV (Nov 12), WKRN and Yahoo. |
| 70% feel safe, up from 67% in 2025 | Confirmed. A 3-point change is likely within the margin of error for n=1,000 (about +/-3 points). |
| Crime perceived as increasing: 58% to 49% | Confirmed by secondary reports. |
| North Nashville/Bordeaux feel-safe 60% to 76% | Reported in the article. Not independently verified. Subgroup sample is small, so a big margin of error. |

## Misleading context
- The survey was commissioned by an advocacy alliance tied to city public-safety efforts, not an independent pollster.
- The headline pairs "crime down 17%" (a mayoral claim) with the survey as if the two corroborate each other. A 3-point rise in feeling safe is not statistically strong.
- Reported-crime figures depend on reporting rates and category definitions. The article gives no baseline counts.
- Other polling in the same survey: 47% positive vs 48% negative on the city's handling of public safety, which the article's headline does not reflect.
- 70% in 2026 is still below the 73% seen two years earlier (per Axios), so "more feel safe" is a partial rebound.

## Rating
**1 Pinocchio (some shading).** The core numbers are accurately reported and consistent with other sources, but they are largely unverified mayoral claims presented with little scrutiny, the auto theft figure conflicts with other reports, and the survey gain is small and from an interested sponsor.
