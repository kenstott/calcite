# Fact-check: The 51st, "D.C. evictions hit 12-year high as safety net shrinks and cost of living soars"

URL checked: https://51st.news/dc-record-evictions-housing-crisis. It was published Nov. 5, 2026, by James Jarvis, with Street Sense Media and American University's Investigative Reporting Workshop.

**Overall grade: 2 Pinocchios (mixed, leaning mostly accurate).** I verified the broad trend and several figures, but I could not match the headline counts to any primary source I could reach. The piece also has a framing problem, described below.

**Limits of this check**
- WebFetch returned a model-written summary of the article, not its verbatim text. I could not check exact quotes word for word.
- I reached no primary agency dataset directly. That means no OTA data, no Superior Court summary and no CLCPP report. The New America PDF came back undecodable.
- All verdicts rest on secondary sources: New America, UPO, DCFPI, Legal Aid DC and Planetizen. "Not checkable here" means I found no accessible source.

## Materially misleading or weak points
1. **Headline count conflicts with other sources.** The 2,420 figure, the "nearly 30%" rise and "12-year high" do not match New America (1,933, 7-year high) or UPO (2,614, 10-year high). The same outlet's earlier piece used 1,869. The piece does not explain which definition it uses (completed versus scheduled, OTA versus court data, fiscal versus calendar year). This is the main reason for 2 Pinocchios.
2. **Causal framing.** The piece leans on ERAP cuts as the main driver. Legal Aid attorneys hold that view, and the article does include the landlord-side rebuttals from Hunter and Frumin. But the ERAP comparison is a peak year against a proposed or approved budget. The data also show filings falling while completed evictions rise, which points to a more complex cause than funding alone.
3. **Anecdote versus data.** Pleasant's case involves a DCHA voucher and inspection failure, not ERAP. It is illustrative only, and the piece does not claim otherwise.

**To upgrade or downgrade the grade:** pull the OTA eviction dataset and the Superior Court 2025 Statistical Summary, and check whether 2,420 is a defined count that reconciles with the other figures.
