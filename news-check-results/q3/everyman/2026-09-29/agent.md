# Fact-Check: Rosen's "3.5 million work-permit holders" claim

## Bottom line
The claim is **accurate** and, if anything, conservative. Both the underlying policy claim (DHS rescinded automatic EAD extensions) and the numeric figures (3.5M+ total, ~600K construction, 500K+ hospitality) check out against a credible, methodologically transparent source. The rule remains in effect today.

## 1. Underlying policy claim: verified accurate
- On **October 30, 2025**, DHS/USCIS published an Interim Final Rule in the Federal Register ("Removal of the Automatic Extension of Employment Authorization Documents") ending the practice of automatically extending EAD validity for renewal applicants (previously up to 540 days).
- Applies to EAD renewals (Form I-765) filed **on or after October 30, 2025**; applications filed before that date retain their automatic extension.
- Stated DHS rationale: enhanced screening/vetting before extending work authorization.
- Sources: [Federal Register rule](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/10/30/2025-19702/removal-of-the-automatic-extension-of-employment-authorization-documents), [USCIS news release](https://www.uscis.gov/newsroom/news-releases/dhs-ends-automatic-extension-of-employment-authorization)

## 2. Rule status as of today (2026-09-29): still in effect
- A lawsuit (*Jane Doe 1 et al. v. DHS*, C.D. Cal., No. 8:26-cv-00060) sought a preliminary injunction; heard June 24, 2026, with an ambiguous dismissal docket entry August 7, 2026. No nationwide order restoring automatic extensions is in place.
- A second, narrower D.C. challenge (Public Citizen) was mooted when USCIS adjudicated the individual plaintiff's case and was voluntarily dismissed June 2026.
- Rosen's own framing (April 29, 2026 floor vote; her April 10 Las Vegas roundtable) is consistent with the rule being live and unresolved through 2026, matching the prompt's reference to her July 10 and September 18, 2026 follow-ups on continuing USCIS/DOJ delays.
- Rosen cites real, cross-checkable processing-time data: renewals can only be filed up to 6 months before expiration, but actual processing has run up to ~18 months, with under half completed within that 6-month window — meaning many renewal filers now face a real gap with no work authorization.

## 3. The numeric claims: hold up well
Rosen's figures trace to a **FWD.us** analysis (labor-market/immigration research org), not a DHS estimate — Rosen does not cite a source in her floor remarks or press release, but the numbers match FWD.us's published breakdown almost exactly:

| Figure | Rosen's claim | FWD.us estimate |
|---|---|---|
| Total EAD holders affected | "more than 3.5 million" | 3.8 million |
| Construction | "roughly 600,000" | 613,000 |
| Leisure & hospitality | "more than half a million" | 514,000 |

FWD.us methodology: augmented 2024 American Community Survey data, projected to end of FY2025, with immigration-status assignments matched against FY2025 administrative EAD-approval data. Composition of the 3.8M: ~2.3M asylum applicants, ~1.1M TPS holders/applicants, ~200K refugees/asylees/green-card applicants, ~100K H-1B spouses/dependents (H-4 EAD holders) — a real, itemized population whose work authorization depends on timely renewal processing, not a vague or invented aggregate.

Rosen's numbers are actually **rounded down** from FWD.us's figures (3.5M vs 3.8M actual estimate), which argues against exaggeration — if anything she understated the total.

Source: [FWD.us: Ending Automatic Work Authorization Extensions Will Cost Billions](https://www.fwd.us/news/ending-automatic-work-authorization-will-cost-billions/)

## 4. Do the sub-figures make sense against total labor-force size?
- Total construction industry employment (BLS, 2025): **~8.1–8.3 million**. FWD.us's 613,000 EAD-holding construction workers = **~7–7.5%** of the construction workforce. Plausible — construction has an outsized share of immigrant/asylum-status labor relative to other sectors.
- Total leisure & hospitality employment (BLS, Dec 2025): **~16.9 million**. FWD.us's 514,000 EAD holders = **~3%** of that workforce. Also plausible and proportionally smaller, consistent with hospitality's larger, more diverse total workforce (many more citizens/LPRs) versus construction's heavier reliance on immigrant labor in ‌certain trades.
- Neither sub-figure requires an implausible share of the respective industry's total employment; both are consistent with independently known immigrant-labor concentration patterns in these two industries.

## Caveats
- FWD.us is an immigration-advocacy-aligned research group (pro-immigration reform), so its estimate should be read as an advocacy-adjacent but methodologically documented projection, not a neutral government statistic — DHS/USCIS has not published its own comparable aggregate estimate of affected renewal applicants.
- Rosen does not cite FWD.us (or any source) in her own remarks, so a listener can't independently verify her numbers from her statement alone — but the figures match a real, traceable, and methodologically transparent source almost exactly.
- "Could affect" language in Rosen's claim is appropriately hedged — not all 3.8M renewal filers will necessarily experience a work-authorization gap; the size of the actual gap depends on USCIS adjudication speed, which Rosen separately documents (up to 18-month processing against a 6-month filing window).
