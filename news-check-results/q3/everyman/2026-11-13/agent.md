# Fact-check: "Homicides Drop 15.2% Across Major U.S. Cities as Violent Crime Declines Continue in 2026" (yourNEWS, 2026-11-12)

Method: WebFetch of the article (returned only a paraphrased summary, not verbatim text), WebSearch, and WebFetch of Axios-derived coverage (Yahoo reprint). The MCCA PDF itself could not be text-extracted via WebFetch (image/compressed PDF), and Python was not available as a tool in this session. The Axios page returned 403. So the primary report was checked through secondary coverage that quotes it, plus arithmetic.

## Misleading / context flags
- Houston's absence (large city, 95 homicides in H1 2026 vs 154) plus Tulsa's reporting-system change (its -71.4% figure) mean the 66-agency comparison is not like-for-like.
- The 15.2% is smaller than the midyear 17.2%, so the decline is slowing; "declines continue" is true but understates the deceleration.
- Counts are not population-adjusted and cover only large agencies; 15 jurisdictions rose, including NYC, the nation's largest.
- Possible spurious precision/attribution on the 547-agency 16.6% claim.

## Rating
**1 Pinocchio (minor problems, mostly accurate)**. Core figures check out against MCCA-derived coverage; the one unsupported item (547-agency figure) and unconfirmed White House attribution are peripheral. Would drop to 2 if the 547-agency claim proves fabricated.
