# Fact-check: Peters, Whitesides, Crank urge inclusion of forestry bill in NDAA (press release dated Oct 23, 2026)

Note: this is a congressional press release (advocacy), not a news report. WebFetch returned a summary, not verbatim text, so the quotes could not be checked word for word. No dashboard image or HTML report was produced.

## Claims

| # | Claim | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Peters, Whitesides (D-CA) and Crank (R-CO) led 28 House members in a letter to Johnson, Jeffries, Rogers and Smith urging FOFA in the FY27 NDAA | Not checkable here | I found no independent record of the letter or its signer count. Only the release itself reports it. |
| 2 | In 2026 about 66,000 fires burned over 9 million acres, 132% and 141% of the 10-year average | Roughly supported, dated | NIFC data through 10/30/26 show 67,457 fires and 9.39M acres. That is about 133% of the average fire count (50,746) and 146% of average acreage. The release's figures match an earlier mid-October snapshot. Season totals now run slightly higher. |
| 3 | Over 1,600 firefighters and support personnel assigned to catastrophic incidents | Not checkable here | I did not find an IMSR snapshot. The figure is also tied to a specific date. |
| 4 | Fires near military installations in Washington and Arkansas threaten munitions and divert National Guard | Not checkable here | I did not search these incidents specifically. |
| 5 | Wildfires cost the U.S. between nearly $400B and almost $900B a year | Supported, with a caveat | This is the JEC Democratic majority's October 2023 report, $394B to $893B. It is an advocacy-leaning estimate, more than double prior government estimates, and not a bipartisan finding. |
| 6 | Prevention saves $3.75 per $1 spent | Supported | A 2026 UC Davis study in Science of fuel treatments across about 300 western fires found $3.75 of avoided damages per $1. The study is specific to fuel treatments. Other sources give $4 to $6. |
| 7 | Quote: "It is the only package that is bipartisan, bicameral, and has undergone a comprehensive committee process" | Partly verified | The quote text itself was not verifiable. The facts behind it hold: the House passed FOFA 279-141 in January 2025, and Senate Ag advanced S.1462 18-5 in October 2025. "Only" is an unprovable superlative. |
| 8 | Peters has been House lead on FOFA (H.R. 471) for two Congresses; the House passed it in January 2025 | Supported | The House passed H.R. 471 on Jan 23, 2025. Westerman (R-AR) is the primary sponsor with Peters as lead Democrat. The 118th Congress version was H.R. 8790, passed 268-151. |
| 9 | Peters led the Save Our Sequoias Act (H.R. 2709); the House passed it in March 2026 | Mostly true, overstated | The House passed it by voice vote on March 16, 2026. The sponsor is Rep. Vince Fong (R-CA), and Peters is a co-lead or cosponsor, not the lead. |
| 10 | About one-fifth of giant sequoias destroyed since 2015 | Roughly supported, imprecise | NPS estimates that the 2020-21 fires killed 13-19% of large sequoias. A coalition figure says more than 17% of mature sequoias died since 2015. The Forest Service has said about one-fifth over two years. "Destroyed" and "one-fifth" lean toward the high end. |

## Materially misleading or missing context

- FOFA has passed the House and sits on the Senate calendar, so it is already bipartisan. The framing as "only package" bundles together other forestry proposals.
- The JEC cost range is a broad estimate that includes health and economic impacts, not government spending.
- The $3.75 return applies to fuel treatments in the study's sample, not to all prevention spending.
- Acreage figures are a snapshot. The 2026 season data were still rising when the release was written.

## Rating: 1 Pinocchio (mostly accurate)

The statistics trace to real sources. The main defects are that Peters's role on the sequoia bill is overstated, some figures are rounded up, and the release presents advocacy numbers without caveats. Three claims (the letter and signer count, firefighter count, and the military-installation incidents) could not be verified here.
