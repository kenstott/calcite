# Fact-check: FOX 9, "Twin Cities records 2nd-warmest September, October in 2026" (Nov 10, 2026)

Method: WebFetch of the article (returned as a summary, not verbatim) plus a web search. Primary data (NWS/MSP, MN State Climatology Office series) were not directly retrieved.

## Claims
| # | Claim | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sept+Oct 2026 was 2nd-warmest on record in Twin Cities | Plausible, not independently confirmed | No DNR/NWS page found stating the combined ranking. Consistent with DNR: Sept statewide preliminary 62.9F (+3.4F), Oct tied 3rd-warmest (57.8F, FOX 9 Oct piece). |
| 2 | 2026 averaged 62.4F | Not checkable here | Needs MSP daily data. Rough consistency: Oct 57.8F, so September would be about 67F. Not verified. |
| 3 | Only 2024 warmer, 63.8F | Not checkable here | FOX 9's own Oct article ranks Oct 2024 only 6th, so 2024 must have had a very warm September. Plausible, unverified. |
| 4 | Records begin in 1872 | Supported | FOX 9 Oct article says records date to 1872. DNR says "154 years of record". |
| 5 | 5 of the last 6 years are in the top 10 (2021, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026) | Not checkable here | Plausible (DNR says the last four Septembers ranked at or near the top). Oct 2021 ranks 11th alone, so it depends on the combined series. |
| 6 | Cody Matz: falls have warmed for at least two decades; warmer falls "may feel normal"; a return to typical could feel cold | Opinion/attributed | Quote not located outside the article. Direction is consistent with climate trends. |

Related, supported: Oct 10-11, 2026 reached 90F, the first year with two October 90F days (DNR).

## Rating
1 Pinocchio (mostly accurate). Nothing found contradicts the piece, and context matches DNR and FOX 9 data. The central numbers are unverified here.

## Caveats
- A 2-month window with a stated "second-warmest" ranking is sensitive to the dataset (MSP airport versus the adjusted long-term record). The article does not say which one.
- The piece is a short climate-trend story, so "warmer falls" is trend framing rather than attribution. It makes no claims about cause, which is fine.
- No charts produced.
