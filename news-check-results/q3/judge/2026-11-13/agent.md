## Assessment
- **Correct verdict: 2 Pinocchios.** The headline direction is right, but every national figure comes from a release MCCA replaced the day the article ran, and the one counterexample the piece builds on (New York City rising) is false.
  - **Two versions exist.** Nov 9 original: homicide 3,394 vs 4,000 (-15.2%), robbery 57,209 vs 62,939 (-9.1%), rape -3.5%, agg assault -3.4%, NYC homicide 239 (2026) vs 192 (2025). Nov 12 Updated: homicide 3,347 vs 4,047 (-17.3%), robbery 55,786 vs 64,362 (-13.3%), rape -3.8%, agg assault -4.0%, NYC 192 (2026) vs 239 (2025). The Updated version is current and is the only one MCCA now lists. NYPD's row had its 2026 and 2025 columns swapped in the original (NYC robbery 11,413/9,990 and agg assault 28,919/28,357 were swapped too).
  - **Combined totals.** Original: 270,560 vs 284,382 (-4.9%). Updated, summed from the PDF: 268,501 vs 286,441 (-6.3%).
  - **NYC direction.** NYPD's own figures through October: 230 murders, down 15.7%, a ten-month record low. The article's "NYC homicides up 24.5%" is false against both the current MCCA report and NYPD.
  - **547 agencies, murder -16.6% through August.** Source is a secondary post (crimeforecast Substack) quoting the Real-Time Crime Index "falling 16.6%"; the post does not give the agency count, and expert reported 547 agencies on crimeindex.org. It is a real but secondary figure, not a fabrication. It is not the MCCA midyear robbery -16.6%.
  - **Mitigation.** The error originates in MCCA's original release and the article matched it exactly, so the outlet did not invent the numbers. That keeps this below 3. It stays above 1 because the false NYC claim is a central example and the piece was not corrected, and the national figures understate the decline.
  - Houston missing, preliminary data, 66 of 67 agencies, Memphis 156 to 99, Las Vegas 64 to 79: true in both versions.
