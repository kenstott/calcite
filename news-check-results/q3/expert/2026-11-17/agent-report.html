Fact check: "Texas maternal death rate drops to pre-pandemic levels as mental health becomes the leading cause"

San Antonio Current republication (15 Nov 2026) of a Texas Tribune article by Ayden Runnels and Terri Langford (13 Nov 2026). Text is identical to the Tribune original. Checked 17 Nov 2026.

Summary of findings: 1 Pinocchio. The numbers are almost all right; the framing is looser than the data

Of 38 claims and quotes extracted, 26 verified against the DSHS/MMMRC Joint Biennial Report 2026, 7 are accurate but shaded, 2 are wrong, and 3 are not checkable here (two quotes given by text message or spokesperson, and a reporter characterization of Rep. Howard's concerns). The headline figures hold: 2024 maternal mortality ratio 20.5 per 100,000 live births versus 23.5 in 2021 (excluding COVID-19), 77% and 76% of pregnancy-related deaths preventable, Black pregnancy-related mortality 2.4 times white (47.7 vs 20.1), Black severe maternal morbidity 146.2 vs 65.7.

  • Wrong figure: the article gives severe maternal morbidity rising "from 80.4 to 83.5". The report says 83.9 per 100,000 delivery hospitalizations (not births), 2023 to 2024. 83.5 appears nowhere in the report. The article also omits that the comparison year is 2023.
  • Wrong reference: "last year's report" is the September 2024 report, two years old.
  • Headline overstated: 2019's ratio was 17.2 (2024 report). The 2024 ratio of 20.5 is 19% higher, not "slightly above". It is inside the 2013-2017 band (18.3-20.7) and the report calls it "similar to before 2020", so "pre-pandemic levels" is defensible but "slightly above 2019" understates the gap. The 2021 comparison number was also revised upward between reports (23.0 to 23.5), unmentioned.
  • Materially misleading by omission, small numbers: "mental health becomes the leading cause" rests on 12 of 59 reviewed deaths versus 9 for hemorrhage and 8 for hypertensive disorders. In 2021, 30 of 33 infection deaths were COVID-19, so the cause shift is mostly the pandemic leaving. Both years are samples (2021: 158 of 277 deaths reviewed; 2024: 127 of 193 provisional deaths).
  • Materially misleading, data capture: the "doubling" of contributing factors (and the 6-fold nicotine rise) is partly a measurement change. The new review system recorded nicotine and chronic-condition information for 57 of 59 cases in 2024 versus 22 of 60 in 2021 (report footnotes 18-19). Violence (3 to 11 deaths) is a small count.
  • Framing of the delay: the article says officials were "supposed to" release on Sept 1 and "delayed until after the midterms". A 2025 law (HB 4666) lets health agencies file by Dec 1, and the report was released 13 Nov, ahead of that date. The article reports the denial and the law, but states the political-timing framing as fact. The abortion-ban date is also wrong: the near-total ban took effect in 2022, not 2021 (SB 8 in 2021 was an earlier six-week law).
  • Political claim: "Republicans in the minority party" is true of the House, governor, lieutenant governor and attorney general, not the Senate, which Republicans keep.
  • Could not do: verify the Howard and Van Deusen quotes beyond the Tribune; compute 2022-2023 ratios (they sit on the Texas Health Data dashboard, not in the report text, and I did not interpolate them); reproduce 83.5 (possible dashboard value, not found).

What would change the grade: a published correction of the 83.5 figure and "last year's report" would move this toward Geppetto (0). Treating the cause-of-death and contributing-factor comparisons as the story's main findings, without the small-sample and data-capture caveats, would move it toward 2.

Step 3. Missing context a reader needs

Answer: the piece leaves out sampling, the COVID composition of 2021 infection deaths, the change in data capture, and the fact that 2024 counts are provisional. These bear on the headline cause and trend claims. The two-year comparison is also not causal evidence about the abortion ban (before/after with two reviewed years; 2022-2023 skipped).

Key Values: 2021 reviewed 158 of 277 pregnancy-associated deaths; 2024 reviewed 127 of 193 provisional; 60 and 59 pregnancy-related deaths.

Sources: Report pp. 5-6, 11, Appendix B footnotes 15-19.

Confidence: high on the facts, medium on how much they alter reader inference.
