# Fact-check: IBTimes, "HUD 2026 PIT count homelessness decline" (R. McMahon, 7 Nov 2026)

## Summary
Rating: **1 Pinocchio** (mostly accurate). The headline numbers match HUD and NAEH: 698,425 people on one January 2026 night, a 6.3% decline, NAEH's 6.7% estimate and +1% chronic homelessness. The weaknesses are framing: the piece relays HUD's causal credit (border/public safety) without evidence, omits the 2025 baseline (745,652 implied), and relies on HUD's "largest ever" claim, for which HUD published no series. HUD has not released its AHAR, so there is no detailed primary dataset.

## Claims
1. **698,425 homeless, Jan 2026; 6.3% decline "since Trump took office in 2025".** Verified against HUD release No. 26-090 (6 Nov 2026) as relayed; the HUD text itself gives no prior count. Implied 2025 baseline 745,652 (press) gives -6.33%. Caveat: the baseline is the Jan 2025 count, taken at the start of the term, so the "since" wording hides that it is a year-over-year change. Confidence: high on number, medium on baseline.
2. **Largest annual decline on record.** HUD's own claim, in quotes "largest on record"; HUD gives no series. PIT counts only exist since 2007 and 2024-25 was -3.3%; plausible but not independently checkable here. Confidence: medium.
3. **Turner credited border, crime and public-safety policies.** Accurate attribution to HUD/Turner. Causality unsupported; one-night count; HUD release provides no analysis. Misleading if read as established.
4. **771,480 counted in 2024.** Consistent with the 2024 AHAR (more than 770,000, +18.1%). Confidence: high.
5. **Shelter use up 43% 2022-2024, tied to asylum seekers in NYC, Chicago.** 43% (about 149,000) corroborated by secondary summaries of HUD PIT; NBER working paper attributes about 60% of the rise to asylum seekers, concentrated in NYC, Chicago, Denver, Massachusetts. HUD AHAR PDF could not be parsed here. Confidence: medium.
6. **NYT: declines in those localities were nearly half the recent drop.** Not found; not checkable here (NYT could not be located).
7. **Community Solutions about 6% decline, national trend, civic cooperation/cooling housing market.** Appears in relayed coverage (Adam Ruege); Community Solutions primary page not read. Confidence: low-medium.
8. **NAEH: 6.7% decline, nearly 50,000, about 693,000.** 6.7% confirmed on NAEH page (6 Nov 2026, Schachner/USC, model estimate); 693,000 not on the page, computed from baseline. Confidence: high on 6.7%, medium on 693,000.
9. **Chronic homelessness up about 1% (NAEH).** Confirmed on NAEH page. High.
10. **Oliva: PSH "vastly underfunded"; defunding would erase gains.** Verified in coverage (Yahoo; Smart Cities Dive quotes "would surely erase these declines"). Primary NAEH release not read in English. Medium-high.

## Misleading context
- Piece reports HUD's causal framing with little skepticism; NAEH credited local systems and providers.
- Two different 2025 baselines are in play (since 2024: -9.5%; since 2025: -6.3%), and HUD itself says homelessness remains near historic highs and rose 28% 2022-2025.
- No AHAR, no state or sheltered/unsheltered breakdown from HUD; NAEH figures are modelled (some CoCs estimated).
