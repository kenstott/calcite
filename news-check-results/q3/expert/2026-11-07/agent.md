# Fact-check: The 51st, "D.C. evictions hit 12-year high as safety net shrinks and cost of living soars"

## Verdict: 3 Pinocchios (Significant Errors / Mostly False framing on the headline numbers)

Article: "D.C. evictions hit 12-year high as safety net shrinks and cost of living soars" (James Jarvis, The 51st, Nov 5 2026). I fetched the URL directly (HTTP 200) and read the full text.

**Short reasoning.** The direction is right: evictions are elevated, ERAP funding is down, and the RENTAL Act changed process. But the headline figures (2,420 FY2025 evictions, "12-year high", ~1,600 pre-pandemic average) do not match any published figure I could find. The independently published FY2025 figure is 1,933, a seven-year high. Several CLCPP statistics are attributed to "the first half of 2026", yet the linked report covers July-Dec 2025 and says different things. The unemployment claim is stale and the direction is wrong. I could not access any primary source for the headline numbers, so this is not a finding that the figures are fabricated. It is a finding that the piece's central claim is unverified and contradicted by the best public data.

### Materially misleading, even where facts check out

1. **Headline figure unreconciled.** The article does not disclose that other published FY2025 figures are 1,933 (New America) and 2,614 (UPO). A headline of 12-year high / near-record rests on one unverified number.
2. **Wrong time frames on the CLCPP statistics.** "First half of 2026" appears to be the wrong period for numbers that come from a Jul-Dec 2025 report. At least the ERAP share (32% vs 24%) and the possession rate (63% vs 66%) do not match the linked report.
3. **Stale and mis-directed unemployment.** The article says it "rose" when August fell from July and is below a year earlier.
4. **Causation implied from correlation.** The article lets ERAP cuts and the RENTAL Act carry the story. The SMOA view (a backlog of pandemic-era cases) is included, but no analysis separates the explanations. Completed evictions also lag filings, and the CLCPP caseload is a self-selected sample of low-income tenants seeking legal help.
5. **FY vs calendar year mixing.** Fiscal-year evictions are set against calendar-year filings (13,051 in CY2025).

### What I could not do
- The OTA data page returned 404, so FY2025/FY2026 completed-eviction counts could not be read at the source.
- The UPO and CFO trend report PDFs were not machine-readable via WebFetch. The CFO PDF text had no unemployment line. I used BLS and DOES (via search) for unemployment instead.
- No H1 2026 CLCPP report was found, so claims 14-16 are graded against the Jul-Dec 2025 report the article links to.
- I did not independently check the DCFPI, EPI, and Council-legislation claims.
