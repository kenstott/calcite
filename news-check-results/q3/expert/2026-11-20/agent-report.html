Fact check: Governor's "record low crime" release (2026-11-17)

Mostly accurate, but selectively framed: 2 Pinocchios

The statewide numbers check out against the California DOJ 2025 data: homicide rate 3.5 per 100,000 (lowest since 1966), homicides 1,666 to 1,374 (-18.6%) in 2025. The multi-year claims (homicide -20%, robbery -31%, property -24%, vehicle theft -19%, violent crime -3%) match the Governor's own July 2026 release. The weak points are framing and unverifiable city figures.

  • Misleading emphasis: violent crime is down only 3% since 2019, while down 10.2% in 2025 alone; so violent crime was higher in the intervening years and the "record low" headline rests on homicide. The headline's "record low crime rates" applies to homicide, not all crime.
  • Baseline wording: the July release graphic says "since 2019"; the text says "since Newsom took office". These are near-identical but the 2019 baseline is a chosen year; I did not rebuild the series from raw DOJ tables.
  • Not checkable here: MCCA first-nine-months 2026 figures for 9 California jurisdictions (violent -6%, homicide -12%; Oakland violent -19%, Sacramento violent -15%; homicide Sacramento -44%, LASD -30%, Oakland -22%, LA -13%). The Q3 PDF URL returned 404 via search and the midyear PDF was image-only. Partial support: MCCA midyear shows Sacramento homicides 17 vs 23 (-26%), directionally consistent. MCCA data are preliminary, self-reported by agencies.
  • Not confirmed: CDC homicide rate 5.1 to 4.5 per 100,000 in 2024. DOJ shows a -10.4% change 2023-24 (consistent) and 4.3 per 100,000 DOJ rate in 2024; the CDC figures themselves I could not retrieve (CDC WONDER is not reachable by search). Comparisons to other states were not independently checked.
  • Not checked: $2.1 billion investment, $107 million and $350 million grants, $534 million fentanyl seizures, 15,300 hemp/kratom/7-OH products: government-reported program figures; no independent source found. These are not crime facts and are self-reported.
  • Would change the rating: if the MCCA city numbers are wrong, or CDC figures differ, move toward 3 Pinocchios.
