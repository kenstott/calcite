# Fact-Check: Rosen EAD/Work-Permit Rule Claim (Senate Floor, April 29, 2026)

## Claim 1: Rosen's exact quote ("more than 3.5 million," "roughly 600,000 construction," "more than half a million hospitality")

**Verdict: TRUE (verified verbatim)**

**Evidence:** Rosen's own April 29, 2026 press release ("VIDEO: Rosen Forces Bipartisan Senate Vote to Reverse Trump Regulation Ending Automatic Extension of Work Permits," rosen.senate.gov) contains these exact statements:
- "More than three and a half million legally authorized workers nationwide could be impacted by this misguided rule."
- "In construction alone, roughly six hundred thousand workers could be forced off the job."
- "In hospitality, an industry that is the backbone of Nevada's economy, more than half a million workers nationwide could be affected."

This matches the claim's paraphrase ("more than 3.5 million... including roughly 600,000 construction workers and more than half a million hospitality workers") word-for-word in substance and rounding.

**Confidence: High** — sourced directly from the senator's own official press release, not a secondary account.

## Claim 2: FWD.us figures (3.8M total, 613K construction, 514K leisure/hospitality)

**Verdict: TRUE**

**Evidence:** FWD.us's own published analysis ("Ending Automatic Work Authorization Will Cost Billions," fwd.us/news/ending-automatic-work-authorization-will-cost-billions) states approximately 3.8 million EAD workers are affected nationwide, with sector breakdowns of ~613,000 in construction, ~514,000 in leisure & hospitality, ~499,000 in professional/business services, ~466,000 in wholesale/retail trade, ~350,000 in manufacturing, ~326,000 in transportation/warehousing/utilities, and ~202,000 in healthcare. Methodology: augmented 2024 American Community Survey (ACS) data projected to end of FY2025, cross-validated against actual USCIS administrative EAD-approval data for FY2025; the analysis excludes population subgroups representing <1% of total EAD holders because they can't be reliably identified in survey microdata.

**Notable finding:** Rosen's "more than 3.5 million" is actually a conservative understatement of FWD.us's own 3.8 million figure — she rounded down, not up. The construction (600K vs. 613K) and hospitality (500K+ vs. 514K) figures likewise round down from FWD.us's numbers. So the senator did not inflate the advocacy group's estimate; if anything she softened it.

**Confidence: High** — verified against FWD.us's own report page with stated methodology, not a secondhand citation.

## Claim 3: Sanity-check industry sub-figures against total BLS employment

**Verdict: PLAUSIBLE / order-of-magnitude consistent, with minor imprecision in the "1 in 20 / 1 in 25" shorthand some Rosen-adjacent materials use**

**Evidence:**
- BLS Employment Situation, August 2026: total construction employment (seasonally adjusted) = 8,359,000. FWD.us's 613,000 EAD-holder estimate in construction = ~7.3% of the construction workforce.
- BLS/FRED leisure & hospitality employment has run roughly 16.9–17.0 million in 2026 (FRED series USLAH). FWD.us's 514,000 EAD-holder estimate in leisure/hospitality = ~3.0% of that workforce.
- Rosen's own office, in a related press item, characterized EAD holders as "1 in 20" construction workers (5%) and "1 in 25" leisure/hospitality workers (4%) — these round shorthand ratios are somewhat lower than the 7.3%/3.0% implied directly by FWD.us's raw numbers divided by current BLS totals, likely because the "1 in X" framing uses a different (possibly broader or differently-dated) workforce denominator than the August 2026 BLS snapshot used here. The discrepancy is modest (roughly 2 percentage points in construction) and doesn't change the overall magnitude — several hundred thousand workers is a real, non-trivial share of each industry, in the single-digit percent range.

**Confidence: Medium** — the BLS totals used for sanity-checking are current (Aug 2026) national seasonally-adjusted figures, a reasonable comparison basis, but FWD.us's underlying denominator/vintage for its "1 in X" ratios wasn't independently reconciled here, so the exact percentage match (as opposed to order-of-magnitude) carries some uncertainty.

## Claim 4: Current legal status of the underlying rule

**Verdict: TRUE — the rule remains in effect nationwide; no court has blocked it nationwide as of Sept 29, 2026**

**Evidence:** DHS/USCIS published the Interim Final Rule "Removal of the Automatic Extension of Employment Authorization Documents" in the Federal Register on October 30, 2025, effective the same day, ending the up-to-540-day automatic extension for EAD renewal applications filed on or after that date. It remains in effect. Multiple lawsuits have been filed challenging it:
- H-4 visa holders filed suit in the U.S. District Court for the Central District of California (filed ~Jan 8, 2026); a TRO application (Feb 13, 2026) was converted to a preliminary-injunction motion (May 8, 2026); a hearing was held June 24, 2026; the court subsequently issued only a **limited order protecting the named plaintiffs** from certain adverse actions without notice — it did **not** restore the 540-day automatic extension nationwide.
- A separate coalition suit was filed in U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia on April 20, 2026, alleging APA violations (no notice-and-comment).
- As of the most recent available reporting (through early September 2026), no nationwide preliminary injunction or vacatur has been issued in either case.

**Confidence: Medium-High** — corroborated by multiple independent legal-industry sources (Spar & Bernstein, LegalClarity, VisaHQ, Presidents' Alliance litigation tracker) describing the same procedural posture; no primary court-docket check was performed, and litigation status can change quickly, so this should be treated as accurate as of the last available reporting rather than a docket-verified real-time status.

## Overall Assessment

Rosen's floor statement is **highly accurate**. Her exact quote matches her own press release verbatim, and every number she cited (3.5M+, ~600K construction, 500K+ hospitality) is a conservative rounding-down of FWD.us's own published estimates (3.8M, 613K, 514K) — she did not inflate the advocacy figures. FWD.us's methodology (ACS data cross-validated against USCIS administrative approvals) is transparent and the resulting industry sub-figures are order-of-magnitude consistent with BLS total employment in construction and leisure/hospitality (roughly 3–7% of each sector), though the "1 in 20 / 1 in 25" shorthand some allied materials use is somewhat rounder than a strict division of FWD.us's numbers by current BLS totals would yield. The underlying rule (10/30/2025 Interim Final Rule) is confirmed still in effect nationwide, with litigation ongoing but no nationwide injunction as of late September 2026.

**The single most significant problem, if any:** not with Rosen's numbers themselves, but with precision/sourcing transparency — her floor remarks present FWD.us's advocacy-group estimate as settled fact without attribution, and the estimate itself rests on a modeled projection (ACS microdata + administrative validation) rather than a direct government count of affected workers, so the "3.5 million" figure is a credible best estimate, not a hard census number. This is a normal advocacy-communication practice, not a factual error, but it means the figure carries model uncertainty that isn't conveyed in the floor speech.
