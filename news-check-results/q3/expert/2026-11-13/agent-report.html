Fact-check: "Homicides drop 15.2% across major U.S. cities" (yourNEWS, 2026-11-12)

Summary: the headline direction holds, but the article used a superseded MCCA release and reversed New York City. Two Pinocchios.

The article's numbers match the Major Cities Chiefs Association (MCCA) release dated Nov 9, 2026 exactly (homicides 3,394 vs 4,000, -15.2%; 66 of 67 agencies). MCCA reissued the report on Nov 12 after correcting the New York City PD row. The corrected release shows homicides 3,347 vs 4,047, -17.3%; robbery -13.3% (article -9.1%); rape -3.8% (-3.5%); aggravated assault -4.0% (-3.4%); four categories combined -6.3%, 17,940 fewer offenses (article -4.9%, 13,822).

  • Materially false claim: the article says NYC homicides rose from 192 to 239. In the corrected release 2026 = 192 and 2025 = 239, a 19.7% decline. The original PDF had the two columns swapped. NYPD independently reports 230 murders in Jan-Oct 2026, down 15.7%, which confirms NYC fell.
  • The NYC swap explains the whole homicide gap: 47 = 239-192, and 3,394-3,347 = 47 (4,047-4,000 = 47).
  • Verified as stated: Memphis 156 to 99; Las Vegas 64 to 79 (an increase); declines in Indianapolis (125 to 83), Philadelphia (165 to 130), Los Angeles (187 to 163), Detroit (128 to 105), Chicago (333 to 322, only -3.3%); Houston absent (records-system transition); data preliminary, not final UCR/NIBRS; Canada -21.3% (169 to 133, 9 agencies).
  • Independent corroboration: Crime Index (crimeindex.org, 547 agencies) murder -16.6% through August; the 547-agency count is on the site; the -16.6% comes from a secondary post by James Tuttle (Nov 9), not from Crime Index itself.
  • Not checked here: the White House's claim of credit (attributed statement, no primary text fetched); the claim that the decline began before Jan 2025 (no primary source fetched this session). I could read only a summary of the article from the fetch tool, not its full text, so any attributed quotes not in that summary were not individually checked.
  • Would change the grade: if the article was corrected or the author had only the Nov 9 file at publication, the grade softens to one Pinocchio; the NYC reversal is the only claim that flips a conclusion.
  • Misleading framing even where accurate: "major U.S. cities" is a 66-agency MCCA member sample (Houston missing, some are county sheriffs such as Los Angeles County), preliminary, not a national figure. A Tulsa PD records change (SRS to NIBRS) is footnoted in the MCCA table.

Step 1: Headline and national totals

Answer: Stale. Original -15.2%; corrected -17.3%. Key values: Jan 1 - Sep 30, 2026 vs 2025, n=66 agencies; computed combined original 270,560 vs 284,382 (-4.86%), corrected 268,501 vs 286,441 (-6.26%). Sources: MCCA PDFs below. Confidence: high; both PDFs read directly.
