Fact-check: 2026-cycle independent expenditures

Question dated 2026-09-26 · Report built 2026-09-26 · Time period covered: 2025–2026 election cycle, with a focus on Aug 24–Sep 14, 2026
UNVERIFIED — single-source figures

1. Cycle total: $1,452,997,175 across 881 candidates

Claim: total independent expenditures for the 2026 cycle have topped $1.45B across 881 candidates.

Every search for this exact figure returns the same page: thepoliticalgroup.com's "PAC Spend Tracker 2026." No second source repeats it, and the tracker discloses its own method — it sums raw Schedule E line items pulled from the FEC's public API (processed reports plus 24/48-hour notices, "deduplicated"), not a number FEC.gov itself has published as an official aggregate.

This is exactly the failure mode to watch for: a column labeled "independent expenditure" in FEC's raw itemized data is unvetted at filing and can include duplicate, erroneous, or spam entries. A third party's own dedup logic is not the same as an FEC.gov-certified total.

FEC.gov's own official Statistical Summary series (a genuine primary source) gives cumulative IE totals at three earlier checkpoints in this same cycle:

As ofCumulative IE total (FEC.gov official)
Jun 30, 2025$16.0 million
Dec 31, 2025$56.4 million
Mar 31, 2026$252.1 million
Sep 21, 2026 (claimed)$1,452,997,175 (secondary tracker only)

Growth to $1.45B by September is plausible in order of magnitude — IE spending is known to concentrate heavily in the months right before a federal election — but FEC has not published a statistical summary that recent, and I could not query FEC's own API to check the tracker's arithmetic: api.open.fec.gov's shared demo key was rate-limited (OVER_RATE_LIMIT) on every attempt across this session, and fec.gov's browse-data pages are a JavaScript app that returns an empty shell to a non-browser fetch.

Sources: thepoliticalgroup.com/research/pac-spend-2026 (claim origin) · FEC.gov 12-month summary · FEC.gov 15-month summary
UNVERIFIED — same single source

2. Four-week window (Aug 24–Sep 14, 2026): $328,333,233

Claim: that four-week window alone saw $328,333,233 in independent expenditures.

Same origin, same caveat as above: this number appears only on thepoliticalgroup.com's tracker page, which itself warns that "the newest week is partial until filings catch up" and that "the FEC's processed totals can trail the newest filings by days to weeks." No FEC.gov page or second independent tracker was found repeating this figure.

Source: thepoliticalgroup.com/research/pac-spend-2026

Claim: $59,564,521 across 17 committees, split $2,086,549 support / $57,477,973 opposition, in the Aug 24–Sep 14 window.

The precise dollar figures and the "17 committees" count again trace only to the single tracker. But independent, named-source news reporting does corroborate the direction — that outside spending opposing Talarico (i.e., pro-Paxton) sharply escalated in September 2026 and dwarfs support for him:

Bottom line

  • All three headline dollar figures in the question trace to one secondary aggregator (thepoliticalgroup.com), which openly describes its method as a raw, deduplicated sum of FEC Schedule E API line items — not an FEC.gov-published aggregate.
  • I could not independently reproduce or refute the exact numbers: FEC's own bulk API was rate-limited throughout, and FEC.gov's browse-data UI is JavaScript-rendered and unreachable by a plain fetch.
  • The overall magnitude (spending accelerating toward $1B+ as of September, after $252M as of March) is plausible against FEC's own trend data, but not confirmed at the cited precision.
  • For the Talarico race specifically, the direction of the claim — that opposition to Talarico heavily outweighs support in this window — is corroborated by multiple independent, named-source news reports (Texas Tribune, Axios, CNBC, The Hill, TPR). The specific dollar amounts and "17 committees" figure are not.
  • n = 881 candidates (per the single tracker, unconfirmed); time period = 2025–2026 cycle, with the disputed window Aug 24–Sep 14, 2026.
