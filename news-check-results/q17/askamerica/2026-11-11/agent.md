**Checked and true**
- Uninsured 19-64: 8.9% (2024) to 11.2% (2026). The Commonwealth Fund primary report returned HTTP 403, so this is confirmed from Medical Daily (quoting the Fund's release) and Healthcare Dive.
- The 46% inadequately insured split (11% uninsured, 12% gap, 23% underinsured) matches.
- Sample details match: SSRS, 9,360 adults, 7,362 aged 19-64, May 7 to July 20.
- Forgone care for cost rose from 46% (2022) to 50% (2026).
- Marketplace and employer subgroup figures match: more than half and one-third for marketplace enrollees, 43% and 31% for employer coverage.
- The 73% who say the federal government should ensure coverage matches. The party splits (93/76/45) were not seen in any readable copy.
- Effectuated marketplace enrollment fell 13% to 19.2M. The warehouse table health.aca_effectuated_enrollment_monthly shows February 2025 at 21.78M and February 2026 at 19.17M, a 12% like-for-like drop. KFF's 22.1M is its 2025 peak, which is a minor wording issue. KFF and the HHS ASPE brief confirm the figures and the administration's antifraud attribution.
- Medicaid work requirements of 80 hours per month start in 2027.

**Unsupported (could not verify)**
- The $750 to $4,035 premium increase.
- The Medicaid net loss of 11.5M and the CBO marketplace reduction of 7.5M. Healthcare Dive cites a different Urban Institute estimate of up to 10M.

**Misleading context (the facts check out)**
- The 11.2% is a self-reported survey figure for ages 19-64 only. The Fund says its survey runs lower than federal ones (CDC NHIS 2025, all ages: 8.3%). The headline omits the age limit, though the body states it.
- The causal link to expired enhanced tax credits and the end of continuous Medicaid enrollment is the report authors' interpretation of coincident trends. It was not tested.
