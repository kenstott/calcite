The Investing.com/Reuters article's claims about first-half 2026 US traffic deaths, the fatality rate and the 2021 spike are balanced and mostly accurate.

**Rating: 1 Pinocchio.**

Caveat on sourcing: investing.com returned HTTP 403 on both fetch tools. The article text came only from a sub-model summary, not verbatim. NSC page also 403.

Checked against warehouse (transport.fatal_crashes, FARS 2008-2024) and NHTSA Crash Stats DOT HS 813 833 (Q1 2026 brief, read in full):
- 2021: 43,230 deaths, +10.8% from 39,007. Warehouse and NHTSA match. Highest of 2008-2024; the "most since 2005" is not testable in the warehouse window.
- H1 2025 baseline: 8,120 + 9,055 = 17,175, matching the article's prior-year figure. 16,750 is exactly -2.5% of that.
- Full-year 2025 (NHTSA projection): 36,640 deaths, rate 1.10. Fits "lowest since 2019" (2019 was 36,355) and the 1.10 rate. "Second-lowest in history" is not verified beyond 2014 (1.08).
- Fifth straight annual decline: 2022, 2023 and 2024 declines are confirmed in the warehouse (42,721, 41,025, 39,254). 2025 is a projection (-6.7%). 2026 would be the fifth, so this is mostly true but rests on projections.
- "Lowest rate since 2014": the H1 2014 rate was roughly 1.01 versus a reported 1.02 for H1 2026, so it is plausible.

Not verified: the NHTSA H1 2026 release itself (not located), the 1.02 rate, the bicyclist figure (1,148, +4%), and the $340B/$1.37T cost study.

Misleading or loose points:
- NHTSA's own Q1 2026 estimate was -4.3% (rate 0.99). The article reportedly says -5%, possibly a revision.
- The headline cites rate "lowest since 2014" while the body reportedly cites deaths "lowest since 2015", which is a mix of metrics.
- All 2025 and 2026 figures are statistical projections that will be revised, not final counts.
- NSC's separate H1 2026 estimate (18,460 deaths, rate 1.12) uses a different definition and is not comparable.
