# The Investing.com (Reuters) article's claims about first-half 2026 US traffic deaths, the fatality rate and crash costs are mostly accurate.

**Rating: 1 Pinocchio at most.** Article: Reuters (David Shepardson), Nov 10, 2026, via Investing.com. Investing.com and NHTSA pages returned HTTP 403 to the engine. Full text was read from the identical Reuters copy at Taylorville Daily News.

## Claims and verdicts
1. 2021 deaths up 10.8% to 43,230, most since 2005: **TRUE**. FARS (warehouse) 2021 = 43,230, 2020 = 39,007.
2. H1 2026 deaths down 2.5% to 16,750, lowest since 2015, from 17,175: **MOSTLY TRUE (central claim)**. FARS Jan-Jun: 2015 = 16,193, 2016 = 17,717, 2024 = 18,681. 16,750 sits between 2015 and 2016, consistent with "lowest since 2015". The -2.5% arithmetic is correct. The 2026 and 2025 counts are NHTSA early estimates beyond warehouse coverage (2008-2024), and I could not read NHTSA's own release.
3. Fifth straight year of declines: **MOSTLY TRUE**. FARS shows 2021 > 2022 (42,721) > 2023 (41,025) > 2024 (39,254), and 2025 and 2026 are estimates.
4. Rate 1.02 per 100M VMT; 2025 rate 1.10 and lowest count since 2019; Q1 -5% and Q2 -0.2%; bicyclist deaths up 4% to 1,148; 2023 NHTSA crash-cost study ($30B / $340B / $1.37T): **not checkable here**. The warehouse has no VMT, bicyclist or crash-cost series, and 2025-26 are outside coverage. A web search and secondary reports are directionally consistent. They include NHTSA's Q1 estimate of -4.3% with a rate of 0.99, and a search summary saying vehicle miles traveled rose 0.8% in H1. NHTSA and NSC pages could not be opened, so none of this is primary-verified.

## Misleading or loose points
- The headline says "lowest rate since 2014" while the body says the death count is the "lowest since 2015". These are different metrics, and both appear in the body, so this is minor.
- These are early estimates that will be revised. Q2 improved only 0.2%, so "on pace" for a fifth decline is a projection.
- The pandemic-driver explanation is attributed to unnamed experts and was not tested.
- The National Safety Council reports a different first-half figure (18,460 deaths, -2%) using a different counting method. The two should not be mixed.
