**The Investing.com article's claims about US traffic deaths in the first half of 2026 are accurate; 2 claims could not be confirmed.**

Rating: 0 Pinocchios (Washington Post scale).

Access: investing.com returned HTTP 403. Claims were extracted from the Fox 5 copy of the same Reuters story (https://www.fox5ny.com/news/traffic-deaths-national-highway-traffic-safety-administration). The Reuters text on AOL was also read.

Checked against primary sources:
- NHTSA DOT HS 813 875 (Nov 2026), read in full via crashstats.nhtsa.dot.gov. The H1 2026 estimate is 16,750 versus 17,175 (-2.5%), and the rate is 1.02 versus 1.05. Q1 is -5.0% and Q2 is -0.2%. H1 totals for 2014 (15,035) and 2015 (16,193) are lower, so "lowest first half since 2015" holds. The H1 2014 rate of 1.01 is lower, and 2015-2025 H1 rates are all 1.05 or higher, so "lowest rate since 2014" holds. 2025 is projected at 36,640, the lowest since 2019.
- NHTSA DOT HS 813 800 (2025 early estimate). The 2025 rate of 1.10 is "second lowest in recorded history (behind only 1.08 in 2014)". True.
- NHTSA DOT HS 813 403 (2023 cost study). $340B economic cost, $30B public revenues and $1.37T societal harm are confirmed, but they are for 2019 crashes, which the article omits.

Checked against the AskAmerica warehouse (transport.fatal_crashes, FARS 2008-2024):
- Annual deaths were 43,230 in 2021, 42,721 in 2022, 41,025 in 2023 and 39,254 in 2024. That confirms the 2021 figure, the +10.8% rise from 39,007 in 2020, and four straight declines from 2022 to 2024. NHTSA's 2025 projection extends that to a fourth completed decline (2022-2025). The warehouse does not hold 2025 or 2026. The "highest since 2005" comparison falls outside the warehouse window, so I could not test it there.

Unconfirmed (graded unsupported):
- Bicyclist deaths rose 4% to 1,148 in 2025, after a four-decade high in 2023. Only the Reuters text repeats it. No NHTSA document containing the figure could be opened.
- The unnamed experts' explanation for pandemic-era risky driving (emptier roads, less ticketing, impaired driving). Only Reuters repeats it, and I found no independent source for it.

Misleading-framing flags (the facts check out, but context is missing):
- "Lowest rate since 2014" is a first-half rate. The full-year rate for 2025 (1.10) is second-lowest in history.
- All 2025 and 2026 figures are statistical projections that NHTSA will revise.
- The "fifth consecutive annual decline" is not yet realized. Progress has slowed: Q2 was only -0.2%, May and June were up year over year, and 15 states plus DC were projected to have more deaths. This is a framing caveat, not an error.
- NHTSA's April Q1 estimate (7,770, -4.3%) was revised to 7,715 (-5.0%), so the Q1 figure moved between releases.
