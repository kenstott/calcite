The Investing.com (Reuters) article's claims about US traffic deaths in the first half of 2026 are balanced and accurate.

Rating: 0 Pinocchios.

Access: Investing.com and nhtsa.gov returned 403. I read the Reuters wire text (David Shepardson) via AOL and the Fox5 rewrite. Primary NHTSA release was NOT read; NHTSA figures are as quoted by Reuters and search summaries.

Claims (all graded true unless noted):
- Lowest rate since 2014 (1.02 per 100M VMT); count lowest since 2015: true. Consistent with warehouse FARS (2014 32,744; 2015 35,484).
- 16,750 deaths, -2.5% from 17,175: true (16,750/17,175 = -2.47%). Preliminary early estimate.
- Fifth straight annual decline "on pace": true. FARS 2021 43,230, 2022 42,721, 2023 41,025, 2024 39,254; 2025 lower per NHTSA; 2026 is a projection and correctly worded as such.
- 2021 +10.8% to 43,230, most since 2005: true (warehouse 43,230; 2020 39,007 = +10.8%; 2005 comparison from outside knowledge).
- Q1 -5%, Q2 -0.2%: true as quoted by NHTSA.
- 2025 lowest since 2019, rate 1.10 second-lowest ever; bicyclist deaths +4% to 1,148; 2023 highest in four decades: mostly true, relayed from NHTSA, not verifiable in the warehouse (FARS ends 2024) or the primary release.
- Experts on pandemic risky driving; 2023 NHTSA cost study ($340B, $30B, $1.37T): attributed statements, not independently re-checked.

Misleading-context check: figures are preliminary and will be revised. The slowdown (Q2 -0.2%, with May-June year-over-year increases per NHTSA) is disclosed. NSC's broader estimate is 18,460 (-2%), a different definition, not a contradiction. Headline "lowest rate since 2014" refers to the rate, while the count is lowest since 2015; the article states both.
