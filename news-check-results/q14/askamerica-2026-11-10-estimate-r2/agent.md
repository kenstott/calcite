**The Investing.com (Reuters) article's claims about first-half 2026 US traffic deaths, the fatality rate and pandemic-era trends are balanced and mostly accurate.**

Rating: 1 Pinocchio.

Method: investing.com returned HTTP 403, so claims were extracted from the Reuters copy on Fox5 NY (fox5ny.com). The warehouse carries FARS only through 2024 (transport.fatal_crashes_by_state_year), so only the pandemic-era claims could be tested there. NHTSA and NSC pages returned 403 as well. The 2026 figures were therefore checked against search results, not the NHTSA release.

- 2021 deaths of 43,230, +10.8% from 39,007 in 2020: TRUE. FARS matches exactly.
- Declines in 2022, 2023 and 2024 (42,721; 41,025; 39,254): confirmed in FARS. The "fifth consecutive annual decline" claim is MOSTLY TRUE. It rests on NHTSA estimates for 2025 and 2026.
- H1 2026 deaths of 16,750, down 2.5% from 17,175: MOSTLY TRUE. The arithmetic checks, and this is an agency estimate. It fits NHTSA's Q1 2026 estimate of 7,770 (-4.3%, rate 0.99). NHTSA's earlier H1 2025 estimate was 17,140, so 17,175 is a plausible revision. I could not read the NHTSA H1 2026 release itself.
- Rate of 1.02 per 100 million vehicle miles, and "lowest first half since 2015": MOSTLY TRUE. Both are consistent with the Q1 data.
- Q1 -5% and Q2 -0.2%: MOSTLY TRUE. The earlier NHTSA Q1 estimate was -4.3%, so -5% is likely a revision.
- 2025 rate of 1.10, second-lowest in history: MOSTLY TRUE. Search results confirm NHTSA said this.
- Bicyclist deaths +4% to 1,148 in 2025, with the 2023 peak the highest in more than four decades: MOSTLY TRUE. Secondary outlets repeat the same wire data. NHTSA was not read directly.
- NHTSA study of $340 billion in crash costs and $1.37 trillion including quality-of-life losses: MOSTLY TRUE. It matches the 2023 NHTSA study as I recall it. I did not re-fetch it.
- Experts' explanations for the pandemic surge (emptier roads, less ticketing, impaired driving): MOSTLY TRUE. These are the commonly cited explanations, but the "experts" are unnamed.

Misleading framing: the headline compares a half-year rate (1.02) with full-year rates. First-half rates run seasonally lower, so "lowest rate since 2014" overstates the improvement. The same article says the first-half death toll is lowest since 2015, and its 2025 annual rate of 1.10 was only second-lowest ever. Also, deaths fell only 0.2% in Q2, so the trend is flattening. The article does say this, but the headline does not. NSC's separate estimate (18,460, -2%) uses a different counting basis and is not comparable.

Gaps: no primary NHTSA H1 2026 release was read (403). The 2025 and 2026 FARS data are not in the warehouse.
