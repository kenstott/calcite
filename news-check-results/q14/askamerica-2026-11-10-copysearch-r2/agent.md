Central claim (deaths fell 2.5% to est. 16,750 in H1 2026 from 17,175): mostly true, but a projection. NHTSA's Q1 2026 report gives Q1+Q2 2025 = 8,120 + 9,055 = 17,175. The article's Q1 -5% and Q2 -0.2% reconcile to about 16,750 (-2.5%). NHTSA's July Q1 report said -4.3%, so Q1 was revised. NHTSA's own H1 2026 release could not be fetched or found, so the 16,750 and 1.02 figures are checked by arithmetic only.

Other claims:
- Lowest first half since 2015: true. H1 2015 = 16,193, H1 2016 = 17,717.
- Rate 1.02, lowest since 2014: mostly true. H1 2014 was about 1.01 and H1 2025 about 1.05. The 1.02 figure itself is unverified.
- Fifth straight annual decline: true, conditional on 2026. Warehouse FARS: 43,230 (2021), 42,721, 41,025, 39,254 (2024); NHTSA projects 36,640 for 2025.
- 2021: +10.8% to 43,230: true. Warehouse FARS 39,007 to 43,230. The "highest since 2005" part rests on NHTSA's statement, since FARS in the warehouse starts in 2008.
- 2025 rate 1.10 and lowest deaths since 2019: mostly true (36,640 vs 36,355 in 2019). "Second-lowest in history" was not independently checked.
- Bicyclist deaths +4% to 1,148: mostly true. NHTSA 2024 was 1,103, and +4% gives 1,147. The 1,148 figure was found only in a secondary report.
- $340B, $30B taxpayer, $1.37T crash costs: true. They come from NHTSA's 2019-data study, released in 2023. The article omits that the data year is 2019.
- Experts' causes (empty roads, less ticketing): consistent with NHTSA commentary, not tested statistically.
- US pandemic rate rose more than other developed nations: unsupported here. It is widely reported, but no primary page was fetched and the warehouse has no international road-death table.

Misleading context omitted:
- All 2025/2026 figures are NHTSA projections that will be revised.
- The Q2 decline is nearly flat, a tough comparison after Q2 2025's -10.4%.
- The National Safety Council's separate H1 2026 estimate is 18,460 deaths (-2%, rate 1.12), on a different counting basis.
