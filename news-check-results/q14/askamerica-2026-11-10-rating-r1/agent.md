The Reuters/Investing.com article's claims about first-half 2026 US traffic deaths, the fatality rate and the multi-year trend are accurate; 1 claim could not be confirmed.

**Rating: 0 Pinocchios** (Washington Post 0-4 scale).

Access: investing.com returned 403. I used the Reuters (David Shepardson) copy on srnnews.com. nhtsa.gov also returned 403, so NHTSA's own H1 2026 figures (16,750 deaths, 1.02 per 100M VMT) were corroborated only through the Reuters copy and search summaries of the NHTSA release, not read from NHTSA's page.

Claims:
- Central claim, "lowest rate since 2014": true, as an NHTSA early estimate. NHTSA's own wording is the second-lowest half-year rate on record, which is consistent.
- 16,750 deaths, -2.5% from 17,175: true. The arithmetic gives -2.47%.
- "Fifth straight year of declines": true. Warehouse FARS totals (transport.fatal_crashes) fall every year from 2021 (43,230) through 2022 (42,721), 2023 (41,025) and 2024 (39,254). 2025 is down per NHTSA, and 2026 is a projection ("on pace").
- 2025 deaths lowest since 2019 and rate 1.10, second lowest in history: true. FARS 2024 (39,254) is still above 2019 (36,355). The 2025 figure is from NHTSA via secondary copies.
- Q1 down 5%, Q2 down 0.2%: true. NHTSA's July Q1 estimate was 4.3%, revised in the H1 release. The revision is not a misstatement.
- Bicyclist deaths up 4% to 1,148 in 2025: true, per NHTSA as summarized in secondary reports. Not in the warehouse.
- 2021 deaths up 10.8% to 43,230, most since 2005: true. The warehouse matches exactly (2020: 39,007, so +10.82%). 2005 is outside the warehouse range.
- 2023 NHTSA study, $30B to taxpayers and $340B to society: true. Both are in NHTSA's report DOT HS 813 403 (2023), which covers 2019 crashes. The $1.37T total was not confirmed; sources say "nearly $1.4T".
- Pandemic US fatality rate rose much higher than in other developed nations: unsupported. This is a qualitative comparison. No table in the warehouse carries it, and I fetched no international comparison source.

Misleading-framing flags (none material):
- The headline says "lowest rate since 2014", but the larger move is in the count. H1 deaths fell only 2.5%, and Q2 only 0.2%. That is a flattening, which the article does disclose.
- The figures are early estimates and subject to revision.
- The NSC's separate H1 estimate (18,460 deaths, -2%, rate 1.12) uses a different definition and is not comparable to NHTSA's. The article does not mix the two.

Warehouse coverage: FARS covers 2008-2024 only, so 2025 and 2026 figures rest on NHTSA via Reuters.
