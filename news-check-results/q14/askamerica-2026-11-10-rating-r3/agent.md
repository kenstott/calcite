The Investing.com/Reuters article's claims about first-half 2026 US traffic deaths, the fatality rate and pandemic-era trends are accurate; 1 claim could not be confirmed.

**Rating: 0 Pinocchios.**

Investing.com returned HTTP 403, so I graded the Reuters text from a syndicated copy (wiky.com) against NHTSA's own brief, DOT HS 813 875 (Nov 2026), which I opened in full. The warehouse table transport.fatal_crashes (FARS) covers only 2008-2024, so it checked the historical claims and NHTSA's table covered 2025-26.

| Claim | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Lowest rate since 2014 (central) | True | H1 2026 rate is 1.02 per 100M VMT; H1 2014 was 1.01 and every H1 from 2015 to 2025 is higher. |
| 16,750 deaths, down 2.5% from 17,175; lowest since 2015 | True | NHTSA Table 1. H1 2015 was 16,193 and H1 2016 was 17,717. |
| Rate 1.02 | True | Matches NHTSA (H1 2025: 1.05). |
| Fifth straight yearly decline "on pace" | True | FARS: 43,230 (2021), 42,721, 41,025, 39,254 (2024). NHTSA projects 36,640 for 2025. |
| 2025 lowest since 2019, rate 1.10 | True | 36,640 is above 2019's 36,355 (FARS) and below every year from 2020 to 2024. I did not verify "second-lowest ever" against years before 2014. |
| Q1 down 5%, Q2 down 0.2% | True | -5.0% and -0.2%. |
| 2021 up 10.8% to 43,230, most since 2005 | True | FARS 39,007 to 43,230 is +10.8%, and 2022-2024 are lower. |
| Pandemic causes (emptier roads, perceived lax enforcement), attributed to unnamed "experts" | Mostly true | A plausible, widely published explanation, but unnamed and not testable here. |
| 2023 NHTSA study: $30B to taxpayers, $340B to society, $1.37T with quality of life | Mostly true | The figures match the NHTSA report, but they are for 2019 crashes (the report came out in 2023), which the article does not say. |
| Bicyclist deaths up 4% to 1,148 in 2025 | Unsupported | No warehouse table carries it and I did not find NHTSA's own bicyclist table. Only news coverage repeats it. |

**Misleading-framing check.** Nothing is materially misleading, but a reader should know:
- All 2025 and 2026 figures are NHTSA statistical projections, which the article does not say outright.
- 1.02 is the second-lowest first-half rate, not the lowest. 2014 was 1.01.
- Progress stalled in Q2, and May and June actually rose. The article does disclose the Q1 and Q2 split.
- "Fifth straight decline" is a pace for 2026, not a result.
- The National Safety Council's separate first-half estimate is 18,460 deaths, down 2%. It uses a different definition and is not comparable.
