# Fact-check: "US traffic deaths fall in first half of 2026, lowest rate since 2014" (Reuters/David Shepardson via Investing.com, Nov 10, 2026)

Verdict: 1 Pinocchio (mostly accurate, minor framing issue). The raw numbers check out against NHTSA-derived sources. The one real wrinkle is the "lowest rate since 2014" headline.

Caveat: nhtsa.gov returned HTTP 403 to me, so I could not read NHTSA's press release directly. I relied on search-engine excerpts of it, the NHTSA Q1 2026 early estimate, and secondary outlets (Carscoops, AOL/Reuters syndication).

## Claims

| # | Claim | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | NHTSA: H1 2026 deaths fell 2.5% to 16,750 from 17,175 | Verified (secondary) | Search excerpt of NHTSA release (nhtsa.gov/press-releases/decline-traffic-fatalities-first-half-2026) gives 16,750 and a 2.5% drop from 17,175. Note NHTSA's original H1 2025 estimate was 17,140, so 17,175 is a later revision. Consistent. |
| 2 | Fatality rate 1.02 per 100M VMT, "lowest since 2014" | Partly supported, framing disputed | 1.02 confirmed (vs 1.05 a year earlier). The search excerpt says NHTSA itself called it the second-lowest half-year rate, not the lowest since 2014. I could not read the release to confirm the exact wording. The Q1 rate of 0.99 was the lowest Q1 since 2014. |
| 3 | Lowest H1 death count since 2015 | Plausible, not independently confirmed | Q1 2026 (7,770) was the lowest Q1 since 2015. |
| 4 | 2026 on pace for a fifth straight yearly decline | Consistent | Carscoops/NHTSA reporting says the same. |
| 5 | Deaths down 5% in Q1, only 0.2% in Q2 | Consistent | NHTSA's Q1 estimate was -4.3% (7,770 vs 8,120); the 5% likely reflects revised data. Implied Q2 is about 8,980, which is consistent with H1 16,750. The Q1 revision itself is not directly verified. |
| 6 | 2025 deaths lowest since 2019; rate 1.10, second-lowest in US history | Verified | NHTSA early estimate: 36,640 deaths, -6.7%; rate 1.10. |
| 7 | Bicyclist deaths up 4% to 1,148 in 2025; four-decade high reached in 2023 | Verified via secondary source only | Carscoops and Reuters syndication; not seen on nhtsa.gov. |
| 8 | 2021 deaths up 10.8% to 43,230, most since 2005 | Verified | Matches NHTSA's published 2021 figure. |
| 9 | Attributed to less police enforcement and more impaired driving; US rate rose more than in other developed nations | Reporting, not a checkable statistic | Attributed to experts and NHTSA commentary; widely reported (ITF, IIHS). |
| 10 | 2023 NHTSA study: crashes cost $30B taxpayers, $340B society, $1.37T with quality-of-life | Consistent | Matches NHTSA's 2023 economic-cost study (published 2023, covering 2019 data). Figures are stale and are not 2026 data. |

No direct quotes appear in the piece beyond NHTSA attribution.

## Not checkable here
- The exact wording of NHTSA's H1 2026 release (403 block).
- The revised Q1 2026 figure behind the "5%".

## Materially misleading or missing context
1. Headline rate framing: "lowest rate since 2014" versus NHTSA's reported "second-lowest" half-year rate. Both may be true (second-lowest overall, lowest since 2014), but the headline leaves out that the rate is not a record.
2. The Q2 improvement was only 0.2%. The first-half decline is almost entirely a Q1 story, and the trend is stalling. The article reports this, but the headline does not.
3. NHTSA numbers are early estimates and get revised. The National Safety Council's separate estimate for H1 2026 is 18,460 deaths (-2%) and 1.12 per 100M VMT. The methodologies differ (NSC counts beyond 30 days), but the article presents one data source as the whole picture.
4. The cost figures are from 2019 data, not current.

## Rating
1 Pinocchio on the Washington Post 0-4 scale. Figures are accurate to their sources and the article discloses the weak Q2 and the bicyclist exception. The headline's "lowest" framing and reliance on preliminary data are the only deductions.
