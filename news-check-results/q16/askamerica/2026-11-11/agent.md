# The Newsweek article's claims about Rollins's ground-beef price remark, record beef prices, and Trump's ranching orders are mostly accurate.

Article: https://www.newsweek.com/rollins-americans-good-with-surging-beef-prices-12468996 (fetched successfully)

**Rating: 1 Pinocchio.**

## Claim-by-claim

Warehouse (AskAmerica) checks:
- TRUE: Ground beef (all uncooked, BLS APU0000FC1101) was $7.158 in Aug 2026, $7.116 in Jul, $6.631 in Aug 2025. Series max since 1995 (record); Sept eased to $7.062. [econ.average_prices]
- TRUE: Diesel $6.5107 (AAA). EIA No. 2 diesel was $6.529 on Mon 2026-09-21; different survey, consistent. [energy.eia_retail_gasoline_prices]
- MOSTLY FALSE (Rollins, central): "Ground beef was $6.50 ... most Americans say 'I'm good with that.'" Aug 2026 was $7.158 (100% beef $6.923); $6.50 was the Jul 2025 level, so the figure is about a year stale and about $0.66 low. No survey supports "most Americans say". The article prints $7.16 earlier but does not tell readers the Secretary's figure is wrong.
- MOSTLY FALSE (Rollins): herd expansion "stopped under the Democrats." NASS Jan 1 cattle and calves (sum of states): 2019 94.7M (peak), 2020 93.8M (before Biden), 2021 93.6M, 2022 91.8M, 2023 88.8M, 2024 87.2M, 2025 86.5M. The decline began in Trump's first term and continued under both parties. The CPA report cited by the article blames drought, screwworm and packer concentration. Caveat: the warehouse has no 2026 inventory rows.

Independent sources:
- TRUE: CPA report (dated 6/30/2026, read directly): 86.2M head, smallest in 75 years; over 450,000 lost cattle ranches since 1997 (the article says "ranchers"); about 75% of beef cows in drought. USDA's Jan 2026 count was 86.15M (Agri-Pulse).
- FALSE: "Earlier in August, Trump signed two executive orders." DTN: signed Friday Sept 4, 2026. The Aug 21 action was a Truth Social announcement of an import quota.
- MOSTLY TRUE: 300,000 t tariff waiver, 90 days, announced in August. DTN says the added quota is lean beef trimmings, in three 100,000 t tranches from Sept 1, not finished ground beef.
- TRUE: NCBA quote ("Cattle markets have already turned sharply lower this morning...") appears verbatim in DTN's Aug 21 coverage.
- TRUE (Rollins): Americans "began to eat more beef." USDA/ERS per-capita use is 59.2 lb (2025) rising to 59.5 lb (2026 projection), a slight increase; direction right, size tiny.
- UNSUPPORTED (USDA spokesperson, central): demand "grown almost 10% in the past decade." No such figure found; Agri-Pulse shows about a 0.5% rise year over year, and IBISWorld estimates a slight five-year decline. Per-capita consumption is not in the warehouse.

## Misleading even where the facts check out
- The lede lets Rollins's "good with that" line stand for consumer contentment. The only evidence is purchase volume, which Agri-Pulse economists tie to low demand elasticity, not satisfaction.
- The "record high" applies to one series (all uncooked ground beef, since 1995).
- The import plan is described as ground beef; the quota is for trimmings.

## Caveats
- The White House order pages and the USDA NASS Jan 2026 release were not fetched; order dates rest on DTN and on a sibling Newsweek page.
- ag.nass_livestock_inventory has no 2026 rows, so the Jan 2026 herd figure is not warehouse-verified.
- No Rollins interview transcript was found, so the quotes were taken as reported.
- Engine scores (this article only): author 79 (mostly honest); Rollins 50 (mixed); USDA spokesperson: no checkable claims.
