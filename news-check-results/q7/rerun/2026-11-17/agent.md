The American Action Forum article's claims about the October 2026 federal deficit, revenue and spending, and the national debt are very biased and accurate.

Rating: 0 Pinocchios (the engine's score from the claim grades). My own reading is that the framing deserves a caution flag, set out below.

Every figure tested matches Treasury data (MTS Table 9 and debt-to-the-penny, via the warehouse):
- Deficit $404B ($773B outlays minus $369B receipts). That is +$119.7B (42%) on October 2025's $284B.
- Receipts -$35B; individual income tax -$31B (14%); corporate +$3B; customs -$12B (39%).
- Social Security +$8.6B; Medicare +$19.5B; defense +$28.9B (29%); veterans +$4.6B; income security -$3.7B; net interest +$12.2B (13%). Net interest was the fourth-largest outlay.
- Debt held by the public $32.74T against $30.57T, so +$2.17T. It is 1.93 times the October 2019 level, so "nearly doubled" holds.
- CBO's early estimate ($409B, $778B outlays) is close to the final Treasury figure.

Framing problems:
1. The comparison base was distorted. October 2025 fell inside the Oct 1 to Nov 12, 2025 shutdown, and the piece does not say so. Defense was $103.5B in October 2024, $99.9B in October 2025 and $128.9B in October 2026, so the rise is about 25% against a normal base, not 29%. The deficit is +$147B (57%) over October 2024 rather than +$120B (42%). Nov 1 fell on a weekend in 2025 and 2026, so payments moved into October in both years. That makes the year-over-year comparison like-for-like on timing but not the two-year one. I could not read CBO's October review (HTTP 403). The shutdown dates are from general knowledge, not a source I fetched.
2. "Customs duties have declined since May" is loose. Customs went from $22.1B in April to about $0 in May, -$25.6B in June and -$8.5B in July, then recovered to $12.8B, $5.9B and $19.2B. The cause is the Supreme Court's February 2026 IEEPA ruling and the refunds that followed (about $22B in May), not simply executive-action tariff changes.
3. The stated drivers for the spending lines (enrollment, military personnel and R&D, OBBBA reforms) are AAF's own and were not independently verified.
4. Medicaid is not a separate line in the loaded table. The Health function total (+$5.4B) is consistent with the article's Medicaid +$7B and other health -$2B.
5. The four chart images were fetched but are PNGs with no extractable text.
6. Minor: typos ("$404 deficit", "lover revenue").
