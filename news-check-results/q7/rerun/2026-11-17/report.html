Where the framing misleads

1. Distorted base month. Defense was $103.5B in October 2024, $99.9B in October 2025, $128.9B in October 2026, so against a normal base defense is up about 25%, not 29%, and the deficit is up $147B (57%) over October 2024 rather than $120B (42%). Nov 1 fell on a weekend in 2025 and 2026, so benefit payments moved into October both years (Medicare $77B in Oct 2024 vs $151B and $170B later); year over year is like-for-like on that factor, the two-year comparison is not. CBO's October review was not readable (HTTP 403).

2. Customs. April $22.1B, May about $0, June -$25.6B, July -$8.5B, then $12.8B, $5.9B, $19.2B. 'Declined since May' misdescribes a collapse then partial recovery; the cause is the Supreme Court IEEPA ruling and refunds (about $22B in May), not simply executive-action rate changes.

3. Causal explanations for spending lines are AAF's own and not independently verified. Typos ('$404 deficit', 'lover revenue').
