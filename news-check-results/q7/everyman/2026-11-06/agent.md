# Fact-check: "Arkansas farm bankruptcies on the rise, most in the nation" (Talk Business & Politics, Oct 2026)

Method note: the fetch returned a summary of the article, not verbatim text. Quotes and wording are therefore as summarized. Sources checked: UA Division of Agriculture release of Oct 22, 2026, plus secondary search results.

## Verdict: 1 Pinocchio (mostly accurate; minor gaps and framing issues)

## Claims

| # | Claim | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Arkansas led the nation with 12 Chapter 12 filings in Q3 2026; Iowa was second with 6 | Verified. The UADA release (from National Agricultural Law Center data) says the same. |
| 2 | 32 Arkansas ag operations have filed so far in 2026 | Verified (same release). |
| 3 | Arkansas had 33 filings in 2025, more than double the prior year | Supported by secondary reporting that attributes it to the Arkansas Farm Bureau. The UADA release links an article saying Arkansas led in 2025 but does not give the figure. Not checked against court data. |
| 4 | 292 Chapter 12 filings nationally so far in 2026, about 19% above 246 in the first three quarters of 2025 | Verified. 292/246 is +18.7%. |
| 5 | Chapter 11 filings up 11.5%, from 6,838 to 7,623 | Verified. These are all Chapter 11 filings, not only farm ones. The piece's wording makes it hard to tell. See the framing note below. |
| 6 | 1,401 ag bankruptcy filings Jan 2021 to Jun 2026, about 14% (201) under Chapter 11 | Not checkable here. It is not in the UADA release, and I found no independent source. The arithmetic holds: 201/1,401 = 14.3%. |
| 7 | Diesel averaged $3.58-$3.62 before March and about $6.27 by October (EIA) | Partly verified. A secondary report puts the Oct 20 weekly average at $6.276. The same sources show a record of about $6.53 in late September, so "about $6.27" is a point-in-time value, not a peak. The pre-March figure is consistent with the roughly $3.6 of 2025 but I did not confirm it against EIA's table. |
| 8 | Fuel and fertilizer prices rose sharply after a March military action against Iran; 2025 tariffs raised equipment and parts costs | Not independently confirmed. The diesel spike is real. The cause is as stated by the piece. The UADA release names no causes. The tariff effect on equipment is a plausible but unquantified claim. |
| 9 | About 28% of Midwest bankers named equipment dealers as under the greatest stress (Creighton); October was the 38th straight month of farm equipment sales below growth-neutral | Not verified. I could not find the October 2026 Creighton report. The 38-month count is consistent with earlier counts (26th in Oct 2025), so it is plausible. The 28% figure is unconfirmed. |
| 10 | Arkansas rice: 901,000 acres this year vs more than 1.25 million last year; first time under one million since the early 1980s | Partly contradicted. The "below 1 million for the first time since 1983" part is verified (UADA). But the June NASS figure was 851,000 acres, and I found no source for 901,000. It may be a later revision from the August or later NASS reports, which raised long-grain acres. The 2025 figure was not confirmed. |
| 11 | Jackson County farmer: margins went "very negative, very quickly"; costs roughly doubled | Not checkable here. This is a single anecdote and could not be traced. Diesel at roughly 72% above plan (extension economist Ryan Loy) supports the direction of the claim, but "doubled" is the farmer's own figure. |

## Materially misleading or missing context

- Small numbers. 12 filings in a quarter is a count of 12 farms. "Most in the nation" is true in absolute terms, but no per-farm rate is given and national counts are small, so one state's rank can swing from quarter to quarter. The Q3 national total was 91, so Arkansas had 13% of it.
- The Chapter 11 numbers cover all filers (7,623), most of them not farms. Putting them beside the farm Chapter 12 numbers can suggest farm distress is wider than it is.
- Attributing causes (Iran, tariffs) to the filings is the piece's inference. Bankruptcy filings lag cost shocks, so a March event would hardly explain the 2025 doubling in Arkansas. The rice collapse is also tied by other sources to drought and low prices, not just input costs.
- The rice acreage figure appears to conflict with the NASS number I found (851,000). Ask the publisher which report it used.

## Overall

The core headline claims (Arkansas first in Q3 with 12, Iowa 6, 32 year to date, 292 vs 246 nationally) match the primary release exactly. The headline is supported. The weaknesses are an unverifiable bankers survey number and an anecdote, a rice figure that does not match the June NASS report, and causal framing stronger than the evidence. Rating: **1 Pinocchio**.
