# Fact-check: WOWT (AP) "Thousands of flights canceled as airlines work to dig out after nationwide ground stop" (Nov 14, 2026)

Method note: WebFetch returned only a summary of the WOWT/AP page (it declined verbatim reproduction), so claims were extracted from that summary and from the AP text as republished by MPR News, then checked against CBS, ABC, Wikipedia, and Fox/search snippets. No raw FAA, FlightAware or Cirium primary data was reachable; "primary" here means officials' on-record statements as reported by multiple outlets. Quotes below are as relayed, not verified against video or transcripts.

## Claim-by-claim

1. Airlines canceled thousands of flights Saturday (Nov 14) recovering from a Friday nationwide ground stop. SUPPORTED. Wikipedia: more than 2,500 additional Saturday cancellations. Fox reported later counts nearing 7,000.
2. FAA said it was the longest ground stop in 25 years. SUPPORTED, with a caveat. The framing is AP's and Fox's. The 2023 stop lasted about 90 minutes and the 2001 stop lasted days. BBC (via Wikipedia) calls it the longest since 9/11. This is the second nationwide stop since 9/11, and the first since January 2023 (CBS, ABC, AP). Whether the FAA itself said "25 years" was not verified.
3. Duration "more than four hours." SUPPORTED, but sources differ on the exact figure. ABC: 3:54 to 8:15 p.m. ET (about 4h20). CBS: 3:36 to 8:20 p.m. (about 4h45). Wikipedia's infobox says 4h21, and its lead text says 3.5 hours.
4. Bedford: Saturday would still have "delays and cancellations," but the "worst is behind us." SUPPORTED. This is in AP via MPR and in search summaries.
5. Bedford: schedule back to normal by end of Sunday. SUPPORTED (CBS, Fox). Duffy separately said "full capacity" by Monday, which is consistent.
6. Officials said new technology added to the ATC system overloaded older equipment, causing communication and radar gaps. PARTLY SUPPORTED / CONTESTED. Wikipedia says Mullin said new FAA equipment appeared to have overwhelmed older systems. But Duffy on Friday blamed an "incredibly old" analog system and said the SMART AI tool was not involved (ABC). The later account is a router being installed during a copper-to-fiber swap that flooded other routers. "New technology" is a loose paraphrase of that.
7. Duffy blamed contractor L3Harris Technologies and said investigators were checking contractor error or a faulty router. SUPPORTED as to the substance. CBS: Duffy said an L3Harris contractor at a remote site was switching copper to fiber while replacing a router, and the router flooded others with about 1,000 login attempts every 4 to 5 seconds. Bedford said that was not a full root-cause explanation, and the FAA is still investigating human error versus a faulty router. "Blamed" slightly overstates a preliminary finding.
8. FlightAware: more than 4,100 cancellations Saturday. SUPPORTED as an AP snapshot (MPR). Other outlets report different Saturday totals (Wikipedia: more than 2,500; CNN early on: more than 2,000; Fox later: nearly 7,000). The counts are time-dependent. Friday's FlightAware count was 9,159 (CBS).
9. Cirium: more than 3,200. SUPPORTED as an AP snapshot. I did not find Cirium's own data.
10. Duffy or DHS: no one was in danger; more than 3,000 airborne flights landed safely. SUPPORTED, with attribution nuance. AP/MPR attributes it to Homeland Security Secretary Mullin, and Wikipedia attributes the 3,000 figure to Mullin. Duffy said 3,500 planes were airborne when the stop began and that "everyone landed." The two figures are not in conflict, but the attribution to Duffy in my summary of the WOWT page may be loose.
11. The FAA has replaced thousands of copper wires with fiber using $12.5 billion from Congress. PARTLY SUPPORTED. The $12.5B appropriation is confirmed, and "thousands of copper wires replaced" is AP's narration. But Reps. Larsen and Carson said only about $1B of the $12.5B had been spent (CBS). So the funds are appropriated, not all spent.
12. Duffy said $30 billion more is needed. SUPPORTED but incomplete. He lobbied for $30B on Nov 12. After the stop, Wikipedia reports a request for $50B in total (Duffy and Mullin).
13. Experts said the nationwide scale, with controllers unable to reliably reach pilots, distinguished it from earlier outages (Arroyo, former United pilot). SUPPORTED as a quote in AP (MPR).
14. Other attributed quotes in the AP text: Duffy "you will have more of these"; consultant Charlton on the "knock-on effect"; UK CAA's Tim Johnson on "follow-on disruption." Reported in AP via MPR. Not independently verified against audio.

Not checkable here: the exact on-page WOWT wording (only a summary was retrievable), Cirium's raw counts, and whether the FAA formally used the phrase "longest in 25 years."

## Rating: 1 Pinocchio (minor shading)

Almost everything checks out against multiple outlets. The problems are minor:
- The causal story shifted within 24 hours (old analog system, then "new technology overloaded old equipment," then a contractor router swap). Presenting "new technology" and "contractor" side by side, without saying the cause is unsettled, could mislead.
- Cancellation totals are snapshots and were already stale or low relative to later counts (up to about 7,000 on Saturday).
- The $12.5B is appropriated, but only about $1B reportedly spent. The headline numbers on funding come from officials who are pressing for more money, and the piece largely relays their framing.
- It does not note the Friday cancellation total (about 8,000 to 9,000), which makes "thousands" on Saturday look smaller than the full two-day disruption (about 15,000 flights per Fox).

Nothing found is fabricated; no 2 or higher rating is warranted.
