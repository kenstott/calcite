# Fact-check: Gazette guest opinion, "2026 historic drought and the value of being prepared" (Nov 17, 2026)

Author: Travas Deal, CEO of Colorado Springs Utilities (CSU). This is an advocacy piece by the utility's own chief executive. Method note: WebFetch returned only a summary of the article, not verbatim text, so claims were checked as summarized. Verification relied on web search snippets, not full reads of primary documents.

## Claims

1. **Water year 2026 (ended Sept 30) was Colorado's worst in modern history; record heat, very low snowpack, warmest winter on record.** Mostly supported. The Colorado Climate Center and NOAA data put Dec-Feb 2025-26 as the warmest winter on record, about 1.6F above the prior record (1980-81). Statewide SNOTEL snowpack hit record lows, but SNOTEL records only start in about 1987, and some climatologists think 1977 or 1981 may have been worse. Precipitation was only the 15th driest, not a record. "Worst in modern history" is defensible but is a superlative that rests on a short record.
2. **CSU had about 40% of its typical water yield.** Supported. CSU lowered expected yield to about 41% of average, the lowest on record.
3. **CSU plans on a 50-year horizon.** Not checked. I did not find a source.
4. **CSU began 2026 with three years of customer demand in storage; declared preliminary Water Shortage Preparation.** Supported. Reporting says about three years of storage in late March, and the board enacted Water Shortage Preparation without added restrictions. The Water Shortage Ordinance sets a trigger of 1.5 years of storage by April 1.
5. **Total water use has fallen by nearly half since 2000 while the city grew 42%.** Partly supported, and likely overstated. Independent figures (Water Desk/KUNC) show about 40% growth from 2000 to 2023, a 39% fall in per-capita use, and only about a 25% fall in total deliveries. CSU's own materials say per-capita use fell more than 40%. "Total use down nearly half" appears to conflate per-capita and total use. CSU's 2022 plan shows demand fell from a peak above 94,000 acre-feet to mid-1980s levels.
6. **CSU expects to start 2027 with more than two years of demand in storage.** Not checkable here. This is a projection, and I found no source for it. It is consistent with reporting that storage declines through 2026 from about 3 years.
7. **Too early to know whether tighter restrictions are needed; snowpack forecast no better than average.** Not checkable here. It is a forecast and an opinion.
8. **Quote: water is "a shared resource and a shared responsibility."** Not checkable. It is the author's own words.

## Misleading or missing context
- The piece comes from the utility's CEO and presents CSU's planning favorably. It omits that storage is falling through 2026 and that recovery can take multiple years.
- The "nearly half" use claim blurs per-capita and total figures.
- Per CSU's own plan, per-person use declines stalled after 2013.
- "Worst ever" snowpack claims rest on records starting around 1987.

## Rating
**2 Pinocchios (mostly accurate, with an overstated conservation statistic and an unqualified superlative).** The drought, yield and storage facts check out; the main problem is that the total-use claim looks inflated.
