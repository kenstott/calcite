# Fact-check: "PSO rate increase approved for December 2026 in Oklahoma" (Radio Oklahoma News)

Limits: the full article text was only available as a WebFetch summary. NonDoc returned 403. I did not find the OCC order text itself. Sources: KGOU (2026-06-30, 2026-11-05), NonDoc and OKCFox via search summaries and fetch, Tulsa Flyer.

## Claims
| # | Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | OCC voted 2-1 on Nov 5, 2026 to approve a 3.55% residential increase for PSO | Supported (KGOU, NonDoc, OKCFox). Commissioner David voted no, with a partial concurrence per NonDoc. |
| 2 | The increase is "permanent" | Partly misleading. OKCFox describes an interim order because large-user power-supply issues remain open. PSO says a further base-rate case is possible. |
| 3 | Increase appears on bills in the December 2026 cycle | Only PSO's expectation. The order applies new rates from the first regular billing cycle after revised tariffs are approved. The headline's "approved for December" overstates certainty. |
| 4 | Dollar impact on the average bill not yet released | Supported. PSO said it was still calculating, with figures due the following week. |
| 5 | PSO has six days to file revised tariffs and financial calculations | Roughly supported. OKCFox says six calendar days to file updated accounting and a revised cost-allocation study, then objections, then tariff approval. "Revised tariffs" is a slight simplification. |
| 6 | Residents have paid about $11 a month in interim charges since July 1, 2026 | Supported (KGOU, 2026-06-30). The $11 was an average-bill figure PSO said excluded some offsetting adjustments. |
| 7 | Residential interim payments will not be refunded | Supported as PSO's statement (OKCFox). The OCC has not confirmed refund eligibility; staff are still reviewing the financials. |
| 8 | Industrial groups get bill credits within 90 days | Partly accurate. The 90 days run from when the revised tariffs take effect, not from the Nov 5 vote. The credits are refunds of excess interim collections, with interest. |
| 9 | New large-load tariff makes data centers and other large users cover their infrastructure costs | Partly supported. The order includes a large-load tariff (OKCFox/search summary). OKCFox says the order actually excluded tracking of part of the transmission costs, which could push costs toward other classes. |
| 10 | The tariff follows Oklahoma's Data Center Customer Ratepayer Protection Act | Not checkable here. I could not find the act or any source linking it to the order. |

## Missing context that changes the picture
- 3.55% is a base-rate figure relative to pre-case rates, not an additional 3.55% on current bills. The comparison point is unclear in coverage.
- PSO asked for about 15% in January 2026. The AG-PSO settlement would have meant about 1% (about $2.45 a month) for residential customers.
- The OCC rejected the settlement's rate design. Residential customers get slightly higher impacts than under the settlement, and industrial customers get lower ones (AG's office, via search summary).
- The headline implies residents face a new December increase. They have already paid roughly $11 a month since July, so the net change at December depends on the final tariffs.

## Rating: 1 Pinocchio (mostly accurate, with some missing context)
The core facts (the 2-1 vote, 3.55%, the interim charge, no residential refund, and the six-day filing deadline) check out. The problems are the unqualified "permanent" and "December" timing, the 90-day refund clock, the lack of comparison to the settlement and the interim charge, and the data center act, which I could not verify.
