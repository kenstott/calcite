# Fact-check: Chalkbeat Colorado, 2026-11-24, HEA Group analysis of Colorado college payoff

Limits: WebFetch returned only a summary of the article (no verbatim text). I could not reach HEA Group's own data. Verdicts are provisional.

## Claims and verdicts
1. Colorado grads earn about 55% more than HS-only residents, second-lowest premium nationally (US 76%): NOT CHECKABLE HERE. No HEA Group page found. Colorado's own ROI data (CDHE) shows a Colorado College figure of +$16,630 over a $38,743 HS baseline, about 43%, so the premium depends on method. Not contradicted, not confirmed.
2. About $61,000 vs about $39,000 (HS), four years out: PARTIAL. The $38,743 CDHE HS baseline matches the roughly $39,000 figure. The $61,000 is unverified.
3. Bachelor's $68,264 (US $67,988), associate $51,848 (US $53,315), certificate $45,214 (US $42,509): NOT CHECKABLE HERE (HEA data not accessible).
4. About 11% of 2017-18 completers are in programs earning below HS graduates, nearly double the national rate; a third are cosmetology: NOT CHECKABLE HERE.
5. "Do no harm" rule: fail in two of three consecutive years, lose Direct Loan access: CONFIRMED (NAICU, EdTrust, TICAS).
6. Designations begin 2028-29: MOSTLY TRUE. EdTrust puts first loan loss at July 2028; TICAS says the final rule pushed it to 2029. Timing is fuzzy, not wrong.
7. Quotes (Itzkowitz, Hultquist, Nicholas, Gianneschi) and CDHE statement that no programs were restricted: NOT CHECKABLE HERE (no transcripts or primary records).

## Rating
1 Pinocchio (provisional). Nothing found is false, and the policy description is accurate. The central numbers rest on one advocacy-oriented analyst and could not be independently confirmed.

## Potentially misleading
- The premium compares against a state HS benchmark. Colorado has a high-wage HS-only baseline relative to some states, so "second-lowest" reflects the benchmark as much as degree quality. Colorado's bachelor's earnings are about equal to the national figure, so the weak ranking is not a sign of weak degrees.
- Earnings four years out undercount long-run returns.
- Cosmetology and allied-health earnings reflect part-time and tipped work; the "low earning" label may overstate poor value.
- Much of the 2017-18 cohort data is dated relative to the 2028-29 enforcement.
