# Fact-check: "The midterms were the largest win for either party in 50 years" (NPR via OPB, 2026-11-19)

Method: WebFetch of the OPB page (returned a paraphrase, not verbatim text), plus two web searches that found NPR syndications (WLRN, others) and ABC voter-poll coverage. No primary tallies (clerk of the House, state certified results) were retrieved. Check depth is limited.

## Claims
| # | Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | About 56% voted Democratic and 42% Republican in House races (14-point spread) | Reported identically in the NPR syndications. I did not find an independent tally (AP, Cook, Clerk). Counts are still incomplete, so the figure is provisional. Partly checkable. |
| 2 | Biggest win for either party in 50 years, since after Watergate (1974) | Consistent with the 14-point figure. From memory (not re-fetched), the 1974 Democratic margin was about 16 points, and no later House popular-vote margin comes close. The "since Watergate" framing is plausible. The strictly accurate wording is "largest since 1974", which is slightly longer than 50 years. |
| 3 | Dem wins in 2006, 2010 (as a GOP win), and 2018 were under 10 points | From memory (not re-fetched): 2006 about +8, 2018 about +8.6, 2010 GOP about +6.8. A search result separately says 2018 was "nearly 9 points". Consistent. |
| 4 | Obama 2008 was the closest, at almost 11 points | From memory: about +10.6. Consistent. |
| 5 | Democrats have flipped 46 GOP seats, possibly 49 with three uncalled races | Matches NPR syndications. Not checked against the House Clerk or AP. |
| 6 | Second-most flipped seats this century; Republicans flipped 66 in 2010, netting 64 | The 2010 flip count and net are from memory and unchecked. Most sources give a net GOP gain of 63, or 64 counting a party switch, so the "64" depends on counting method. Minor. |
| 7 | If Democrats win every race they lead, they hold 260 seats, the most since the 1991-93 Congress | Matches the syndications. Plausible, since the 102nd Congress had about 267 Democrats. Provisional. |
| 8 | House control changed hands five times in 20 years | 2006, 2010, 2018, 2022 and 2026 make five. Correct. |

No attributed quotes were visible in the text I retrieved. The tool paraphrased the page, so I cannot rule out quotes.

## Not checkable here
- The final national House popular vote totals: no primary source was retrieved, and counting is not finished.
- Final seat counts for the uncalled races.

## Context and possible misleading points
- The headline compares popular-vote margin, not seats. In seats, 260 would be below the 1970s Watergate-era majorities and other past wave levels. "Largest win" is therefore a claim about vote share only.
- Aggregate House vote share is distorted by uncontested seats and by uneven turnout. Where seats are uncontested, the national margin is not a clean measure of the electorate's preference.
- Final counts (late California ballots and others) can move the margin by a point or more.
- Comparing 2026 with 1974 uses a different mix of contested seats.

## Rating
1 Pinocchio (leaning mostly accurate), provisional. The claims are internally consistent and match the NPR syndications and the seat-flip reporting. The main caveats are that the 14-point margin is unconfirmed by an independent tally and the vote count is not final. I found nothing false.
