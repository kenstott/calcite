## Misleading context (facts right, picture incomplete)
1. Calendar timing. CBO and 247wallst say Nov 1 payments shifted into October in both years (weekend). The calendar-adjusted deficit is about $285B, up $94B (49%), not $404B. The "record October deficit" is real but the size of the jump partly reflects timing quirks, e.g. a defense trust-fund payment delayed by last year's shutdown.
2. Base effects. Non-withheld individual receipts fell partly because California wildfire deadline delays inflated Oct 2025; withheld taxes were flat (156.4 vs 155.9). The "individual income tax down 14%" is not a signal of weak underlying income.
3. One month is a noisy basis for "lack of progress"; the piece's debt-unsustainability framing is opinion.

## Grade: 1 Pinocchio (minor omissions)
Every number I could check matches Treasury. The weaknesses are missing timing and base-effect caveats and an unsupported tariff-ruling causal claim.
