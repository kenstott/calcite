# Fact-check: Cyprus Mail, "2026 set to be one of four hottest year on record, UN report says" (9 Nov 2026)

Method note: WebFetch returned only a summary of the article, not verbatim text, so claims below are the ones the summary reported. I did not see the exact wording.

## Claims and verdicts

1. 2026 is on track to be one of the four hottest years on record (WMO). SUPPORTED. The WMO "State of the Climate Update for COP31" (wmo.int/node/39187) says this. Caveat: the WMO page says the past four years (2023-2026) are the four warmest, so "one of four" is accurate but understates a firmer finding.
2. The link to a powerful El Nino. SUPPORTED. WMO says El Nino has been intensifying since August and is expected to be the strongest on record by December.
3. Jan-Sep 2026 global mean temperature was 1.47C above the pre-industrial average. SUPPORTED. WMO gives about 1.47C and says it is likely to rise.
4. Each of the past 12 years, including 2026, ranks among the 12 warmest. SUPPORTED in substance. WMO says the past 12 years are the warmest on record. The "12 warmest" phrasing is a trivial restatement.
5. Records run from about 1850. SUPPORTED. WMO uses 1850-1900 as its baseline.
6. Copernicus says 2026 is almost certain to be the second calendar year after 2024 above 1.5C. SUPPORTED. Copernicus's 6 Nov assessment (reported by several outlets) says "virtually certain". The WMO page does not mention Copernicus, so this comes from Copernicus itself.
7. Ocean temperatures kept rising from 2025's all-time highs. SUPPORTED in substance. WMO says sea-surface temperatures set monthly records in 2026 and 2026 is on track for a tenth successive year of record ocean warmth.
8. Global mean sea level at a record. SUPPORTED. WMO says sea level is at its highest on record (satellite record since 1993).
9. Quote from WMO's John Kennedy: "Every indicator here is telling the same story." NOT CONFIRMED. The WMO release page quotes Guterres and WMO Secretary-General Celeste Saulo, not Kennedy. Kennedy (a WMO scientific officer) did speak at the March 2026 briefing, and he plausibly spoke at the COP31 briefing, but I found no source for this wording. The summary also gave his title as "Head of Climate Information", which I did not verify. Grade: not checkable here, because I could not retrieve the briefing transcript or the article text.
10. Report released ahead of UN climate talks in Turkey, 9-20 Nov. MOSTLY SUPPORTED. WMO says it was released on COP31's first day in Antalya, so "ahead of" is loose (it was the opening day). The 9-20 Nov dates were not confirmed on the WMO page.

## Materially misleading or missing context

- The data run through September only and exclude El Nino's later impact. The headline "one of four hottest" is accurate.
- Copernicus rates 2026 most likely the second-warmest year, with about a 20% chance of matching or beating 2024. Berkeley Earth is more bullish. The piece, as summarized, does not say which rank is most likely.
- Exceeding 1.5C in a single year is not a formal Paris breach, since the limit is judged on multi-year averages. If the article omits this, readers could infer a breach.
- The WMO figure is a provisional update. The final State of the Global Climate 2026 report is due in March 2027.

## Rating

Overall: 1 Pinocchio (on the 0-4 scale, "some shading", close to a clean 0). Every checkable numerical claim holds. The deductions are for possible omissions (the Paris-threshold nuance, and the second-warmest likelihood), and one quote I could not confirm. No evidence of a false claim.
