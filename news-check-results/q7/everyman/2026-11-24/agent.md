# Fact-check: Bridge Michigan, "Mega farms are booming in Michigan, drowning rivers in manure" (Kelly House, Nov 19, 2026)

Limits: WebFetch returned only a summary of the article, not verbatim text, so the claim list is built from that summary. Verification was one pass of web searches. No charts were produced.

## Claims and verdicts
| Claim (per article) | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Michigan has 272 regulated CAFOs | Roughly consistent | An Oct 2025 CRC analysis gives 271. ELPC figures give about 290. Counts differ by method. No current EGLE dataset was found. |
| Gratiot County has 24 CAFOs, the most per square mile in the state | Partly verified | A 2026 advocacy fact sheet also says 24. A 2020 Michigan Public piece citing EGLE said 27. Gratiot is second statewide in permitted land-application acreage, behind Huron. The per-square-mile ranking was not checked. |
| Since 2000 Gratiot's cattle herd more than doubled and hogs tripled | Not verified | The searches did not surface 2000 census figures. They did show 1997 hogs of about 19,325 and 2017 hogs of 46,355, which is a rise of roughly 2.4x. The 2017 cattle figure is 54,333. The 2022 profile snippet was internally inconsistent (46,765 cattle and 19,657 hogs). Direction is plausible. Exact multiples need the 2000 and 2022 NASS tables. |
| About 420 million gallons of waste a year in Gratiot | Not checkable here | Likely derived from permit annual reports. Not found. |
| The 272 largest CAFOs produced 1.1 million solid tons and 3.93 billion liquid gallons in 2024 | Not checkable here | Not found in public sources. |
| About three-quarters of Michigan rivers and streams exceed standards for human and animal waste | Not verified | The EGLE Integrated Report was not checked. |
| E. coli up to 10 times the safe-contact limit after rain (Alma College professor Murray Borrello); quote "The watershed is dying" | Not checkable here | It is a researcher's finding and a quote. No primary source was found. |
| The 2020 permit update was sued over by the Farm Bureau and industry | Verified | Michigan Supreme Court 2024 sided with EGLE. The EGLE director issued an Oct 29, 2025 opinion. The intervenors include the Farm Bureau. |
| Both sides are appealing a June 2026 ruling | Partly verified | An ELPC-hosted application for leave to appeal dated June 29, 2026 exists. The ruling's contents and the posture of both appeals were not confirmed. |
| A new 3,450-head CAFO approved in July, adding up to 34 million gallons | Partly verified | The New Lede (Aug 2026) reported that regulators approved a dairy CAFO in a pollution hotspot. The size and volume figures were not confirmed. |
| Industry declined comment; its position is that manure is beneficial and septic systems also pollute | Not checkable here | Journalistic report. |

## Overall rating
1 Pinocchio (mostly accurate). The checkable facts line up with public records, with small count discrepancies (24 versus 27 for Gratiot, 272 versus 271 to 290 statewide). Several headline numbers could not be confirmed.

## Potentially misleading
- The headline "drowning rivers in manure" is figurative.
- Attributing the pollution to CAFOs is advocacy-sourced. Septic systems and other sources also contribute, and the article notes this.
- The 10x E. coli spike comes from creeks near application fields after rain, so it should not be read as typical conditions.
- CAFO counts vary by source and year.
