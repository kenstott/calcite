**The Better Markets press release's claims about FDIC merger rules, community bank asset share and bank lending are accurate; 2 claims could not be confirmed.**

Rating: **0 Pinocchios** on the checkable numbers, with framing caveats below.

Source: https://bettermarkets.org/newsroom/fdic-merger-rules-threaten-survival-of-community-banks-and-the-main-street-economy/ (Nov 23, 2026; fetched successfully). I also read the linked comment letter (PDF), the "impact" page and the May 2026 analysis.

## Claims and grades
1. **Community banks' share of banking assets fell from 45% in the early 1990s to 15% last year (central claim): true.** I recomputed from banking.financials with the group's own thresholds (community under $10B): 14.4% at 2025-12-31, 13.4% at 2026-06-30. The letter itself says 13% this year. The 1992 base (45%) is not in the warehouse, which starts in 2010 (21.6% at 2010-12). It rests on the group's own FDIC BankFind analysis.
2. **Large banks' share rose from under 20% to over 70%: true.** The warehouse gives 69.9% at 2025-12-31 and 71.8% at 2026-06-30 for banks over $100B. The letter says 72% now. The 1992 base is unverified.
3. **The proposal has timelines that trigger automatic approvals and application acceptance: true.** Law-firm summaries (Skadden, Reed Smith) confirm deemed-approval windows. They give as few as 5 business days for de minimis deals and 90 or 150 days for standard ones, and an application is deemed substantially complete on a clock. "Absurd" and "unreasonable" are characterization. "Would make the dynamic much worse" is a prediction.
4. **"Can only serve one purpose": not checkable.** It is a claim about motive and exclusivity.
5. **Big banks do the least lending to Main Street, and the rules favor them: unsupported.** This rests on Better Markets' own analysis (large banks use 40-43% of deposits for real-economy lending, community banks about 75%) and its own definitions. The warehouse has no loan-by-size series, and I found no independent replication.
6. **Headline, "FDIC rules threaten survival of community banks": unsupported.** No estimate of the proposal's effect was found. About 4,000 community banks remain (4,036 under $10B in the 2026-09-30 snapshot).
7. **The review process has "long had serious deficiencies" and is becoming a "rubber stamp": opinion, not checkable.**

## Flags
- Causal overreach. The letter ties the 45% to 13% decline to the 1994 Riegle-Neal Act and decades of growth by large banks. The press release implies the new proposal threatens survival, but the proposal's marginal effect is never quantified.
- Inconsistent vintages. The release says "15 percent last year" and "over 70", while the letter says 13% and 72% "this year". The warehouse says 14.4% and 69.9% at end-2025.
- The thresholds are the group's own ($10B and $100B, adjusted for inflation back in time), and the release does not say so.
- The letter's FDIC staff cut (about 1,300 positions, about 20%) matches Bloomberg Law reporting of the 2026 plan, not an official FDIC figure. It is not in the press release itself.
- The piece is an advocacy statement with no FDIC response.

Not verified: all early-1990s shares (45% and under 20%). The warehouse starts in 2010.
