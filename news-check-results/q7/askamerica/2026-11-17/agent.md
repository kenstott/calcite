The AAF October 2026 Debt and Deficit Progress Report's claims about the FY2027 first-month deficit, revenue, spending and debt are balanced and accurate.

**Rating: 0 Pinocchios.**

Every headline number reconciles to Treasury's own data (MTS Tables 1, 5, 9; Debt to the Penny; MSPD):
- Deficit $404.1B vs $284.3B (+$119.7B, +42%); outlays $773.0B (+$84.3B, +12.2%); receipts $368.9B (-$35.4B, -8.75%).
- Revenue lines: individual income tax -$30.6B (-14.1%); corporate +$3.0B (+19.7%); customs -$12.1B (-38.7%); estate/gift and excise flat; other +18%.
- Spending lines: Social Security +$8.5B (6.4%); Medicare +$19.5B (12.9%); Medicaid +$7B (about 12.5% on an Oct 2025 base of $55.8B); defense +$28.9B (28.9%); veterans +$4.6B (9.2%); income security -$3.7B (-5.9%); other +$8.9B (+67%); net interest +$12.2B (+13.4%), fourth-largest outlay (Medicare 170.0, Social Security 142.7, defense 128.9, interest 103.1).
- Debt held by the public $32.738T vs $30.571T (+$2.17T); up 1.93x since Oct 2019 ("nearly doubled" is correct).

**Flags (not enough to move the rating):**
- Customs framing: "declined since May" is loose. Net customs were about $22B a month in March-April, about zero in May, negative in June (-$25.6B) and July (-$8.5B) as IEEPA-related refunds exceeded collections, then recovered to $19.2B in October. The October drop is a year-over-year comparison, not a continuing slide, and the piece does not quantify refunds versus replacement tariffs (Treasury does not split them). The Supreme Court ruling (Feb 20, 2026, 6-3) and May refund onset are confirmed by outside sources.
- Payroll tax "+$4B (3%)": Treasury shows employment receipts +$4.8B (3.9%), all social insurance +$3.2B (2.5%).
- Causal explanations (enrollment, per-enrollee costs, OBBBA reforms, military personnel and R&D) are the author's attributions; the data confirm direction and size, not cause.
- Typos: "$404 deficit", "lover revenue". Omitted context: CBO's earlier estimate was $409B; a one-month 42% jump is not a trend.
- "The National Debt Is Unsustainable" and the projection of further rises are opinion and projection, not graded.
- The four chart PNGs were fetched but contain no extractable text; I verified the underlying numbers directly from Treasury data. The piece contains no quotes.
