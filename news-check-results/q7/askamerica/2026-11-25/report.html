The KFF Health News article's claims about single-payer costs, the Oregon universal health plan and Medicare for All are somewhat biased and mostly accurate; 1 claim could not be confirmed.

Warehouse: CMS NHE (health.nhe_summary, 2024 release). Primary documents: CMS NHE fact sheet, White House CEA report (Oct 2026), Oregon UHPGB board deck (Oct 29, 2026), Milliman report (Apr 3, 2026).

Article under reviewhttps://kffhealthnews.org/health-industry/universal-healthcare-single-payer-medicare-for-all-costs-oregon-midterm-election/

Summary

Most numbers in the piece match primary documents: 2024 national health spending of about $5.3 trillion (warehouse: $5,278.6B), the CEA's $47.4 trillion ten-year federal cost for Medicare for All, and the Oregon board's $69.7B 2032 figure. The weak spots are (1) the Milliman attribution: $76.3B is the board's adjusted low status-quo figure with Medicare Part B added, while Milliman's own best estimate is $78.3B ($81.8B with Part B); (2) the board's $69.7B is its most favorable scenario (3% administrative cost), and Milliman's best estimate shows the plan only about $0.6B cheaper than status quo, which the article omits; (3) 'struggled to garner even a quarter' is wrong for California's Prop 186 (26.6% yes); (4) the 'up to 3 of 5 pay less', Starr '43%' and Taiwan '90%' figures could not be confirmed. Key checks

  • NHE $5.3T in 2024: true; CMS fact sheet and health.nhe_summary ($5,278.6B, 18.0% of GDP).
  • $72T over 2025-2034: consistent. CMS projects 5.4% annual growth; compounding 2024 spending gives about $71T. I did not read CMS's projection table.
  • CEA $47T over 10 years: true. The CEA report (2027-2036, constant 2026 dollars) lists Medicare for All at -47.41 trillion. The '$4.7 trillion annual' is that figure divided by ten, not a figure the report states.
  • Oregon $69.7B in 2032: matches the board deck (UHP $66.2B plus $3.5B Part B); the board's column, not Milliman's.
  • Milliman 'at least $76.3B': misattributed. Milliman status quo: $78.3B best, $72.8B low (before Part B).
  • Ballot measures under a quarter: California Prop 186 (1994) got 26.6% yes; Oregon's lost about 4 to 1; Massachusetts not found.
  • El-Sayed Senate win: confirmed.
  • 'Up to 3 of 5 pay less': board says 'most households'; no 3-in-5 figure found. Starr '43%': only the article itself states it; the X post and Starr page did not load. Taiwan '90%': Health Affairs returned 403.
  • Typo: 'Single-player ballot measures'.

Materially misleading framing

The cost comparison sets a federal ten-year add-on (CEA, 2027-2036) against total national spending from all payers (2025-2034) and calls it a possible bargain; the article does acknowledge NHE includes military, IHS and elective services. It presents the board's most optimistic Oregon number without noting Milliman's best estimate shows little savings.
