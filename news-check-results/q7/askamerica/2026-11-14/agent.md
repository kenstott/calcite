Method: the connector fetched the article. The warehouse has no global-temperature, WMO or global sea-level table (only US NOAA station summaries and US-LME SST for 2026), so claims were graded against the WMO press release (wmo.int/node/39188, fetched) and Copernicus coverage (search only).

Claims
- One of four hottest years on record (central): TRUE. WMO key message, an estimate that matches.
- Jan-Sep 2026 was 1.47C above pre-industrial: TRUE (1.47 +/- 0.13C vs 1850-1900).
- Nine datasets; past 12 years among the 12 warmest: TRUE. WMO uses nine datasets, says the past 12 years were individually the warmest, and 2023-2026 are the top four.
- Driven by a powerful El Niño: MOSTLY TRUE, and the one framing issue. WMO says La Niña cooled early 2026 and El Niño only intensified from August. It is expected to be the strongest on record by December and will raise the year-end figure by roughly 0.1-0.2C. The Jan-Sep figure does not reflect it.
- COP31 in Turkey, 9-20 November: TRUE. The WMO release says COP31 opened 9 November in Antalya. I did not separately confirm the 20 November end date.
- Copernicus: 2026 all but certain to be the second year above 1.5C after 2024: TRUE. Copernicus says "virtually certain" (>99%), per Argus and other outlets.
- Ocean temperatures still rising from 2025 highs: TRUE. WMO reports record ocean heat and monthly SST records.
- Record global mean sea level: TRUE.
- John Kennedy quote: UNSUPPORTED. The substance matches WMO findings, but I could not locate the verbatim quote. The WMO release quotes Guterres and Saulo, not Kennedy.

Misleading-context flags
- The lead attributes the warm year to El Niño, but the Jan-Sep average was largely La Niña and neutral-phase warming. Annual comparisons, such as 2024's 1.55C record, exceed 2026's Jan-Sep figure.
- Cyprus Mail is a Reuters wire reprint.
- Independent estimates of 2026 reaching a record differ: Copernicus about 20%, Carbon Brief 67%, Berkeley Earth 95%. The article makes no record claim.
