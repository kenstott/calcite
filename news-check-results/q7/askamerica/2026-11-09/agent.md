# The Radio Oklahoma News article's claims about the PSO rate increase, interim charges and the data center tariff are biased and partly false.

**Rating: 2 Pinocchios.** The core facts are right. The article presents an interim, unconfirmed figure as a permanent, finalized one.

Source: https://www.radiooklahomanews.com/post/pso-rate-increase-approved-for-december-2026-in-oklahoma (fetched successfully).

**Warehouse check:** search_catalog found only EIA state-level retail price tables (energy.eia_electricity_prices and its monthly version). No table carries utility rate-case orders or Oklahoma Corporation Commission (OCC) votes. Claims were therefore checked against independent reporting: OKC Fox/Tulsa's News Pulse (updated Nov 9), the Tulsa Flyer, KGOU, and Oklahoma Energy Today. I did not open OCC Order No. 761871 itself.

| # | Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | OCC voted 2-1 on Nov 5 to approve a "permanent 3.55% residential increase" | **Partially false.** The 2-1 vote on Nov 5 is confirmed (Hiett and Bingman yes, Chair David no). But the order is formally an "Interim Order", so "permanent" is wrong. The 3.55% is only what PSO told OCC's Public Utility Division. OCC staff have not yet confirmed it. |
| 2 | An interim charge of about $11 per month since July 1, 2026 | **True** (Tulsa Flyer, KGOU). |
| 3 | Residential customers get no refund; some industrial customers get credits | **Mostly true.** PSO and Chair David say so, and the order requires refunds with interest within 90 days. An OCC spokesperson said it could not yet confirm eligibility. |
| 4 | PSO has six days to submit revised tariffs and calculations | **Mostly true.** The order sets six calendar days for updated accounting and a cost-allocation study. Revised tariffs follow after an objection period. |
| 5 | Increase appears in the December billing cycle | **Mostly true.** This is PSO's expectation. The order applies new rates from the first billing cycle after tariffs are approved. |
| 6 | A new large-load tariff makes big customers pay for the infrastructure they require | **Mostly true, overstated.** The Tulsa Flyer reports all three commissioners approved a large-load class (over 75 MW). Its regulation, including bring-your-own-generation, needs more study. Other coverage says PSO's proposed tariff will be considered in 2027. |
| 7 | The tariff follows Oklahoma's Data Center Customer Ratepayer Protection Act | **Not checkable here.** The oksenate.gov link returned HTTP 307. The Tulsa Flyer reports a 2026 law requiring separate terms for data centers, which is consistent with the claim. |
| 8 | "The percentage increase has been finalized" (dollar impact not yet released) | **Mostly false** on the first half. The percentage is not finalized. The dollar-impact half is true. |

## Materially misleading even where facts check out

- **Interim vs. permanent.** The article calls the order "permanent" and "finalized". Both OKC Fox and the order's title say Interim Order.
- **"Increase" framing.** Chair David said the 3.55% is not on top of the interim rates, so customers pay "just a little bit more" than now. OCC cautioned customers not to read it as another 3.55% on current bills.
- **Omissions.** The article leaves out Chair David's dissent. It also omits that the order hits residents harder than the roughly 1% settlement that PSO, the Attorney General, AARP and OCC's Public Utility Division backed. PSO says it may challenge the order, and it expects another rate case.
- **Data center protections.** The piece presents them as settled when key terms remain open.
