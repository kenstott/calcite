The Chalkbeat Colorado article's claims about the HEA Group college wage premium, Colorado's rank and the do-no-harm rule are balanced and accurate; 3 claims could not be confirmed.

**Rating: 0 Pinocchios.**

**Central claim, confirmed.** Colorado's college wage premium is 55%, which ranks 49 of 50 states, so "second lowest" is right. The source for this is Inside Higher Ed's coverage of the HEA Group release (Oct 28, 2026), and Deseret News also reports Colorado at 55%. The national premium of 76% is also confirmed.

**Dollar figures, confirmed with small wording errors.**
- Inside Higher Ed gives Colorado completers a median of $60,800 against $39,200 for Colorado high school graduates. The article rounds these to about $61,000 and $39,000.
- The $34,808 national high school figure is in HEA's own FAQ (primary document).
- The "closer to $26,000" national gap checks out: $34,808 x 1.76 is about $26,450.
- Colorado high school pay is above the national figure. HEA puts it at $39,174 against $34,808, and the warehouse (Census ACS 2023, 25 and older) has Colorado at $43,437, Alaska $42,584, Utah $41,486 and Wyoming $41,135. The ACS table uses a different population from HEA's 25-34 benchmark, so it is context, not a check.

**Do-no-harm rule, confirmed.** The two-of-three-consecutive-years loan test is confirmed by NPR, Deseret and Inside Higher Ed. NPR quotes the Education Department fact sheet saying programs could be designated low-earning beginning in the 2028-2029 award year. The ed.gov pages themselves returned HTTP 403.

**Quotes.**
- Gianneschi's account of Colorado Mountain College cutting part of its outdoor education program is mostly true. CMC's Nov 17 release says the cuts followed a review of student demand, industry and community needs. It does not say the programs failed to benefit students.
- The Colorado Department of Higher Education spokesperson's statement that it has restricted no programs over low earnings is unsupported. The agency page returned 403 and I found no independent record.
- Hultquist's and Nicholas's remarks are opinions, not checkable here.

**Not confirmed, graded unsupported.** The HEA interactive does not render for automated fetches, so these could not be read.
- The "11% ... almost double the national average" figure.
- The "about a third cosmetology" figure. IHE does confirm that cosmetology credentials earn less than high school graduates in every state except Minnesota.
- The bachelor's, associate and certificate state-versus-national figures.

**Misleading or imprecise (reservations that do not change the rating).**
- The article says "on average in the four years after graduating." The figures are medians measured in the fourth year after completion, not averages over four years.
- The cohort is 2017-18 and 2018-19 completers, not only 2017-18.
- HEA's FAQ says the Department of Education recalculated the high school benchmark in October 2026 (national $35,678), so the article uses the earlier vintage.
- The low premium is mostly a high-denominator effect. Colorado completers earn about the national level, but Colorado high school graduates earn about 12.5% more than the national benchmark. The article says this, but the headline implies college is the weak link.
- HEA states the premium is not causal. The data cover only federal aid recipients, ignore tuition and debt, and count graduates who leave the state.

**Warehouse coverage.** The warehouse has no HEA Group aggregates. I searched the catalog for the measure and found census.acs_earnings_by_education and edu.college_scorecard_programs, but the HEA figures are not in them.
