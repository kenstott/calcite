What the data show

Colorado completers earn about the national level (the national figure implied by 76% on $34,808 is about $61,300), so the low premium is mainly a denominator effect: Colorado high school earners make about 12.5% more than the national benchmark. The article says this, but the headline implies college is the weak link. The warehouse (census.acs_earnings_by_education, 2023) puts Colorado high school graduates (25+) at a median $43,437 against Alaska $42,584, Utah $41,486 and Wyoming $41,135. That table uses a different population from HEA's 25-34 benchmark, so it is context, not a check of HEA's figures.

Caveats the piece conveys only partly: earnings cover federal aid recipients who graduated in 2017-18 and 2018-19; out-of-state movers are counted; no tuition or debt; HEA says the premium is not causal. HEA's FAQ also says the Department of Education recalculated the high school benchmark in October 2026 (national $35,678), so the article uses the earlier vintage.

Not verified: 'about 11% ... almost double the national average', 'about a third cosmetology', and the bachelor's/associate/certificate state-versus-national figures; the HEA interactive does not render for automated fetches. IHE does confirm cosmetology credentials trail high school pay in every state but Minnesota.
