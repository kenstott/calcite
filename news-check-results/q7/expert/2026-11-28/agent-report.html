Chalkbeat's Colorado college-payoff story: mostly accurate, 1 Pinocchio

Summary of findings

The headline claim checks out: Colorado completers earn about 55% more than high school graduates, confirmed by Deseret News coverage of the same HEA Group report. Grade: 1 Pinocchio (minor omissions, no falsehood found).

  • Verified: Colorado 55% premium (Deseret lists CO 55%, Utah 100%, Delaware 102%, Alaska 28%). $61k vs $39k implies 56%, consistent.
  • Verified: the do-no-harm earnings test (two of three consecutive years, benchmark of state HS graduates, earnings four years out, loss of loan access from 2028-29) matches secondary sources on the 2025 law.
  • Discrepancy: the article says 2017-18 graduates; Deseret says 2017-18 and 2018-19 cohorts.
  • Second lowest nationally: Alaska at 28% is lowest and Colorado at 55% is below Wyoming at 60%, which is consistent, but I could not see the full 50-state table.
  • Not checkable here (HEA report table not retrievable): national 76% average, $26,000 national gap, 11% below-HS share, cosmetology one-third, credential-level earnings, and all attributed quotes (Itzkowitz, Hultquist, Nicholas, Gianneschi, CDHE). Also, the article text came via a summarizing fetch tool, not verbatim.
  • Misleading risk: the premium compares to a state HS benchmark and is not adjusted for cost of living, field or composition; Colorado's high-earning HS baseline and in-migration of degree holders lower the ratio without implying weak colleges. Certificates fall outside the statute's scope per the Senate HELP FAQ, so the 11% figure may not map to the rule. Ecological, not causal.
