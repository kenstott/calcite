# Fact-check: OPB/NPR "Democrats' House win was the largest in 50 years" (2026-11-19)

## Summary: mostly supported, 1 Pinocchio; headline depends on the comparison window
Only a model-summarized copy of the article was readable; no verbatim quotes could be extracted, and the Clerk of the House and AP result tables could not be fetched. Final vote and seat totals therefore rest on NPR and secondary reports.

| Claim | Verdict | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| D ~56% vs R ~42% House vote, 14-pt spread | Not independently confirmed; consistent | NPR; a Newsweek data page gives a two-party split of 56.9/43.1. |
| Biggest win for either party in 50 years | Misleading-risk | By memory (unverified here), 1974 and 1976 House margins were larger than 14 pts. The claim holds only for post-1976 comparisons. |
| 2006, 2010, 2018 under 10 pts; 2008 near 11 | Plausible from memory | Not re-fetched. |
| 46 R seats flipped, possibly 49; 3 races uncalled | Not checkable | No final tally reachable. Early reports: 228-139 on Nov 4. |
| Second most flips this century, behind 2010 | Plausible | Memory only. |
| 260 seats would be the most since 1991-93 | Plausible | NYT via Boston25: 2008 peak was 257. |
| Control changed five times in 20 years | Plausible | 2006, 2010, 2018, 2022, 2026. |
| Republicans back to pre-1994 position | Plausible | Not verified. |

Confidence: low to medium. Caveat: 56/42 are raw shares, with third-party and uncontested seats distorting the margin.
