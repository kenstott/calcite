WOWT/AP ground-stop story: 1 Pinocchio

Summary of findings: mostly accurate (1 Pinocchio)

  • Duration, history (first since Jan 2023), copper-to-fiber contractor cause, $12.5B and the September fiber cut check out.
  • Saturday cancellations (4,100+) not reconciled with Wikipedia's 2,500+; funding ask ($30B) vs later $50B; the football game and Bedford quote not checkable.
  • Cause is still under investigation; AP wording is provisional.

Grade: 1 Pinocchio (mostly accurate; a few figures unverified or differing across sources).

Note: WebFetch returned a summary only, not verbatim text, so quotes were checked as paraphrased by the summary. Primary FAA/DOT releases were not found; sources are Wikipedia, NPR, and secondary outlets.

Claims

1. Ground stop lasted more than four hours. Answer: Supported. Key values: 15:54-20:15 ET Nov 13 = 4h21m (Wikipedia infobox; its lead says 3.5h, NPR says ~4.5h). Confidence: medium-high.

2. First nationwide ground stop since Jan 2023, second since 9/11. Answer: Supported (Wikipedia/search summaries; Snopes notes the 2023 "first since 9/11" framing came from a former official). Confidence: medium-high.

3. Cause: new tech overloaded old equipment; contractor replacing copper with fiber; Duffy blamed L3Harris. Answer: Substantially supported. Duffy said L3Harris was doing a copper-to-fiber switch and that contractor error vs faulty equipment was unclear; Bedford said the router explanation did not fully explain the root cause. Cause is still under investigation, so AP's causal wording is provisional. Confidence: medium.

4. 3,000+ airborne flights landed safely. Answer: Supported in number; Wikipedia attributes it to DHS Sec. Mullin, AP summary to Duffy (attribution unresolved). Confidence: medium.

5. $12.5B from Congress. Answer: Supported (2025 funding, Jalopnik/Reuters). Confidence: high.

6. ~70% of system replaced. Answer: Wikipedia cites Reuters saying the contractor had completed ~70% of the copper-to-fiber conversion; whether it describes "the system" generally is unclear. Confidence: medium.

7. Administration seeking $30B more. Answer: Differs: Duffy lobbied $30B on Nov 12; Wikipedia says Duffy and Mullin later requested $50B. AP figure may be stale. Confidence: medium.

8. FlightAware 4,100+ and Cirium 3,200+ Saturday cancellations. Answer: Not verified. Wikipedia reports 2,500+ additional Saturday cancellations; could be a different time snapshot. Direct FlightAware/Cirium data not accessible. Confidence: low.

9. Disruption ~a quarter of ~45,000 daily flights (consultant estimate). Answer: Not checkable as an estimate; Wikipedia gives ~3,500 flights barred from departing plus ~8,000 Friday cancellations (different measures, ~8% and ~18% of 45,000), so "a quarter" is plausible but unverified. Confidence: low.

10. Mississippi-Oklahoma football game pushed to Sunday. Answer: Not checkable here; no source found. Confidence: low.

11. Bedford: schedule back to normal by end of Sunday; "worst is behind us." Answer: Not checkable (no transcript found). Confidence: low.

12. September Northeast outage linked to construction crew cutting fiber. Answer: Supported: Sept 21, 2026 Philadelphia TRACON primary circuit failed and the Verizon backup fiber was cut by a construction crew (NJ Transit crew per Amtrak/Verizon; Duffy first blamed Amtrak). Confidence: high.

13. Passengers slept in airports; safety expert on controller-pilot comms. Answer: Plausible, not independently verified. Confidence: low.

Misleading-context flags

- Cause stated more firmly than the government's own investigation supports; Duffy's and Bedford's accounts differ.

- Funding ask may be outdated ($30B vs $50B); blaming Congress is a political attribution.

- Cancellation totals depend on snapshot time.
