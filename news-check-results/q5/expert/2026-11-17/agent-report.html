Boston Globe NH wildfire piece: 1 Pinocchio, accurate with a stale headline acreage and a loose "four times" claim

Summary of findings: mostly accurate, shaded on size and on the "four times" framing

The article earns 1 Pinocchio (some shading). Of 18 claims and quotes, 11 verified against primary or official sources, 3 verified but need context, 4 are not checkable here, and none was contradicted outright.

  • Drought figures are exact. The US Drought Monitor for NH (week of Nov 10) shows 93.40% abnormally dry or worse and 40.46% in moderate drought or worse; the Globe said "more than 90" and "about 40".
  • Fire size is shaded. The Globe says "nearly 1,000 acres." The official figure had been remapped to 856 (Nov 5) and stood at 817 on InciWeb on Nov 12, the day of publication. The 982 peak comes from a tracker, not a confirmed InciWeb entry I could see; the Union Leader reported 921.
  • "Four times over" is potentially misleading. The article says 127 other fires burned 180 acres against a 250-acre typical year. By itself that is 0.72 times normal, below average. The 4x result only appears when the Wild River Fire is added (4.0x at 817 acres, 4.6x at 982). The sentence does not say so. I could not verify the 127 fires or 180 acres.
  • Record claim holds, with a hedge removed. The Forest Service supervisor called it "unofficially" the largest fire in the forest's history; the Globe states it flatly. The previous record was reported as about 100 acres.
  • Staffing figure is date-correct but stale. 87 personnel was the Nov 11 count; InciWeb on Nov 12 lists 58.
  • Not checkable: the Matson quotes, the Shelburne selectboard quotes, the Guiterman quotes, and the 2026 year-to-date NH fire statistics. Only the Globe carries them. Matson's title is supported by NHPR, but the state bureau page still lists Steven Sherman.

What would change this: a state year-to-date report showing 127 fires and 180 acres would remove the main unverified number; a Globe correction of how "four times" was computed would move the rating to 0 to 1. If the state's 2026 total excludes mapping revisions, the ratio shifts slightly.

Step 2. Overall rating and misleading-framing check

Answer: 1 Pinocchio. Key values: 817 vs about 1,000 acres; 180/250 = 0.72x; (180+817)/250 = 4.0x. Sources: InciWeb, Globe text, NH DFL. Confidence: medium, because the 127/180 inputs are unverified. Reading the sentence "this year the number has outstripped that four times over" next to "180 acres" would leave a reader thinking routine fires are above normal; they appear to be below the average, and the exceedance is entirely the one fire. Also, the 2025 season had 157 fires and 146 acres, so 127 fires is lower, not higher, than last year.

Gaps and limits

The Boston Globe page returned a summary via the fetch tool rather than raw text; quotes were taken from that extraction and could not be compared character by character. The Union Leader page was blocked (403). The USDM query returned data only when the state FIPS code (33) was used. Territories were not considered.
