Mostly accurate, with unverified state-law details: 1 Pinocchio (provisional)

Summary of findings

The KFF Health News piece holds up on the figures that could be checked, but several state-law claims could not be confirmed, so the 1 Pinocchio grade is provisional.

  • Verified: Steward Health Care was ordered to pay $951 million by Utah's Third District Court (default judgment, 2019 birth at Jordan Valley Medical Center, Steward later bankrupt). Idaho lost 94 of 268 obstetric OB-GYNs (35%) Aug 2022-Dec 2024. Medicaid financed 41% of births in 2023 (KFF), consistent with "roughly 40%".
  • Discrepancy: the article reportedly gives the award as both $951 million and "nearly $1 billion" (loose but consistent). My search results did not mention Wyoming for the family; unconfirmed.
  • Not matched: "at least 146 hospitals" stopping deliveries since end of 2020 attributed to CHQPR. Published CHQPR counts I found were 124 and 139 rural hospitals, and vary by edition; 146 may be a later update or include non-rural hospitals. Not confirmed.
  • Not checkable here / contradicted by secondary sources: Arkansas five-year window (guides show two years in older law), Oklahoma limits, Utah asset protection, New Mexico punitive cap (HB 99 passed Senate; signing unconfirmed), Federal Medicaid cut timing, FTCA. These are 2025-26 changes that older guides would not show; I did not reach statutes.
  • Limitation: I could not read the article verbatim (the fetch tool returned a summary), so attributed quotes were not individually verified. Misleading-framing flag: the headline links malpractice risk to OB-GYN loss, but the piece itself notes abortion bans (Idaho) and Medicaid as drivers; causal weight of liability is unestablished.
