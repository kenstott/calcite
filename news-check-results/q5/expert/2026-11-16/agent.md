# Fact-check: Fortune, "colleges summer melt / yield / direct admissions" (2026-11-16)

**Rating: 1 Pinocchio (mostly accurate; some imprecision, many figures second-hand).**

## Verified
- Hanover survey: 1,000 US high schoolers 16-19, fielded July 2026; bachelor's interest 66% vs 79% (2025); associate 36% to 45%; 25% more interested in human-centered skills due to AI. Source: Hanover press release (highereddive.com/press-release/20261015-...). Confidence high.
- BLS: 62.8% of 2024 HS grads enrolled in college (Oct 2024), computed from BLS table hsgec.t01. The 2016 figure (70%) was not on the page I could fetch; recalled as about 69.7% but unverified. Medium.
- NSC preliminary fall 2026 undergraduate enrollment +0.6% (46% of institutions reporting as of Sept 24, 2026). High.
- NACUBO 2025-26 preliminary discount rate 57.1% (first-time full-time, private nonprofit); 2024-25 finalized 54.5% (preliminary had said 56.3%, so the rise is flattered by comparing preliminary to final). Medium-high.
- Whitman 10% Promise, fall 2027 entrants: confirmed on whitman.edu. Applies to tuition only. High.
- WICHE 3.9M peak (2025) to about 3.4M by 2041: consistent with WICHE Knocking at the College Door; not re-fetched. Medium.

## Discrepancies
- Common App: 234 institutions is the 2026-27 count (Common App blog, Aug 2026). 820,000 is the Innovations Guide figure for students served, tied to 215 institutions in the prior cycle. Fortune pairs 820,000 "offers in 2025-26" with 119 to 234, which blends two cycles. Offers vs students served wording also unconfirmed.
- Georgia State: no source found for 22% to 8%, $125,000 or $3.2M. Related published figures: 21.4% relative reduction in melt (RCT), melt 19% to about 9%. "22%" may be a relative reduction mistaken for a rate. Unverified, possible error.
- Yield 32%->22% public, 24%->18% private: NACAC figures I found differ (public 25%, private 33% in fall 2022; public 20.1%, private 24.2% 2023 per secondary source). Not matched; source is probably IPEDS-based Hechinger analysis. Not confirmed.

## Not checkable here
Whittier 17% melt (WSJ, paywalled/not found); Human Capital Education survey figures (10% multiple deposits, 6% not attending, 60% affordability, 51% cost) and Colavita/Anselment quotes; DePaul $15.5M/$372M (FY26 base $355.6M + 15.5 = 371.1, rounding plausible); Hechinger attributions.

## Misleading context
Hanover interest is stated interest, not enrollment; NSC enrollment is still rising, so "falling interest" and "up 0.6%" coexist. Vendor-sourced melt results (Georgia State, Human Capital) have commercial interest. Preliminary-vs-final NACUBO comparison.

## Sources

Caveat: a WebFetch summarizer and WebSearch snippets were the only access; the Fortune text was a summary, not verbatim.
