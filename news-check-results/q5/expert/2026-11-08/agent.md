## Fact-check: OPB "Fewer fires, record damage: Oregon's 2026 wildfire season by the numbers" (Oct 24, 2026)

**Method and limits.** WebFetch returned only a summary of the article, not verbatim text. Quotes are therefore checked only as summarized. I could not access ODF's or NWCC's primary data tables. The acreage figures come from secondary reporting of NWCC and ODF data. The Axios Oct 28 piece returned HTTP 403, and I found no final NWCC season report.

**Overall grade: 2 Pinocchios (mixed). Provisional, because of the acreage question.** Most figures match other reporting: costs, the funding actions, the home counts and the snowpack. The headline record-acreage claim is the weak point. The article's 2.1M acres and "13% more than the 2024 record of 1.9M" do not match any other source I found. They are also internally inconsistent: 2.1/1.9 is about 10.5%, not 13%. I could not tell whether the error is OPB's or my fetch summary's.

**Materially misleading or at-risk framing**
1. The headline "Fewer fires, record damage" rests on a record-acreage figure that conflicts with mid-season NWCC counts of 2.3M to 2.5M. A "more than 2.1M" figure published in October looks like a regression from numbers OPB itself reported in August and September. The article should say which agency and which definition it uses (ODF-protected acres vs. all-jurisdiction NWCC).
2. A 2024 baseline of "1.9M" does not match the 2.08M or the roughly 1.8M reported elsewhere.
3. "Fewer fires" is a count comparison against a 2022-onward average. A count of fires says nothing about size, because a small number of very large complexes drove the acreage: the Rowe Creek Complex at about 368,000 acres and the Hay Creek Complex at more than 200,000 acres.
4. Name and quote claims could not be verified, so my grade should be read as provisional on those.

**What would change the grade.** If the final NWCC or ODF report confirms 2.1M under a stated definition, the grade drops to 1 Pinocchio. If the final total is 2.3M or higher, or the 2024 baseline is wrong, it rises toward 2.
