# Fact-check: Climate Central "Billion-dollar US wildfires" (Nov 2026)

**Rating: 1 Pinocchio.** Headline figures (31 wildfires, ~$186B, 373 deaths, 79,500 structures) come only from Climate Central's non-public database; not reproducible. Everything checkable is consistent or plausible.

## Answer per step
- **Context (NOAA discontinued database May 2025; Adam Smith led it; relaunch 403 events/$2.9T through 2024).** Key values: 1980-2024. Sources: NPR, Fox Weather, Insurance Journal. Confidence: high, multiple outlets agree.
- **California share 23/31 = 74%; $167B/$186B = ~90%.** Arithmetic correct. Source: article. Confidence: high for arithmetic, low for inputs.
- **"At least 28x more than any other state."** 167/6 = 27.8x; borderline. Lahaina estimates: $3.2B insured (Karen Clark), $5.52B rebuild (FEMA), $14-16B economic (AccuWeather). Confidence: medium.
- **453 events / $3.2T since 1980.** Consistent with 403/$2.9T plus ~50 events; not reproducible. Confidence: medium.
- **5x frequency / 10x cost growth, October peak, top-5 costliest all California, 17,200 deaths, "2020 top three year".** Not checkable here; the 2020 claim looks off and found no support. Confidence: low.

## Misleading risks
Splitting annual wildfire totals into individual events changes the series, so frequency growth is partly methodological. Direct modeled costs only; 2026 preliminary; author built the database (non-independent). No third-party quotes found.

## Limits
WebFetch returned a summary rather than verbatim text; no database download; Cal Fire/NIFC not checked directly. Territories excluded.
