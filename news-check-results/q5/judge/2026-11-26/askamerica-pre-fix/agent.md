The White House ONDCP release's claims about overdose death trends, drug-class declines and credit to Trump administration policies are balanced and accurate.

Rating: 0 Pinocchios.

Source of checks: warehouse table health.cdc_state_drug_overdose_deaths (CDC VSRR, data as of Nov 1, 2026), plus the CDC VSRR page the release links.

Claims and verdicts
- 65,724 predicted deaths, 12 months ending June 2026: TRUE (warehouse predicted 65,724; reported count 64,433).
- 41.5% below the 112,418 peak (subtitle rounds to 42%): TRUE. The peak is the predicted value for the 12 months ending Aug 2023, and 65,724/112,418 - 1 = -41.5%.
- Lowest since December 2016: TRUE. Dec 2016 predicted was 64,557, Jan 2017 was 66,194, so 65,724 is the lowest since Dec 2016.
- Declined every month since January 2025 (18th consecutive month): TRUE. The predicted 12-month-ending series falls every month from Dec 2024 (81,313) to June 2026 (65,724).
- Drug-class declines (synthetic opioids 18.7%, natural/semi-synthetic 5.4%, heroin 34.8%, psychostimulants 14.1%, cocaine 6.3%): all TRUE, recomputed exactly from predicted June 2026 against June 2025.
- Carter quote crediting Trump policies (causal): UNSUPPORTED. The release offers no evidence for the cause.
- "Unequivocally winning the fight": opinion, not checkable. For scale, the 12-month count was about 53,000 in Jan 2016.

Context the release omits (not counted as inaccuracy)
- The peak was Aug 2023, under the prior administration, and the count had already fallen 28% to 81,313 by Dec 2024, before Trump took office.
- The decline is decelerating: about 25,000 fewer deaths in 2024, 10,000 in 2025, and 5,000 in the first half of 2026.
- The drug-class percentages are year-over-year changes in 12-month-ending counts, not changes since the peak, and the release does not say so.
- The headline figures are CDC predicted (adjusted) provisional estimates, which are not final data.

Exclusion disclosure: one query left out the total-deaths indicator so it could list drug-class rows. The total was queried separately, and the headline figures do not change.
