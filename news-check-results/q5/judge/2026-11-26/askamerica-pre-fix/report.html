The White House ONDCP release's claims about overdose death trends, drug-class declines and credit to Trump administration policies are balanced and accurate.

CDC VSRR provisional overdose deaths (health.cdc_state_drug_overdose_deaths), 12-month-ending through June 2026, data as of Nov 1, 2026

Article under reviewhttps://www.whitehouse.gov/releases/2026/11/u-s-drug-overdose-deaths-decrease-for-18th-consecutive-month-under-trump-administration/

Summary

Every number in the release matches CDC's provisional data (65,724 predicted; -41.5% from the 112,418 peak; 18 straight monthly declines; five drug-class changes exact). Rating: 0 Pinocchios on the facts. Context the release omits: the peak was Aug 2023 and the 12-month count had already fallen 28% to 81,313 by Dec 2024, before Trump took office; the pace of decline has slowed (about 25,000 in 2024, 10,000 in 2025, 5,000 in H1 2026). The causal quote is unsupported. The drug-class percentages are year-over-year, not changes since the peak, which the release does not say. The figures are predicted (adjusted) provisional estimates; the reported count is 64,433.
