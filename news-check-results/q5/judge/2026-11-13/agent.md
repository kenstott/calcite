## Assessment
- **Correct verdict: 2 Pinocchios.** Most individual facts check out, but the headline and lede overstate the position, and the body drops context that changes the picture.
  - **Class scope: misleading.** The article says relief would reach 330,000+ importers "whether or not they filed individual lawsuits" and lists no exclusions. DWT states the class excludes entities that separately sued, covers duties Feb 1, 2025 to Feb 19, 2026, and leaves out Brazil and India order duties. About 330,000 is the total importer count, "most" of whom are expected in the class.
  - **Stay omitted.** DWT reports Eaton's universal injunction is stayed for importers who have not yet sued, pending the government's partial appeal (filed June 3). The article mentions the appeal but not the stay.
  - **"One step closer" headline: overstated.** Certification is not a refund order. Classwide relief needs a further injunction, and the government is expected to appeal certification.
  - **$166B framing.** $166B is the total at stake, and more than $115B was reportedly disbursed by August. The article does report the $115B, so this is framing, not concealment. Askamerica matched the figure to MTS customs refunds of $116.8B for Apr-Aug.
  - **True or consistent:** SCOTUS ruling, Oct 8 certification, Eaton's March ruling and April order, June appeal, Wyden and Warren material. The DOJ quote appears in the filing, but askamerica found it limited to finally liquidated entries, so the article's version may drop a qualifier. I could not confirm that independently.
  - **Unverified by anyone:** Tuesday motion, Nov 13 and Dec 9 deadlines.
- **askamerica (1st):** the strongest findings, though the rating is one step light.
  - It alone caught the class exclusion of prior filers and that the 330,000 and $166B are total-duty estimates, not the pending class amount.
  - It matched $115B to Treasury MTS data and located the DOJ quote and its limitation.
  - It separated unconfirmed claims (deadlines) from false ones.
  - Weaknesses: 1 Pinocchio understates the misleading class-scope and headline problems it identified. It did not surface the stay for non-filers. Its summary line is garbled ("misleading on who the class covers are biased").
- **expert (2nd):** good on the stay and class exclusion, with careful confidence labels.
  - It found the exclusion of prior filers, the Feb 1, 2025 to Feb 19, 2026 period, that the universal injunction is stayed for non-filers, and that certification is not a refund order. It flagged the headline as overselling.
  - Weaknesses: it did not match the $115B to a primary source ($106.6B as of Aug 21 from secondary sources), did not find the DOJ quote, and said 330,000 was not found (DWT carries it). Its 1 Pinocchio is one step light for the headline problems it listed itself.
- **everyman (3rd):** least accurate on what matters.
  - It marked 330,000 and class certification verified but missed that the class excludes prior filers and missed the stay. It treated the "whether or not they sued" claim as only partly unverified.
  - It did frame certification as not a refund order and noted the $115B measure ambiguity, but its rating rests mainly on what it could not confirm. It did not find the DOJ quote.
