Data and method

Table used: econ.employment_statistics (BLS Current Population Survey / Current Employment Statistics, long-format, one row per series per period, seasonally adjusted). Two series were pulled:

  • LNS14000000 — civilian unemployment rate, 16+, seasonally adjusted (percent)
  • CES0000000001 — total nonfarm employment, seasonally adjusted (thousands)

Query 1 (unemployment rate, 2025-2026):

SELECT "year", "period", value FROM econ.employment_statistics WHERE series = 'LNS14000000' AND "year" IN (2025,2026) ORDER BY "year", "period"

Result, Sept 2025 through Aug 2026 (percent): 4.4, null (Oct 2025 — a known reporting gap, not a real value), 4.5, 4.4, 4.3, 4.4, 4.3, 4.3, 4.3, 4.2, 4.1, 4.1. The minimum of this trailing-12-month window is 4.1, reached only in July and August 2026 — so August's 4.1% is indeed tied for the low of the past year and unchanged month-over-month, matching "remained at a one-year low."

Query 2 (nonfarm payrolls, 2025-2026):

SELECT "year", "period", value FROM econ.employment_statistics WHERE series = 'CES0000000001' AND "year" IN (2025,2026) ORDER BY "year", "period"

July 2026 level = 158,913 thousand; August 2026 level = 159,075 thousand. Month-over-month change = +162 thousand, i.e. +162,000 jobs — an exact match to the article's reported figure.
