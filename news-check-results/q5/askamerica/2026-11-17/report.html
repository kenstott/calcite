The Boston Globe article's claims about the Wild River Fire, New Hampshire drought and wildfire acreage are biased and accurate; 2 claims could not be confirmed.

Article under reviewhttps://bostonglobe.com/2026/11/12/metro/nh-historic-wildfire-risk

Summary

The article is largely accurate. Rating: 0 Pinocchios. Every claim I could test against primary sources held up: the 982-acre peak, Nov. 1 start, largest fire in the White Mountain National Forest since 1918, the 1941 Marlow-Stoddard fire (24,000 acres), and the drought shares (warehouse: ~93% abnormally dry or worse, ~38% in moderate drought). Two figures could not be confirmed (Nov. 11 containment/personnel, and the 127-fire/180-acre/250-acre baseline). Framing caveats, which a stricter reader might weigh: the headline says firefighters 'fought back' a 'massive blaze', yet the fire was only about 53 to 60 percent contained and officials expect it to smolder until winter; the lede gives 'nearly 1,000 acres' although the NIFC perimeter is 817 acres (NHPR: 821); and the '4x typical year' statistic only holds if Wild River is included.

Findings

  • Acreage: InciWeb 11-04: 982 acres, 11% contained, 89 personnel, believed human-caused. NHPR 11-10: 821 acres, 53% contained. NIFC WFIGS perimeter in the warehouse: 817 acres. The article does say 'at one point reached 982'.
  • Largest since 1918: Forest Service via NHPR. True.
  • Marlow-Stoddard: Forest Society confirms 4 days, 24,000 acres, 1941. True.
  • Drought: weather.drought_monitor_weekly, NH, week of 2026-11-10, unweighted 10-county mean: no drought 7.2% (so ~92.8% abnormally dry or worse); D1 38.4%. Matches. Area-weighting and the 'northern part and Seacoast' detail not tested; droughtmonitor.unl.edu returned an empty page.
  • 127 fires / 180 acres / 250-acre average: not found in any opened source; no warehouse table carries NH state fire statistics. (180+982)/250 = 4.6x, or 4.0x with 817 acres, so 'four times' needs Wild River included. Unsupported.
  • 'Nearly 60 percent' and '87 people' on Nov. 11: trajectory (11, 20, 29, 53 percent) is consistent but no Nov. 11 primary update found. Unsupported.
  • Quotes: Matson is consistent with his Nov. 10 Executive Council remarks (NHPR). Evans and Guiterman quotes are first-hand and not independently testable. The Globe's linked tick article is a tick-avoidance guide and says nothing about fire reducing ticks; that claim is plausible ecology but not supported by the cited link.
  • Linked Globe July 31 drought piece: confirms long NH/Maine drought, consistent with 'underlying drought'.

Misleading framing

'Fought back' implies near resolution; Matson told the Council the fire will smolder until winter. The 'four times over' sentence follows a clause giving 180 acres elsewhere, which alone is below normal; Wild River accounts for the entire excess. The caption and lede give the peak, not the current mapped size.
