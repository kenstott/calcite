The Epoch Times article's claims about Realtor.com's October rent report, mortgage rates and buying versus renting are balanced and accurate; 1 claim could not be confirmed.

**Rating: 0 Pinocchios.**

The article text was fetched directly, with no 403.

**What was checked**
- Realtor.com October 2026 Rent Report (PR Newswire copy, fetched). The figures match exactly:
  - median asking rent of $1,692, up 0.1% ($1) year over year;
  - first year-over-year gain since June 2023;
  - 4.6% ($82) below the peak and 15% ($221) above the pre-pandemic level;
  - 2-bedroom $1,889 (+0.3%), 1-bedroom $1,566 (+0.1%), studio $1,411 (-1.2%);
  - Q3 multifamily completions 330,000 (-26%), under construction 674,000 (-2.7%), starts 403,000 (-9.2%).
- Freddie Mac 30-year rate, from the warehouse (econ.fred_indicators MORTGAGE30US):
  - 7.28% on Nov 19, up from 7.11% on Nov 12 (matches);
  - Oct 29 was 7.24% and Oct 22 was 7.32%.
- NAR pending home sales, from the NAR release and the Epoch Times article of Oct 20:
  - -4.7% month over month and -16.7% year over year (matches);
  - index of 63, a record low (confirmed by TheStreet/HousingWire coverage; the NAR page text did not show the level).
- Neighbors Bank study:
  - the 23-of-50 metros finding and Birmingham/Pittsburgh/Memphis at $625/$609/$597 were confirmed through a search summary of the study page. I did not find them in the fetched page text;
  - the 1,166-buyer survey, 2 in 5 citing equity, and 9% citing rent increases matched the page.
- The warehouse has no Realtor.com, NAR or Neighbors Bank tables, so those claims were graded against the primary releases.

**Could not be confirmed**
- "Chicago, Cleveland, and St. Louis would save more than $300 per month... Washington buyers would save only $11 monthly." These figures did not appear in the Neighbors Bank text I read, so I graded the claim unsupported.

**Framing caveats (accurate facts, loose framing)**
- "U.S. national median rent" is the Realtor.com median for studio/1BR/2BR listings in the 50 largest metros, not a national figure.
- The "first annual increase in more than three years" is +$1 (0.1%), effectively flat. The headline overstates a turn.
- "Highest level in four weeks" for the 7.28% rate is loose. The rate is above the prior three weeks but below the 7.32% of Oct 22, exactly four weeks earlier.
- The article places the construction boom in 2021-2022. The release cites 1.5 million units completed in 2023-2025.
