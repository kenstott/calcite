Claims by Neil Bradley (subject claims):
- "20 years of roughly 2% real GDP growth, after 60 years of more than 3%": TRUE. FRED GDPCA gives 3.46%/yr 1950-2005 and 2.04%/yr 2005-2025. The warehouse (econ.fred_indicators GDPC1, quarterly, from 1967) cross-checks 2005Q1 15,844.7 to 2025Q4 24,125.5.
- "A little-known record: 5.68 million new business starts last year": PARTIALLY FALSE (central). The claim says 5.68M new business starts; the evidence shows 5.67M (SA) to 5.70M (NSA) business applications, a record since 2004 (2024 NSA 5.23M, 2021 NSA 5.39M). Applications are IRS EIN requests, not businesses started, and high-propensity applications fell to about 1.67M in 2025 (1.84M in 2023, 1.71M in 2024). Fortune itself corrects this wording.

Claims by the reporter (author claims):
- Compound growth about 3.5% (1950-2005) and about 2% (2005-2025): TRUE (3.46% and 2.04%).
- 11 elections since 2006 with a control flip every time except 2012: TRUE. Democrats took both chambers Nov 3, 2026 (NPR/AP), so "less than two weeks" before Nov 16 is also TRUE.
- 2025 applications about 5.67M "before seasonal adjustment", the most since 2004: MOSTLY TRUE. 5.67M is the seasonally adjusted sum; the unadjusted sum is about 5.70M. The record holds.
- High-propensity applications about 1.69M in 2025, down from 1.86M in 2023, a second straight decline: MOSTLY TRUE. Current vintage after the Nov 12 revision is 1.67M and 1.84M; direction and streak hold.
- Trump's one-year 10% card cap, Sanders-Hawley bill, and Senate-only NDAA Section 815 buyback and dividend limits: TRUE.

Claims the reporter relays from sources:
- Chetty et al. (90% to 50%; more than 70% reversal): TRUE, per the paper abstract.

Omitted context: the 3%+ era coincided with faster labor-force growth, so the slowdown is not purely a policy outcome. Bradley's central "starts" claim is the only claim that overstates, and the article itself flags it.
