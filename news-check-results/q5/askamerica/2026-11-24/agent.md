The KFF Health News article's claims about malpractice limits, OB-GYN shortages and the $951 million Utah verdict are accurate; 1 claim could not be confirmed.

## Warehouse check
Catalog searches found no table for malpractice, OB-GYN counts, labor-and-delivery closures or payer of birth (the NCHS state tables carry no Medicaid share). Every claim was therefore checked against primary documents.

## Graded claims
- TRUE: At least four states enacted malpractice restrictions since 2025. This is the central claim. Arkansas Act 124, Utah HB 503, New Mexico HB 99 and Oklahoma SB 453 (a general noneconomic cap effective Sept. 1, 2025) all check out.
- TRUE: Arkansas now limits childbirth-injury claims to the minor's fifth birthday; other child claims run to the later of the 11th birthday or two years (Act 124 text).
- TRUE: New Mexico requires "clear and convincing" evidence for punitive damages and caps them (HB 99 section 41-5-7.1).
- TRUE: Utah shields physicians' personal assets (HB 503). Utah's noneconomic cap is $450,000 for causes of action since May 15, 2010, and no punitive cap was found.
- TRUE: Medicaid paid for 40.2% of 2024 births (CDC NVSS).
- TRUE: Idaho lost 94 of 268 OB-GYNs practicing obstetrics (35%, net), Aug 2022 to Dec 2024 (JAMA Network Open, read via the PMC copy). The study also cites recruiting problems, consolidation and no in-state residency.
- TRUE: Idaho voters rejected the abortion-rights measure 56-44; Nevada (64-36) and Virginia (62-38) approved theirs, and Missouri rejected the repeal measure. Virginia's amendment text bars penalizing or prosecuting people for exercising or assisting the right.
- TRUE: Steward sold the Utah hospital to CommonSpirit on May 1, 2023, as part of a five-hospital deal.
- TRUE: Judge Corum found Steward liable on Aug. 3, 2025 and awarded $951 million by default after Steward's counsel withdrew. Steward filed for bankruptcy in 2024 (Salt Lake Tribune).
- TRUE: A federally qualified health center is covered by the Federal Tort Claims Act (HRSA). The article's linked house.gov page is about House members, so the citation is wrong even though the claim holds.
- TRUE: Fewer than a third of Arkansas counties have a birthing hospital. Search results give 22 of 75, or 29%; the ACHI page itself did not show the figure.
- MOSTLY TRUE: "At least 146 hospitals" stopped or plan to stop delivering babies. The CHQPR report says 144 have stopped and 4 more announced stops by early 2027 (148 in total).
- MOSTLY TRUE: Idaho's law puts doctors at risk of prison for "lifesaving care." The ban carries 2-5 years in prison but has an exception for preventing the patient's death, and 2025 court rulings broadened protection. This is a compressed, contested characterization.
- UNSUPPORTED: The "nearly 12 hours" from fetal distress to cesarean, "according to court records." The Tribune says the C-section came more than a day after admission and that distress lasted "hours," which is consistent but does not state the figure.
- UNSUPPORTED: Zancanella's statement that bills exceeded $1 million in five years. It is her own account and no total was found.
- UNSUPPORTED: Rep. Pilkington's claim of only one Arkansas birth-injury lawsuit in 10 years. No count was found, and the article does not flag it as unverified.
- UNSUPPORTED: Doroshow's statement that verdicts are "rarely, if ever, paid fully." It is an advocate's general claim; the Steward case fits it but does not prove it.

## Misleading-framing flags
- The headline pairs OB-GYN loss with malpractice limits. The article's own evidence ties exits mainly to abortion bans, Medicaid rates and staffing, and it gives critics space, so the framing is balanced.
- "Utah's cap didn't stop her verdict": reports say the cap's reach over the default judgment was still disputed, so the $951 million may shrink. Nothing has been paid.
- Pilkington's unverified lawsuit count is the main justification offered for the Arkansas law.
- The Idaho sentence omits the life-of-the-patient exception and the 2025 rulings.

## Limits
Georgia Recorder, Stateline, the Commonwealth Fund scorecard and Fierce Healthcare returned HTTP 403. Stateline's result was confirmed through U.S. News and Fierce's through the Steward sale release. The Commonwealth Fund claim (Alabama, Arkansas and Idaho among the fewest maternity providers) was not verified and is not graded.
