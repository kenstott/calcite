The Patently-O article's claims about record weekly USPTO patent grants and issue-fee-to-grant wait times are too few to rate; 5 claims could not be confirmed.

**Verdict: 1 Pinocchio, provisional.** The article is a short, paywalled post (Dennis Crouch, Patently-O). Only the two-paragraph lede is public. Nothing I read contradicts it. Most of it could not be verified.

**Claims and grades**
1. "Before November, the record was 7,837 on October 3, 2023." **True.** Warehouse patents.patent_grants (PatentsView, 2010-2025): Oct 3, 2023 is the largest week, with 7,874 utility patents (7,891 across all types). The 0.5% gap with the USPTO-basis 7,837 is a counting-basis difference. No other week 2010-2025 is higher (next: 7,688 on Oct 24, 2023).
2. "The USPTO issued 9,828 utility patents on November 3, 2026 -- the largest weekly issue in records back to 1976." (Central claim.) **Unsupported.** The warehouse ends in 2025. The USPTO Official Gazette lists a Nov 3, 2026 issue, but the page I fetched shows no counts. A web search found no confirming source. The figure is about 25% above the 2023 record. That is a big jump, though consistent with the author's backlog thesis. The pre-2010 record was only checked against the warehouse's 2010-2025 range.
3. "The three issues since then (Nov 10, 17, 24) rank second through fourth." **Unsupported.** No 2026 weekly counts were found anywhere.
4. "The wait from issue-fee payment to grant had doubled earlier this year and is now 49 days for the Nov 24 issue." **Unsupported.** This is the author's own computation, and no public series exists.
5. Causal: "due to a surge in fee filing earlier in the year." **Unsupported.** The evidence is behind the paywall.
6. Causal: "These high numbers are really a catch-up on the part of the patent office." **Unsupported.** The interpretation is plausible, since a doubled fee-to-grant wait followed by a record issue is internally coherent. It cannot be tested here.

**Materially misleading?** No. The one checkable fact is right, and the piece is internally coherent. Caveats: the record comparison mixes counting bases (USPTO versus PatentsView), the methodology and data sources sit behind the paywall, and nobody outside the paywall can audit the 2026 numbers.

**Gaps:** The article text beyond the lede was not read. The six linked pages were fetched. They are paywall, membership and boilerplate pages with no bearing on the claims.
