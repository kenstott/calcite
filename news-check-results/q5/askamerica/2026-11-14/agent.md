The Climate Central article's claims about billion-dollar U.S. wildfires, California's share, and the revised disaster count are balanced and mostly accurate; 7 claims could not be confirmed.

**Rating: 1 Pinocchio.**

Source: https://www.climatecentral.org/climate-matters/billion-dollar-us-wildfires (fetched successfully, Nov 11, 2026 Climate Matters brief).

**Warehouse check.** search_catalog found no table with per-event wildfire cost, deaths or structures lost. disasters.wildfire_perimeters has burned acres for 2020 onward and cannot test any of the claims. This is a data gap, not a finding. Cost figures therefore rest on Climate Central's own database, which is the originating source rather than independent corroboration. NOAA stopped its series in 2025.

**Verified true**
- Headline totals are consistent: 5+26=31 events and $15.6B+$170B=$185.6B. Frequency rose 5.2x and cost 10.9x. California has 23 of 31 events (74.2%).
- 453 total disasters, $3.2T and 17,200 deaths match the Climate Central database page. The 2023, 2020 and 2025 year costs ($100.86B, $125.97B, $118.48B) match the database page's cumulative series.
- Adam Smith led the NOAA program for 15 years, and NOAA cut it in 2025. NBC News (Oct 22, 2025) confirms Climate Central's first analysis from the redeveloped database was published in October 2025.
- Camp Fire: 85 deaths and 18,804 structures match CAL FIRE-based lists.
- Lahaina at about $6B: governor cited nearly $6B, Commerce's official total was $5.5B in 2023 dollars, and CPI adjustment to 2026 dollars is about $6B.
- Spokane $1.1B is within Cotality's $1.0-1.3B preliminary insured-loss estimate.

**Minor errors**
- "More than three-quarters since 2016" works out to 23/31 = 74.2% on the article's own counts.
- "At least 28 times" works out to $167B / $6B = 27.8x.
- Tubbs structures are listed as 7,010. CAL FIRE-based sources give 5,636-5,643. Deaths (22) are correct.
- Spokane Complex burned "3,176 acres." Reports give about 8,000-10,546 acres for the fires combined. 3,176 may be one component fire.

**Unsupported (not confirmed or refuted)**
- Per-fire Palisades, Eaton and Oakland Hills figures. NBC's report of more than $61B for the LA fires combined is consistent with $36.3B + $26.8B = $63.1B in 2026 dollars.
- Colorado $4.1B and Oregon $3.5B.
- Wildfires as third most frequent (22 events) and third most costly ($162B) type in 2016-2025.
- Legacy-method 25 events and $221B, which are available only on request.
- 2024, 2017, 2021 and 2026 year totals.

CAL FIRE's PDF returned HTTP 403. The database page was truncated at 20,000 characters and its wildfire data loads interactively.

**Misleading-framing flags**
1. The new individual-fire accounting (31 events, $185.6B) replaces legacy yearly aggregation (25 events, $221B). The "rising frequency" is partly a method change, and counts are not comparable to the old series.
2. Rising cost reflects exposure growth as well as hazard. The article concedes this, but the headline reads as a hazard trend.
3. The 20-year windows include a partial, preliminary 2026.
4. The piece is self-referential. The publisher owns the database it reports on.
