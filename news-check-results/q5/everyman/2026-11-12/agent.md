## Overall rating
Provisional: 1 Pinocchio (a few shots, mostly accurate), with low confidence. The checked facts (H.R. 1 cost-share provisions, direction and scale of the SNAP child decline, arithmetic) hold up. The headline numbers are unverified against primary data.

## Misleading or missing context
- Causation. The piece ties the child SNAP drop to H.R. 1 and Medicaid red tape. These are time-correlated state totals, not matched children. The article says so in its data note, but the headline "losing both" implies the same children lose both programs. State totals do not show that.
- Comparison design. The article compares a SNAP window ending May 2026 (26 states) with a Medicaid window ending Aug 2026 (all states), and only 26 states have comparable SNAP data. The 2.5x ratio is for those states.
- Advocacy source. CCF is an advocacy organization using its own tracker plus CBPP. The article's policy framing (an "unwelcome mat") is opinion.
- Some SNAP decline predates H.R. 1 and part of any decline may reflect cleanup of ineligible cases or economic factors. CBPP says reduced need is very unlikely to explain it.
