# Fact-check: "Ahead of midterms, backlash grows over soaring electricity bills" (ABC News, Elizabeth Schulze, Oct 29, 2026)

Method: the ABC page was fetched, but only as a summary. I could not read the exact wording. I checked claims against web search results. I did not reach EIA or Ipsos primary tables directly, so most grades are "consistent with secondary sources", not "confirmed at the primary source".

## Overall rating: 1 Pinocchio (on the 0-4 scale, a minor concern)

No factual error was found. The national, Maine and Ohio price figures fall in a plausible range given the sources I could reach. The weakest points are that the 57% poll figure and the Robinson quote could not be independently confirmed, and the Ohio number is not exactly matched.

## Possibly misleading context (the facts may be right, but the framing needs care)

- **Cherry-picked states.** The headline says "soaring", but the national rise is about 5%, roughly in line with a typical year. The 16% and 12% figures are among the largest in the country. Because the piece highlights states with competitive Senate races, readers may overestimate how typical those increases are.
- **Metric mismatch.** The "5 of the 10 states" finding combines price changes with political competitiveness, which makes a causal political story look stronger than it is. Price per kWh is not the same as the total bill, which also depends on usage and weather.
- **The $850 anecdote.** A bill of $850 is a dramatic outlier. Next to the Loudoun data-center discussion it suggests a data-center link that the article's own "mixed evidence" line says is unproven.
- **Poll time frame.** The 45% baseline is from February. The 57% figure is not in any independent release I could find.

## Limits

I read ABC's text only as a summary and did not pull the EIA Electric Power Monthly tables or the Ipsos topline. The EIA figures came from third-party pages. For a firmer grade, check EIA Table 5.6.A for the month and state used, and the ABC/Post/Ipsos release for the 57% figure.
