# Fact-check: CRFB "Trump CEA Projections Tracker" (Nov 11, 2026)

Overall rating: 1 Pinocchio (mostly accurate, with caveats). This is a limited-verification result. Many figures could not be confirmed against primary sources in this session.

## Verified (primary or near-primary sources)
- FY2026 deficit about $2.0T: CBO's Monthly Budget Review (published Nov 9, 2026) says $2.0T, up $217B (12%) from FY2025 and $140B above CBO's Feb 2026 baseline. Confirmed via search snippet and AAF summary.
- FY2026 revenue about $5.4T and outlays $7.4T: consistent with AAF's summary of CBO. Receipts up 3%.
- CBO estimates OBBBA adds about $4.1T conventionally through 2034: consistent with CRFB and other coverage.
- CEA did publish a chart book and a June 2025 release claiming deficit and debt reduction and much higher growth: confirmed to exist. Its growth claims are widely described as very optimistic (Fiscal Times, CRFB "Fantastical Economic Assumptions").
- Chart-book values were reconstructed by CRFB from graphics and may not match exactly: CRFB discloses this itself, which is accurate and fair.

## Not checkable here
- CEA's specific series (4% growth through 2028, 97% and 94% debt/GDP, $1.7T and $1.5T deficits, $660B and $35B primary balances). The chart-book PDF was unreadable through WebFetch, and search did not surface these numbers.
- CBO's FY2026 primary deficit of $941B and the FY2025 figures of $805B, $1.8T, and 17.2% revenue share. The CBO MBR PDF returned a 403 error.
- BEA GDP growth of 2.5% (FY2025) and 2.2% (FY2026), and CRFB's 100% debt/GDP estimate.
- CBO Feb 2026 baseline details (116% debt in 2034, $2.8T deficit), the OMB FY2027 budget figures, and the Fed Sept 2026 projections.
- The CRFB estimate that Section 301/338 tariffs replace about 70% of the lost IEEPA revenue, and the SCOTUS ruling details.

## Misleading or noteworthy
- The headline claim "none of CEA's projections have materialized" is strong. It compares a policy-scenario projection (assuming OBBBA plus tariffs) against realized outcomes shaped by many other factors. CRFB discloses this caveat, which mitigates the problem.
- Internal inconsistency: the piece says new tariffs replace "about 70%" of lost revenue, but the linked analysis title says "less than 60%". The two need reconciling.
- The comparison is against a CEA chart-book approximation, not CEA's underlying data. Revenue figures are CRFB-derived "implied" values, not CEA-published numbers.
- Partisan-adjacent framing: CRFB is a deficit-hawk advocacy group. It does not omit the OMB's more optimistic forecast, which is a point in its favor.

## Verdict
1 Pinocchio. The data direction (deficits near $2T, debt rising, growth below CEA's) is supported where it could be checked. Most numeric specifics remained unverified in this session.
