# Fact-check: Inside Climate News, "Maine storms / power bill costs" (25 Nov 2026)

Limits: WebFetch returned only a summary of the article, not verbatim text. Attributed quotes could not be extracted, so they are "not checkable here". Claims below are the ones the summary conveyed. Python and charts were not used.

| Claim | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Maine prices up 64% 2019-2025 vs 38% national (EIA) | Not confirmed | I found no EIA figure for exactly this window. Related figures (EIA 2015-2025 about +78%; 2014-2024 Maine more than twice the national rate) point the same way. Outcomes depend on the base year and on nominal vs real terms. Maine's Public Advocate disputes some 2025 spike figures. |
| PUC rejected temporary $69M increase in October | Confirmed | PUC denied about $69.3M on Oct 6, 2026, by a unanimous vote. The reason was no "undisputed" amount, as state law requires. Chair Bartlett: "a delay, not a no". |
| PUC decision on main case expected April 2027 | Plausible | Sources say "spring 2027". The main request is $189M in new revenue, filed April 17, 2026. |
| CMP request would raise the average bill about $18/month | Not confirmed | The temporary piece was about $7/month. I did not find an $18 figure for the full request. |
| FERC ordered New England refunds of about $1.5B, about $130M to Maine | Partly confirmed | FERC cut the base ROE to 9.57% in March 2026 and backdated it to 2014. The Maine Public Advocate cites more than $1.5B. Massachusetts and Connecticut advocates estimate about $900M. The $130M Maine share is reported only secondhand (Maine Monitor via The Cooldown). The refunds are being appealed, and FERC refused to stay them. |
| CMP storm costs above $600M, 2021-2025; Maine most outages per customer in 2023-24; liability cap structure; Versant five-year average | Not checked | I ran out of time and did not reach primary sources. |
| ROE expert witness testimony | Not checkable here | The testimony is in the rate-case docket, which I did not reach. |

Pinocchio rating: 1 (mostly accurate), provisional. The checked claims largely hold. The weak points are figures that vary with the chosen window and measure.

Misleading risks:
- The refund total is presented without noting the contested $900M-$1.5B range and the pending appeal.
- The $18/month figure may be conflated with the denied temporary increase of about $7/month.
- Attributing price rises to storms alone overlooks other drivers in the Public Advocate's and Brattle's cost-driver analyses.
