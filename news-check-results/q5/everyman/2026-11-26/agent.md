# Fact-check: White House release "U.S. Drug Overdose Deaths Decrease for 18th Consecutive Month Under Trump Administration" (Nov 2026)

Primary source used: CDC NCHS VSRR provisional drug overdose counts, data.cdc.gov dataset xkb8-kh2a (national rows, "data as of" Nov 1 2026). Note: WebFetch returned a model summary of the release, not verbatim text, so wording below is as summarized.

## Claims and verdicts

| # | Claim | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ~65,724 predicted overdose deaths, 12 months ending June 2026 | TRUE | CDC predicted_value 65,724 (reported 64,433). |
| 2 | 112,418 peak under previous administration | TRUE | Predicted peak is 112,418, 12 months ending Aug 2023 (Biden era). |
| 3 | ~41.5% below peak | TRUE | 65,724 / 112,418 = -41.5%. |
| 4 | Lowest since December 2016 | NOT PRECISELY CHECKED | Not pulled the 2016-17 series. Plausible (2016 about 64k) but the exact month was not verified. |

Year-over-year, the 12-month total fell 11.3% (74,120 to 65,724). Axios (3 Nov 2026) headline reports a ~12% drop; I could not fetch it (403).

## Material context missing or misleading

1. Attribution/timing. The decline began after the Aug 2023 peak. The predicted 12-month total was already 81,313 by Dec 2024 and 79,626 by Jan 2025. About 31,100 of the roughly 46,700-death fall (about two-thirds) occurred before Jan 2025. "Under Trump Administration" counts months, but the trend and its pace of decline predate it; the monthly drops were actually larger in 2024 (about 2,000-2,600 per month) than in 2025-26 (about 500-1,000 per month). The slowdown is not mentioned. The "41.5% from peak" figure mostly measures Biden-era decline.
2. Provisional data. The June 2026 count is predicted, with about 26% of records pending investigation and CDC footnoting recent months as underreported. Final figures typically rise, so declines may be somewhat overstated. Oregon and some states have reporting delays.
3. Variation: some states (e.g. Colorado) show increases year over year; the national figure hides that.
4. Drug categories: methadone fell only 1.1%, and the cocaine and prescription-opioid declines are small relative to the headline.
5. Source is the administration's own press release; it is advocacy, not independent reporting.

## Rating (Washington Post Fact Checker scale)

**2 Pinocchios.** The numbers are accurate and traceable to CDC. The framing implies the administration is responsible for a decline that began about 16 months before it took office and was already two-thirds complete, and it omits the data's provisional status and the slowing pace.
