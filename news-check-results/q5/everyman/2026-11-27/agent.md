# Fact check: Patently-O, "Issue Fee to Grant: Record Weekly Patent Issues Are a USPTO Catch-Up" (Dennis Crouch, Nov 24, 2026)

Access: only the opening section was readable (paywalled for the rest). The USPTO Official Gazette confirms issue dates but publishes no per-issue grant counts on the pages I could read. No independent source for the counts turned up in web search. I did not generate a chart.

## Claims
| # | Claim | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 9,828 utility patents issued Nov 3, 2026 | Not checkable here. The Gazette has a Nov 3 issue (index confirms it exists), but no count is shown. |
| 2 | Largest weekly issue in records back to 1976 | Not checkable here (needs USPTO bulk data or PatentsView). |
| 3 | Nov 10, 17, 24 issues rank 2nd-4th | Issue dates confirmed to exist (Nov 10 is Vol. 1552 No. 2). Ranks not checkable. |
| 4 | Previous record 7,837 on Oct 3, 2023 | Date was a Tuesday, a normal issue day. Count not checkable. |
| 5 | Crouch wrote in October that issue-fee-to-grant wait had doubled after an earlier fee-filing surge | Not checkable. Searches did not surface the post. |
| 6 | Nov 24 issue had a 49-day wait; further decline expected | Not checkable (the 49 days is the author's own metric; the expectation is a forecast). |
| 7 | Chart: weekly issues vs issue fees paid, Jan 2025 - Nov 24, 2026 | Chart not examined. |

Quotes: only one, the 1976 record phrase, which is the author's own wording.

## Rating
Pinocchio scale: no rating assigned (0-4 not applicable). The visible claims are plausible and internally consistent, but I could verify none of the numbers. Calling it Four Pinocchios or Geppetto would both overstate what I know.

## Possible misleading framing
- "Record" refers to a weekly count in a single week, and the author himself frames it as catch-up of a backlog rather than higher productivity. A reader skimming the headline could read it as a surge in output; the piece itself says otherwise.
- The rest of the article (methodology, any caveats) was behind the paywall, so I cannot judge it.

Verdict: unverified, not disproved. A proper check needs USPTO weekly grant counts from PatentsView or the Official Gazette bulk data.
