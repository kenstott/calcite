# Fact-check: CSM "Their harvest is plentiful. Why are US farmers struggling?" (2026-11-10)

## Access limits
The article body could not be read. WebFetch returned HTTP 403 on both URL variants, and I found no copy of the text. I only have search-engine summaries of it, so a claim-by-claim check of the full text is not possible. Everything below covers only the claims those summaries reported.

## Claims reported in summaries, and what I could check

1. USDA expects 2026 net farm income to fall 5.5% from 2025 after inflation. SUPPORTED. USDA ERS farm income forecast (released 2026-09-03): net farm income $158.4B, down $9.1B (5.5%) in real terms from 2025.
2. Government support rises sharply. SUPPORTED. ERS shows direct government payments of $27.9B in 2025 and a forecast $47.4B in 2026. A separate estimate cited elsewhere was $44.3B, so figures differ by source and date. I could not see which number CSM used.
3. The 2026 harvest is plentiful but profits are thin, mainly for Midwest corn and soybean growers. PLAUSIBLE. Consistent with the same ERS forecast and other coverage. I did not pull USDA crop production or price data to confirm it.
4. Wars are raising fuel and fertilizer costs. PLAUSIBLE. CSM's own March 2026 piece tied fertilizer spikes to Strait of Hormuz disruption, and a Minnesota report cites higher diesel costs. I did not check primary price series (USDA AMS or EIA).
5. Tariffs raised equipment costs and cut foreign market access. NOT CHECKED. No primary source examined.
6. The Trump administration repeatedly paid aid to offset tariff damage, and many farmers dislike relying on it. The payments part is consistent with ERS data. The farmer sentiment part is NOT CHECKABLE HERE, since I cannot see which farmers were quoted.
7. A strong harvest can ease inflation. NOT CHECKED.

## Possible misleading framing (cannot confirm without the text)
- Real net farm income is falling from 2025, but the nominal level ($158.4B) is high by historical standards, and government payments make up a large share of it. Whether CSM says this is unknown.
- A "bounty" combined with low prices is a standard story, and the blame is split among tariffs, wars and costs. Whether CSM weights these fairly is unknown.
