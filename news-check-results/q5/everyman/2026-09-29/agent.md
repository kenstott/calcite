# Fact-check: Lawmakers' Sept 24, 2026 letter on DCMP funding for Eaton/Palisades fire survivors

## Verdict: Largely accurate. The claim's sequence of events and dollar figures check out against the lawmakers' own press release and independent local news coverage.

## What's confirmed

**The letter and its signers.** On September 24, 2026, Rep. Judy Chu (CA-28), Rep. Brad Sherman (CA-32), Sen. Adam Schiff (D-CA), and Sen. Alex Padilla (D-CA) sent a follow-up letter to FEMA Administrator Cameron Hamilton. This is confirmed directly by Rep. Chu's official House press release ("Reps. Chu, Sherman, Sens. Schiff, Padilla Press FEMA Again to Prevent Shutdown of Critical Wildfire Recovery Program," chu.house.gov) and corroborated by independent coverage (Pasadena Now, ColoradoBoulevard.net).

**The Disaster Case Management Program (DCMP) is real** and serves survivors of the January 2025 Eaton and Palisades Fires. FEMA approved it to run 24 months with roughly $13 million in total funding, of which only about $3 million had actually been released to the state/local providers as of the letter. Program providers report having served 3,000+ survivors, with (per the letter) 1,288 active cases, 84 people on an immediate waitlist, 1,023 high-complexity cases, and roughly 7,000 people awaiting case-manager assignment — figures that illustrate real ongoing need, not a wind-down.

**FEMA Region 9's September 11 denial.** Confirmed: FEMA Region 9 denied California's request for an additional **$6,568,265.60** (~$6.57 million) needed to sustain DCMP, citing insufficient cost justification for funds beyond the original award. California has appealed. This matches the claim precisely, including the date.

**The unanswered September 10 letter.** Confirmed: the same four lawmakers sent an earlier letter on September 10, 2026 demanding release of outstanding DCMP funding (including remaining installments of the existing award), and per the September 24 follow-up, that demand had gone unanswered. The letter also set a September 28 deadline for FEMA to respond — which passed without the requested action, prompting continued pressure as of this writing (September 29).

**The September 30 shutdown risk was real and imminent** at time of writing — DCMP services were set to lapse on that date absent FEMA action, and related rental assistance was separately reported as expiring October 9, 2026.

**What the letter asks for**, matching the claim: (1) grant California's appeal and approve the additional $6.57M, (2) release the remaining installments of the existing ~$13M award (three installments were reportedly still under review), and (3) prevent a service interruption for survivors mid-recovery.

## Context: is $6.57M large or small relative to total federal response?

The $6.57M figure is very small in the context of the overall federal response to the January 2025 Eaton/Palisades fires:
- More than **$177 million** had been awarded to over 35,000 households through FEMA's Individuals and Households Program as of June 2026.
- More than **$2.5 billion** in FEMA public-assistance projects remained pending as of September 2026.
- Gov. Newsom had separately requested a **$34.2 billion** federal supplemental to replenish FEMA's Disaster Relief Fund, SBA's Disaster Loan Program, and other long-term recovery programs.
- The Army Corps of Engineers debris-removal effort alone cleared over 2.5 million tons of material from thousands of properties.

So $6.57 million is a small fraction — roughly 0.02% of the pending public-assistance total, and negligible relative to the tens of billions sought for the broader recovery — even though it is significant relative to DCMP's own ~$13 million total award (about half of it).

## Minor caveats
- Figures (case counts, waitlist numbers) come from the lawmakers' letter/press release itself, not an independently audited FEMA source, though they are consistent with prior reporting on DCMP caseloads.
- I could not independently verify FEMA's internal rationale for the September 11 denial beyond "insufficient cost justification," as reported; FEMA's own statement on the denial was not located in a primary FEMA source during this search, only in secondary press coverage.
