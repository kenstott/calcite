Method: WebFetch of the article (summarized by the fetch tool, not verbatim), plus WebSearch checks. Verification was limited; primary documents (court filings, statutes, CHQPR report, CDC tables) were not opened directly.

## Context and potential misleading elements
- The $951M headline anchors the story, but it is a default judgment, not a jury verdict, against a bankrupt defendant; the family has not been paid. The article discloses this, but the opening still gives an impression of typical verdict exposure. Doroshow's point that such verdicts are rarely paid is the counterweight.
- The causal link between malpractice law and OB-GYN supply is weakly evidenced. The piece itself also cites abortion bans, Medicaid reimbursement, and private equity as drivers; the Idaho exodus is largely tied to abortion law, not liability.
- Sponsor claim (one birth injury suit in 10 years) cuts against the premise that liability drives shortages, and is unverified.
- A Washington Post Fact Checker style note: CHQPR's counts shift between rural-only and all-hospital tallies and between report dates, so "146" needs its definition.

## Rating: 1 Pinocchio (mostly accurate)
Verified claims hold up, with sourcing mainly via interviews and advocacy-group data. Main weakness is framing and unverified attributed numbers, not demonstrated errors.
